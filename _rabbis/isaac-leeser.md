Isaac Leeser was born in Westphalia and came to America as a young man in 1824, settling first in Richmond, Virginia. In 1829 he was called to serve as hazzan of Congregation Mikveh Israel in Philadelphia, where he introduced the regular English-language sermon to the American synagogue — the discourses collected in this library are the fruit of that pulpit.

He founded and edited *The Occident and American Jewish Advocate* (from 1843), produced an English translation of the Hebrew Bible (1853), and helped establish many of the institutions of early American Jewish life, among them the first Hebrew Sunday School in Philadelphia and Maimonides College.
