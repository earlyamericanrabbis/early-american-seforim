Rabbi Tzvi Avraham Henry (c. 1800–1879) served as Rav in Cincinnati, Syracuse and Manhattan, and was the first Rav in San Francisco. He learned at the Jews' Free School in London and received semicha from Rabbi Shlomo Hirschell, Chief Rabbi of England. He was a relative of Rabbi Yaakov Moshe Raphall.

He was the mechaber of several translations of siddurim and machzorim, *A Class Book for Jewish Youth of Both Sexes*, *A Series of Six Discourses on the Principles of Jewish Belief*, *Religious Belief of Israel as Productive of Human Happiness and Moral Improvement*, *Discourses on the Book of Genesis*, and several books on Jewish history, as well as many articles in periodicals and newspapers in every city he lived in, mainly London.
