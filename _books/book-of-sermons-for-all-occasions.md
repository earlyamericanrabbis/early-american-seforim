Volume III of *Kisvei Rav Yeshayahu Glazer*, the collected writings of Rabbi Simon (Yeshayahu) Glazer, edited, annotated and expanded by Simon Springer. Sermons for the Yomim Tovim and for every occasion in the life of a Jew and a community.

## Preface

The need for a volume of sermons based upon Biblical and Talmudical texts is apparent from the very meagerness of the supply now available. Preaching is becoming the main work of the modern Rabbi. The traditional "two-drashot-per-annum function is now extinct. The Rabbi hardly comes in touch with his communicants, save only with the few leaders. To the majority of the members he remains a stranger. His whole career depends upon his ability to preach. As Israel advances in years on this hemisphere, the gradual departure from the Old-Country customs becomes more conspicuous every year. No longer is the Rabbi the actual head, the real teacher, the spiritual father of his community. Every congregation is a community by itself. The system of double-membership, followed by the better-to-do element, oftentime affords one member to have the choice of two Rabbis; sometimes, two Rabbis of two different schools, and, of course, the comparison between the two Rabbis is based solely upon the sermons.

This work is completely taken out from my Hebrew work "The Vision of Isaiah", and because the matter contained therein was tested and purged so many times in the past thirty years, I desired to preserve the original Hebrew name. Of course, this entire work is only about one per cent. of the matter contained in the "Vision of Isaiah"; but the reception American Rabbis and preachers gave my "Bar Mitzvah Pulpit ', which is also a collection from the same source, encouraged me exceedingly to draw upon the spiritual reservoir which served me so well since 1898, when I first commenced to preserve the material I used for sermons.

I really should point out to the Rabbis that this work, because it is so compact, is merely "material" for sermons (Homar Le'drush), and should be treated as such when utilizing its contents before congregations, or other audiences.

RABBI SIMON GLAZER

New York, Tebet, 26, 5690 (Jan. 26, 1930)
