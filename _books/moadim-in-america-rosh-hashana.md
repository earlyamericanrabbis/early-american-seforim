## Foreword

<span dir="rtl">לֹא עָלֶיךָ הַמְּלָאכָה לִגְמֹר, וְלֹא אַתָּה בֶן חוֹרִין לִבָּטֵל מִמֶּנָּה</span>.

*It is not your duty to finish the work, but neither are you at liberty to neglect it.*

Pirkei Avos 2:16

This sefer has come about through hundred of hours of work, research, frustrations, and learning. Through the beginning of the Early American Rabbis project at the end of 2025, one of my unstated (to the public, at least) goals has been to release a set of seforim on the Moadim from the Gedolim who shaped the American Jewish landscape.

Naturally, as I wrap up before Elul 5786, 2026, I am thinking about the next anthology of Divrei Torah for other Moadim, but can’t shake the feeling that the work is never really done for this volume as well. As I wrap this volume up, I have come across dozens of Divrei Torah on Rosh Hashana that have fundamentally changed my outlook on this Yom Tov and my Avodas HaKodesh as well.

Yet, with the extra Divrei Torah being filed away, I’m hopeful it will lead to more Torah and more awareness of the greatness that our American community was built on.

I can only look back at my own personal history, when I first stepped foot into Sephardic Bikur Cholim in Seattle, and Rabbi Simon Benzaquen, shlit”a, stepped down from his seat near the Aron and welcomed me and my neophyte family into a community with love and acceptance and imagine these Rabbanim lovingly shepherd their flocks, both Observant and otherwise, into the loving embrace of community and HaKadosh Baruch Hu.

These masters of walking the fine line of myriad of communal issues and personalities continue to show us the ways of Torah and Avodah, decades after they were received into the Olam HaEmes.

As we enter into 5787, our global community continues to face challenges, both unheard of and those we hoped we would never have to face again, the giants of the previous generations can help us navigate with pride, safety, and Emunah Shleimah, if you’re willing to listen and learn.

In the following derashos, you will read from Chasidim, Litvaks, and Sephardim. Those aligned with Mizrachi and those aligned with the Agudas HaRabbanim. You will see that their differences were in approach only, but the end goal was the same: Achdus and a relationship with God. Worthy goals.

It is my sincere tefillah that, like me, the reader is changed on the deepest of levels, taking an accounting of their lives and changing positively towards to Geulah Shleimah.

I can never not thank my incredible family: My wife, Danielle, and my children, Michal, Hadassah, and Yosef Chaim Moshe. Your support in this project is, perhaps, the reason that the “stars aligned” and the Yad Hashem has shown itself in its success time and time again. To the hundreds of followers on WhatsApp, Instagram, X, Facebook, the Website, and Amazon, who have liked, commented, purchased, reached out privately – you have made this entire project worth it.

When you’re sitting at home or in your respective shuls and you open this sefer, you’re opening a view into a world that we think doesn’t exist anymore, but is very much still here and still thriving, different maybe, but still thriving. Reflect on what you read and imagine yourself listening to these master darshanim extolling the virtues of Emes, Teshuva, Torah and Mitzvos. Imagine their words shaking your very world to its core, only to be rebuilt as an even stronger Oved Hashem. You will not be disappointed in your transformation.

Our work continues…

Shimon Shlomo Springer

South Bend, Indiana
