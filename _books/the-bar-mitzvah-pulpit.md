Volume I of *Kisvei Rav Yeshayahu Glazer*, the collected writings of Rabbi Simon (Yeshayahu) Glazer, edited, annotated and expanded by Simon Springer. A sermonette for a bar mitzvah boy on every parsha of the Torah.

## Foreword

The present volume is published at this time, because there is a real need for such sermonettes. Rabbis know the kind of ‘speeches’ young boys are taught to deliver before large congregations,

Every paragraph is an excerpt from my Hebrew book, ‘‘The Vision of Isaiah.” I do not give references, as I really want it to be studied, and delivered by young people.

The discriminating in taste will recognize the care and effort of this work, in dealing out to young Jewish children, in the English-speaking countries, the fundamental principles of Judaism in a clear, classical language.

Rabbis and preachers, may find in this work a ready fountain of pure material for longer sermons. “This was my aim in picking out some gems from “The Vision of Isaiah,’’ and exhibiting them in a setting which equals the original.

It is my belief that the Orthodox pulpit needs English sermons of this type. There are very few publications available, out of which the Orthodox Rabbi can draw upon for the use in the synagogues. which are becoming more and more filled with English-speaking worshipers.

SIMON GLAZER,

Rabbi Temple Beth-El of Borough Park, Brooklyn, N.Y.,

Tebet, 11, 5688—Jan. 4, 1928.

## From the Editor

There was a point where my only goal was to find interesting seforim written in America by long-forgotten Rabbanim who grace old shul documents and various shelves, collecting dust.

Then came the onslaught of seforim and Rabbanim one day in December 2025 and the idea to find and reprint started becoming a reality.

Finding this sefer from Rav Glazer זצ״ל, has been an experience of a lifetime. Besides for cutting my teeth on editing and formatting, the conversations I’ve had with friends and Rabbanim really opened by eyes to the utmost care and leadership that Rav Glazer had for his congregation.

Just imagine this scenario – an Orthodox Rav, before the Holocaust, before the Gedolim we know of today that formed American Jewry, Rav Moshe Feinstein and Rav Aharon Kotler, there were giants here, setting the foundation for the explosion of Orthodoxy in America that we see today.

Rav Glazer spent time as a leader to congregations in Seattle, Des Moines, Kansas, Montreal. Rabbanim like HaRav Papermaster in Forks, North Dakota, Rav Avraham Rice and Rav Yitzchak Leeser, Rabbanim in the Philadelphia area around the Civil War, fighting for the freedoms Orthodoxy holds today.

As Orthodox Jews in America, it behooves us to understand what these Gedolim did and established for us so we can then understand where we can go to from here.

It has been one of the many great zechusim of my lifetime to work on this. Never in a million years would I think a Baal Teshuva, Bibliophile, living in South Bend, Indiana, but from Woodinville, Washington, would have the ability to do something like this, but as my Grandfather once told me, History isn’t a subject. Our history as Orthodox Jews is too important to forget. So I can do my little part here to help remember.

Some points to ponder:

- This sefer was written around 1928. I tried to keep his voice at that time period. All translations of pesukim are his, how they would have been spoken at that time;

- I encourage everyone to take the time, when reading this, to ALSO look up the maarei makomos, it’ll help you understand the depth of knowledge that Rav Glazer had, but integrated into such a seemingly simple sefer;

- This sefer was written at a time when most Bar Mitzvah bachurim were not in cheder, not in Yeshivos, most likely not even in Talmudei Torah. As I said before, the care taken by Rav Glazer for these members of his congregation took all of this in mind to create a drasha for a bachur who likely had very little, in terms of Torah, under his belt.

This is the first book I am republishing. IYH, there will be many more. The importance of this work is clear to me: We have giants in Torah that need to be remembered, their Torah learned, to help us navigate the time in which we live. They set the foundations in America, and I believe, its time to get back to the basics and strengthen ourselves in the Yiddeshkeit we love.
