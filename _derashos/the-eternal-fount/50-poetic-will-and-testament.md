<span dir="rtl">האזינו השמים ואדברה ותשמע הארץ אמרי-פי</span> “Give ear, O ye heavens, and I will speak; and hear, O earth, the words of my mouth.” Isaiah begins differently: <span dir="rtl">שמעו שמים והאזיני ארץ</span> “Hear, O ye heavens, and give ear, O earth.” The sages thus distinguish the two utterances:

<span dir="rtl">לפי שהיה משה קרוב לשמים ורחוק מארץ, לפיכך אמר האזינו השמים ותשמע הארץ</span> “Since Moses was near to Heaven and far from earth, he addressed the heavens with the word *haazinu*, ‘give ear,’ and the earth with *tishma*, ‘hear.’ But Isaiah was nearer to earth than to Heaven; hence he reversed the forms of address” (Sifri). To give ear is to listen to a quietly presented secret; while to hear implies a loud declaration readily caught by others.

This interpretation of the sages provides a suitable estimate of the true greatness of Moses, as contrasted with the world’s later prophets and lawgivers. Moses always stood close to Heaven; the fundamental aim of his teaching was to bring earth nearer to the heights, to offer mankind a more lofty ideal to strive for than satisfaction of material and bodily needs. <span dir="rtl">ונשמרתם מאד לנפשתיכם</span> “Take ye therefore good heed unto yourselves (your souls)” (iv, 15). Keep your souls clean and sound, so that Heaven need not be ashamed of them. This is the essence of the Torah, taught to Israel in order that the people undertake the mission of spreading its ideals among all peoples. Hence the Law speaks to both heaven and earth, forasmuch as <span dir="rtl">אין הקב</span>”<span dir="rtl">ה גואל את ישראל אלא על ידיהן</span> “The Holy One blessed be He will redeem Israel only by virtue of both of them” (Midrash Rabbah). “In Israel’s redemption, which includes the liberation of all mankind, the earthly must be bound up with the heavenly; there must be a synthesis of soul and body” (Rabbi Jacob Levinson).

The Midrash Tanhuma offers an additional explanation of the invitation by Moses to heaven and earth, to hear his last will and testament:

<span dir="rtl">לפי שהם קיימין לעולם וישראל קיימין לעולם, לכך הזמינן לעדות שיקיימו ישראל התורה והמצוות</span>

“Insomuch as these will exist forever and Israel too will exist forever, therefore did he invite them to serve as witnesses that Israel will maintain the Torah and the divine commandments.” Eternity is the basis of Moses’ teaching. God is eternal, His Torah is eternal, and His people will live eternally. The Torah is not a national constitution, devised for a specific time under specific circumstances. <span dir="rtl">לא תהא מוחלפת</span> “It cannot be altered or amended,” for it embraces all generations and all times. It is a book of laws for eternity, to which men can at all times refer for proper conduct of their lives, which they may thus render immortal. God created heaven and earth for all time, but their existence is dependent on the practice of the Law; if Torah does not exist, neither will heaven and earth; and all will return to primordial chaos.

## Torah and Life

<span dir="rtl">יערף כמטר לקחי</span> “My doctrine shall drop as the rain” (xxxii, 2). <span dir="rtl">מה מטר חיים לעולם אף דברי תורה חיים לעולם</span> “Just as rain brings life to the world, so does the Torah bring life to the world” (Sifri). Ignorance bears in its train chaos and destruction; it puts a halt to human progress and development.

<span dir="rtl">על כל עשב ועשב עומד עליו מלאך ומכהו ואומר לו גדל</span> “Over every blade of grass stands an angel, to give it the impulse and the order to grow.” Inertia is overcome by idealism, which creates enterprise; without ideal motives the world remains stagnant and immovable. All achievement must be motivated by some spiritual source; without it only purposeless dissolution can follow. As the rain provides the earth with the possibility of producing, so does the Torah give men the initiative to soar ever higher and higher.

<span dir="rtl">תזל כטל אמרתי</span> “My speech shall flow as the dew.” <span dir="rtl">מה טל כל העולם שמחים בו אף דברי תורה כל העולם שמחים בם</span> “Just as dew brings joy to all the universe, so do the words of the Torah bring joy to all the universe” (Sifri). The Torah was not devised for a specific sect or group; like the dew, it descends over all creation, without distinction. Many ideal undertakings are restrained by limitations, by laws that exclude certain classes from their functioning and benefits. The Torah is the dew that is democratically intended for all men alike.

“My speech shall flow as the dew” <span dir="rtl">מה מטר משחקת אבנים אף התורה משחקת לב האבן</span> “Just as rain abrades stone, so does the Torah wear down the stony heart” (Tanhuma). The Torah serves to improve human character; its laws ennoble and soften human hearts, creating an understanding of the problems of brethren in other circumstances. <span dir="rtl">קודם שלא אכל אדם ושתה יש לו שני לבבות, לאחר שאוכל ושותה אין לו אלא לב אחד</span> “Before a man has partaken of food and drink, he has two hearts; after he has eaten and drunk, he has but one heart” (Baba Batra 12). This passage is explained by the “Shpaler Grandfather”: “The poor man, lone and hungering, has two hearts, for he senses the heart of others in the same circumstances. But the rich, sated person has one heart, his own — he does not sense the heart of another. He is not prepared to enter into the spirit of other men, but is like a beast that lives for itself alone.”

<span dir="rtl">כי שם ה</span>’ <span dir="rtl">אקרא הבו גדל לאלהינו</span> “Because I will call the name of the Eternal; ascribe ye greatness unto our God” (3). From this sentence the sages have derived the requirements of reciting a benediction both before and after one is called to the reading of the Law (Jerusalem Berakot ch. 8). The Maggid of Kaminetz thus addressed a Bar Mitzvah boy in Whitechapel, London: “Many of our parents are satisfied with the prior benediction — which means the instruction they give their sons in the prophetic portions before they become Bar Mitzvah. But after the Bar Mitzvah ceremony they no longer are interested in the sons’ Jewish education, and permit them to grow up ignorant of matters Judaic. It is because of this that the Jewish spirit in England, France, and America, has been so unproductive during the last century. If we wish our young to retain their Judaism, we must see that they recite the latter benediction, in that they continue their Jewish studies until they are able to appreciate the greatness of the Torah. Because of the ignorance of the current generation, their nationalist feelings have been enfeebled, and they fall into despair in times of Jewish stress. If our children are given a basic Jewish training, and an acquaintance with the imposing spiritual treasures of our people, their faith in God will be strengthened, and they will derive hope and courage to fight for our ultimate redemption.”

## God’s Own

<span dir="rtl">הלא הוא אביך קנך</span> “Is not He thy father that hath obtained thee?” (6). Pirke DeRabbi Eliezer relates a legend that when the generation of Babel was about to be divided into the various classes of human beings, God gathered all his angels about him to assist in separating men into their nationalities and tongues. He suggested that lots be cast to decide which celestial being should have control over which division. <span dir="rtl">הטילו גורלות ונפל ישראל לחלקו של הקב</span>”<span dir="rtl">ה</span> “They cast the lots; and the Jewish people was allotted to the Holy One blessed be He.” Since then He has kept us as His peculiar possession, and we have been called <span dir="rtl">עם ה</span>’ “God’s people.”

<span dir="rtl">שחת לו לא בניו מומם</span> “The corruption is not his; it is that of his children, the blemish is theirs” (5). This can be otherwise translated to mean if Jews are corrupted by denying themselves as God’s children, the fault is their own. When Jews no longer call themselves children of Israel, but become Germans or Frenchmen “of the Mosaic persuasion,” they lose their uniqueness as Jews, and are found faulty by the very ethnic groups they attempt to emulate, who accuse them of disloyalty and treason. Those who are not loyal to their own origins cannot expect to be accounted loyal citizens of other nations.

<span dir="rtl">אל אמונה ואין עול</span> He is “a God of faithfulness and without iniquity.” If one at times feels that God has been unjust to one, one must not sin by bringing accusations against the Lord. We cannot understand His ways; we must retain our faith. Once Rabbi Moses Leib Sasover cried out, during the reading of the weekly portion: “Master of the universe! You wish us to have faith in You even though we do not understand Your ways, and to believe that there is no iniquity in You. Repay us after the same fashion! Even though now You see no good deeds among us, have faith that ultimately we shall change and become true penitents!”
