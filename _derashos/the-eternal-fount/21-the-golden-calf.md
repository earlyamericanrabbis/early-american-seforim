After the giving of the Law, Moses spent forty days and forty nights in the heavenly realms. Only the Ten Commandments were handed down to Israel at Mount Sinai. These were merely heard by them: <span dir="rtl">וכל העם רואים את הקולות</span> “And all the people saw the thunderings and the flaming torches” (xx, 15) — and thereby understood that they were hearkening to the voice of God. But His words had to be set down for future ages; hence we read, <span dir="rtl">מכתב אלהים חרות על הלוחות</span> “The writing of God was engraved on the tablets.” The Talmud says, Do not read the word as *charut* (engraved), but as *cherut* (freedom) (Erubin 54). Words etched by the hand of God Himself are aimed to bring freedom to man and permit him to develop spiritually.

In addition to the Ten Commandments, Moses was impelled to take over the entire Torah, with all its laws and judgments. He was also to be taught the true meaning of every passage, so that he might transmit the right doctrine to the generations yet to come.

And all this he learnt during his forty days and forty nights on high.

It must not be forgotten that tradition declares all of Jewish thought, with all its later interpretations and development, to have been given Israel on Mount Sinai. <span dir="rtl">מה שהנביאים עתידים להתנבאות בכל דור ודור קבלו מהר סיני</span> “Whatever the prophets were destined to prophecy in future generations was transmitted to them on Mount Sinai” (Exodus Rabbah 28). The Torah remains the essence of the Jewish world outlook; all that may enter the minds of seers and sages is already indicated therein.

<span dir="rtl">בשעה שעלה משה למרום מצאו להקב</span>”<span dir="rtl">ה שהיה כותר כתרים לאותיות</span> “When, after receiving the Law, Moses ascended to Heaven, he found the Holy One engaged in placing crowns upon the letters of the Law” (Shabbat 89). God did not wish His Law to be formed of dead characters — of rules utterly apart from actual life. Every word, every letter of the Torah was to be a living fact, part of the eternal life which was its crown. On each letter, therefore, God inscribed a crown of immortality. A Torah lacking these scriptural ornamentations is invalid.

## Israel’s Distinctions

When Moses completed his study of the Law and all its details, he turned to inquire of God, <span dir="rtl">במה תרום קרן ישראל</span> “How shall the horn of Israel be exalted?” (Baba Batra 10). For God had given His people so lofty a Torah, with such immortal ideals, that Moses felt it but proper that Israel too should be similarly distinguished among the nations. <span dir="rtl">ונפלינו אני ועמך</span> “Let me and Thy people be distinct from the rest.” <span dir="rtl">יהי רצון שתשרה שכינה על ישראל</span> “May it be Thy will that Thy Divine Presence ever rest over Israel” (Berakot 7).

And God replied: <span dir="rtl">כי תשא את-ראש בני ישראל... ונתנו איש כפר נפשו</span> “When thou takest the sum of the children of Israel.... then shall they give every man a ransom for his soul” (xxx, 12). The strength of the people was not to lie in its physical prowess or numerical extent, but in the spiritual power of its descendants. For physical power does not last; many great nations, numbering countless millions, quickly disappeared from the earth. But if Israel could retain its great soul, its mighty spirit, it would outlive all nations and ultimately attain the high destiny promised by the Lord.

When Moses first heard God’s declaration that every Israelite required a ransom for his soul, he was frightened. He imagined that a large sum would be required, which not every person could provide. <span dir="rtl">א</span>”<span dir="rtl">ל, לא כמו שאתה סבור, אלא זה יתנו, מחצית השקל</span> “God said, It is not as you think; each one must give but half a shekel.” <span dir="rtl">העשיר לא ירבה והדל לא ימעיט</span> “The rich man may not give more, nor the poor man give less” (Tanhuma Ki Tissa). I seek no huge impost from anyone, but that each person take part in this tax; of these shekel funds will the tabernacle be maintained, and also the daily sacrifice (*tamid*), and the light of the menorah. These things combine to form the spiritual basis of Israel; and in them every person must have his share.

<span dir="rtl">ולא-יהיה בהם נגף בפקד אתם</span> “That there be no calamity among them, when thou musterest them.” God declares to Moses His wish that Israel be not enumerated in times of extraordinary calamities, such as war or plague; but that their numbers be established each year through the reckoning of the generally given half shekels. Amiel, in Hezyonot el Ami, offers a comment on this passage which applies even at this day.

“At one time it was very easy to determine the census of Jews in any city. One needed merely to visit the synagogues, and the meat shops, or read in the communal ledger — and know all the Jews. In these days, however, few Jews attend the synagogues; many do not observe the dietary laws; and numbers of them are not even recorded as in the community. Having been largely assimilated among their neighbors, they take little interest in Jewish life. The one thing they universally observe is Jewish burial. After living their days as non-Jews, to all practical purposes, they expect at the end of their years to be interred in a Jewish cemetery. This is the meaning of the biblical phrase, ‘that there be no calamity among them, when thou musterest them.’ The Torah has no wish that a man die ere he be reckoned in the roster of his people.”

## The Golden Calf

Moses, bearing the tablets of the Law, began his descent, elated over the great events just brought about for his people. Suddenly he heard the voice of God, who had performed these deeds: <span dir="rtl">שחת עמך אשר העלית מארץ מצרים</span> “Thy people, which thou broughtest out of the land of Egypt, have corrupted themselves!” (xxxii, 7). <span dir="rtl">לך רד מגדולתך</span> “Go, get thee down from thine eminence!” <span dir="rtl">כלום נתתי לך גדולה אלא בשביל ישראל, עכשיו שחטאו אתה למה לי</span> “I have bestowed greatness upon thee only for the sake of Israel; now that they have sinned, what need have I of thee?” (Berakot 32).

Moses, in great fright, turned back to God to ask, “Wherein have they sinned?” First they are called Thy people, and then Thou refusest to know more of them. <span dir="rtl">כד אינון זכאי דידך וכד אינון חטאי דידי</span> “Why is it that when they conduct themselves properly they are Thy people, and when they act improperly they are mine?” (Pesikta). But God explains further, <span dir="rtl">סרו מהר מן הדרך אשר צויתים, עשו להם עגל מסכה, וישתחוו לו</span> “They turned speedily from the way I had commanded them. They have made themselves a molten calf, and are bowing down thereto.”

But Moses, undaunted, spoke up:

<span dir="rtl">במה אתה מקנא בשור אוכל עשב, עומד לשחט, למה יחרה אפך בעמך</span> “Against whom dost Thou show Thy zeal? Against an ox that eateth grass and is made ready for the slaughter? Wherefore doth Thy anger burn against Thy people?” (Tanhuma). With all their faults, the Israelites are still the best you could choose as your own. Though occasionally deluded by alien influences, they remain in your heart as your chosen people.

Then spoke God: <span dir="rtl">הניחה לי ויחר-אפי בהם ואכלם ואעשה אותך לגוי גדול</span> “Now therefore let Me alone, that My wrath may glow against them, and that I may consume them; and I will make of thee a great nation” (xxxii, 10).

<span dir="rtl">ראה משה שאין עמידה לישראל, וחבר נפשו עמהם, שבר את הלוחות ואמר, מחני נא מספרך אשר כתבת</span> “Moses saw that Israel had no standing before the Lord, and determined to throw in his lot with them. He shattered the tablets, and cried to God, ‘Obliterate my name from the book which Thou hast written!’ ” (Exodus Rabbah 45). You seek to make a great nation out of myself. I do not wish it. I shall go nowhere without my people. Where are the promises made to Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob? What will the nations of the world say?

<span dir="rtl">וינחם ה</span>’ <span dir="rtl">על-הרעה אשר דבר לעשות לעמו</span> “And the Eternal repented of the evil which He thought to do unto His people” (xxxii, 14). Thus did Moses succeed in placating the Lord.
