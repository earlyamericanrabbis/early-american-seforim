<span dir="rtl">שלח לך אנשים</span>—<span dir="rtl">מדעתך, אני איני מצוה אותך</span> “ ‘Send thou men’ — on thine own account; I do not command thee thereon.” The Israelites demand a troop to spy out the land; this becomes the responsibility of Moses, and is none of God’s doing. <span dir="rtl">אין אני מעכב על ידך</span> “I shall in no wise prevent you” (Rashi, Sotah 34b).

One does not approach the Jewish homeland in Palestine as one reckons with other lands. It is not to be contemplated superficially, on the basis of what the eye sees alone. Common, dispassionate judgments do not apply to Eretz Israel. What the spies spoke of the land was true: <span dir="rtl">עז העם היושב בארץ</span> “The people are powerful that abide in the land,” and every advance of the Israelites would be fraught with hardship. But the territory would be theirs nevertheless. For the divine promise had been given in the time of Abraham: <span dir="rtl">לך אתננה ולזרעך</span> “To thee shall I give it, and to thy seed.” This promise was oft repeated thereafter; hence the Israelites should have known and believed that victory would ultimately be theirs — they had no need of the calculated reports of sober investigators.

It appears that even then there were Jews who had no heart for the general attitude toward Eretz Israel. They are called <span dir="rtl">אספסוף</span>, a conglomeration, composed of converts, and of certain wealthy men fearful lest their brethren be transformed into an independent folk.

These persons placed obstacles in Moses’ way at the time of the Exodus. <span dir="rtl">היו להם פטרונין והיה להם עושר וכבוד ולא היו רוצים לצאת</span> “Among them were men of wealth and note, and these did not wish to depart” (Exodus Rabbah 13). Even when Israel stood on the bank of the Red Sea, and on command of the Lord <span dir="rtl">דבר אל העם ויסעו</span> “Speak to the people and let them journey on” — the tribe of Benjamin plunged into the waters, <span dir="rtl">היו שרי יהודה רוגמים אותם</span> “the princes of Judah pelted them with stones” (Sotah 36).

This obstinate group, declares the Gemara, was comprised in large measure of persons who <span dir="rtl">סרו מאחרי ה</span>’, “had turned from following the Eternal.” They were almost completely lacking in faith, and were bent on destroying the popular confidence in God and in Moses, their leader. <span dir="rtl">והיכן מקומם</span>? <span dir="rtl">אמר ר</span>’ <span dir="rtl">אשי בדגלים</span> “And what was the focus of their activities? Rabbi Ashi says they acted among the banners, that is, among the various parties in Israel” (Shabbat 116). They formed no party of their own; but bored within other groups, to create dissension and disrupt all possibility of unity. Whenever an emergency arose demanding the harmonious effort of all the people, they were first to incite the folk to rebellion against Moses: <span dir="rtl">נתנה ראש ונשובה מצרימה</span> “Let us appoint a new leader and return to Egypt!”

When the time came for preparing the land for settlement, these gentry persuaded the tribes to make demands on Moses:

<span dir="rtl">נשלחה אנשים לפנינו ויחפרו-לנו את-הארץ וישיבו אתנו דבר את הדרך אשר נעלה-בה ואת הערים אשר נבא אליהן</span>

“We will send men before us, and they shall search us out the land, and bring us word again by what we must go up, and into what cities we shall come” (Deut. i, 22).

<span dir="rtl">אמר ר</span>’ <span dir="rtl">חייא בר אבא מרגלים לא נתכוונו אלא לבושתא של ארץ ישראל</span> “Said R. Hiya son of Abba, The only intent of the spies was to uncover the ingloriousness of Eretz Israel” (Sotah 34). The words <span dir="rtl">לחפור את הארץ</span> “to search out the land” can also mean “to undermine.”

Moses was at first unwitting of the true purpose of the group. He thought the Jews truly eager to reach the land, hence desirous of proper investigation and information to expedite their settlement. <span dir="rtl">וייטב בעיני הדבר</span> “And the matter found favor in my eyes,” later spoke Moses. He fulfilled the wishes above expressed — but what were the consequences?

The men sent ahead to search out the land and gain a knowledge of its economic and political status returned as traitors to the Jewish ideal. <span dir="rtl">ויעלו ההרה ויבואו עד נחל אשכול וירגלו אותה</span> “They went up into the mountain, and came unto the brook of Eschol, and spied it out.” Their investigation was altogether superficial; and they came to the immediate decision that the land was of no worth. Instead of a profound search, that might lead to easing the people’s way, they conducted themselves after the manner of common spies, and by their hasty and unfavorable report definitely prolonged the era of the exile.

## Tragic Results

<span dir="rtl">איש אחד, איש אחד למטה אבותיו תשלחו</span> “Of every tribe of their fathers shall ye send a man.” These were chosen not by God nor by Moses, but by the people alone. But when Moses asked God for judges to help him rule and instruct the people, God spoke: <span dir="rtl">אספה לי שבעים איש מזקני ישראל</span> “Choose for Me seventy men of the elders of Israel.” Moses chose and conducted to the tabernacle six men of every tribe. There were seventy-two; on an equal number of cards Moses wrote “elder” seventy times, leaving two blank. <span dir="rtl">כללן ונתנו בקלפי ואמר להם באו וטלו פתקיכם</span> “He put these together in a receptacle, and bade each draw his card.” <span dir="rtl">כל מי שעלה בידו זקן אמר, כבר קדשך שמים</span> “To each one who drew the word ‘elder’ Moses said, ‘God has sanctified thee.’ ”

<span dir="rtl">מי שעלה בידו חלק אמר, המקום לא חפץ בך, אני מה אעשה לך</span>?

“He who drew a blank was thus addressed by Moses, ‘God has no desire in thee; what then can I do for thee?’ ” (Sanhedrin 17).

The Midrash relates that when Pharaoh promulgated his decree <span dir="rtl">לא תוסיפון לתת תבן לעם</span> “Ye shall not continue to give straw to the people,” though he demanded the regular production of bricks nevertheless, he placed Jewish overseers over the slaves, to be responsible to him for the carrying out of his orders. When the king’s emissaries found that the assigned number of bricks were not forthcoming, they used to beat the Jewish overseers — <span dir="rtl">ויכו שוטרי בני-ישראל</span>. But these watchers permitted themselves to be assaulted, loyally shielding those responsible for the lapse. “It is better,” they said, “that we be beaten than the people.”

When God bade Moses elect the seventy elders, Moses cried out that he knew not whom to choose, who would be fit to judge the people. To this God replied, <span dir="rtl">אשר ידעת כי הם זקני העם ושוטריו</span> “Those whom thou knowest to be elders of the people and their *overseers*.” The men who had willingly suffered physical torment for the sake of their brethren — these were fit to become their judges and masters (Midrash Rabbah, Behaalotecha).

The spies dallied forty days on their mission, returning to announce first that the land was flowing with milk and honey; then they added, “The people are powerful that abide in the land.” To this Rabbi Kalischer has commented, “This is ever the way with spies, with bearers of false reports. At first they give praise to the matter they wish to injure, but then dampen the warm expectations of their hearers with the damning word, *but*. . . .”

The report of the spies had a powerful effect on the populace.

<span dir="rtl">ותשא כל העדה ויתנו את קולם, ויבכו העם בלילה ההוא</span>

“And all the congregation lifted up, and raised their voice; and the people wept that night” (xiv, 1). This vain weeping aroused the anger of the Lord: <span dir="rtl">בניכם יהיו רועים במדבר ארבעים שנה</span> “And your children shall wander in the desert forty years. . . . <span dir="rtl">יום לשנה יום לשנה</span> “each day (of the spies’ dallying) for a year. . . .” <span dir="rtl">וידעתם את תנואתי</span> “and ye shall know my estrangement....” (xiv, 33-34).

Not alone was the generation of the Exodus fated to suffer because of the conduct of the spies; they brought injury even to the later generations. <span dir="rtl">הלילה ערב תשעה באב היה</span> “That night was the eve of the ninth of Ab.”

<span dir="rtl">אמר להם הקב</span>”<span dir="rtl">ה, אתם בכיתם בכיה של חנם ואני קובע לכם בכיה לדורות</span> “Said the Holy One blessed be He to them, Ye weep now to no purpose; for this I shall establish unto you a permanent lamentation for this night” (Taanit 19b). And indeed, both Temples were destroyed on that same date, the ninth of Ab.

The Rabbi of Ostrowicz thus commented on the phrase <span dir="rtl">ארץ אוכלת יושביה היא</span> “It is a land that eateth up the inhabitants thereof”: “Palestine will not suffer the idler, the waster; there men must ever strive and advance.”
