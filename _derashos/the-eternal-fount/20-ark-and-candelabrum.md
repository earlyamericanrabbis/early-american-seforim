<span dir="rtl">ואתה תצוה את-בני ישראל ויקחו אליך שמן זית זך</span> “And thou shalt command the children of Israel, that they bring thee clear olive oil. . . .” (xxvii, 20). <span dir="rtl">אליך ולא לי, לא לאורה אני צריך</span> “For thee and not for Me; I have no need of its light” (Menahot 86). The ark wherein repose the tablets of the Law contains all the light of Heaven. In order, however, that you and your people be enabled to approach and comprehend it, you may employ a menorah, a candelabrum, that will illumine the way to the ark and lead to the light of the Torah. But the illuminants must be altogether clear and pure, not mingled with other, alien stuffs.

“Malbim,” in his Rimoze Hamishkan, asserts that all commentators and aggadists are agreed that the vessels of the tabernacle are symbols of Jewish life, as prescribed by the Torah. The ark containing the tablets symbolizes <span dir="rtl">חכמה עליונה</span>, heavenly wisdom, which can be attained only through <span dir="rtl">רוח הנבואה</span>, prophetic vision, of which few can boast. The Torah transcends human understanding. Its true contents can be understood only by one who is raised to the rank of seer, prophet, superman. And since not all men can achieve that place, its laws and statutes must be observed even where a limited intelligence prevents full understanding.

The ark, most sacred of the vessels of the tabernacle, is to be placed in the holy of holies, into which only the high priest may enter, and that but once a year, on Yom Kippur. It must be separated from the tent of assembly by a curtain bearing two cherubs, a boy and a girl (Hagigah 13). The cherubs, representing the entire people of Israel, must be <span dir="rtl">סוככים בכנפיהם על הכפרת</span> “sheltering the cover with their wings” (xxv, 20). The ark of the Law can be maintained by Israel only when the people protect it with their lives — as has indeed occurred countless times in Jewish history.

The cherubim must also be <span dir="rtl">פניהם איש אל אחיו</span> “their faces turned one to another.” Each must watch and assist the other. The Law can be kept only by Israel’s united efforts. In connection with the building of Solomon’s Temple, it is said, <span dir="rtl">ופניהם אל הבית</span> “Their faces were turned toward the house” (2 Chronicles 3). The Talmud, noting this contradiction, asks how this can be; and then answers: <span dir="rtl">כאן בזמן שאין ישראל עושין רצונו של מקום</span> “This latter is the case when Israel does not carry out the will of God” (Baba Batra 99). When Jews conduct themselves properly, then are their faces turned one to another. All are laboring together for the welfare of the people, and are making it spiritually and economically strong. But when they are perverted from the ways of God, then each man’s eyes are turned toward his own house; he becomes indifferent to his neighbors. Such a procedure brings on enfeeblement and stagnation, and curtailment of growth.

The ark was assigned the following measurements: <span dir="rtl">אמתים וחצי ארכו, אמה וחצי רחכו, ואמה וחצי קומתו</span> “Two cubits and a half shall be the length thereof, and a cubit and a half the breadth thereof, and a cubit and a half the height thereof” (xxv, 10). Why, asks the “Shadi,” is there this mention of half cubits? To indicate that the ark is complete only when Jewry forms its other half. Without the Jews the ark is but half a thing, still incomplete.

It was prescribed that the ark be overlaid <span dir="rtl">זהב טהור מבית ומחוץ</span> “with pure gold within and without” (11). The container of the Law must be the same within and without. Those who study it must also have Torah’s attributes. <span dir="rtl">כל תלמיד חכם שאין תוכו כברו אינו תלמיד חכם</span> “No disciple of the wise who is inwardly different from what he appears is truly a disciple of the wise” (Yoma 82). The heart and the tongue of a learned man must not differ; thought must not be opposed to speech.

## Menorah for Enlightenment

Malbim speaks of the menorah as symbolizing worldly enlightenment, all forms of wisdom and knowledge that a man can obtain through his own intellect. The seven candlesticks represent the seven fundamental sciences of the world. The ornamental cups, knobs, and flowers are the beautiful results of these worldly subjects of study.

<span dir="rtl">אל מול פני המנורה יאירו שבעת הנרות</span> “The seven lamps shall give light over against the face of the candelabrum” (Numbers viii, 2). All the learning of Israel should be directed to one central point, to the end of enhancing our indigenous culture. The entire menorah, with all its lamps and ornaments, must be formed out of one mass of gold. If any part be taken from a different mass (a strange culture), the menorah is not valid for use. A Jewish candelabrum is to be manufactured out of Jewish gold; if it be formed from bits of metal borrowed and alien, its light is defective — it cannot be the true light of the Torah.

The oil used must be purchased from money collected in the general community fund. The light of the menorah is called everlasting — <span dir="rtl">נר תמיד</span> —and even as the sacrifice called tamid (everlasting) must be provided out of general funds, so must this light be similarly obtained. Olive oil alone may be used, insomuch as Israel is compared to the olive — <span dir="rtl">ישראל נמשלו לזית</span>—<span dir="rtl">כל המשקים מתערבים זה בזה והשמן אינו מתערב</span> “For all liquors mingle with one another, while olive oil mixes with nothing” (Exodus Rabbah 36). Likewise, though all other nations can readily intermingle, the Jewish people cannot be mixed but remains distinct from every alien group.

It is just that separateness that has kept Israel immortal. <span dir="rtl">אמר הקב</span>”<span dir="rtl">ה למלאך המות אין לך רשות על אומה זו, שהיה חלקי</span> — <span dir="rtl">כשם שאני חי וקים כן בני קיימין</span> “Thus spake the Holy One blessed be He to the Angel of Death: Thou hast no power over this people, since it is mine own portion; just as I live everlastingly, so will my children live forever” (Exodus Rabbah 32). Which means also that so long as the Jewish people considers itself the portion of the Lord, it cannot succumb; the Angel of Death, which has destroyed so many nations, then can have no power over Israel.

The laws given Israel by God are of a nature <span dir="rtl">אשר יעשה אותם האדם וחי בהם</span> “which if a man do he shall live in them” (Leviticus xviii, 5). This is compared to an invalid whom the physician has come to visit, and whom the physician has ordered the household they may feed whatever he desires. In the case of another patient, the doctor has prescribed certain foods which may or may not be eaten. <span dir="rtl">אמרו לו, אמרת לאכול ולזה לא</span> “And people remarked, ‘One you gave permission to eat, and to the other not.’ ” <span dir="rtl">אמר להם, החולה הראשון אינו של חיים, והשני של חיים</span> “He replied to them, ‘The first man will not live, hence he may as well eat everything; the second has the possibility of living, hence should heed his diet.’ ” God prescribed to Israel what the people should not eat and should not do — in order that they might live forever (Tanhuma).

## Further Animadversions

<span dir="rtl">בטבעות הארון יהיו הבדים לא יסורו ממנו, המסיר בדי ארון לוקה</span> “The staves supporting the ark must never be removed from the rings; he who does so remove them, is liable to stripes” (Yoma 72). My father used to comment on the passage in this wise:

“One does not find the prohibition against removing the staves in connection with the altar table. But the ark, which contains the Law, must be disturbed in no part. The staves bearing it should be changeless in every land. When Jews remove the customary supports of Judaism, and carry the ark in keeping with local customs, creating Reform temples and habitudes, then is the community indeed punished. Then the youth drifts away, and leaves the faith utterly feeble.

“However, the altar table, which represents the people’s economic life, can be carried as one wills. In each land the Jew can conduct business in accordance with local norms. His table need only have a small hedge to set it off from those of his neighbors. More literally, the table of Israel must be kosher, observant of Jewish law; it may be supported on different economic staves in different lands.”

<span dir="rtl">ונשמע קולו בבואו אל הקדש</span> “And his voice shall be heard when he cometh in unto the holy place.” The high priest wore the household symbols, the *urim vetumim*, and the <span dir="rtl">חושן המשפט</span>, the breastplate of judgment, representing Jewish learning and knowledge. But today congregations are satisfied with rabbis who know little of Jewish household symbols and Jewish judgments. These men are asked only to have a <span dir="rtl">פעמון זהב</span>, “a golden bell”—a good tongue, so that their *only* duty is that their voices be heard when they come in unto the holy place.
