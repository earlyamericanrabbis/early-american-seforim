Israel rested after the victories over Sihon king of Emor and Og king of Bashan. No longer were heard the olden plaints, that Moses had brought the people out of a good land in order that they might die in the wilderness. Fruitful were the lands they had conquered, splendid and mighty the cities they had taken, and plentiful the sheep and cattle that had become theirs. Though they were now very near the land promised them by the Eternal, they were little inclined to continue their wanderings, and appeared determined to remain in the territories so recently acquired.

<span dir="rtl">וישב ישראל בשטים</span> “And Israel abode in Shittim” (xxv, 1). <span dir="rtl">כל מקום שנאמר וישב אינו אלא צרה</span> “Wherever it is said that Israel sought to abide there, trouble inevitably followed” (Sanhedrin 106). Only in Palestine, the land set apart for Israel since the birth of the patriarch Abraham, could the nation build a true home, wherein they might live according to the prescriptions of their Torah, and assuredly become a chosen people. No alien territory was suited to that destiny — for there a perpetual conflict against foreign influences is required, against those factors bringing to spiritual plague and catastrophe, undermining Jewish foundations and robbing them of their pristine strength. Israel was able to persist in such environments only through the zeal and devotion of each generation’s Phineas, who fought without cease against disintegration and intermarriage. <span dir="rtl">ולא כליתי את-בני-ישראל בקנאתי</span> “I consumed not the children of Israel in my jealousy” (xxv, 11). Phineas and those like him did, indeed, preserve their people in every situation.

The territorial section called “Shittim” was also known as “Rephidim.” Remarks the Talmud:

<span dir="rtl">רפידים שמה שרפו עצמן מדברי תורה, שטים שמה שנתעסקו בדברי שטות</span> “It was called Rephidim because there they grew weak (*raffu*) in study of the Law; it was named Shittim because there they occupied themselves in labor of vanity (*shetut*)” (Sanhedrin 106). In addition to this play on words, it is traditionally taught that Balaam counseled Balak to achieve the friendship of Israel through the medium of the handsome Moabite women. <span dir="rtl">ויחל העם לזנות אל בנות מואב</span> “And the people began to commit harlotry with the daughters of Moab” — which culminated in <span dir="rtl">וישתחוו לאלהיהן</span>, bowing down to the gods of these women (xxv, 1, 2). The end result was <span dir="rtl">המתים במגפה ארבעה ועשרים אלף</span> “And those that died in the pestilence were twenty and four thousand” (9). The pestilence of assimilation!

The Midrash Tanhuma comments that Balaam’s advice to seduce Israel through harlotry was designed to bring on God’s vengeance for their grievous sinning, and their complete discomfiture. He was careful not to prescribe besotting them with wine and strong drink, <span dir="rtl">שלא ידונו כשתויי יין אלא כמזידין</span> “so that they be judged not as helpless drunkards, but as wilful transgressors.” He had no intent that Israel offer any excuse for sinning. The people were to be <span dir="rtl">מזידין</span> “conscious malefactors”—hence to be penalized more harshly. But Phineas instituted counter measures, which stayed the tide of misconduct and intermarriage. For this devotedness God proved most grateful — <span dir="rtl">לכן אמר הנני נתן לו את-בריתי שלום</span> “Wherefore, say, Behold, I give unto him my covenant of peace” (12).

<span dir="rtl">ו</span>’ <span dir="rtl">דשלום קטיעה הוא</span> “The letter *vav* in the word *shalom* (peace) is broken, defective.” From this the Talmud derives the assumption:

<span dir="rtl">בעל-מום שעבד עבודתו פסולה</span>—<span dir="rtl">כשהוא שלם ולא כשהוא חסר</span> “When a priest with a bodily blemish conducts the Temple service, the service is invalid; for a priest must be whole and without blemish” (Kiddushin 66b). He who fights for an ideal, if his purposes are sincere and not self-centered, must ultimately achieve victory. Only one so perfectly constituted can properly become a Jewish priest, a Jewish leader. Personal motives, however, create a spiritual blemish which invalidates all his efforts.

For a lengthy period the Israelites remained in the lands they had acquired; during that time the elder generation died out.

<span dir="rtl">ולא נותר מהם איש כי-אם כלב בן יפונה ויהושע בן נון</span>

“And there was not left a man of them, save Caleb the son of Jephunneh, and Joshua the son of Nun” (xxvi, 65). The new generation that arose was comprised of freemen, who had never suffered bondage and subjection like their forbears in Egypt. Moses, sensing that his days were numbered, spoke to his followers: <span dir="rtl">שאו את-ראש כל-עדת בני-ישראל</span> “Take the sum of all the congregation of the children of Israel” (xxvi, 2). <span dir="rtl">כשיצאו ישראל ממצרים מסרן הקב</span>”<span dir="rtl">ה למשה במנין</span> — <span dir="rtl">בא ליפטר מן העולם החזירם במנין</span> “When the Israelites departed from Egypt, God turned them over to Moses with a numerical reckoning; now that Moses was prepared to depart from the world, he wished to turn them back with a reckoning” (Numbers Rabbah 21). He would ascertain those who comprised the army of Israel, and were prepared to fight for the people; he must be certain of them ere they entered the beloved land which, on God’s command, he was not to be permitted to approach.

## Who Is a Fit Leader of Israel?

<span dir="rtl">ויאמר ה</span>’ <span dir="rtl">אל משה עלה אל-הר העברים הזה וראה את-הארץ אשר נתתי לבני ישראל. וראית אתה ונאספת אל-עמיך גם-אתה כאשר נאסף אהרן אחיך</span>

“And the Eternal said unto Moses, Go up into this mount Abarim, and see the land which I have given unto the children of Israel. And when thou hast seen it thou also shalt be gathered unto thy peoples, as Aaron thy brother was gathered” (xxvii, 13, 14). An extraordinary tragedy is depicted in these words. A lifetime of labor for one purpose, countless acts of self-sacrifice toward that end — to be told at length, You alone can have no benefit of the accomplished fact! The land he loved so greatly, and for which he had fought without let together with his brethren — he might only gaze upon it from afar, and never have the boon of entering and living upon it. Yet he did not despair. He was content that his ideals were assured of victory. He desired the creation of a new nation that would stand apart from the other peoples of earth. More important than the mere establishment of a state was assurance of the permanent existence of the nation — and in this aim he had proved highly successful.

He sought knowledge of his successor, knowing well that the fate of a people is dependent on the character of its leaders.

<span dir="rtl">לית עלמא מתקיימא אלא על רישיהן דעמא</span>—<span dir="rtl">אי רישי עמא זכאין טב לעלמא, טב לעמא, ואי לא זכאין ווי לעלמא ווי לעמא</span>

“The world exists only in respect to its national leaders; if these be men of worth, then it must be well with the nation and the world; if they lack worth, woe be upon the nation and the world” (Zohar). The heads of a people may well be men transformed into wild beasts, who imperil the entire peace of the earth. Often the peoples are innocent of the desire to conduct themselves in any brutal, militaristic manner; they have been misled by their rulers, who seize the throne and carry out their iniquities until the end of their time. Moses hardly favored such chieftains.

To God he pleaded for a popular leader, <span dir="rtl">אשר יצא לפניהם ואשר יבוא לפניהם, אשר יוציאם בזכיותיו ואשר יביאם בזכיותיו</span> “who should go before them and come before them, who would lead them forth with honor and bring them in with honor” (Rashi). There are two main classes of popular leaders. One consists of those who seek the favor of the populace, and to that end become followers of their whims, according their conduct with the people’s own desires and purposes. Since they do not lead but are led, their guidance is not of much value. The true leader is he who does not descend to the level of the folk, but who endeavors to raise them to his level. <span dir="rtl">אשר יצא לפניהם</span> “One who goes before them,” and not after them (Derashot el Ami).

<span dir="rtl">אמר ר</span>’ <span dir="rtl">יוסי מימי לא אמרתי דבר וחזרתי לאחורי</span> “Rabbi Jose used to say, Never have I uttered anything, and then looked back to see the impression my words have made” (Shabbat 111). Convinced of the honesty of his statements, he had no desire to discover what the people thought of them. A proper leader and teacher need never permit himself to be influenced by others, in the sense that he must seek to satisfy all his followers.

Rabbi Israel Salanter employed another talmudic passage to express this thought: <span dir="rtl">דור שבן דוד בא פני הדור כפני הכלב</span> “In the generation during which the Messiah will come, the leaders will have the semblance of dogs” (Sanhedrin 98). This is Salanter’s comment: “It is in the nature of the dog, when going with a human, always to run before, as though he wished to be the guide and leader. Yet, at every few steps he turns back his countenance as though to satisfy himself they are content with his guidance. This will be the guise of the Jewish leaders of that time. They will continually look back to make certain that they are satisfactory to everyone. In a generation such as this, Messiah, the true leader, must make his appearance. For he then will elevate the people to his own level, and assume their hardship with veritable pride.”
