The days comprising Rosh Hashanah and Yom Kippur are known as the Ten Days of Repentance. On the eve of the New Year the spark of faith is rekindled in every Jewish heart, steadily growing more luminous until the Day of Atonement, when a great light cleanses the spirit and warms the heart. Thus the Talmud interprets the phrase in Psalms, <span dir="rtl">ה</span>’ <span dir="rtl">אורי וישעי</span> “The Lord is my light and my salvation,” <span dir="rtl">אורי בר</span>”<span dir="rtl">ה וישעי ביוהכ</span>”<span dir="rtl">פ</span> “He is my light on Rosh Hashanah, and my salvation on Yom Kippur” (Rosh Hashanah 11).

Repentance elevates man to Godhood. <span dir="rtl">שובה ישראל</span> — <span dir="rtl">עד ה</span>’ <span dir="rtl">אלהיך</span> “Return, O Israel, unto the Eternal thy God.” The thought is that when a man contemplates his deeds with full conscience, his spirit soars beyond all the heavens to the very presence of God.

<span dir="rtl">גדולה תשובה שמגעת עד כסא הכבוד</span> “Great is repentance, that reaches to the throne of glory” (Yoma ch. 6).

Peretz has related the story of the hassidic rabbi whose followers declared that during the days of penitential prayers his soul would ascend each night to heaven. One day an opponent of hassidism, visiting the town, decided to uncover the extent of the truth of this declaration. He waited all night before the rabbi’s home; before dawn he saw the rabbi leaving with an ax in his hand for the nearby forest. Following, he noted that the rabbi proceeded to chop down firewood, which he brought to a poor hut in which an old woman lay ill. The rabbi himself placed the wood in the stove, and while the fire was warming he recited the penitential prayers. This sight converted the opponent to hassidism. He became a follower of the rabbi; and when others told how their spiritual leader ascended nightly to heaven, he would add, “And even higher!”

Without repentance man would become a wild beast. When conscience dies, all higher feelings also die, and the soul progressively sinks into a quagmire of gloom and filth. Repentance purifies conscience, quiets the nerves, and heals the wounds of the heart. <span dir="rtl">גדולה תשובה שמביאה רפואה לעולם</span> “Great is repentance, which brings healing to the universe” (Yoma ch. 6).

Repentance is also beneficial in preventing despair of a better future in human breasts. Bialik, once addressing the higher students of Yeshiva College on Wisdom and Torah (<span dir="rtl">חכמה ותורה</span>), made this distinction:

“Wisdom, knowledge, provides a penetrating glimpse into the future. But it is too cold, too exact, too severe; it does not form part of man’s life but stands without and gives its orders: So do, and so do not! A person who errs and disobeys its commands, is deserted by it and left to suffer.

“The Torah, however, is friendly and approachable. It is part of man’s life, and talks to him in human phrases. If he errs, it comforts him and restrains his despair. We may therefore call our Torah a <span dir="rtl">חכמה עליונה</span>, a highest wisdom.”

Here may be quoted a passage from the Jerusalem Talmud, which presents an excellent estimate of how Wisdom, Prophecy, Torah, and Faith adjudge the sinner. <span dir="rtl">שאלו לחכמה חוטא מהו ענשו</span>? <span dir="rtl">אמרה להם, חוטאים תרדוף רעה</span> “They asked of Wisdom, What is the punishment of the sinner? Replied Wisdom, Their evil will always pursue those who sin. Prophecy, asked the same question, replied, <span dir="rtl">הנפש החוטאת היא תמות</span> The erring soul will surely die. The Law or Torah, made this response, <span dir="rtl">יביא אשם ויתכפר לו</span> Let him bring a guilt offering and obtain atonement. <span dir="rtl">שאלו להקב</span>”<span dir="rtl">ה חוטא מה יעשה</span> Then they asked the Holy One blessed be He, What shall the sinner do? God answered, <span dir="rtl">יעשה תשובה ויתכפר לו</span> Let him show repentance, and atonement will be granted him” (Makkot ch. 2).

Wisdom, cold and impartial, condemns the transgressor forever. Prophecy, more concerned with the future than the present, postulates the end of his days. The Torah offers a friendly means of extricating him from his difficulties. Religious faith goes still further; it does not demand sacrifices. Repentance, clearing of conscience, a determination to act properly in days to come, is sufficient to elevate man to higher realms, and bring him closer to God.

## Israel’s Redemption

The Talmud bids every man to look upon the universe as in a perfect balance of good and evil; thus, a single crime of his own can turn the entire balance toward evil, upsetting the status of all human society. Conversely, his single good deed will turn the balance toward the side of merit, and improve the state of humanity. <span dir="rtl">תשובה מקרבת את הגאולה</span> “Repentance brings closer the day of redemption” (Yoma ch. 6).

However, repentance must be sought in due time. If one waits too long, until one has become old and weak, it loses its efficacy. The Yalkut Shimeoni declares, <span dir="rtl">עשה תשובה עד שאתה בכחך</span> “Offer repentance while you have the faculties to implement it.” <span dir="rtl">עד שהנר דולק תן בו שמן</span> “While the lamp yet burns, add olive oil to enhance its sheen.” <span dir="rtl">כבה הנר, אין השמן מועיל כלום</span> “Once the lamp is extinguished, the oil will be of no use to it.” Repentance that comes too late in life to be translated into correction of old crimes is of little value.

The Talmud posits two conditions that can bring redemption to Israel. <span dir="rtl">זכו עתידין להגאל בניסן, לא זכו עתידין להגאל בתשרי</span> “If Israel proves its worth, the people will be redeemed in the month of Nissan; if they prove unworthy, they will be redeemed in Tishri.” <span dir="rtl">באחד בתקופת ניסן ובאחד בתקופת תשרי היום והלילה שוים</span> “During the months of Nissan and Tishri the nights and the days are approximately equal.” <span dir="rtl">מכאן ואילך אינם שוים</span> “In later months there is no such equality.” After Nissan the days grow longer and the nights shorter; after Tishri the days grow shorter and the nights longer.

If the Jews offer repentance and understand their obligations, their redemption will come in Nissan, after which the days will become ever larger — a steady and progressive attainment of happiness. But if the people stand off selfishly and will not come near to assist their brethren, then they may be delivered in Tishri, but as the nights grow larger, their difficulties must increase.

In no case, however, should Israel despair. The Rabbi of Tchartkov used to say: “The life of the Jew is like a clock dial. When both hands are at twelve, that is, at their highest point, he should not proudly remark, <span dir="rtl">כחי ועוצם ידי עשה לי את החיל הזה</span> ‘My strength and the might of mine hand hath gotten me this wealth’ (Deuteronomy viii, 17). The hands may begin to droop, indicating the fall of man’s pride and power. Yet, when both are at six, the lowest point, man must not give up hope, and complain, <span dir="rtl">באו מים עד נפש</span> ‘The waters cover my soul.’ While the mechanism works, of clock and man, the hands will move upward once more, and assure amelioration and salvation.”
