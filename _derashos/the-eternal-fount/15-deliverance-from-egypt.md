<span dir="rtl">ויאמר ה</span>’ <span dir="rtl">אל-משה בא אל-פרעה</span> “And the Eternal said unto Moses, Go in unto Pharaoh” — and demand: <span dir="rtl">עד-מתי מאנת לענת מפני</span> “How long wilt thou refuse to be humbled before Me?” <span dir="rtl">שלח עמי ויעבדוני</span> “Let My people go, that they may serve Me.” Since the time of Moses’ first visit to Pharaoh, to transmit God’s command that the Israelites be granted their liberty, Egypt had suffered a series of misfortunes. Plagues, growing in fearfulness, had broken out; the human inhabitants, as well as beasts and fowl, had perished in huge numbers; fields were grown bare and fallow; and poverty stalked the land. There was despair in the hearts of Pharaoh’s underlings, who felt certain that all their punishments had been caused by the king’s obstinacy in refusing freedom to his Hebrew slaves. But they were helpless before Pharaoh’s vast power, and his iron obduracy in refusing to permit Israel to worship its God in the wilderness.

<span dir="rtl">פרעה הרשע עשה את עצמו אלקים</span> “The wicked Pharaoh considered himself a god” (Exodus Rabbah 8). Like all other despotic dictators of history, who attained power by force and ruled by the sword, Pharaoh ascribed supernatural strength to his reign. He could act freely, with no restraining hand to oppose him. His tremendous might convinced him of his superiority to all gods. All concepts to the contrary were to be rooted out.

Pharaoh’s advisers, who had aided him in achieving power, were much like himself. They too utterly despised the Israelites, and helped to harden the monarch’s heart against them. It was their purpose to create greater popular confidence in the king by having him assume the prerogatives of divinity and infallibility as the national leader. To their plaint, <span dir="rtl">הטרם תדע כי אבדה מצרים</span> “Know you not that Egypt is lost?” his Goebbels and Goerings had but one reply: “Have faith in your leader!” They proclaimed widely through the land that the Nile, a creation of Pharaohs, would soon overflood its banks and bring to all a new abundance.

God sent Moses to Pharaoh with this message:

<span dir="rtl">אתה אמרת לי יארי, אראה אותך אם הוא שלי או שלך</span>

“You assert that the river is yours; I shall show whether it belongs to you or to Me” (Exodus Rabbah 10). But the impress of Moses’ words was not great. To the contrary, they further aroused Pharaoh’s ire. <span dir="rtl">ויגרש אותם</span> “And he drove them out,” saying <span dir="rtl">כן ה</span>’ <span dir="rtl">עמכם כאשר אשלח אתכם ואת-טפכם</span> “Let the Eternal so be with you, as I will let you go, and your little ones” (x, 10). Such is ever the tragic fate of Israel. Even though they are hated and viciously oppressed, the oppressor will not let them go.

Later, when Pharaoh’s servants raised the complaint, once more, <span dir="rtl">עד מתי יהיה זה לנו למוקש</span> “How long shall this man be a snare unto us?” the king recalled Moses and Aaron, bidding them, <span dir="rtl">לכו עבדו את-ה</span>’ “Go, serve the Lord!” <span dir="rtl">רק צאנכם ובקרכם יצג</span> “Only let your flocks and your herds be stayed” (x, 24). The Israelites and their children were to be permitted egress, but their possessions, their money, were not to be taken from the land. These tactics have not been altered in Germany, Poland, and other modern Egypts.

## Attitudes toward Deliverance

The sages of the Talmud and Midrash are agreed that not all the men of Israel endorsed the movement for Jewish deliverance instituted by Moses and Aaron. Many of the wealthier refused to forsake the land, and even threw obstacles in the way of the leader.

<span dir="rtl">היו להם פטרונין והיה להם עשר וכבוד ולא היו רוצים לצאת</span> “Among them were officials of state, boasting riches and honors, who did not desire to depart. However, on these men fell the punishment of Heaven, for they perished during the three days’ plague of darkness” (Exodus Rabbah 13).

The sages also have an apt interpretation of the verses, <span dir="rtl">ויהי חשך-אפלה בכל-ארץ מצרים... ולכל בני ישראל היה אור במושבתם</span> “And there was a thick darkness in all the land of Egypt . . . but all the children of Israel had light in their habitations” (x, 22, 23). This special illumination for Israel, say they, lay in that <span dir="rtl">ובחשך קברו את מתיהם ולא היו רואים אותם אויביהם</span> “they interred their dead in the darkness, so that their enemies could not see them.” Had the Egyptians seen that the Jews were burying many corpses, they would have thought that Israel too was suffering from the plague — and this would have held up the redemption (Exodus Rabbah 13).

Many of the Jewish leaders in Egypt, who at first stood with Moses and offered to assist him, later abandoned him. The Midrash asks, <span dir="rtl">היכן הלכו הזקנים</span> “What became of the elders?” — the leaders who had expressed their willingness to join with Moses?

<span dir="rtl">אמרו רבותינו</span>: <span dir="rtl">היו מגנבין את עצמן ונשמטין אחד אחד והלכו להם</span> “Our teachers say that they stole away one by one, and disappeared” (Exodus Rabbah 5). This is also a historic process. Always do numbers of prominent Jews make great promises, yet perform nothing. Other men do what is necessary, while they stand at a distance. In critical moments, these are the first to rebuke Moses and Aaron: <span dir="rtl">הבאשתם את ריחנו בעיני פרעה ובעיני עבדיו לתת חרב בידם להרגנו</span> “Ye have made our odor to be abhorred in the eyes of Pharaoh, and in the eyes of his servants, to put a sword in their hand to slay us” (v, 21).

The masses of the people, despite their profound hope that the project of liberation should be crowned with success, also did not exert themselves sufficiently. <span dir="rtl">כל ישראל כולן נתעסקו בביזה</span> “All the Israelites were occupied with spoil” (Sotah 13). The late Nahum Sokolow on one occasion likened this attitude to that of American Jews, who were always too “busy” to take part in the movement of liberation. However, the work of Moses finally did receive recognition: <span dir="rtl">האיש משה גדול מאד בארץ מצרים, בעיני עבדי פרעה, ובעיני העם</span> “The man Moses was very great in the land of Egypt, in the eyes of Pharaoh’s servants, and in the eyes of the people” (xi, 3).

## Moses Explains

Thus spoke Moses to Israel: <span dir="rtl">החדש הזה לכם, אין לכם חדש אחר גדול מזה</span> “This month is your own; ye have no greater month than this” (Exodus Rabbah 19). The months of Israel’s bondage in Egypt were not theirs. They were compelled to reckon time according to the Egyptian calendar. The lunar changes were not applicable to that calculation. Alien skies hang over alien lands. <span dir="rtl">כל זמן שאורו של עשו מבהיק, אין אורו של יעקב מתפרסם</span> “All the while that Esau’s light gleams in fullness, the light of Jacob is not known” (Yalkut Shimeoni, Bo). A nation in exile dwells in darkness. Individuals here and there may achieve happiness, but not the nation as a whole. <span dir="rtl">אין היחיד מקדש את החודש</span> “A single man cannot sanctify (establish the beginning) of the month” (Rosh Hashanah 25). Individuals are not able to calculate the birth of a nation’s new moon. The month must belong to all individuals, all groups.

Moses bade the Israelites bring a Passover offering, which all must eat together. Families must unite in this observance. <span dir="rtl">אל-תאכלו ממנו נא</span> “Eat not of it half-done” (xii, 9). An offering of the people, only half prepared, and not done as it should be, can bring no atonement to the entire folk. Neither may it be <span dir="rtl">בשל מבשל במים</span> “sodden at all with water.” Such an offering must be brought unmixed with other elements. It is to be <span dir="rtl">צלי-אש</span>—“roast with fire”—which alone will insure the retention of its savor (Exodus Rabbah 18). <span dir="rtl">כל בן נכר לא יאכל בו</span> “There shall no alien eat thereof” (xii, 43). This means that any person who is estranged from his people, and not in consonance with its ideals and beliefs, should have no share in the Passover sacrifice. Such a person may bring in new trends which will definitely hinder the development of the people.

<span dir="rtl">וככה תאכלו אתו</span> “And thus shall ye eat it” (xii, 11). <span dir="rtl">פסח לדורות</span> “A paschal offering for the generations” — for every era in which Israel will unite in labor for redemption, for national upbuilding. If this offering is brought by the entire nation in full keeping with the prescribed regulations, <span dir="rtl">דוחה את הטומאה</span> “it drives off all impurity” (Pesahim 96).

<span dir="rtl">ושמרתם את המצות</span> “And ye shall keep the commandments” <span dir="rtl">כדרך שאין מחמיצין את המצה, כן אין מחמיצין את המצוה</span> “Just as we may not permit the *matzah* to sour, so may we not permit the *mitzvah* to sour” (Mekhilta Bo 9). <span dir="rtl">אם באה מצוה באה מצוה לידך עשה אותה מיד</span> “If a righteous command comes to your hand, perform it at once.” Many great moments in Jewish history were lost through procrastination. The opportunities soured. Nothing came of them. To achieve freedom, “ye shall keep the commandments” always and immediately.
