The final portion of the Torah is not assigned to any separate Sabbath. It is read on the last day of the Succoth festival — Simhat Torah, so called because thereon Israel rejoices over the great spiritual treasure given by God to the people. Israel is the only people that has a “Time of the Giving of the Law” as well as a day known as Rejoicing of the Law. Shabuoth, when the Torah was handed down on Mount Sinai, has the former designation; and on the last of the Succoth festival, there is rejoicing when the cycle of Pentateuchal readings is concluded and recommenced. Shabuoth belongs to the past — a historic feast celebrated in commemoration of an outstanding event in Israel’s national existence. Simhat Torah, however, belongs to the present and future; it expresses the popular thanksgiving over the continuing influence of Sinai.

One cannot rejoice over the mere acceptance of a Torah. For none may know what will result from its laws. Many nations have adopted liberal constitutions, which promised freedom and equality to all inhabitants. But these documents remained dead letters, since the populace and its leaders never grew to the elevation of their constitutional ideals, and never carried them into practice.

Only after the Jew has perused his Torah from beginning to end, and has studied well its lofty principles and ideals; after he has performed as well as hearkened — only then can he rejoice in the Torah. My father often said: “The festival is called Rejoicing of the Law, which means that it too rejoices in us. Hence do we demand that all take part in the joy — ‘Rejoice and make merry on Simhat Torah’ — since the jubilation is mutual. There is a joint festival, prolonging both Jewish life and the Jewish Law.”

<span dir="rtl">תורה צוה לנו משה</span>—<span dir="rtl">מורשה קהלת יעקב</span> “The Torah which Moses commanded us is the inheritance of the entire people of Israel.” It belongs to every Jew, and every Jew shares in it. It was not given to individuals, or to the class of the learned alone; a Law so limited could never exercise an eternal influence on a whole people.

<span dir="rtl">כל המונע הלכה מפי תלמיד כאילו גוזלו מנחלת</span>

“He who refrains from teaching his pupil one law of the Torah is as one who withholds from him part of the rightful inheritance of his parents.” Each Jew is expected to know the Torah completely from its beginning:

<span dir="rtl">מורשה היא לכל ישראל מששת ימי בראשית</span> “It is the legacy of all Israel since the six days of the world’s creation” (Sanhedrin 91b).

The Yalkut inquires why God did not give the Torah to Abraham. For Abraham was the first to recognize Deity, and to teach the concept of God to his children and associates. Holy Writ says, <span dir="rtl">כי ידעתיו למען אשר יצוה את בניו ואת ביתו אחריו</span> “For I know him, that he will command his children and his household after him” (Genesis xviii, 19). Furthermore, Abraham is the father of his people, and founder of the ideal of monotheism. Why then was not he accorded the privilege of transmitting the Torah? Thus is the query answered:

<span dir="rtl">חכה הקב</span>”<span dir="rtl">ה לתת את התורה שהיא בת ששים רבוא אותיות, לעם ישראל בימי משה שהיו ששים רבוא, כדי שיהיה לכל אחד אות בתורה</span>

“God waited to give the Torah, which contains six hundred thousand letters, until the time of Moses, when there was just that number of Israelites, in order that each one should have one letter of the Law” (Yalkut Shimeoni, Jethro).

And because each Jew has a share in the Torah, one must not withhold from a pupil the knowledge of any law within it. He who does so robs the student of a heritage rightfully his. For this reason it is the custom to call to the reading of the Law every Jew who is in the synagogue on Simhat Torah, and young boys as well. Rabbi Seshet adds: <span dir="rtl">כל המלמד תורה בעולם הזה זוכה ומלמדה לעולם הבא</span> “He who teaches the Torah in this world earns the right to teach it in the world to come” (Sanhedrin 92a). The Jewish Torah is eternal, extending from before creation, and destined to exist so long as creation does. He who propagates knowledge of the Torah among his disciples thereby binds himself to the world of eternity, and his name will remain among the living to farthest futurity.

<span dir="rtl">וזאת הברכה אשר ברך משה איש האלהים את-בני ישראל לפני מותו</span> “And this is the blessing, wherewith Moses the man of God blessed the children of Israel before his death.” While the people observe the Torah and carry out its precepts, they cannot succumb. God speaks to them: <span dir="rtl">מכרתי לכם תורתי, נמכרתי עמה</span> “I delivered my Torah unto you, and Myself with it” (Exodus Rabbah 33). As God lives for all time, so will Israel exist forever.

Moses calls all the tribes together, and gives each an individual blessing, according to its character. He ends with the lofty declaration:

<span dir="rtl">אשריך ישראל מי כמוך עם נושע בה</span>’ <span dir="rtl">מגן עזרך ואשר-חרב גאותך</span> “Happy art thou, O Israel: who is like unto thee, O people saved by the Eternal, the shield of thy help, and who is the sword of thy excellency!” (xxxiii, 29). Have no fear of thy enemies, if they be false to thee: <span dir="rtl">ואתה על-במותימו תדרך</span> “and thou shalt tread upon their high places” and be victorious over them.

The sword whereby we fight unintermittently against unnumbered enemies is made of deep pride in our past, and of certainty of our future and the outstanding role which Jewry will yet again play in the world. With this weapon of just pride we have maintained ourselves in the cruelest periods, in the most insufferable circumstances, certain of ultimate victory and of outliving our assailants — just as we have outlived the powerful empires of the past who persecuted us and are themselves no more on earth (*Rayanot veHomer LiDerush*).

## God Takes the Soul of Moses

Moses, having completed the Torah, prepares to leave this world. The sages present a remarkable account of his death: <span dir="rtl">אמר הקב</span>”<span dir="rtl">ה לגבריאל הבא לי נשמתו של משה</span> “Said God to the angel Gabriel, ‘Bring me the soul of Moses.’ ”

<span dir="rtl">אמר לו מי שהוא שקול כנגד ששים רבוא, האיך אני יכול לעשות חציפות כזאת</span>?

“To this Gabriel returned, ‘A man who is held equal to all six hundred thousand of the people — how can I act so boldly toward such a one?’ ” How can I take away the leader of a people, who will immortalize Thy great name through the Torah Thou gavest them?

<span dir="rtl">אמר למיכאל, הבא לי נשמתו של משה</span> “Thereupon God asked Michael to bring in the soul of Moses.” But Michael begged off: <span dir="rtl">אני הייתי רבו והוא היה תלמידי, איני יכול ליטול נשמתו</span> “I was his teacher and he was my disciple; I cannot take his soul.” A large measure of my spirit is now in his; how can I remove from him that which is myself?

<span dir="rtl">בא סמאל והוא הלך בשמחה</span> “Then came Samael (angel of death), and he undertook the task happily.” <span dir="rtl">מצאו כותב שם המפורש וזקוקין של אש יוצאין מפיו</span> “He found Moses writing the ineffable name of God, while sparks of fire issued from his mouth.” <span dir="rtl">חזר סמאל ואמר איני יכול לעמוד בפני בן עמרם</span> “Samael returned and declared, ‘I cannot stand before the son of Amram.’ ” He is wrapped in eternity, and cannot be taken by death.

<span dir="rtl">עמד הקב</span>”<span dir="rtl">ה ונשקו, ויצאה נשמתו בנשיקה</span> “Upon which the Holy One blessed be He Himself arose and kissed Moses, whose soul departed with the kiss.” Moses’ death could not be a natural one, as his life was not natural. A man who lives in realms of Heaven cannot die in the manner of all the earth. His soul, part of God, must always be near God’s throne of glory.

<span dir="rtl">היו מלאכי השרת בוכים ואומרים, והחכמה מאין תמצא</span>? “The ministering angels wept and cried, ‘Whence now will the world’s wisdom come?’ ” The fountain of wisdom must surely have dried up (Bet HaMidrash 71). <span dir="rtl">וימת שם משה</span> “So Moses died there” (xxxiv, 5). He died only *there*, in the narrow circle of his family and friends. For humanity, however, for the generations to come, <span dir="rtl">עומד ומשמש</span> “he remains and serves them.” When Moses was born his home was filled with light. He it was who kindled the light of faith in the house of Israel; and his radiance still guides the living inhabitants of the world (Sotah 13b).
