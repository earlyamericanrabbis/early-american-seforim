The incident of the calf lasted but one day, but this day is accounted the most tragic in Jewish history. Every occasion when Israel suffered a national calamity was thus described by the sages: <span dir="rtl">היה קשה לישראל כיום שנעשה בו העגל</span> “It was troublesome to Israel, even as the day on which the calf was made” (Shabbat 17). They believed that the sin committed with the golden calf was responsible for all the misfortunes that befell the later generations of Israel. Were it not for this crime, the Jews would ever have remained the chosen people, the “kingdom of priests and holy nation” as originally destined by God, and would have known no future pain and hardship.

The first pair of tablets were made by God alone — runs the account — and were written by the very finger of God. Hewed out of huge diamonds, they were exceedingly heavy. But <span dir="rtl">האותיות היו סובלין את עצמן</span> “The letters bore their own weight.” <span dir="rtl">כיון שחטאו בעגל פרח הכתב מעליהם ונמצאו כבדים</span> “As soon as they sinned with the calf, God’s inscription vanished from the stones, and they became too heavy for Moses to bear.” They fell from his hands and were shattered (Tanhuma). While Israel observes the prescriptions of the Torah, the laws and judgments remain alive, carrying themselves along with the people. When, however, they are forsaken or surrendered for other precepts, they fall heavily upon the people, who suffer greatly because of the aliens they would thus emulate.

The second tablets had to be prepared by Moses himself. Said God:

<span dir="rtl">פסל-לך שני-לחת אבנים כראשונים וכתבתי על-הלחות את-הדברים אשר היו על-הלחות הראשונים אשר שברת</span> “Hew thee two tablets of stone like unto the first; and I will write upon these tablets the words that were in the first tablets, which thou brakest” (xxxiv, 1). Because the Jews did sin against God in bending to the calf, they no longer merited that God himself make the tablets. Moses was to prepare them, and then the holy words would be inscribed upon them.

## For the Tabernacle

<span dir="rtl">ויקהל משה את-כל-עדת בני ישראל</span> “And Moses assembled all the congregation of the children of Israel” (xxxv, 1) to request that they assist in building the tabernacle and all its vessels. If they possessed enough gold to mold the calf, which brought upon them the anger of the Eternal, they should be able to provide more than sufficient for a tabernacle, wherein the Divine Presence might rest and dwell among them. The Israelites, oppressed by feelings of guilt, at once carried out Moses’s request, both men and women proffering gold, silver, and all other needful materials. Shortly Moses was given the joyous news: <span dir="rtl">מרבים העם להביא מדי העבדה</span> “The people bring much more than enough for the work” (xxxvi, 5). And by his order it was proclaimed, <span dir="rtl">איש ואשה אל-יעשו-עוד מלאכה לתרומת הקדש</span> “Let neither man nor woman make any more work for the heave offering of holiness” (6).

In this manner did the Israelites atone for their sin of the calf —by their generous donations to the tabernacle. God was gratified by this evidence of repentance, and to the plea of Moses he replied, <span dir="rtl">סלחתי כדברך</span>, “I have forgiven, according to thy word” (Numbers xiv, 20).

There is much interest in the dispute between Moses and God. Says the Eternal, <span dir="rtl">לא אעלה בקרבך כי עם קשה-ערף אתה</span> “I will not go up among thee; for thou art a stiff-necked people” (xxxiii, 3). Elsewhere He says, <span dir="rtl">אמר אל-בני-ישראל אתם עם-קשה-ערף</span> “Say unto the children of Israel, Ye are a stiff-necked people” (5). Moses, after letting the Eternal express his anger, then pleads, <span dir="rtl">ילך-נא אדני בקרבנו כי עם-קשה-ערף הוא</span> “Let my Lord, I pray Thee, go among us; although it is a stiff-necked people” (xxxiv, 9). Obduracy is without doubt a grievous fault. The obstinate man will not learn, nor follow leadership. But this sin carries with it the virtue of persistence. Such a one will complete an undertaking. He will endure every hardship to perpetuate his ideals. Precisely because Israel is a stiff-necked people, is it certain that God will walk among us. Despite all persecutions, Israel will remain with its God.

## Torah and Talmud

<span dir="rtl">ויהי שם עם-ה</span>’ <span dir="rtl">ארבעים יום וארבעים לילה</span> “And he was there with the Eternal forty days and forty nights” (xxxiv, 28). Upon this the Midrash inquires, <span dir="rtl">מנין היה יודע משה אימתי יום ואימתי לילה</span> “In the everlasting light of Heaven, how could Moses know when it was day and when it was night?”

<span dir="rtl">אלא כשהקב</span>”<span dir="rtl">ה היה מלמדו תורה בכתב היה יודע שהוא יום, וכשהיה מלמדו על פה משנה ותלמוד היה יודע שהוא לילה</span> “When the Holy One blessed be He taught him the Torah, he knew it was day; when God taught him Mishnah and Talmud, then he knew it was night” (Tanhuma).

This midrash at first appears odd and incomprehensible. But we are able to grasp its meaning when we understand the process of Jewish thought and the effect of the sages upon all Jewish history. This is an expression of a readily discoverable historic fact, that any student of our enigmatic history may establish.

What preserved the unity of Israel during a thousand long and gloomy years of exile? Scattered over land and sea, widely separated, subject to varied influences, Jewry was still able to maintain its individuality, its special and peculiar character. It is generally agreed that this sustaining force was the Oral Law, or Talmud. The Talmud, adopted by the Jewish people as their own source book of law and ethics, kept them unified in all the confines of earth.

This literature was the constitution of Jewish life. The “fences” placed about the Jew’s conduct by the Talmud, protected him from the customs and manners of alien peoples. Its regulations formed the basis of Jewish existence everywhere, so that every community of Israel, wherever found, became a link in the chain of Jewish immortality — a chain extending from Sinai to the present.

During all the period of day, of light, of independence in the land of Israel, Jews studied the Written Law. But when they fell under the yoke of the Greeks and the savage Romans, and our sages saw that a long and ebon exile was closing upon us, then they — the Pharisees of history — began creating both Talmuds, first the Palestinian and then the Babylonian, to serve as national and spiritual bulwarks. The Saducees and later the Karaites, who cleaved to the Torah alone, refusing to recognize the validity of the Talmud, vanished from the earth; but the descendants of the Pharisees still live, and will live forever.
