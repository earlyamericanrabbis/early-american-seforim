The first sidrah of the two being read today begins with the following: “And the Eternal spake unto Moses after the death of the two sons of Aaron, when they approached unto the Eternal, and died. And the Eternal said unto Moses, speak unto Aaron, thy brother, that he come not at all times into the holy place . . .” (Lev. XVI:1-2).

The second sidrah begins: “And the Eternal spake unto Moses, saying, Speak unto all the congregation of the children of Israel, and say unto them, ye shall be holy, for I the Eternal, your God, am holy” (Lev. XIX:1-2).

The first sidrah speaks of the holiness of place and the second sidrah deals with the holiness of man.

Any MITZVAH, if it does not involve a fellowman, has to have a “HACHANAH,” “preparation.” As it is said, “Prepare to meet thy God, O Israel” (Amos 4.12). A Jew, before he makes the BRACHAH for a Mitzvah, says “HINNI MUCHAN U’MEZUMAN L’KAYEM MITZVAT BORRI,” “I am ready and prepared to perform the command of my Creator.” But when the MITZVAH involves a fellowman, then no “HACHANAH,” “preparation” is necessary. One does not make a “BRACHAH” when he gives charity, for when assistance is required by a human being he must not be kept waiting until the giver will prepare himself to do the MITZVAH. — “That he come not at all times into the holy place,” for “KDUSHATI L’MALAH MIKDUSHATCHEM” (Midrash, R. Lev. 24). “My holiness is greater than yours” (said God). The holiness of God is already there, man can not add to it. He has to be extraordinarily careful not to profane it. (“CHILUL HASHEM,” “to profane God’s name is one of the greatest sins.”) “Because the revelation of My SHECHINAH takes place there, he should take care not to make it his habit to come there” (Rashi). Each commandment that one performs puts him that much closer to God, therefore one has to show reverence for the MITZVAH. It must never become a commonplace thing to man. When he is about to perform a MITZVAH, he must feel and see himself as entering the Holy of Holies, before which he must make preparation, as a matter of awe and reverence, for what the Holy of Holies or the MITZVAH represents.

Entering the Holy of Holies or the performance of MITZVOT, “BEN ADAM L’MAKOM,” “between man and God,” does not always inspire man to do good. There is always the danger that he may use this particular right as a cover-up for some wrong he is committing. To be regarded by the people about him as a pious man, may be of great benefit to him. One may not believe it, but nevertheless it is true, that the “YETZER HARA,” “the Tempter to evil” is found in the Holy of Holies. In the Talmud (Yoma 69b) we are told that once the Jews prayed and fasted for three days and three nights to enable them to catch the “YETZER HARA,” whereupon he was surrendered to them. He came forth from the Holy of Holies like a young fiery lion. Thereupon the prophet said to Israel: “This is the evil desire of idolatry . . . (The evil desire for idolatry is also the evil desire for immorality).” As they took hold of him a hair of his beard fell out, he raised his voice and it was audible four hundred parsangs. Thereupon they said: how shall we act? Perhaps, God forbid, they might have mercy upon him from heaven! The prophet said unto them: Cast him into a leaden pot, closing its opening with lead. Because lead absorbs the voice . . . They said: Since this is a time of grace, let us pray for mercy for the Tempter to evil. They prayed for mercy and he was handed over to them. He said to them: Realize that if you kill him, the world goes down. They imprisoned him for three days, then looked in the whole land of Israel for a fresh egg and could not find it . . . They put out his eyes and let him go. It helped in as much as he no more entices men to commit incest (literally, against relatives). — This tale teaches us that one will not receive the proper inspiration in his contact with the holy places or with holy things in life unless he himself is fully prepared outside of this contact, with a good deal of holiness. Even the High Priest is not permitted, without preparation, to enter the Holy of Holies. He must first prove his holiness in his daily affairs, in the street, in his dealings with people. All these outer preparations cause the inspiration within him when he enters the Holy Place.

In the second sidrah (KEDOSHIM) that we read today, the Torah enumerates many things a man has to do in order to become holy. “Ye shall fear everyman his father and his mother; ye shall keep My Sabbaths. Ye shall not reap the corners of thy field nor glean thy vineyard nor gather the fallen fruit of thy vineyard, leave them for the poor and for the stranger. Ye shall not steal, neither shall ye deal falsely, nor lie one to the other . . . Thou shalt not avenge, or retain any grudge against the children of thy people, but thou shalt love thy fellow-man as thyself, I am the Eternal” (Lev. XIX:1-18). The Psalmist (in Psalm 121:5) says that God is your shadow at your right hand. When man opens his hand the shadow also opens his hand. And just as the shadow does all that man does, so it is with God. If you show mercy to people and help them, God shows mercy to you and helps you. “KOMOCHA ANI HASHEM,” “as you do, I, God will do.” — In other words, whatever you are prepared to do for others, you can expect God will do for you. What you donate to the Holy of Holies, that is how much you can expect to benefit from it. — If one purchases his way into the Sanctuary, it won’t change him a bit. He has to bring that change into it, before he can expect to take it out.

The lesson from today’s parshiyot is that the holy place will not make one holy unless he enters prepared and holy.
