LAMAH PARSHAH ZU STUMAH L’FI SHEKIVON SHENIFTAR YAAKOV OVINU NISTMU EINEHEM V’LIBOM SHEL YISRAEL M’TZORATH HASHIBUD SHEHISCHILU L’SHABDOM, DAVAR ACHAR, SHEBIKESH L’GALOS ES HAKETZ VENISTAM MIMENU (Midrash Rabah).

“Why is this portion of the Torah without the usual space separating one portion from another,” the Rabbis ask. They answer that “when Jacob our father passed away, the eyes and the hearts of Israel were covered with the agony and the suffering of oppression, for the Egyptians began to oppress them. Another interpretation is that Jacob wanted to reveal the time of redemption and it was covered up and concealed from him.”

This week’s portion of the Torah actually contains the chapter of our life in GALUT, a chapter that is closed, closed in the sense that each people has its specific achievements and accomplishments through which it attains recognition, makes progress, develops itself and produces its own history, and as soon as the Jewish people went into exile everything was suddenly severed. Nothing after that occurred in keeping with the normal course of events. The future could no longer be predicted in accordance with the standard happenings of the present. No wise man, no prophet, no diplomat could foretell what lay ahead. The Rabbis remark: “MEYOM SHECHORAV BEITH HAMIKDOSH NITNO NEVUOH LESHOTIM,” “From the day the Beth Hamikdosh was destroyed, prophecy was handed over to fools.” After the tragic circumstances of the destruction of the Temple, slavery, oppression and exile, only a twisted kind of mentality can result. When people can no longer think in a straight and logical manner, the expression of a prophetic remark can come only through an abnormal way. — Jacob lost the power of prophecy and could not foresee the future nor reveal the redemption. He saw before him “VAYEOCHZU BOH VAYIFRU VAYIRBU,” “they took hold of it, and became fruitful and multiplied.” In other words, Jews prospered in the land of Egypt . . . This was one side of the picture. On the other side the scene before him was the “TZOROS HASHIBUD,” “the agony of oppression,” the terrible slavery and the torture his children would have to endure. And Jacob could no longer construct a theory which would give him some indication of the future.

Jacob begged Joseph, “VEOSISA EMADI CHESED VEEMETH AL NO TIKBERAINI BEMITZRAIM,” “You shall do me the kindness and truthfulness, do not bury me in Egypt.” He realized the great danger in “VAYEOCHAZU BOH,” in this tenacious clinging of the Jews to the land of Exile. Therefore he said to Joseph: “Take me away from Egypt. Let my children know that not only do I not wish to live in the land of Egypt, but even after life, Jacob does not want to remain in Egypt.” — Jacob’s being there was after all only “AL PI HADIBUR,” “by a special commandment of God,” but he never wanted it despite all the promises and fortunes of Egypt. — To Jacob, the hard rocks of the Holy Land were much more precious, and even after death, that was where he would be. He begs Joseph, “Do me kindness of proving the truth, that I was not satisfied with Egypt, so that the coming generations shall do likewise and strive to return to the land of our fathers.” Joseph understood very well what his father meant and answered, “ONOCHI ESEH KIDVARECHO,” “I will do as you do,” meaning, I too will order my children to transfer my remains to the Holy Land. Although I am Joseph, the viceroy of Egypt, I will never forget that here is not my place. I became viceroy only because the Egyptians needed me. As soon as I am no longer needed, they will no longer recognize me. Soon there will be none who will know that there was one called Joseph. “ASHER LO YODA ES YOSEF,” “Who did not know Joseph.”

This demonstration by Jacob to Joseph against the Exile was the first seed sown for redemption. And our grandfathers followed in this pattern. They too, in their old age, went to the Holy Land. Though they could not live there due to various circumstances, they came there to die and with this act demonstrated their union with the land of our fathers, its sacredness to them even after death.

We went through diverse countries of exile in various periods. In some places we “VAYEOCHZU BOH,” “took hold of it,” let down roots and became passive and quite lukewarm to the Holy Land. At times there was the so-called Paradise of Exile, still some pious Jews went to the Holy Land to die, showing with this action that only Eretz Israel is our true homeland. In other countries we suffered the agony of oppression and some elements lost spirit and morale. There is no end to Jewish affliction. Here, too, the demonstration “UKVARTANI BIKVUROSOM,” “Bury me in their burial place,” the desire of our patriarchs to be buried among their own, helped to lift the spirit of our forlorn people. This at least made them feel that there was a land of our fathers.

But Jacob’s fear was not yet allayed. He no longer perceived in his children the united power he was accustomed to see in them before they came to Egypt. He saw each one as a world unto himself. “REUBEN PACHAZ KAMAIM,” “Reuben his son is swift like water.” And Jacob searched for a common denominator to unite them in order that they should be able to overcome the Exile. The Talmud relates, “OMRU KULOM SHEMA ISRAEL,” “They all said ‘SHEMA ISRAEL.’” The sons made known to their father their great and unshakeable belief in God, in the One and Only God. They might be apart in everything else, but the Oneness of God bound them together. This answer gave Jacob the assurance of their united strength, and he replied: “BORUCH SHEM KVOD MALCHUSO LEOLOM VOED,” “Blessed is the Name of the Glory of His Kingdom forever,” and he blessed them and said to them “HAOSFU,” “Gather together.” This was the secret for their redemption. This power of uniting in God’s given way of life would ultimately redeem them from exile.

We Jews in our long GALUT spread out over the four corners of the earth. In many countries we accepted the languages of the land and the traits of the people. Still one power united and unites us — the belief in the One God and His teaching, which will bring us to quick redemption. Amen.
