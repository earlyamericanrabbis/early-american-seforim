“And the Eternal spake unto Moses saying: Command Aaron and his sons, saying: This is the law of the burnt offering.” Rashi comments: “The expression ‘command’ always implies urging on to carry out a command, implying too, that it comes into force at once, and is binding upon future generations.” The MIDRASH (VAYIKRA RABAH) says: “that the burnt offering is brought as atonement for evil thoughts.” Rabbi Simeon said: “Especially must scripture urge on the fulfillment of the commands in a case where monetary loss is involved” (SIPHRA; KIDD. 29a). The words in Hebrew for “monetary loss” are “CHISARON KIS,” literally, “missing a cover.” Thus one of the rabbis explained that it must make reference to a sin committed through evil thoughts, because every physical part of the body can be covered, can be concealed and protected from being involved and damaged. The eyes can be shut in order not to see what may be damaging to look at. The ears can be stuffed up in order not to hear things that are damaging in nature. So it is with the hands, feet, etc. But not so with the “MACHSHAVAH,” thoughts, with the mind of man. That can not be easily arrested to think in one direction only. Therefore, Aaron the high priest had to be urged to learn how to deal with this extremely difficult problem with the burnt offering which was to be atonement for the wicked way of thinking.

The part of the human being hardest to train is his mind. Every part of man can be easily taught to do certain things in a certain way, but not so the mind. — Psychologists have proved that the mind of man is responsible for many physical ailments, and that is where the psychoanalysts enter. Searching deep in the subconscious, they may find exactly which emotional disturbance caused the physical ailment. The cure, then, consists of a form of brainwashing the patient from his unhealthy way of thinking. That is a very long process. Sometimes this is accomplished through hypnotism after the confidence of the patient has been gained, and the hypnotist’s suggestions make the proper impressions. Sometimes the shock treatment is used, and this causes partial amnesia in the patient and he is relieved.

Some political movements have used and are now using the media of brainwashing their subjects to bring about obedience to their wills. They direct men’s “MACHSHAVAH” to their own whims and fancies. Superstitions, noisy spirituals, and mass hysteria are all means of directing the minds of men in certain ways of thinking.

Our Torah is made up of precepts, the following of which will keep our minds as well as our bodies in good health and contentment. Many of our “MITZVOT” remind and train us not to be overwhelmed with too much joy and not to fall prey suddenly to a state of extreme unhappiness. “The precepts of the Lord are right, rejoicing the heart; the commandment of the Lord is pure, enlightening the eyes” (Psalm 19:1-9). Yes! The precepts bring inward happiness, which flows from a clear conscience, and light-giving to man in his search for the right way of living. The “MITZVOT” of the Torah shield men from the constant spiritual hunger that causes them to have their hearts open to any sort of influences. The Torah calls the people of Israel “AM KSHE OREF,” “A hard-necked people,” a people whose minds can not be easily influenced.

The Torah may also be called a form of brainwashing. It is a training of the heart of man, which in turn also controls his mind and keeps it from imagining and desiring things that may be injurious to him and to those about him. The Torah is the only fully proven covering or protection for the “MACHSHAVAH,” the mind of man. We find very few disturbed minds among observant Jews, and many of those who were already disturbed were cured by the “OR HATORAH,” by the enlightening words of the Torah. The Torah does not make suggestions to man under the influence of hypnosis and does not use the shock treatment. The Torah opens his eyes to see the truth, that which is real. “REEH,” see! Stop, look, and listen, is the command of the Torah. As the psalmist said: “The commandment of the Lord is pure, enlightening the eyes.” They are light-giving to man in his search for the right way of living.

The “KORBON OLAH,” the burnt offering which is brought to atone for the sin that is committed in the “MACHSHAVAH,” in the mind, may in no part or any part be eaten by either the owner or by the priest. That is to teach us that no compromise can be made as far as the mind of man is concerned. The mind is the control power of the man and even a small amount of evil influence must be totally rooted out. Scripture said “The burnt offering shall remain on the fire-place upon the altar all night unto the morning, and the fire of the altar shall be blazing on it” (Leviticus VI:2). The word for fire-place in the Hebrew text is “MOKDAH” and the letter “MEM” is smaller than the other letters. That is to denote that regardless how little one is subjected to influence of the wrong environment, he is still in danger and must be completely removed from it. As the psalmist said (Psalm 1): “Happy is the man that hath not walked in the counsel of the wicked, nor stood in the way of the sinner . . . but his delight is in the law of the Lord . . .”

This law of the “KORBON OLAH,” the burnt offering, does scripture urge to fulfillment. It is fashioned to cure one of the most serious of the maladies that attack the world’s society, the contaminated mind that comes from loose, selfish, uncontrolled, desirous hearts. When man observes this “KORBON” he sees himself. He sees that by committing a sin he forfeited the gift that was given to him as a human being, the gift of his mind, and he is now no better than the creature lying on the altar. This realization will bring him to repentance, to mend his ways.
