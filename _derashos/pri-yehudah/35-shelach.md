The greatest punishment inflicted upon the children of Israel for sins committed by them, was the one recorded in today’s parshah. For the evil report the leaders of the people brought about the Promised Land, God decreed that the whole generation be annihilated, and only Yehoshua and Kalev entered the Promised Land. Even for the greatest of sins, such as the making of the golden calf, we find that God forgave them and meted out a suspended sentence, as it is written, “U’VYOM POKDI UFAKADTI” (Exodus XXXII:34), “Nevertheless in the day when I visit, I will visit their sin upon them.” For that transgression alone, they would not have been prevented from entering the Promised Land.

Why was the sin of the “MERAGLIM,” spies, greater than the sin of the “EGEL HAZAHAV,” the golden calf? At the beginning of the text we read: “Send thou men, that they may spy out the land of Canaan . . .” Rashi comments: “LEDAATACH,” on your own. I am not commanding you to do it, but if you so desire, send them.” It is interesting to note that in Deuteronomy, the word “V’YACHPERU,” “to spy out,” is used and here the word “LATUR,” “to tour” is employed. To spy out could mean to find a weak point for attack, as in the case of Yehoshua, who in today’s HAFTORAH sent two spies to Jericho to report to him on some vulnerable point that would be easy to conquer. But to tour a country denotes that one does it for pleasure and not for war purposes.

The entering of the Promised Land was preordained by God to be in the same manner as the departure from Egypt. That came about through the greatest of miracles. “HASHEM YELACHEM LACHEM V’ATEM TACHARISHUN” (Exodus XIV:14), “The Lord will fight for you, and you shall hold your peace.” So was to have been the entering into the land of Israel. For in the same promise that God made to Moses, “V’HOTZEITI ETCHEM MIN HAARETZ,” “I will take you out of the land,” He said: “V’HEVIETI ETCHEM EL HAARETZ,” “I will bring you into the land” (Exodus VI:6-8). All the organization of the armies of the children of Israel in the desert was in preparation for the time when they would already be settled on their land, to enable them to live normally like any other nation. The initial entering into the land had to be as promised by God. — By asking for men to spy out the land, the people showed mistrust in God. They wanted to see if there was an easy way to conquer the land. Since, up to that time on various occasions they had mentioned the wonderful life they had in Egypt, the plenty they enjoyed there, it was certainly questionable, if and when they entered the promised land, they might not compare it to Egypt and possibly even reject it. Therefore God said to Moses: “SHELACH LECHA ANASHIM V’YATURU ET ERETZ KNAAN,” “Send thou men, that they may spy out the land of Canaan.” Let them see what the land has to offer. As it is said: “And see the land, what it is, and the people that dwelleth therein, whether they are strong or weak, whether they are few or many. And what the land is that they dwell in, whether it is good or bad.” When they returned, they could not help but tell the truth, the land was good. For we read in Deuteronomy I:25, “And they took of the fruit of the land in their hands, and brought it down unto us, and brought us back word, and said: Good is the land which the Lord our God giveth unto us.” Also, in today’s parshah (Numbers XIII:27) “And they told him and said: We came into the land whither thou sendest us and surely it floweth with milk and honey; and this is the fruit of it.” But when they later spoke to the constantly complaining population, it was in a different vein. As we read (Deuteronomy I.27) “And ye murmured in your tents, and said: Because the Lord hates us, He hath brought us forth out of the land of Egypt, to deliver us into the hand of Emorites, to destroy us.” By rejecting God’s own choice for the Promised Land, they evidenced the greatest mistrust they could have shown. The sin of the golden calf can be readily explained. It was not a rejection of the God of their fathers, but was only search for a symbol as a reminder of their belief (see drashah to Parshat Tisa). But the sin of the “MERAGLIM,” the spies, was definitely “KFIRAH,” denial of God’s choice of the land, and mistrust in Him. — Although “TESHUVAH,” repentance, and “TFILAH,” prayer for forgiveness, usually helps, in this instance it was of no avail. Why?

TFILAH, prayer, is called by our sages “AVODAH SHEBELEV,” “the work of the heart.” It is not, as it is mistakenly understood to be, an asking or demanding of God things that we need or want. This is not our approach to “TFILAH.” — The psalmist said: “IVDU ET HASHEM B’SIMCHA.” . . . The word “IVDU” comes from the word AVODAH, work, which refers to “TFILAH,” prayer. That is to say, prayer must be done in an enjoyable fashion. How then can one, who is in great need and begging God for help, show joy? We will know the answer to this question when we will state that begging directly from God for our needs is not what “TFILAH,” prayer, means to us.

Rabbi Samson Raphael Hirsch, in his commentaries on the Psalms, observes on the words “HOREINI HASHEM DARKECHA U’N’CHENI B’ORACH MISHOR . . .” (Psalm 27:11), that the word “DERECH” refers to “BEN ADAM L’MAKOM,” between man and God, and the word “ORACH” refers to “BEN ADAM L’CHAVERO,” between man and man. In other words, in order to serve God one has to have the knowledge of how to do so, but to lead or serve people takes more than the knowledge of how to do that. It also requires “SIYATTA DISHMAYA,” God’s help, to succeed in it. “V’AHARON ACHICHA YIHYEH NEVIACHA” (Exodus VII:1), “And Aaron thy brother shall be thy prophet.” “HANAVI HU BA BESOD HASHEM V’YODEA ET DARKO BEDERECH PELE” (Yael Or), “The prophet is the one who enters the secret chambers of the Eternal and knows His ways — in a very surprising manner.”

The word “TANUG,” pleasure, is many times used to translate the word “TFILAH,” prayer. “AZ TITANAG AL HASHEM” (Isaiah 58:14), “V’HATANAG AL HASHEM” (Psalms 37:4). The commentary “RADAK” explains it to mean “ENYAN HAHISTASHIOT V’HANEIMOT BEHANAAT HANEFESH,” “Referring to simple enjoyment and pleasure of man.” Rabbi Samson Raphael Hirsch comments: “BAKESH RAK TANUGIM KEELEH USMACH RAK B’HANAOT KEELEH SHEINCHA TZARICH LEHITBAYESH BAHEM BIPNEI HASHEM . . . V’KI REIACH NICHOACH HEM L’HASHEM V’YITEN LECHA MISHALOT LIBECHA, MILUY LEVAV’CHA LO YEACHER LAVO,” “Search for the kind of pleasures, and amuse yourself only with the things that you will not be ashamed of before God. And God will then give you all that your heart desires without any delay.” Also Ibn Ezra on Exodus VII:1, “V’AHARON ACHICHA YIHYEH NEVIACHA, SH’AGALEH LO SODI, V’ZEHU HATAAM HASHEV ET ESHET HAISH KI NAVI HU, V’HU MITANAG ALY V’YITPALEL B’ADECHA V’CHAYE,” “and Aaron your brother will be your prophet, that I’ll tell him My secret, and this is the meaning (Genesis XX:7) of ‘Return the wife of that man (Abraham) for he is a prophet and he will enjoy himself before Me and will pray for you and you will live.’ ” All of these prove what we understand prayer to be a normal way of living, including all the enjoyments but of the kind that is in accord with the teachings of God. Then, and only then, will our wants and desires be fulfilled. As the psalmist said: “HASHEM KOL TAAVATI ALECHA V’ANCHATI MIMCHA LO NISTARAH,” “When all my pleasures are in accord with Your teachings, then my troubles will not be hidden from Thee.”

At the time when the children of Israel made the golden calf, scripture tells us: “VAYAKUMU LETZACHEK” (Exodus XXXII: 6), “and rose up to make merry” (See drashah to Parshot Tisa). When Moses prayed, the word “VAYECHAL” is used for prayer, literal translation is “ordinary” from the word “CHULIN,” and indicated that he showed the weakness of the people and pled for mercy. Had he instead prayed “V’YITANAG,” proving that what they did came from being strongly overwhelmed in their belief and they merely wanted to symbolize it, God would probably have forgiven them the sin of making the golden calf without any conditions, without a suspended sentence. — But here, in the case of the evil report, scripture tells us “VAYIVKO HAAM BALAYLAH HAHU” (Numbers XIV:1), “and the people wept that night.” Instead of changing their ways, repenting for their transgressions of mistrust in the good judgment of God, they bemoaned His choice. Instead of living normally, trying to enjoy life with God, they rejected Him by mourning when they heard the evil report. Scripture used the words “VAYOTZIU DIBAT HAARETZ,” “they brought forth evil reports about the land.” The word “DIBAH” is found also when Joseph brought to his father an evil report of his brothers (Genesis XXXVII 2), “VAYAVEH YOSEPH ET DIBATAM RAAH EL AVIHEM.” Literally the word “DIBAH” refers to words normally spoken. Every people has some jests about itself for amusement. But the stranger, hearing these jokes, can sometimes think that such is the true situation in that country. One must be able to differentiate between jokes and truth. To accept the “DIBAT HAARETZ” as the true state of condition of the land was wrong. Scripture tells us that the spies saw only good there and even brought proof of it, the “ESHKOL ANAVIM,” “a branch with a cluster of grapes” (Numbers XIII:23). But the “DIBAT HAARETZ” misled them.

Had the people repented, by showing again their full trust in God, that as He brought them out of Egypt with miracles so would He also bring them into their Promised Land with miracles, God would have forgiven them. But what does the Torah tell us, “VAYAPILU LEALOT EL ROSH HAHAR,” “But they presumed to go up to the top of the mountain.” Rashi comments: “‘LASHON CHOZEK’ (Numbers XIV:44), which means ‘with force.’ ” They did not show trust in God, but tried to use their own force. Therefore they were so severely punished.

In summary, “TFILAH” as we understand it, is not direct begging for our needs. It is instead doing things and even enjoying life in accordance with God’s Torah. Then God will give us all He knows we need and must have. “TFILOT OVOT TIKNUM” (Sages), “The patriarchs fashioned the prayers for us.” But the word “TIKNUM” does not mean that they originated them. One can hardly think that before the patriarchs, no one prayed. It is but natural for one to pray for the things he needs. But “OVOT TIKNUM,” the patriarchs formed the prayers the way they ought to be, “LEREIACH NICHOACH LASHEM,” “for a savory smell to God.” “KOL HAOMER TFILAH LEDAVID BECHOL YOM MUVTACH LO SHEHU BEN OLAM HABAH” (Sages), “Any one who recites the Psalm ‘TFILAH LEDAVID’ daily is certain to receive his share in the world to come.” Read carefully “TFILAH LEDAVID” and you will find that though it is a TFILAH, prayer, it consists only of praise to God. Every prayer in the AMIDAH or elsewhere is not called Prayer, but “BERACHAH,” “blessing.” It is surrounded with “BERACHOT,” “Blessings,” before and after. Our YOMIM HANORAIM, fearful days, the High Holy Days, are called YAMIM TOVIM. We must be joyous in these days. This is how our patriarchs have determined our prayers, that they are not prayers of begging directly for our needs, because then we could not be happy and in good spirit. But our way of praying must be by praising the Eternal, enjoying the things in life in accordance with the laws of the Torah. Then God will give us our needs.

The word “TFILAH,” prayer, is not like “CHANUN,” “begging,” but more like “ATAR.” Pharaoh used this word when he asked of Moses “HATIRU EL HASHEM,” “entreat the Lord” (Ex. IX:28). Moses used the same word when he asked of Pharaoh, “LEMATAI AATIR LECHA,” “for what time shall I entreat for you.” Or when Isaac and Rebecca prayed for a child, scripture employed the same word: “VAYETAR YITZCHOK,” “and Isaac entreated.” But in Jeremiah XXXIII:6, we find the word “ATAR,” to have the meaning of “ABUNDANCE,” as in “ATERET SHALOM V’EMET,” “abundance of peace and truth.” Thus the word “ATAR” may well mean riches and abundance, physical fullness, pleasure and joy.

The word “TFILAH,” prayer comes also from the word “PILEL,” thought, as in “REOH PANECHA LO PILOLTY” (Genesis XLVIII:11), “I had not thought to see thy face,” and “AVODAH SHEBELEV ZEHU TFILAH,” “the thought of the heart is TFILAH” (Sages). Thus TFILAH is actually the thought of the heart and not begging. It is as the psalmist said (Ps. XIX:7), “The law of the Lord is perfect, quieting the soul.” When one is happy with the Torah, then God provides all his needs.
