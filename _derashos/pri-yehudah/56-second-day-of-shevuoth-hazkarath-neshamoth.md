“VEYITZROR BETZROR HACHAYIM ET NISHMATAM,” “May He bind up their souls in the bond of eternal life” (Memorial Service). The soul of man, which is a portion of God Himself, was given to man not only to keep him alive in the ordinary sense, but also to make his soul become the master over his physical and material self, over his own individuality. — Every human being has his own particular characteristics. As our sages remarked: “KESHEM SH’EIN PARTZUFEIHEM DUMIN ZE LAZEH KACH DEOTEIHEM ENON SHAVOT,” “Just as the faces of people are not alike so are their characteristics not alike.”

Physical and bodily variations somehow influence character. Psychologists already know how to analyze a person’s character according to his physical features. In other words, man is an individualist in so far as his body is concerned. Only his soul which is a portion of the Divine Power itself, pulls him to unite with the people around him. “VAYIPACH BEAPAV NISHMAT CHAYIM,” “And breathed into his nostrils the breath of life” (Genesis 2:7). Through the soul, man was given not only life for himself, not only that individual alone was made alive, but “VAYEHI HAADAM LNEFESH CHAYA,” “And man became a living soul.” All humanity was given life. It is not written “NESHAMA CHAYA” in the singular form, but “NISHMAT CHAYIM,” “a soul of lives.” This is to denote that only when a man’s soul is united with collective life, with the life around him, only then is he “LNEFESH CHAYA,” only then is he really alive. — The life of a person is recognized only when it is woven into the lives of those around him. In other words, it is in accordance with his conception of the people around him that his own significance is determined. “KI HAADAM EITZ HASADEH,” “that man is the tree of the field” (Deuteronomy 20:19), is only a pessimistic example. A tree grows up and lives a limited time and it dies. But the meaning of man’s life is not to be found in the days or in the years which he as an individual has spent on our little planet called the earth, but rather what his life meant to his family, to his friends, to the people around him. The life of man can be seen only in the light of other lives.

Light is very close to warmth. So also, in order to have meaning, must a mortal stand together with people around him. He must illuminate, and with the warmth of his own soul contribute to the fulfillment of the lives of others. And as life is not limited to the individual, so also is the death of the individual not limited to him only. When a man dies it is not he alone that dies but a part of the life of other people dies with him. When one heart ceases to beat, then there is an emptiness in many hearts. For those who survive him, it is perhaps worse. For the dead feel no more, but the living must endure the absence, the pain of separation.

KOHELETH, in his pessimism, saw man in life and in death, as only an individual. Looking at the life and death of an individual, naturally the underlying conclusion is emptiness and pointlessness. He depicted life as an end in itself. “KI MIKREH HAADAM UMIKREH HABEHAMAH MIKREH ECHAD LAHEM,” “For what happens to the beast is what happens to the human beings” (Ecclesiastes 3:19). When a beast dies its life is ended and so also is it with man, says KOHELETH. But the life of man has no end if we look upon it as a portion of the life around him. The word “end,” or “finish” cannot even be said about him. Who is able to calculate the end, the middle, or the conclusion of his life? If to live means to give life to others then actually man exists even after his physical span of years has ended. — After the philosophical “HAVEL HAVALIM,” “Vanity of Vanities” (Ecclesiastes 1), KOHELETH comes to the conclusion “SOF DAVAR HAKOL NISHMA ET HOELOKIM Y’REI V’ET MITZVOTAV SH’MOR KI ZE KOL HAADAM,” “The end of the matter is, let us hear the whole, fear God and keep His commandments, for this is the whole duty of man” (Ecclesiastes 12:13). Unite your soul with the Divine, the source of all human life, and thus you will become a part of the human race and as such, your life will be tied up with the lives of others around you and your death will not spell the end of your life. No computation can be made about human life if we look upon man as a part of collective life. Then his existence is unlimited and curtailed no longer, as it seems on the surface. We sometimes see a life that is suddenly severed before its time, but looking deeper we find that it is not ended, but continues in the lives of those who were around him. We can say that his being ended only in a sense, in that it is no longer creative. Only the power to create has died, but life continues on. “VEYITZROR BITZROR HACHAYIM ET NISHMATAM,” “May He keep their souls alive.” Upon our lives depend the lives of our dear ones whose creative periods are already finished. They are no longer creative, but we must continue their lives and their ideas. We must unite ourselves with our brethren in the land of Israel, with all the children of Israel. Only in this way will our lives become eternal and will be continued for the coming generations.

“HAZKARAT NESHAMOT” is not meant only to remember the souls of the dead but also to remember the souls of the living. By thinking of the lives of our departed ones we will remind ourselves to do something for our own good. We will be reminded to unite our own souls with the Source of all souls, with God Himself, through uniting ourselves, our lives with the lives and souls around us, all together striving towards one goal — to build a world of peace and tranquility. Such a union can be created above and beyond the various traits and characteristics each of us possesses.

Writing on the verse “VAYIPACH BEAPOV NISHMAT CHAYIM,” “And breathed into his nostrils the breath of life,” Rashi, the great commentator, remarks: “ASAHU MIN HAELYONIM UMIN HATACHTONIM KEDEI SH’LO THEI KINAH BEMAASE B’REISHIT,” “He fashioned the man from above and from below, a body from below, from the earth and a soul from above, from the Divine, in order that there would be no jealousy in the work of creation.” Even in the beginning of creation, body and soul were formed as equal partners. God gave the same measure from the earthly material as from the spiritual so that there be no jealousy in the work of creation. Only with these two, the earthly and the heavenly combined, can man rise to be a true partner to God in the continuance of Creation.

Today we celebrate the giving of the Torah, which is divided into two parts, commandments between God and man and between man and man. The two holidays, PASSOVER and SHEVUOTH are united with a bridge of “SEFIRAT HAOMER,” “counting of the OMER,” because Passover is dedicated to physical freedom and Shevuoth to spiritual freedom. In order to attain a complete and perfect deliverance, with the redemption from Egypt they had to have the Torah as the soul to accompany the physical liberation. Body and soul as equal partners become, through the Torah, the Jewish concept as a way of life for man. “VECHAI BAEM,” “You shall live with them,” is the message of the Torah. It is the pathfinder for the Jew throughout his earthly existence. Therefore it is no wonder that in all the world literature there is nothing that can compare to the popularity of the Torah. Three hundred millions of Mohammedans have through their Koran paraphrased only the word “Life” from the Bible. The holy writings of India are at best references for the archeologist and the collector of antiques, rather than a guide for daily life. Buddhism left the world a most beautiful doctrine which is not so much a religion as a philosophy for self-denial and obedience.

The Torah of Moses begins with redemption from slavery and the first proclamation is: “The Jewish people are only slaves unto Me (says the Lord) but not slaves to slaves” (Leviticus 25:55). And so the Torah, the soul of the Jewish people, came to the rescue of the body of Israel, to complete its freedom and to make it permanent.

Now at the moment of “HAZKARAT NESHAMOT” we give an opportunity to our souls to strengthen this power of unity for the welfare of our people and of all mankind. Amen.
