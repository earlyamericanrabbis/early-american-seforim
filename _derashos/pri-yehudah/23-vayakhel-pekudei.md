“He intentionally mentioned to them the prohibition in reference to the Sabbath before the command about the building of the tabernacle in order to intimate that it does not set aside (supersede) the Sabbath” (Mech.).

In this parshah we are told about the sanctification of time and place. The commandment to build the tabernacle represents the holiness of place. The commandment to observe the Sabbath represents the holiness of time. Both time and place are of utmost importance for the development of our spiritual values.

We are commanded: “K’DOSHIM THIYU,” “Ye shall be holy,” but one cannot be holy at all times and in all the places. “EIN TZADIK B’ARETZ ASHER YASEH TOV V’LO YECHTA,” “There can not be found a righteous one in the world that sinneth not.” The many disturbing factors of the street will not permit a perfect holiness. Therefore God ordered: “Let them make ME a sanctuary; that I may dwell in the midst of them” (Exodus XXV:8). There, in the sanctuary, one can meditate undisturbed and he can be at one with God. The same is true with time. One can not perfect himself in holiness during the working day. When there are hours of labor and anguish of spirit, one can hardly use his thinking faculties. Only during the Sabbath or other rest days, can one find himself, and undisturbed develop and expand on the real values of life. The source of the worst human lamentation of all times is when God called on Adam as he hid from Him in the midst of the trees of the garden, “AYEKAH ADAM?” “Where are you, Adam?” What is your aim in life, oh man? What are your values in life? How can you even think of concealing the emptiness, the void that your unpurposeful life will cause in the long chain of humanity? All the disturbances of the world stem from that grievous factor, that man did not answer the call of God, “Where are you, Man?” Man became worse than the beast because, as Adam of old thought, he too thinks he can hide himself from God in his own Garden of Eden. In the case of Adam, scripture tells us: “God drove out the man (from the Garden of Eden) and He stationed at the east of the Garden of Eden the “CHERUBIM,” (angels of destruction — Rashi), and the flame of the sword turning itself to guard the way of the tree of life” (Genesis III:23-24). So it is today. No one can hide from the continuous call of God, “Where are you, Man?” God knows what you are doing, and if you merit it, you will be punished by being driven out of the Garden of Eden which you think you have secured for yourself and your family. Wake up, Man, before it is too late and the Angels of Destruction and the Flaming Sword are again placed before you. To facilitate contemplation, we are told to consecrate certain times and certain places, that we may be able within ourselves to answer God’s call.

The difference between the holiness of time and the holiness of place is thus. The place that we have to make holy, we build ourselves and whatever sanctification we put into it, that is how much will be there. “LO HAMAKOM MECHABED ES HAADAM ELO HAADAM MECHABED ES HAMAKOM.” “The place does not honor the man, but the man honors the place” (Sages). The Sabbath is holy from the time of creation. “And God blessed the seventh day and He sanctified it” (Genesis II:3). And in our prayers we say: “MEKADESH ISRAEL V’AZMANIM,” “God sanctifies Israel and the times.” The initial “KEDUSHAH,” holiness of the rest days was given by God and it was handed over into our custody to promote it. Therefore the building of the tabernacle does not supersede the Sabbath. For the Sabbath is originally holy, but the tabernacle depends on what we will make of it.

In PARSHAT PEKUDE, we are told: “And they brought the tabernacle unto Moses, the tent, and all its furniture, etc.”; Rashi comments: “The actual work of assembling the parts of the tabernacle and of placing the sacred vessels in position was reserved by the command of God for Moses, no one of the other workers being able to do it” (Exodus XXXIX:33). — Yes! the “MISHKAN,” tabernacle, was ordered by God to be built to serve the spiritual needs of the desert-wandering people of Israel. It took more than the mechanical skill of the great gifted architect Bezalel and that of his helpers to make the “MISHKAN” for the purpose for which it was meant. The gold, silver, copper, wood, etc. could not possibly provide for the spirit within. Only Moses, who delivered the Torah, could do this.

We build many “MISHKANS” today in the form of synagogues, and temples, whose construction is made to perfection, but they accomplish very little in the way of the spirit. A “MISHKAN” where physical comfort is the main achievement, will not fulfill its real purpose. A place where permitting mixed seating is more important than providing for a site where one can meditate clearly and meet with God undisturbed by the daily physical impressions, will not be what we expect of a sanctuary. Such a structure will not influence us for the best in life. A synagogue can not be only a house of assembly, a house of prayer, but it must also be a center of spiritual uplifting, a center of enlightenment and religious thought.

“These are the accounts of the tabernacle, even the tabernacle of the testimony, etc.” (Exodus XXXVIII:21). The Parshah gives the full account of all the precious materials donated by the people for the building of the “MISHKAN.” Credit is given for the goodness of their hearts which donated more than required. But it is defined here as the tabernacle of the testimony which is the most valuable thing about it. The testimony refers to the Torah, for it testifies to its inherent worth and value. It is the beacon that spread the light in the days of old, whose light will never be dimmed even though the breath of man would obscure or extinguish it. It is our balm of comfort amid the trials, the rock of strength amid the temptations of the world now and then. Yes! All the parts of the “MISHKAN” were made to fit perfectly but it would not stand till Moses, the true representative of the testimony, brought the spirit of the Torah into it. Sanctification of time has been made by God. But sanctification of place depends wholly on man. Only “PEKUACH NEFESH,” “saving life itself,” may set aside the Sabbath, because man himself is the center of “KEDUSHAH,” of “holiness,” and he is the one who makes the place holy. Therefore, the “MISHKAN,” “Sanctuary,” does not set aside Sabbath, for its holiness depends on man.
