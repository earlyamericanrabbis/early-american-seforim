This sidrah brings to a happy climax the drama of M’CHIRAT YOSEPH, the selling of Joseph by his brethren. Joseph puts his brothers to a test to learn if they have truly repented for the evil perpetrated against him and against their father who never ceased mourning for his son Joseph. He detains his brother Benjamin, hoping to find out if it would trouble them to return to their father without the lad whom he loved so much and whom he did not want to send in the first place, or if they were still the selfish, reckless, unfeeling men who had sold him, Joseph.

The brothers had changed and repented. In the opening words of today’s sidrah we read: “Then Judah came near to him and said: ‘Pardon, my lord, let thy servant, I pray thee, speak a word in my lord’s ear, and let not thine anger burn against thy servant.’” Rashi says that from these words you may infer that he spoke in harsh terms. Judah was speaking to Egypt’s lord, yet he spoke so boldly. “The life of our father is bound up with the life of the lad,” he said, “Permit me, my lord, to be a bondsman in his stead. Only let the lad go with his brothers.”

This was enough for Joseph. This was all he wanted. He was convinced by the earnestness of Judah’s plea that they were not the same men who sat down to eat bread in Doian, while he, their brother, was lying in the pit. He could restrain himself no longer, and said: “I am Joseph, your brother, whom you sold into Egypt.” Great was the joy, strong was the impulse among the brothers to run quickly and tell their father the wonderful news that Joseph lived and was the ruler of all Egypt. But Joseph cooled them down by saying: “AL TIRGZU BADERECH.” — “See that ye be not agitated on the way.” Rashi gives several interpretations to this passage. Firstly, “AL TITASKU B’DVAR HALACHAH,” “Do not busy yourself with halachic discussion lest you lose your way.” Secondly, “AL TIFSEU P’SIAH GASAH, V’HIKANSU B’CHAMAH L’IR,” “Do not take very long steps and enter the city while the sun is still shining.”

When Joseph said: “Do not busy yourselves with halachic discussion,” he reminded them of their discussion before they sold him. We read in Genesis XXXVII 17, “NOSEU MIZEH, KI SHAMATI OMRIM NELACHA DOYTANAH,” “They have journeyed hence, for I heard them say, Let us go to Dothan.” Rashi says: “They have departed from all feelings of brotherhood, let us go to seek some legal (DATOT) pretext to put you to death.” — Joseph did not say to them: “AL TILMEDU,” “Do not study,” which he would not forbid them, but he did say: “AL TITASKU,” “Do not make an ‘AISEK,’” a business out of the HALACHAH. Do not make the HALACHAH serve your ambition. When your ambition was to destroy your own brother and cause great pain to your father, you interpreted an HALACHAH to suit you. Joseph said to them: “Do not busy yourselves with HALACHIC discussions that serve only your thoughtless action, lest you lose your way as you did when you sold me.” Alas! It is rooted in the nature of many people that they will lightly commit a sin or wrong a friend, and though deep in their hearts and in their inner moral selves they will be troubled, still they will try to free themselves of the heavy bad feeling by justifying their deeds. Many times one gets so blinded in ambitions that he honestly thinks that his evil doings are within the realm of the moral law. For example, people try to find justification for eating TREIFAH by saying that KOSHER costs too much. This is an easy way out of the guilt a Jew has when he takes the first bite of TREIFAH into his mouth.

There is no doubt that anti-Zionists could see that the sun began to throw its golden rays on Zion when the State of Israel was founded, that the glory of our people has been to a great extent restored by it. Had they so desired, they could have found enough HALOCHOT for AHAVAT TZION V’ISRAEL, love for Zion and for the people of Israel. Instead, they looked for and found an HALACHAH which they interpreted to the contrary. Such is their design, and under the guise of self-interpreted HALACHOT, they draw in so-called true and naive Torah scholars.

The second suggestion that Joseph made to his brothers was: “Do not make very long steps.” He understood that in a mood of overwhelming joy, they would try to make long, tiring steps to rush to their father with the news that Joseph still lived and the other wonders about him. Therefore Joseph told them, “It will be better for your father if you tell him the news when you are in a rested state of mind. It will be much more refreshing to him when you impart the tidings in not too much haste, but happily and clearly describing what you have seen here.”

In our movements for the redemption of our people from the GALUT, we were always either too slow or too fast. There were times when we could very easily have built our homeland, when we could have bought all the land of Israel from the Arabs for a pot of lentils and without too much effort have rebuilt at the same time the body and soul of our people. But to our sorrow, we had too many Torah authorities who slowed down the wheel for GEULAH, redemption. They quoted self-interpreted passages of the Talmud and Midrashic literature to give meaning to their actions. Finally, after the greatest CHURBON, destruction, that our people ever suffered and there remained no other place to which Jews could escape, we began to make our steps much too fast into the land of Israel. If our Torah authorities had accompanied our early settlers in Israel and helped them physically and spiritually prepare the land for the later influx of our people, the observant Jew of today would not be faced with the problems that he encounters in our homeland. The full joy of spirit and body combined is somewhat missing, because our people ran too quickly to save their very lives and left the spirit of Israel lagging behind.

The final suggestion that Joseph made to his brothers was: “Enter the city while the sun is still shining.” The word used in the text for “sun” is CHAMAH which is from CHAM, meaning warm. What he meant was: Carry this warmth that we have now built up between us to your father. Let your father notice the total change in your attitude towards each other, that you are no longer jealous, selfish men, but wholeheartedly united and warm to one another.

Our people, all through the many generations of our GALUT, had greatly inspired dreams for SHIVAT ZION, for the return of the people to Zion. Nothing else helped us so much in keeping our equilibrium in exile as the hope for Messiah. Our prayers and our songs for every occasion pulsated with words of inspiration for the ultimate redemption of our people from GALUT. We hoped that when the realization of our dreams came true we would still have the warm feeling for our homeland that we had during our days of dreaming. But, alas, this warmth has cooled slightly, due to friction in our midst. There are too many factions among us, each interested only in its own welfare, and not in the country as a whole. Instead of uniting all forces so that our land could withstand the pressures exerted on us by our enemies who threaten our very existence, there are still among us those who for so-called (self interpreted) religious reasons, would rather see our centuries-long hoped for homeland taken over, God forbid, by these enemies — all because of the lack of religion in our midst. In the Torah (Genesis XLVI 3-4), we read that God said to Jacob: “Fear not to go down into Egypt; for I will make you there a great nation, I will go down with you into Egypt.” “V’ONOCHI AALCHA GAM ALOH,” “I will bring you up and I will also come up.” First it says “AALCHA,” “I will bring you up,” and then “GAM ALOH,” “I will also come up.” Godliness can best be formed within a people that is already on its land.

“GODOL HASHOLOM SHEHAMELECH HAMASHIACH ENO POTEACH ELO B’SHALOM,” “Great is peace that when Messiah comes, he will begin with peace.” The true believers in the Messiah must have this in mind: that only through peace in our ranks can we hope for a speedy redemption of our people, that only with a united effort can we bring back to our people the patriotism and inspiration that existed during the days of our dreaming for SHIVAT TZION. With God’s help we will live to see our people in our homeland living in accordance with those glorious expectations.
