“CHOT’U B’KIFLAYIM V’LAKU B’KIFLAYIM U’MITNACHMIM B’KIFLAYIM. CHOT’U B’KIFLAYIM, CHET CHATAH YERUSHALAYIM (Isaiah I), V’LAKU B’KIFLAYIM, KI LAKTA M’ET HASHEM KIFLAYIM B’CHOL CHATOTEAH (Isaiah 40:2), U’MITNACHMIM B’KIFLAYIM, NACHMU NACHMU AMI” (Isaiah 40:1) (Midrash Echah). “They sinned double as it is said Jerusalem has grievously sinned (the word sin written twice). They were smitten twice as it is said: that she has received of the Lord’s hands double for her sins. And she will be comforted double as it is said: Comfort ye, comfort ye, my people.” — In Tractat Taanit 26b, R. Simon b. Gamliel said: “There were never in Israel greater days of joy than the fifteenth of Ab and the day of Atonement. On these days, the daughters of Jerusalem used to walk out in white garments . . . The daughters of Jerusalem came out and danced in the vineyards exclaiming at the same time: Young man, lift your eyes and see what thou choosest for thyself.”

R. Johanan (Berakot 4b) says: Why is there no “NUN” in “ASHRE”? Because the fall of Israel’s enemies begin with it (euphemistic for Israel) for it is written: Fallen is the Virgin of Israel, she shall no more rise (Amos V:2). — In the west (Israel) this verse is thus interpreted: She is fallen, but she shall no more fall. Rise, O Virgin of Israel. R. Nachman b. Isaac says: Even so David refers to it in inspiration and promises them an uplifting, for it is written: The Lord upholds all that fall (Ps. CXLV:14).

“SINAT CHINAM,” “Hatred without cause” among our people contributed mostly in bringing on the “CHURBAN,” destruction, of our homeland and of our people (Sages). Our people then were split up into a number of groups and each group formed its own direction in life and thus weakened the position of the country as a whole. A nation that is made up of various factions will try to unite them by finding for all a common purpose and interest. One such thing was the Torah which gave us direction in life. Once we abandoned the Torah, we became divided and nothing else helped to unite us. — We tried to find unification in nationalism, in the direction of rebuilding our homeland and we failed. There was only one unifying force then, and the only one that will bring us together again — the Torah and its MITZVOT.

Some sages found the cause of the “CHURBAN” to be “SHELO BORCHU BATORAH T’CHILAH,” “that they did not bless the Torah first.” That is to say, they did not try to find the blessing of fusing the nation through the Torah. Yes, they did try many mediums of bringing the people of Israel together, but failed, because they did not use the well-proven one, the Torah. The slogan: “Be a Jew within (in your house) and a man without” proved to be betraying to our real Jewish belief. “How long halt ye between the two opinions?” (I Kings XVIII:21). The greatest factor for the continued existence of our people through all the trials and tribulations was expressed by one who is considered as the greatest prophet next to Moses, and this is no other than Balaam, who said: “Lo, it is a people that shall dwell alone, and among the nations it shall not be reckoned” (Numbers XXIII:9). Only when we dwell alone in accordance with the teachings of the Torah, then and only then are we indestructible. Those who have thrown off their ties with Judaism and its way of life find that as soon as they meet up with any kind of distress, they come knocking at the synagogue doors for those within to pray for them. Hillel said: “Do not keep aloof from the community, be not sure of yourself till the day of your death” (Pirke Ovot 1:5). What the sage was saying is that a Jew ought never separate himself from his Jewish community, for in the togetherness lies the survival of our people. Our forefathers’ adherence to each other at Sinai caused God’s deliverance to them of the Torah, which gave spiritual light to the entire world. As it is said: “And all the people answered unanimously, and said, all that the Lord hath spoken will we do” (Exodus XIX:8).

The letter “NUN” is omitted in the psalm of “ASHRE,” because the word “NAFLAH,” “she fell,” begins with the “NUN,” and because this referred as a curse to the people of Israel. But King David, whose descendent will be the Messiah of our people, promises that even the “NUN” will, in the end of the days, become an uplifting factor when it will unite and combine itself with another “NUN” and together will make up the letter “SAMACH,” which is the letter that begins the sentence “SOMECH HASHEM LECHOL HANOFLIM,” “The Lord upholds all that fall” (Ps. CXLV:14). That which R. Simon b. Gamliel said, that there never were in Israel greater days of joy than “TET VAV B’AB” means as follows. “TET VAV” is fifteen and B’AB is the abbreviation of “ALEF-BET.” The fifteenth letter of the ALEF-BET is the letter “SAMACH” which is made up of two “NUNS.” In other words, though each one by itself refers to “CHURBAN,” when the two “NUNS” come together they bring the full unification of Israel. As the letter “SAMACH” is closed in on all sides, so will the people of Israel be united for their common cause. Yes! The total salvation will come to our plagued people only when we will reunite our forces under the flag of our Torah.

Now we will try to understand the above MIDRASH. “CHOT’U B’KIFLAYIM,” “they sinned double,” means that their sin consisted of living a dual way of life, inconsistent with their heritage. This brought about a misunderstanding in their midst and ultimately caused the total split in their forces. “V’LAKU B’KIFLAYIM,” “they were smitten twice.” Not only was their homeland destroyed but they, too, as a people were (seemingly) destroyed by their enemies who scattered them all over the world. “U’MITNACHMIM B’KIFLAYIM,” “and they will be comforted double.” Their homeland will be comforted with the return of her children, and the children of Israel will be comforted when their forces will be reunited again under the flag of the Torah. As it is said, “Comfort ye, comfort ye, my people.”

Yes! No greater days of joy were in Israel than the fifteenth of Ab — meaning the accomplishment of uniting our people into one force as it is symbolized in the fifteenth letter of the Alef-Beth, the “SAMACH” which is made up of two letters “NUN.” The letter “NUN” was removed from the psalm of “ASHREI” because it refers to the fall of Israel. But King David brought back the “NUN” by doubling it, which makes the letter “SAMACH,” which in turn begins the sentence: “SOMECH HASHEM L’CHOL HANOFLIM,” “The Lord upholds all that fall” (Ps. CXLV:14).

In our own time we are already witnessing the fulfillment of the Psalmist’s inspiration and the Prophet’s double consolation for our people. The wilderness is becoming a garden, the deserted land repopulated. We can thankfully repeat with the Prophet Isaiah, “Comfort ye, comfort ye, my people.” Amen.
