“And the Lord spoke unto Moses in the wilderness of Sinai in the tent of meeting, on the first day of the second month, in the second year after they were come out of the land of Egypt, saying: Take ye the sum of all the congregation of the children of Israel . . . from twenty years old and upward, all that are able to go forth to war in Israel . . .” (Numbers 1:1-4). “The children of Israel shall pitch by their fathers’ ‘houses,’ every man with his own standard, according to the ensigns . . .” Rashi comments: “The color of each of the four standards was the same as the corresponding stone (Exodus XXVIII:17) for each of the four tribes in the breastplate of the High Priest.” Ibn Ezra comments: “According to tradition the standard of Reuben had a figure of a man, the standard of Judah that of a lion, the standard of Ephraim that of an ox and the standard of Dan the figure of an eagle.”

Up to this time, every success the children of Israel made on their way to the Promised Land was not through their own effort. God brought them out from Egypt. Even the war with Amelek was fought by God, as Rashi comments: When the hearts of the Israelites were directed toward God, they prevailed and that happened when Moses’ hands raised heavenward (Exodus XVII:11-12). But now, in the second year after their departure from Egypt, they were told to form an army with all the different branches necessary for functioning properly. The army was not made up of slaves or mercenaries, but from their own people. Everyone from twenty years and upward had to give military service.

A complete plan for forming a perfect national order was given to the children of Israel in the desert of Sinai, in the same way as when God created the first man. He formed him out of the earth and then blew into his nostrils the spirit of life, that is, the body was formed first and then the spirit. And this pattern of creation was used when God molded the people of Israel into a nation. This denotes the importance of recognizing that man’s main characteristics are earthly in nature, that people are not like celestial spirits who are all alike. Man’s nature differs one from the other. “K’SHEM SH’EIN PARTZUFEHEN DOMIN KACH DEOTEIEM ENON SHAVOT” (Sages); “Just as their faces are not alike so are they in nature not equal.”

The children of Israel were now told to mold themselves into a single nation, but with each tribe retaining its own inborn characteristics. A peoples’ advancement and progress comes from the multiplicity of aptitudes of its population. Today, we know the importance of first finding the extent of a man’s ability before he decides on a vocation. He will be of greater value to himself and to the society in which he lives, if he is encouraged to do the things for which he is best fitted. — Yes! The twelve tribes became a single people, but each tribe was allowed to pride itself with its own flag and symbol depicting its inherent quality and field of greatness. The tribe of “LEVI” was chosen to lead the people in their spiritual needs because they had not sinned in the making of the golden calf and had even tried to wipe out that sin by killing three thousand of those who had participated. Their record of protecting the honor of their people stemmed from their forefather “Levi” who killed all the people of Shechem because they had defiled his sister Dinah (Genesis XXXIV:1-26). Also Pinechas killed an officer of a tribe for bringing shame to his people (Numbers XXV:7-9).

Up to that time, the “B’CHORIM,” first-born, were the spiritual leaders of the people of Israel. But they proved to be a great disappointment. Cain, the first-born, killed his brother Abel. Yishmael, the first-born, was a “PERE-ADAM,” a wild man. Esau, the first-born, acted terribly towards Jacob. Reuben, the first-born son of Jacob, did not receive high praise from his father. Menasheh, the first-born son of Joseph, was not recognized as the B’CHOR by his grandfather, Jacob. — The responsibility for the “MISHKAN,” the place for the development of the people’s spirit, was of too great importance to be entrusted to the B’CHORIM, who came from the various tribes, and all with different aptitudes. The tribe of Levi was found to have the proper inclination for this service, therefore, they replaced the B’CHORIM. The significance of this ministry is to be noted in the age limit the Torah imposed, from thirty years and upwards, whereas the army age requirements started at twenty. Spiritual responsibility is much greater than that involved in carrying a weapon.

Father Jacob pointed out the difference in nature of each tribe (Genesis XLIX:1-28) and all standards carried by the tribes bore symbols depicting the nature of the various people involved, and at the same time told somewhat of the particular tribe’s ability. When they entered into the land of Israel, these pictorial standards helped in the decisions regarding which part of the land was apportioned to the various tribes. As it is said: “Zevulun shall dwell at the shore of the sea, etc.” (Genesis XLIX:1-28).

The children of Israel prepared their physical and spiritual armies long before they entered their promised land, and even before they departed from Egypt. “HINEH AM B’NAI ISRAEL RAV V’ATZUM MIMENU” (Exodus I:9), “The people of Israel are too many and too mighty for us.” Yes! They were already recognized as a nation even when they were in Egypt or in the desert. — Today, after two thousand years of Galut, we returned to our homeland fully organized and united as an old nation.

We read about Joseph’s interpretation of the dreams of Pharaoh’s chief of butlers and the chief of bakers, and in both cases he used the word “YISA,” “lift up.” But the difference was that the “YISA” for the butler denoted returning to his previous glory, and for the baker it meant he would have his head removed. The latter interpretation was based on the baker’s dream, in which a bird was eating from a basket placed on top of his head. That could only indicate that the man under the basket was not alive for the simple little bird was not afraid. Yes! Many people who lost a war were extinguished, vanished from the world arena. Their existence as a nation was ended, because as soon as they lost the physical war, there remained nothing to keep them alive as a people. They completely lost their social and spiritual hold of themselves. But, our people, in spite of the terrible sufferings endured in the long Galut, managed to keep order in our midst and our lives regulated in accordance with the Torah that kept us prepared for our physical and spiritual needs as a nation.
