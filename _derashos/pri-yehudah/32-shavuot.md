The SHAVUOT holiday is blessed with many different names, and each of the names has some meaning. In Exodus XXIII:16, the Torah calls it “CHAG HASHAVUOT” or “CHAG HAKATZIR,” “the Holiday of the Weeks” or the “Holiday of the Harvest.” In the book of Numbers XXVIII:26, it is called “YOM HABIKURIM,” “the Day of the First Fruits.” In the MISHNAH MEGILAH 30 CHAGIGAH 17, etc. it is called “ATZERRET,” “a Festival of Consecration.” But in our prayer books we refer to it as the “Z’MAN MATAN TORATEINU,” as the time of the deliverance of the Torah. — Today, when we have no Temple in Jerusalem and none of the ancient rituals that took place in the Temple, the most significant meaning of this holiday is that on this day we received the Torah.

Although it would be fitting to call this holiday “CHAG HATORAH,” “the Celebration of the Torah,” still tradition has purposely called it “CHAG HASHAVUOT,” “the Festival of the Weeks,” which refers to the counting of the seven weeks from the second day of Passover up till SHAVUOT, as it is written: “Seven weeks shalt thou count thyself, from when the sickle begins to be put to the corn thou shalt begin to count seven weeks. And then keep the festival of weeks unto God” (Deut. XVI:9-10). And in Leviticus XXIII:15, “And ye shall count unto you from the morrow after this Sabbath (referring to the first day of Passover) from the day of your bringing the “OMER” of the waving, seven complete Sabbaths shall it be.” — By calling this festival “SHAVUOT,” meaning “Weeks,” our tradition points to the importance of “HACHANAH,” preparation, before one does a MITZVAH.

Unlike its treatment of any other holiday, the Torah does not tell in what month and on what day of the month we celebrate the festival of SHAVUOT. Instead, the Torah directs us to count fifty days from the second day of Passover, and the fiftieth day is SHAVUOT. Because SHAVUOT is the day when the Torah was delivered to our people, therefore it is more important to show the fifty days of preparation to the receiving of the Torah than the date itself. Accordingly, this important festival is named SHAVUOT to denote the importance of “HACHANAH,” preparation to any commandment of the Torah, and more so, to the whole Torah.

Tradition puts great emphasis on the preparing for a MITZVAH. Before one does a MITZVAH, he has to say: “HINNI MUCHAN U’MEZUMAN LEKAYEM . . .” “I am prepared to do such and such a MITZVAH.” If the MITZVAH is of the type that is between man and man, then no preparation is required. For when someone requires help, it is to be given at once without any hesitation. But if it is a MITZVAH between man and God, the proper “HACHANAH,” preparation, is necessary.

We consider the eve of the Sabbath or a holiday as a part of the MITZVAH. Actually it is much harder to make the preparation for the Sabbath or the festival than it is to celebrate the Sabbath or festival itself. But we Jews, being a people of deeds as well as of belief, know that before one can make or do anything of importance, it is first necessary to plan it out.

Our people were ready to be given the Torah long before the day on which it was actually received. Immediately after the departure from Egypt they began counting the days till the great moment of revelation. They had a perfect foundation, for it is said: (Exodus XIX:17) “And they placed themselves standing upright at the foot of the mount.” The Talmud (Sabbath 88) comments: “MELAMED SH’KAFAH ALEIHEM HAR KEGIGIT . . .” This is to say that God suspended over them the mount and said to them: If you will take My Torah, well and good. But if not, ye will be buried under the mount. Nothing was hidden from them. They knew very well of the troubles and tribulations that they would endure in the future because of the Torah. “LAMAH NIKRA SHMAH SINAI, MISHAM YARDAH SINNAH L’UMOT HAOLAM.” “Wherefore is the name of the mount “Sinai,” because out of this mount came the hatred of the people of the world towards the people of Israel” (Talmud Sabbath 89). The Torah tells us (Exodus XX:15), “And all the people saw the sounds and flames and the tones of the Shofar and the mountain smoking . . .” which may mean as Rabbi points out “ROIN ET HANISHMA,” “They saw what they heard.” Yes! The people of Israel very well heard and imagined what the acceptance of this Godly mission would bring upon them in the future. Inquisitions, crematoriums, etc., but nevertheless they placed themselves standing upright at the foot of the mountain, ready and willing to accept the Torah.

The sages tell us (Yalkut Numbers) that at the time when the people of Israel received the Torah, the people of the world became jealous and asked: O Lord! Why are the people of Israel more privileged to have the Torah than we are? And God answered them: Bring your proof of pedigree. — The people of the world could not understand why the Jews accepted the Torah knowing that it would bring on them trials and tests of the severest type. The answer was the pedigree of the Jewish people, which is such that they are not easily frightened by tests and trials. Abraham, their forefather endured and passed many. So did Isaac and Jacob. So did the people themselves in Egypt. Yes! The people of Israel saw all that would come to them through accepting the Torah, but they also saw the “KOL HASHOFOR,” “the sound of the Shofor,” “SHEL MOSHIACH,” “of the redeemer” who will eventually enter into the heart of the peoples of the world and open their eyes so that they will see and recognize the true value of a life in accordance with God’s teaching through His Torah.

It is customary to read the Book of Ruth on SHAVUOT. Some of the sages say that it is read in honor of King David, who was a descendant of Ruth and was born on SHAVUOT night.

King David, the anointed king and sweet singer of Israel, whose descendant was designated by Providence to be the long awaited Messiah, is privileged to have his birthday celebrated together with the observance of the receiving of the Torah. David’s ancestry as it is pictured for us in the Book of Ruth is far from admirable. Ruth was a Moabite, of the people the Torah forbids forever from entering the congregation of the Lord. No other peoples were punished so severely as the Ammonites and the Moabites. The Egyptians were punished up to the third generation and the Edomites up to the tenth generation. But the Ammonites and Moabites were punished forever (Deut. XXIII:4-5) “Because they met you not with bread and water on the wandering when ye came forth out of Egypt . . . Thou shalt not seek their peace nor their well-being for ever.” — The very beginning of the Moabites came through an act of evil in the highest degree so that even the most uncivilized of people consider it to be incest. Their first mother broke a very elementary law accepted by all the people through the history of humanity (Genesis XIX:31-38). Although our sages did forbid only the males of the Moabites to enter into the congregation of the Lord, as they said: “AMONI V’LO AMONIT MOABI V’LO MOABIT” (Herioth X:2), “that only the Ammonites male but not the female, are forbidden to enter the congregation of the Lord,” nevertheless, we find the relative of Naomi refused to marry Ruth and even Boaz hesitated to take her as a wife. “PEN ASHCHIT ET NACHLATI,” “Lest I mar mine own inheritance,” and Rashi comments: For it is said that the Moabites shall not enter the congregation of the Lord (Ruth IV:6). — Also King David’s paternal family was not much to brag about. Even a simple man would be shamed with an ancestry that came to be from such an incestuous act as that of Judah and Tamar his daughter-in-law (Genesis XXXVIII: 15-30).

We were chosen by Providence to bring to humanity God’s teachings, which alone will lead to the great era when the world will be perfected under the reign of the Almighty. Therefore was the Torah given to us that we shall move mankind to call upon the Name of God and all the wicked of the earth shall turn unto Him. Our aim must be to bring to culmination the prophecy of Isaiah (XI:6) “And the wolf shall dwell with the sheep, and the leopard shall lie down with the kid, and the calf and the young lion and the fatling (shall be) together, and a little boy shall lead them.” And also the prophecy (II:4) “And they shall beat their swords into ploughshares, and their spears into pruning-knives: Nation shall not lift up sword against nation, and they shall not learn anymore war.” In other words, our mission is to help destroy evil, not the evil doers, by teaching and showing them the ways of God. Our Messiah, the son of David, was designated to bring this happy ending. Therefore Providence has chosen the Messiah from a line of ancestors possessing injurious nature or qualities, so he can learn to free himself of the ill-repute that he inherited. He must himself be one who can prove that he changed from morally bad to conforming in disposition and conduct to the divine standard of right and justice.

Ruth the Moabite showed an extraordinary love for her mother-in-law. She left her country and life of abundance and chose to suffer hunger, together with her beloved mother-in-law, Naomi. Yes, she, Ruth the Moabite, laid the foundation for the birth of Messiah. She proved able to bring out something most magnificently pure and noble from what was known to be ugly and repulsive to the moral sentiments. The total perfection of that change-over was then embodied in her great grandson David, the anointed King of Israel.

Our sages (Brochot 3b) tell us: “KINOR HAYAH TALUY L’MALAH MIMITATO SHEL DAVID U’BACHATZOT LAYLAH NASHAVAH BO RUACH TZIFONIT U’MINAGEN M’ELOV U’MIYAD HAYAH OMED MISHNATO,” “A harp hung down over the bed of King David and at midnight a northern wind came and moved the strings and it played by itself and soon King David woke up and arose to help the people in their problems.” The same King David who said (Psalms XXVII. 3) “If an army should encamp against me, my heart shall not fear . . .” And in the Book of Samuel (XVII:10), it is said about him: “And he also that is most valiant, whose heart is as the heart of a lion . . .” awakened to the mild tone of an harp. It so moved him that he would not even think of falling asleep again. David said of himself, “At a time when all the kings of East and West sleep in their beds, I am taken up with answering to questions of the law about “SHFIR,” if a woman discharges a sac of foetus, or “SHILIA,” the after-birth, when will she be permitted to be with her husband.” Yes, at a time when kings turned over to their other sides and continued to sleep, King David helped various people to sleep well and in accordance with God’s law. And when the morning star came out, there appeared before him the wise men of Israel and said, “Our lord the King, your people of Israel need ‘PARNASAH,’ a livelihood,” and he advised them on that (Sages).

From where came the unnatural power in the harp to be able to so affect the mighty King David, asked our sages. And they answered. Rabbi Chanina Ben Doso said that of the ram which was created during twilight and served as the “KORBON” offering instead of Isaac, nothing was wasted. Even its veins were used as strings in the harp of King David. These strings that reminded David about the “AKEIDAH” and many of the tests that were put to our forefather Abraham, and about the “AKEDOT” that his children faced since the time they accepted on Sinai the mission from God, these strings moved him to a point where he could sleep no more. (This paragraph and the preceding paragraph was sent to me by Rabbi Jacob L. Baker of the Bronx.)

And though the beginning of King David’s ancestry was not that of the perfect man, the change within him led to the designation of his descendent as the Messiah.

These are the things that Messiah is destined to bring to the world: a total change from the greatest of evil to a life of complete peace in the whole world. Therefore our sages combined the “CHAG HATORAH,” the festival of the Torah, with the birth of the Messiah. Because it is the teachings of the Torah that could make Ruth what she became, and it will be the teachings of the Torah that will finally bring about the establishment of the wonderful prophecy of Isaiah pertaining to the days of Messiah.

The Book of Ruth is further proof to the importance of “HACHANAH,” preparation. Our sages, commenting on the sentence (Ruth IV:12) “And may thy house be like the house of Perez, whom Tammar bore unto Judah, through the seed which the Lord will give thee of this young woman,” said: “HAZERA SHEHU BO MIMAKOM ACHAR V’EIZEH ZEH MELECH HAMOSHIACH,” “The seed that came from another place and that is the king Messiah.” Our sages also said: “MEHUSO NIVRA KODEM BRIAT HAOLOM” (Pesachim 53), “the qualities of Messiah were created before the creation of the world.” Yes! The Torah as well as the creation of the world served in the preparation for the Messiah to come.
