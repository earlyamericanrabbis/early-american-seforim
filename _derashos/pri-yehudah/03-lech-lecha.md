“And God said to Abraham, “Isolate thyself from thy country and from thy birthplace and from the house of thy father, unto the land which I shall show thee.” Rashi comments, “LECH LECHA LHANAASCHA ULTOVASCHA, V’SHOM EASCHA LEGOI GADOL, V’KAAN EY ATA ZOCHA L’BONIM, V’OD SHEODEA TIVACHO B’OLOM.” “Go away for your own benefit and your own good, and there will I make thee unto a great nation, for here you cannot have children. Furthermore, I will cause that you and your ideals will become known all over the world.”

The life of Abraham as it is narrated in today’s parshah mirrors the life of his children, the people of Israel. Abraham devoted himself entirely to the benefit and improvement of humanity. He was kicked and trampled by the unbelievers, the Nimrods. He was put into a fiery furnace for the preaching of the so-called new ideas. But Abraham still believed that by showing kindness even to those who hated him, he would ultimately win them over to his side. And he did have a great number of followers. And he did convert many people to his way of life, for which he received the title AV HAMON GOYIM, Father of a Multitude of Nations. Abraham now felt that his mission was a success. But, alas, in devoting himself to the world, he had forgotten completely his own life and his future. Then came God’s call, “LECH LECHA.” “Go, isolate thyself from thy country, etc. — and there I will make thee into a great nation.” “Here you cannot have children.” The Hebrew word for children is “BANIM” and the same word may be pronounced and translated “BONIM,” builders. Children are those who continue to build on their parents’ foundations, the kind of children who are willing to struggle and bring offerings for their parents’ ideals. “LECH LECHA,” — You must go, Abraham, for in this land even your children will not be yours, your belief and your ideas will not take root.

God did not tell Abraham to which land he should proceed. He only said to him: “Unto the land which I shall show thee,” “EL HARETZ ASHER AREACHA.” But our sages delved into a similar expression by God to Abraham, “LECH LECHA EL ERETZ HAMORIAH,” when God told Abraham to take his son Isaac to the Mount Moriah from where Israel’s Torah and its teachings come. Therefore, the first LECH LECHA, “Go into the land which I shall show thee,” meant to the land where his children could be raised in accordance with the teaching of the Torah, that is, in Zion, as it is written, “KI METZION TETZEI TORAH UDVAR HASHEM MIRUSHALAYIM,” “Out of Zion will come forth the Torah and the word of God out of Jerusalem.”

Abraham’s children, the people of Israel, have been carrying the message of God and the teachings of the Torah to the people of the world. We were given the title “OR L’GOYIM,” “The Light for the Nations.” Twice were we scattered and exiled among the nations but we continued in our mission to teach the morality of the Torah to the nations of the world. To our sorrow, it did not take root. We were kicked, trampled and persecuted throughout our history because of our teachings. We too, as Abraham of old, should have heard the call of God, “LECH LECHA,” “Go back to your homeland. Only there can you build your life on a solid foundation. Only there can you build in accordance with your belief.” All the nations of the world have been benefiting from Israel’s outstanding talents and genius, but as long as we were not producing in our own land, it was not we who were being credited with the harvest of our minds, it was the nations wherein these things took place. Now, with the return of our people to their homeland, we become more and more the “light for the nations.” Since the time that Israel regained its independence, many other nations became independent. However, one can easily see the difference between the progress made by Israel in such a short time, and the progress of the other nations. Israel has gathered together her people from all over the world and melted them into a united people, whereas the other newly independent countries cannot even govern themselves although these nations are made up of the people who had previously lived there. Small wonder, then, that many nations send representatives to Israel to learn the methods of government, economy, etc. We are, indeed, OR L’GOYIM, a light for the nations.

Further, the Torah tells us that Abraham continued on his journey till he came to the land of Canaan. There God appeared to Abraham and said: “Unto thy seed will I give this land,” and there he built an altar unto God, Who appeared unto him. Rashi comments that Abraham built the altar AL B’SURAT HAZERA V’AL B’SURAT HARETZ, for the promise that he will have children and for the promise that the land of Canaan will be theirs. Abraham, as well as his children, the people of Israel, never fought so hard and so valiantly as for their own land. In our own time as well as in Biblical times, our people have shown extraordinary strength and courage in defending their country even against greater numbers.

Abraham again asks, “BAMAH EDA KI IROSHENAH?” “Whereby shall I know that I am to take this land into possession?” Rashi comments: Abraham said to God, “B’EISEH Z’CHUS YEKAIMU BI?” “Why am I privileged to take it into possession?” And God said to him. “B’Z’CHUS HAKORBONOT,” “That is on account of your sacrifices” — Abraham brought many offerings on the altar of the world. So did his children through the generations bring offerings for every country in which they lived. But nowhere could they build a permanent home for themselves. Always they heard the call, “LECH LECHA,” “Go from this land.” — Therefore, Abraham wants to know why in the land of Canaan they will be able to build and root whereas in any other land they failed. The answer was, “B’Z’CHUS HAKORBONOT” — for the sacrifices that you are bringing on your own altar, for your own land, for your own people, are greater in scope and bring better results.

B’SHAH SHEIPIL NIMROD HARASHAH ET AVRAHAM L’TOCH KIVSHAN HAESH AMAR L’FONOV GAVRIEL, RIBONO SHEL OLOM ERED VEATZIL ES HATZADIK MEKIVSHON HAESH OMAR LO, ANI YACHID BEOLOMI VEHOO YACHID BEOLOMO, NOEH LAYACHID LEHATZIL ES HAYACHID. When Abraham was hurled by the evil Nimrod into the furnace, Gabriel entreated God to permit him to rescue Abraham. But the Divine Answer was: “I am One in the world and he is one; it is proper that He Who is One shall succor him who is also one.” Abraham stood alone in his belief in One God. KOL HAOLOM MEIVER ECHOD VEHOO MEIVER ECHOD. The whole world was on one side and Abraham was on the other side. Therefore, God Himself came to his rescue. We, his descendents, are also alone in carrying the message of the One God to the people of the world, and God Almighty will surely save us from our adversaries and help the little country of Israel from strength to strength, to regain the old title of “OR L’GOYIM,” “The Light for All the Nations.” Amen.
