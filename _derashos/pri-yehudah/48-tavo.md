“And it shall be, when thou art come in unto the land which the Lord thy God giveth thee for an inheritance, and dost possess it, and dwell therein; that thou shalt take of the first of all the fruit of the ground, which thou shalt bring in from thy land . . . and thou shalt put it in a basket, and shalt go unto the place which the Lord thy God choose to cause His Name to dwell there. And thou shalt come unto the priest . . . and say unto him: I profess this day . . . that I am come unto the land which the Lord swore unto our fathers to give us. And the priest shall take the basket out of thy hand, and set it down before the altar of the Lord thy God. And thou shalt speak and say before the Lord thy God: An Aramean near to ruin was my father (Jacob), (the Passover Haggadah renders it, “An Aramean, i.e., Laban sought to destroy my father”), and he went down to Egypt, and became there a nation, great, mighty, and populous. And the Egyptians dealt ill with us . . . And the Lord brought us forth out of Egypt . . . and He has brought us into this place, and hath given us this land . . . And now, behold, I have brought the first of the fruit of the land, which Thou O Lord, hast given me . . . And thou shalt set it down before the Lord thy God, and worship . . . And thou shalt rejoice in all the good . . . given unto thee, and unto thy house, thou and the Levite and the stranger that is in the midst of thee . . .” (Deuteronomy 26:1-11).

The “SIFRI” comments: “ASEH MITZVAH HAAMURAH B’INYAN SHEBISCHARAH TICHNAS LEARETZ.” “Perform the commandments said in this parshah for which you will enter into the land.” — The MITZVAH of BIKKURIM, first fruits, like many MITZVOT of the Torah was designed by Providence to be accomplished only in the promised land. As it is said: “And it shall be when thou art come in unto the land which the Lord God giveth thee for an inheritance and dost possess it and dwell therein . . .” Therefore, in order for the Israelites to observe the Torah in its entirety they must be possessing and dwelling in their promised land. But, by the same token, if they do not observe the commandments pertaining to the promised land, they will be surely expelled out of the land, as it is narrated in the “TOCHACHAH,” “Threat of Punishment” of this Sidrah.

The “SIFRI” has chosen the MITZVAH of BIKKURIM from among many other MITZVOT as the outstanding example of what are the “MITZVOT HATLUYOT B’ARETZ,” “Commandments that can only be performed when the Israelites dwell in their promised land.” — The MITZVAH of BIKKURIM, according to the sages, was given to implant within the heart of every Israelite the understanding that whatever one possesses has been given to him by God. When one learns this, then it is easier for him to accomplish the other commandments of the Torah.

The greatest factor that causes man to stray from the commandments of the Torah is material success in life. As it is said: “But Jeshurun waxed fat, and kicked — Thou didst wax fat, thou didst grow thick, thou didst become gross — And he forsook God Who made him, and condemned the Rock of his salvation” (Deuteronomy 32:15). — In order to train man to feel and even see that it is God Who gave him everything and anything, the sages found the MITZVAH of BIKKURIM to be the most suitable. As we read: “And thou say in thy heart: My power and the might of my hand hath gotten me this wealth. But thou shalt remember the Lord thy God for it is He Who giveth thee power to get wealth . . .” (Deuteronomy 8:17-18).

The Mishnah (Bikkurim 3) tells how to set apart the BIKKURIM, first fruits. When a man goes into his field and sees a fig that has ripened, a cluster of grapes, or pomegranate that ripened, he shall bind them with a cord and say, “These are BIKKURIM.” — In other words, he does not wait till all of the fruits are gathered together and then set apart for BIKKURIM. No, even before he knows if he has a good year’s crop he must set apart the very first ripened fruits to God. The Mishnah describes the impressive procession of the bringing of the BIKKURIM. All the villagers of a district assembled and spent the night sleeping in the streets of the town. In the morning the leader exclaimed: “Arise and let us go up to Zion!” When the Temple was reached, even the king had to place his basket of BIKKURIM on his shoulder and enter the court where the Levites met them singing the psalm “I will extol Thee O Lord for Thou hast lifted me up” (Psalm 30:2).

Yes! The MITZVAH of BIKKURIM and its impressive ceremonials help man to understand the saying of the Torah: “KI LO AL HALECHEM LEVADO YICHYEH HAADAM KI AL KOL MOTZA D’VAR HASHEM YICHYEH HAADAM,” “That man doth not live by bread only, but by everything that proceedeth out of the mouth of the Lord doth man live” (Deuteronomy 8:3). A man would not be considered higher than the other creatures if he had no ideals. The conception that it is God Who gave us the ability to acquire wealth, not our own efforts only, protects us from becoming self-centered and helps us understand the importance of sharing our blessings with the people about us. God entrusted us with those blessings, believing that we would do His Will with them. Those nations who thought they were mighty only through their own powers have already vanished from the face of the earth. The words “bread alone” in the text refers to man or nation living selfishly with no concern for others. Therefore when an Israelite brought the BIKKURIM he had to say: “I say it today after I came to the land.” Yes, today, at the time when he is happy, he must wholeheartedly acknowledge that God is the Giver of all. Finally the long wandering in the desert was ended and he was in his Promised Land. Then came the toil and labor to cultivate that wasted earth. And, at long last, he noticed a fig that had ripened, a cluster of grapes or a pomegranate ready for picking, and he is told by the Torah, “This is not yours, it belongs to God.” The Torah commands him to rejoice in all the good which the Lord hath given him. The Rabbis tell us that the BIKKURIM could be brought only during the season of rejoicing. When a man rejoices he is most vulnerable to stray from God, therefore the Torah tells him: “Stop! Recite the story of your own past (see Deuteronomy 26:5-16) and you will come to the realization that it is the Lord Who has given you all.”

The Sidrah continues: “And Moses and the elders of Israel commanded the people saying: keep all the commandments which I command you this day. And it shall be on the day ye shall pass over the Jordan unto the land which the Lord thy God giveth thee, that thou shalt set thee up great stones, plaster them with plaster. And thou shalt write upon them all the words of this law . . .” (Deuteronomy 27:1-9). — No monument for the triumphant army entering the land it conquered, but a constant reminder of the words of the Torah had to be inscribed on the stones. Again, to anyone glancing at the inscription, this was further emphasis of the realization that it was God Who caused the victory. They were told to erect the wall of stones on the Mount EBAL. In Deuteronomy 11:29-30 we are commanded: “And it shall come to pass, when the Lord thy God shall bring thee into the land whither thou goest to possess it that thou shalt set the blessing upon the Mount GERIZIM and the curse upon Mount EBAL.” Rashi comments: “They who pronounced the blessing were to face the Mount GERIZIM and then turn to Mount EBAL and pronounce the corresponding curse as in Chapter 27.15-26.”

The two mountains were chosen as a symbol. Although they are close to each other and have the same weather conditions, there was a vast difference between them. Mount Gerizim looked green, covered with vegetation, but Mount Ebal was as dry and gray as a desolate land. The wall of stone had to be built on the Mount Ebal and the words of the Torah inscribed on it, to point out that nothing comes to us by coincidence. It is in the power of man to choose for himself either blessing or curse by obeying or disobeying the commandments of God, as it is written: “Behold I set before you this day a blessing and a curse. The blessing, if ye shall harken unto the commandments of the Lord your God . . . And the curse, if ye shall not harken unto the commandments of the Lord your God, but turn aside out of the way which I command you this day . . .” (Deuteronomy 11:26-28). The six tribes standing on the Mount Gerizim toward which the blessing was proclaimed and the other six tribes standing on the Mount Ebal towards which the curse was proclaimed were not affected one way or the other. Only those who followed the commandments of the Torah were blessed.

In summary, BIKKURIM is an excellent example of the MITZVOT that depend on the full possession of the Promised Land by the people of Israel. The performance of this MITZVAH tends to relieve men and even whole nations from a state of selfishness. When delivering the BIKKURIM to the Temple the donor had to answer (“VEANITA,” “And thou shalt answer” \[Lit.\]) to a formal question as to the purpose of his bringing the fruits, whereupon he answered with the declaration that contained the full story of his people from the past to the present day in his homeland and the fact of his rejoicing with the good which the Lord his God had given him. This impressive ceremony filled his heart and mind with the knowledge that all blessing came from God and he was filled with trust in Him.

May it be the will of God to bring the complete redemption speedily in our days for the people of Israel and the entire world, that Israel may be able to rebuild the Holy Temple in Jerusalem, and we will be able to reinstitute this most meaningful of observances, the BIKKURIM, and all the other offerings. Amen.
