“And the life of Sarah was a hundred and seven and twenty years, these were the years of Sarah.” The text is literally “A hundred years and twenty years and seven years, the life of Sarah.” “Years” is repeated with each number to indicate that each has its interpretation. She was as sinless at a hundred as at twenty — for until the age of twenty sin is not punishable and therefore does not count, and as beautiful at twenty as at seven. The extra ending “The Years of the life of Sarah” indicate that they were all alike in goodness (Rashi).

This Sidra that presumably tells of the death of Sarah is named “HAYEI SARAH,” “The Life of Sarah.” In it very little is told of Sarah’s life and nothing of what caused her sudden death. Our sages did find in it hints as to what caused her demise, that also reflect upon her full and perfect life. On the surface, we see Sarah to be a very contradictory personality. She, together with her husband Abraham, accomplished the historic “LECH LECHA,” moved away from the Nimrodic influences of that time, and perfected themselves in the belief in One God Who demands righteousness from everyone as a condition for the existence of the world. But they were faced with a problem of continuity. Abraham’s complaint to God was: “HEN LI LO NATATA ZERA,” “You did not give me children” — to carry our ideals to the coming generations. — Here Sarah appears as a model of extraordinary generosity, when she suggests that Abraham take Hagar as a wife to bear children for him. This was a great “NISAYON” (test) for her; for though she might have lost the love of her husband through this, still she did as she had to, in order to fill the void between the “DOR HOLECH V’DOR BO,” “between the generations passing and the generations to come.” Then, in the sidra of last week, we find Sarah to be rather unkind when she suggests to Abraham “GARESH ES HOOMOH HAZOS V’ES B’NOH” — “Drive out this maidservant and her son.” This to Abraham was a tremendous shock and most difficult to do, until God said to him: “KOL ASHER TOMAR EILECHA SARAH SHMA B’KOLA” — “Whatever Sarah suggests to you, obey.” Yes, Sarah was right! In the commentary on the same sidra, we find Rashi pointing out that Sarah’s death was closely related to the incident of Isaac’s sacrifice. The shock of learning that he had almost been sacrificed, killed her. And one might think that Sarah was not as strong in her faith as was Abraham. He withstood the test of the “AKEIDA,” sacrifice, and she did not.

Our sages found enough material in the very first sentences of today’s sidra to prove the perfection of her character, and the contradictions that appear on the surface only serve to emphasize this on final analysis. The different traits of character, some good and some bad, are called MIDOS” — measurements. In other words, it is not expected of us to use only the good traits and avoid the bad. No! The evil traits can also be of great benefit to us when used at the right time and in the right measure. Many a poison, when used as a medicine, can cure an illness if taken in the proper amount. For example, jealousy is not always a bad quality. It bears within itself the idea of a mode of life which attaches importance to firmness, sincerity, loyalty, and even defiance, when there is inward conviction on the righteousness of a cause, and where cherished principles have been violated.

Why is the word “years” repeated with each number? Says Rashi, that is to indicate they were all alike in goodness. The Midrash also points out: “YODEA HASHEM YMEI TMIMIM V’NACHALATAM L’OLOM TEYEH” (Psalmist), “K’SHEIM SHEHM TMIMIM KACH SHNOTEIEM TMIMIM, V’ZU SARAH SHNOISEH TMIMAH B’MAASEAH.” “God knows the days of the perfect and their inheritance will be everlasting (Psalmist).” “In the same way as the righteous are perfect so are their years perfect, and this makes reference to Sarah who was perfect in her deeds.” What the Midrash wants to say is: The life of every man is divided into several stages. One may be perfect in one stage but fail in another. The perfect man not only forms a bridge that connects all the stages of his own life into one continuous faultless path, but he strives not to leave a void between his generation and the coming one. In and between the various stages of one’s life, or between the generations, can sometimes be formed a twilight zone, which is the time when “MAZIKIM,” forces of destruction, are created. An example of this is Noah, whom we find to be a perfect man in his generation, but who had no influence on the next one. And that void between the generations brought on the “DOR HOAFLAGA” whom God punished by the separation of their languages.

Sarah not only accomplished total perfection within her own life, but with her strong character and clear vision, she closed the gap between her generation and that of her son Isaac. Her accomplishments and her aim were the continuity of the ideals developed since the great “LECH LECHA,” the moving away from their former environment. “VAYOVO AVRAHAM LISPOD LESARAH ULIVKOTA,” “Abraham came to make a eulogy for Sarah and to weep for her.” Our sages point out that the letter “CHOF” in the word “ULIVKOTA,” which means weeping, is smaller than the other letters. In other words, Abraham did not cry very much for Sarah. This is in accord with the words of the prophet: “AL TIVKU LAMET, B’CHU BOCHU L’HOLECH,” “Do not cry for one who died after accomplishing everything demanded of him in life, but you may cry for one who just walked away from life without fulfilling the duties for which he was created.”

Our sages also remark that Abraham came then from Mount Moriah whither he had gone to sacrifice Isaac. There at Moriah he vividly realized that there was no contradiction in Sarah’s behaviour throughout her lifetime, that with her own example she made strong roots in the life of Isaac, and thus she left no void between the generations. Yes, there Abraham found the necessary words for a eulogy for Sarah — her life was perfected in the life of her own son Isaac. She did not die, as our sages point out. “KSHEM SHEZAROH BACHAYIM KACH HE BACHAYIM,” “As her children are alive, so is she alive.” The mission of her life was ended, then, at the Mount Moriah when Isaac proved to be a true “YORESH” following the teachings of his parents, and carrying forward the life of her spirit. Abraham had not yet completed his mission. He had other children in addition to Isaac. But Isaac was Sarah’s only child. Our sages also intimate that Sarah died after Rebekah was born, since one righteous person does not die before another is born.

Therefore is this sidra called “HAYEI SARAH,” “The Life of Sarah,” for it is here we find that only Sarah’s mission ended but her spirit still lived on. Yes, Sarah withstood all the “NISYONOT,” tests, together with Abraham, and perhaps more than Abraham. But her mission in life ended with the incident of Isaac’s sacrifice, when she lived to see the realization that her teachings had formed strong roots in her son’s character.
