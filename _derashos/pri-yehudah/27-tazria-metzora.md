“And the Lord spoke unto Moses, saying: Speak unto the children of Israel saying: If a woman be delivered and bear a manchild, then she shall be unclean seven days . . . But if she bears a female-child, then shall she be unclean two weeks . . .”

Said Rabbi Shimlai: “Just as the creation of man took place after that of animals, so is the law concerning man issued after that concerning animals” (Rashi, Lev. XII:1-5).

Man is the most perplexing creation among all the creatures. In the animal world, we know which one is mild, devoted, and can be trusted, and which one is wild, vicious, and can not be trusted. But, we find man at times to be as mild as a lamb, angelically good, sympathetic, devoted, and trustworthy, and at other times we see him vicious and murderous, worse than the most ferocious animal. Our sages tell us: “BORO MIN HAELYONIM UMIN HATACHTONIM,” “He made him of the nether and upper elements” (Genesis II:7, Rashi). “Man was created to have within himself all the animal world and a part of the celestial world.” Man was formed after all the creatures in order that he should be a part of the world of nature, but with the intent of raising him to heaven. The mind of man can and must direct the different animal instincts within him towards a constant progress that will help to improve his life and the lives of people about him. That is what our sages meant when they said: “Just as the creation of man took place after that of the animals, so is the law concerning man declared (or explained — for the word ‘NITPARSHAH,’ ‘declared’ or ‘explained,’ is used and not the word ‘NICHTAVAH,’ ‘written’) after that concerning animals.”

In the last week’s parshah we are told not to eat the flesh of certain animals, fowls, and fishes, for their influence might combine with the animalism within man (see Drashah on Parshat Shmini). In today’s parshah the Torah explains the perplexities of man, the strangeness of his behavior. As part of the human family, and knowing that an honest, good family life will also benefit him, and knowing that being socially cordial and truthful will bring him also greater joy and happiness, nevertheless, his conduct proves sometimes to be to the contrary. Our parshah deals with the anti-social evil called “LOSHON HARA,” “evil tongue,” for which sin leprosy comes as a punishment (Sages). — The Torah commands the priest to put the leper outside the camp of his people. The outcast proved he could not live peacefully with people, and was therefore punished with a contagious disease so that he would have to stay away from mankind.

Scripture tells us: “V’RAH HAKOHEN ET HANEGA . . . V’SEAR BANEGA AFACH LAVAN . . . V’TIME OTO,” “The priest shall look on the plague . . . and if the hair on the plague has turned to white . . . he shall pronounce him unclean.” White is always a sign of purity and cleanliness, as it is said: “IM YHIYU CHATAECHEM KASHANIM KASHELEG YALBINU, V’IM YADIMU CHATOLA KATZEMER YHIYU” (Isaiah 1:18), “If your sins will be like scarlet, they will become as white as snow, and if they will be as red as a red worm, they will become white as snow.” But this is only true when “KULO AFACH LAVAN TAHOR HU” (Lev. XIII:13), “if it is ALL turned white, he is clean.” When the white is only like a bandage that covers a wound so that it is a camouflage to keep the true nature of the wound from being visible, then is the man declared unclean and unfit to be among people. — Yes! White stands for cleanliness. Nevertheless in moral and spiritual cleanliness, one cannot be half unsoiled and half defiled. As the Torah says: “V’CHISAH HANEGA MEROSHO VAAD RAGLO” (Lev. XIII:12), “And the leprosy covered all his skin from his head to his feet.” Only when the plague has covered the man from head to toe, when the sinner recognizes his transgression and no longer is able to or willing to camouflage himself and desires to mend his ways, then and only then can he become a part of society again and the Kohen pronounces him as clean.

Scripture also tells us: “ADAM KI YAMUT BOHEL KOL HABA EL HAOHEL V’CHOL ASHER BAOHEL YITMA SHIVAT YAMIM” (Numbers XIX:14), “When a man dieth in a tent, every one who comes into the tent, and everything that is in the tent, shall be unclean seven days.” This is to teach us that only life and everything in life can be made holy, but not so death. The “NESHAMAH” of man, the celestial part given to man to help raise him above the animal world, is the only thing that keeps him holy and spiritually clean. Once the “NESHAMAH” is removed from man, then he is worse than other carcasses, for man contains within him all the creatures, even those considered unclean. The dead body of man is not only unclean so that it causes one who touches it to become unclean, but even the air around it becomes defiled. Our sages have gone further in their saying: “RESHAIM B’CHAYEHEM NIKRAIM MATIM,” “The evil one, even during his lifetime, is considered dead.” He can be dangerously contagious and must be separated from the good and wholesome society.

In summary: God has created man after all the other creatures in order to instill in him all the characteristics contained by the various types of creatures. The purpose of this was to put into man a restlessness that would make him adventurous, and by this means he would advance in the world. He is also blessed with a mind that is unlimited in its capacities. And in addition, God has given him the Torah with laws and regulations to live by in order to protect man from straying and thereby destroying himself. The laws of man could not well be explained before those of the animal world. Only after the Torah told us that there were some animals, fowls and fishes of which the flesh was unfit for man to eat, do we begin to understand man’s own nature, and that since he contains within himself the characteristics of the various types of animals, he must beware of adding to those characteristics the flesh of those animals. The combination may become too much for man to control. We also understand why, when he errs against society by using his evil tongue, he is punished with leprosy and is ordered out of the camp of his people till he is cured of his malady. The Torah tells us that an indication of the cure is when the plague has spread and is white from top to the bottom. In other words, when the sinner can no longer hide his true character, when he cannot camouflage himself again in a garment of righteousness, but will be recognized for what he is, that condition will bring a perfect cure and he is considered clean. Also, the category of uncleanliness of man when he is dead being greater than that of any other creature is understood, because man contains within him all that is unclean and ugly among the many creatures, and only during and through his lifetime can he build a holy wall around it. But when he is dead his body exposes all that it contains and thus causes the extraordinary degree of uncleanliness.

Therefore in the beginning of today’s parshah we are told: “ISHAH KI TAZRIA V’YALDAH ZACHAR VETAMAH SHIVAT YAMIM,” “If a woman be delivered and bear a male child, then she shall be unclean for seven days and for a female child she is unclean for fourteen days.” Even the birth of man causes his mother to be unclean, because at birth man is also lower than any animal. He is not yet able to function even as an animal and is less than what is expected of a human being.
