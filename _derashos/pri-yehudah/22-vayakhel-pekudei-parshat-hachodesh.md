“And the Eternal spake unto Moses and Aaron in the land of Egypt, saying: this month (NISAN) shall be unto you the beginning of months. It shall be the first month of the year to you” (Exodus XII:2). Rashi comments: “He showed him the moon in the first stage of its renewal, and He said to him, ‘The time when the moon renews itself thus, shall be unto you the beginning of the month . . .” Moses was in perplexity regarding the ‘MOLAD’ (the exact moment when the new moon begins) — how much of it must be visible before it is proper to consecrate it as a new moon. God therefore pointed to the sky with His finger and said to him: ‘Behold it is like this, and consecrate it . . .’ This chapter was spoken to him close to sunset and He pointed it out to him at nightfall (more literally, near darkness).”

Reading this parshah, one may ask, “If the Torah distinctly points out that NISON is the first month of the year, wherefore do we celebrate ROSH HASHANAH in TISHRI?” In the Talmud, we find that there is a difference of opinion as to when the world was created, in NISAN or in TISHRI. Why not accept what the Torah said, that NISAN is the first month? Further, we may ask why does the Torah point out twice “LACHEM,” that this month is “unto you” the first of the months?

God leads and cares for the world in two ways, “HASHGACHAH TIVIT” and “HASHGACHAH NISIT,” in a form of nature and in a form of miracles. Actually, even the natural way is full of miracles. With all our knowledge we still can not explain everything. However, what we see day in and day out we call nature. And if the Eternal had chosen to lead the world with miracles alone, they would in time be considered as natural phenomena. God has separated the Jews from all the other peoples in the world and gave them in addition the “HASHGACHAH NISIT,” which means that He did not remove from them the “HASHGACHAH TIVIT” that all the people in the world receive.

At no other time or to no other person did God appear with “HASHGACHAH NISIT.” Even to the patriarchs, God appeared only with “SHADDAI” as we read: “and I appeared unto Abraham, unto Isaac and unto Jacob by the name ‘SHADDAI’ God Almighty, but by My Name ‘YEHOVAH,’ Eternal, was I not known to them” (Exodus VI:2.3). Yes! “HATZALAH NISIT,” saving through miracles happened to the patriarchs also. But in general, being lead continuously by miracles, occurred to no one else. Moses was the first one to whom God appeared in “HASHGACHAH NISIT,” when He said to him “EHEYEH ASHER EHEYEH,” “I am that I am” (Exodus III:14). Rashi comments: “I will be with them in this sorrow and will be with them in the subjection they will suffer at the hands of other kingdoms.” — That means: I will lead them continuously with “HASHGACHAH NISIT.” All other peoples conquered by Egypt have totally disintegrated, but our people. From one family of seventy souls there developed in Egypt proper a nation fully organized into twelve separate tribes, with an army. “VACHAMUSHIM ALU B’NAI ISRAEL M’ERETZ MITZRAYIM,” “The children of Israel went up harnessed out of the land of Egypt,” (Exodus XIII:18). Rashi comments: the word “CHAMUSHIM” means provided with weapons. — Besides all the other miracles displayed in MITZRAYIM and at the Red Sea, this itself was the greatest miracle of all.

Therefore “ROSH HASHONAH” is celebrated in TISHRI, for the world and everything in it was created in TISHRI, and we are also a part of nature just like any other people of the world. But God added for us beyond His natural care, the “HASHGACHAH NISIT,” the care by miracles. Therefore the Torah points out “HACHODESH HAZEH LACHEM ROSH CHADASHIM,” “This month shall be unto you the beginning of months,” and reiterates “It shall be the first month unto you.” Only to us was given this month (NISAN) and all the miracles connected with this month. Miracles were never displayed to other people but to us. The same “HASHGACHAH NISIT” that happened in Egypt is happening for us all through the generations of our GALUT. “EHEYEH ASHER EHEYEH.”

We are similar to the moon. Though we see the moon at the end of the month diminished, soon we find her appearing full again. So it is with the people of Israel. As we say in our prayers at the renewal of the moon, “SHEHEM ATIDIM LEHITCHADESH KEMOTAH,” “They will be renewed as she is.” We never disintegrate. Like the moon, we only move away temporarily to a different horizon, but we will surely come back and shine again. Therefore God had to show Moses the phenomenon of the new moon, for it represents the people of Israel. And He showed it to him at nightfall (night represents the GALUT), to point out the “HASHGACHAH NISIT” for his people.

The Midrash tells us that in all the seven days of consecration of the “MISHKAN,” “Moses took it apart and put it up again each day but on the eighth day he did not take it apart.” There were seven MISHKANS during the history of our people and all were destroyed — the MISHKAN at the MIDBAR, in GILGAL, in SHILAH, in NOV, in GIVON, the first BET HAMIKDASH and the second BET HAMIKDASH. But the eighth one that will be built, as we hope, in our days, will stand forever. The Midrash also says that before the B’nai Israel made the golden calf, any one of them could have built the MISHKAN. But after the golden calf, only the entire congregation of Israel could build it. Therefore the Torah says: “And Moses assembled all the congregation of the children of Israel . . .” (Exodus XXXV:1).

Every Jew is protected by the “HASHGACHAH NISIT” that he will not be completely separated. “ISRAEL AF AL PI SHECHATA ISRAEL HU,” “A Jew even when he sinneth is still considered a Jew.” He is never dissociated from his people no matter how much he transgresses or feels himself lost. Always within him are carried scattered sparks which at the proper time will carry him back to his right path. But more so do we find it to be true for the entire congregation of Israel. Just as when the MISHKAN was dismantled and each part was put aside in order to be put up again, so is it with our people. After all the “CHURBONOT” and even in our long GALUT, the various elements necessary for our re-establishment were by the “HASHGACHAH NISIT” kept intact and are awaiting for Messiah to assemble them. The MISHKAN is called “MISHKAN HAEDUT,” “the sanctuary of the testimony.” Yes! It is a testimony to our people that all the parts are there and when God will it, they will be put up again to shine as in the days of yore. “HACHODESH HAZEH LACHEM,” “This month (of NISAN) is only yours . . .” “BENISAN NIGALU UBENISAN ATIDIM LEHIGAEL,” “They were redeemed from Egypt in the month of NISAN and in the same month the future redemption will take place” (Rabbis).
