“And the Eternal said unto Moses, Go unto Pharaoh; for I have allowed his heart to be hardened, and the heart of his servants, that I might set these My signs among them” (Exodus X:1).

Commentaries on the Bible ask: “Wherefore was Pharaoh punished for not obeying God’s warning through Moses to send the children of Israel out of Egypt? If God has hardened Pharaoh’s heart not to listen to Moses and not to be moved by the plagues then his “B’CHIRAH CHOFSHIT,” “free choice” to do good has been removed from him. What choice had he in that matter to be punished for it?

In order to find an answer to this perplexing question, we will try to develop a thesis as to how one improved his mental capacity in order to be able to make his choice properly between good and bad.

Mental fitness as well as physical fitness develops through a system of exercise. Encountering resistance is the best form of exercise in mental growth as well as in physical upbuilding. In physical development, one begins with little resistance, building up the opposition until one is able to withstand much greater stress. So is it true in the development of mental capacities. We find many people who were born with outstanding brains, yet did not develop in accordance with expectations. Since absorption of knowledge came to them too easily and effortlessly, they were unable to develop their mental abilities beyond those capacities with which they were born. In other words, the more resistance encountered while learning, the deeper and broader will be the scope of knowledge and understanding. This is why God forbad Adam to eat from the Tree of Knowledge. Knowledge acquired without effort, like picking and eating the fruit of a tree, is not of great value.

We pray daily: “ATA CHONEN L’ADAM DAAT UM’LAMED L’ENOSH BINAH,” “Thou graciously givest men fundamental knowledge and Thou teaches men to understand.” The gift of “DAAT,” the fundamental thinking ability, is given to men by God, but “BINAH,” understanding, comes through teaching. How does God’s teachings come to us? Only through “NISYONOT,” through trials and tests and resistances.

Abraham went through ten “NISYONOT,” tests, all of which he had to resist and withstand. Isaac had to shake off the influence of his brother Yishmael and also had a major part in the “AKEDAH.” Jacob faced the tests of running away from his parents’ home, struggling against his uncle Laban and his brother Esau, and combating his other children’s jealousy of Joseph, which finally resulted in Joseph’s being sold into Egypt. The children of Israel too, had to be exiled into Egypt, there to find the trials, tests, and resistances which were to develop into a way of living the principles and beliefs inherited from the patriarchs. After their departure from Egypt we find them called “AM KSHE OREF,” “a stiff-necked people, strongly rooted in their choice.”

“B’CHIRAH CHOFSHIT,” “free choice” as to how to live, was given by God to each and everyone of us. But in order to develop our spiritual and moral discrimination we must meet with “HACHBADAT HALEV,” with hardening of the heart, resistance to our choice. Things that come easily, go just as easily. A great number of ideas brought to light throughout the history of the world lost their continuity because their development did not come through tests, through resistance. But our religion, contrary to all predictions, has not lost its force upon our lives. Regardless how far a Jew strays from the prescribed way of Torah living, still his whole existence, his every day life is greatly influenced by the teachings of his fathers. The Jew has learned that the more opposition he encounters in his chosen way of life, the stronger he becomes physically and spiritually to withstand it. “V’CHASHER YAANU OSO KEN YIRBEH V’CHEN YIFROTZ” (Exodus 1:12), “And as they afflicted them, so they multiplied and grew.” The “YETZER HARAH,” “the evil desire,” in men was not created to lead astray. It was rather created to help men with their spiritual development, as a necessary obstacle on the way to free choice. Once a man develops his spiritual and moral way of life through trials, nothing will ever disturb him or take him from his chosen path.

Not everyone passes these tests. The forces of resistance sometimes overpower. “VAYAR HASHEM KI RABAH RAAT HAADAM BAARETZ, V’CHOL YEITZER MACHSHEVOT LIBO RAK RA KOL HAYOM . . .” (Genesis VI:5), “and the Eternal saw that the wickedness of man was great on the earth and that every imagination of thought of his heart was only evil continually.”

Pharaoh too, had the “B’CHIRAH CHOFSHIT,” “free choice” to develop himself and the Egyptian people on the right path of life.. The “HACHBADAT HALEV,” “the hardening of his heart” was used as a resistance in the development of his spiritual and moral capacity for goodness, kindness and understanding for the people about him. However, Pharaoh took the way of least resistance and thereby brought on the destruction of his people. The “ESER MAKOS,” “the ten plagues” by which the Egyptians were punished are the same in number as the “ASARAH NISYONOT SHEL AVRAHAM AVINU,” “the ten tests” which formed Abraham’s obstacles. This also indicates that the plagues must have been trials for Pharaoh, proving-grounds for his moral development. Instead they were the plagues brought on by Pharaoh when he chose the evil rather than the good path of life.

In our everyday life we encounter many people who, when beset by the trials of existence, become much stronger in their belief and convictions. There are a few, however, who become easily disturbed, disappointed, and disillusioned by obstacles in their chosen way of life, and to these, the trials are certainly plagues.

In today’s parshah we have the MITZVAH of “TFILIN.” It says: “The exodus from Egypt shall be to thee as a sign upon thine hand and as a memorial between thine eyes” — meaning that you shall write these paragraphs (dealing with the departure from Egypt) and bind them upon the head and upon the arm (Rashi, Exodus XII:9). — The MITZVAH of “TFILIN” can rightfully be called a sign and a memorial of what the Exile in Egypt has done to our people. Egypt was the testing ground that leads to our physical and mental growth. Therefore the TFILIN are placed on the arm and on the head in order to remind us to develop both, mind and body, to be able to think and act in accordance with the heavenly-descended and inherited ideals of our people.
