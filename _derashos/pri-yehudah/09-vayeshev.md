The SIDRAH begins with the words “VAYESHEV YAAKOV . . . B’ERETZ KNAAN,” “And Jacob dwelt in the land of Canaan.” The SIDRAH continues with the dreams of Joseph which caused his brethren’s hatred towards him, that in turn developed into the drama of “MECHIRAT YOSEPH,” the selling of Joseph.

Rashi comments on the words “VAYESHEV YAAKOV,” and Jacob settled in the land of Canaan, “BIKESH YAAKOV LESHEV B’SHALVAH, KOFATZ ALAV RUGZO SHEL YOSEPH, TZADIKIM M’VAKSHIM LESHEV B’SHALVAH, AMAR HAKADOSH BARUCH HU, LO DAYON L’TZADIKIM MA SHEMETUKAN LAHEM LEOLAM HABA ELA SHEMEVAKSHIM LESHEV B’SHALVAH B’OLOM HAZEH.” Jacob desired to live in perfect peace after he returned to his father’s land, but he was confronted with Joseph’s troubles. God said: “Isn’t it enough for the righteous, that which is prepared for them in the world to come, they are also demanding to have rest in this world?”

The MIDRASH TANCHUMAH says: “KOL MA SHEIRA L’YAAKOV IRA L’YOSEPH V’KOL MA SHEIRA L’YOSEPH IRA L’TZIYON. B’YAAKOV NEMAR VAYACHLOM, U’BYOSEPH NEMAR VAYACHLOM, U’BTZIYON NEMAR SHIR HAMALOS B’SHUV HASHEM ES SHIVAS TZION HAYINU KECHOLIMIM,” “Whatever happened to Jacob happened to Joseph. Jacob dreamt a dream and Joseph dreamt a dream and whatever happened to Joseph happened to Zion where the psalmist says when God will return his people to Zion we will be as in a dream.”

Jacob, when he left his father’s house on his way to his Uncle Laban, had a dream of a ladder standing on the earth and reaching up to the heaven. This was to point out to him how to design his life. Jacob had thought that it was enough for him to deal with the cultural and spiritual things in life, to remain the “ISH TAM YOSHEV HOALIM,” spending his life in the tents of Torah, and leave to Esau his brother the physical achievements. However, he was vividly shown in that dream that he, Jacob, must combine within himself both the spiritual and the physical. While Jacob lived with his Uncle Laban, we are told in the sidrah, he certainly learned the lesson well, so that he was quite a success in his physical attainments. He was the envy of Laban and his family. But after Jacob had returned to his father’s house, away from the demoralized environment of Laban’s house, he thought that here where the teachings of Abraham and Isaac had roots, it would no longer be necessary for him to live a so-called double life of both the physical and the spiritual. It would be enough for him to care for the spiritual needs and Esau would care for the physical needs of life. But God said: No, Jacob! You must strive for both. Esau is not to be trusted with your physical support. You must develop within yourself the ability to provide for all you require in life, physical as well as spiritual. Only in the world to come, when all life will be spiritual, will you be able to rest. All this was again pointed out to Jacob in the dreams of Joseph. Joseph dreamt two dreams: one, that he and his brethren were tying bundles of crops in the field, and Joseph’s bundle stood up and remained standing. But his brother’s bundles circled his bundle and bowed to it. In the second dream, the sun and the moon and eleven stars bowed to him. As pointed out before, these dreams brought on the envy and the hatred of Joseph’s brothers, who interpreted them to mean that Joseph was surely planning to rule over them. But Jacob saw in these dreams an indication from heaven that he had made a mistake. He was unable to afford even in the land of his father to leave for Esau the task of providing for material things. The two dreams of Joseph, one of bundling sheaves and the other of the heavenly bodies, was to imply to Jacob that he must strive to do both.

The Torah tells us that when Joseph told his dreams to his father “VAYIGAR BO AVIV, V’AVIV SHAMAR ES HADAVAR,” his father rebuked him, but he looked into the matter. Jacob rebuked Joseph for his thinking that all of them would bow down to him, but he also took note of the suggestion that he had made a mistake.

Whatever happened to Jacob and Joseph, was happening to their children. Through all the time of our GALUT we learned as Jacob did in the house of Laban, that we must not depend on other people to provide for our physical needs. Though we are supposed to be the predestined carriers of the Word of God to the world, we chose also to be equipped with every trade so that our requirements would be looked after. But how different it was when it came to our homeland, Eretz Israel. There we sent Jews for many generations not to engage in the upbuilding of the country so that it would in turn provide for their sustenance. Instead, these Jews came only to end their lives in the Holy Land and depended on the GALUT for their support. Only our generation awoke to see the mistakes we had made through all the past. The awakening came after the greatest suffering for our people. The pogroms in Russia and Poland brought the chalutzim to Israel where their first task was the drying up of the swamps that caused many maladies, thus preparing wasted land for an influx of our people from eastern Europe. Again, after the Balfour Declaration, with the so-called winds of liberalism blowing all over the world, we again fell asleep and did not heed the constant calls to wake up before it was too late. When the great shattering blow of Hitler’s beasts struck, we were unable to save six million of our best people. Then we came to the realization that we can no longer make peace with the GALUT. That our people must themselves build our homeland and not depend on other nations to do so. — Thank God, the growing Jewish population of Israel is becoming more and more self-supporting and independent, and is now fostering an artistic impulse with a distinctive Jewish character which has already won the acclaim of the world. Alas! Had this happened generations before, we could have saved so many millions from destruction and Israel would be well secured from the enemies from without. We hope that the future will make amends for past neglects.

The psalm that we sing before the grace after meals tells us: “B’SHUV HASHEM ES SHIVAT TZION HAYINU KECHOLMIM.” “When God will cause the return of our people to Zion we will be like in a dream.” But the words “HAYINU KECHOLMIM” in the past tense, can only be translated as “We were dreamers.” This may be explained as follows. Through all the time of our GALUT we had been dreaming of the days of Messiah when the redemption for our suffering people would come. But we did not wake from our deep sleep and give motivation to our dreams, as was the case with Jacob and Joseph who woke from their sleep and interpreted their dreams as a bidding to correct their ways of living. We talked about “SHIVAT TZION” in a passive way. Many of our religious leaders told us it was in vain for us to try to do away with the GALUT, that only God alone, when the time came, would do it. But the psalmist simply denounces such a false interpretation of our dreams. The psalmist says: When God will cause the redemption of our people, we will then realize that “HAYINU KECHOLMIM,” our dreams during all the days of our long GALUT were as those dreamt by our forefathers. Our dreams should have been interpreted as a bid to action in the development of our homeland, as the psalmist ends his song: “AZ YAVO B’RINAH NOSEH ALUMOSOV,” “Then will he come with melodious song, the one who carries the bundles.” Only when we ourselves build, when we carry our own bundles in the way of SHIVAT TZION, can we expect the blessings of God.
