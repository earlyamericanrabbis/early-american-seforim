“And the Lord spoke unto Moses in Mount Sinai saying, Speak unto the children of Israel and say to them, When you come into the land which I give you, then shall the land keep a Sabbath unto the Lord. Six years thou shalt sow thy field . . . But on the seventh year shall be a Sabbath of solemn rest for the land, a Sabbath unto the Lord . . . And if ye shall say: What shall we eat the seventh year? Behold we may not sow, nor gather in our measure. Then I will command My blessing (V’TZIVITI ET BIRCHATI) upon you in the sixth year and it shall bring forth produce for three years” (Lev. XXV:1-14). Rashi comments: “Why is it recorded of the law of the Sabbatical year, that it was communicated on the mount? To teach that as this law was ordained there with its general rules and its minute details, so is it with all the commandments of the Torah.” The Sforno comments: “A Sabbath year to the Lord — during this year of rest, landowners will have an opportunity to seek the Lord.”

The best proof to those who are doubtful that the Torah is “MIN HASHAMAYIM,” “from heaven,” is this Mitzvah of “SHMITAH.” No human being, even of the calibre of Moses, could take such a chance in predicting that the land would yield, on the sixth year, a crop for three years. And this is not a predication that a man may encounter only once in his lifetime, but on every cycle of seven years. Just imagine the time when the Jews lived in their land, and practiced the Mitzvah of SHMITAH. If the land would have failed to yield a crop as the Torah promised, they would have rejected the Torah and returned it to Sinai. History records no complaint pertaining to this promise by the Torah. — It is interesting to note that the Torah does not say “V’NATATI ET BIRCHATI,” “I will give My blessing,” but “V’TZIVITI ET BIRCHATI,” “I will command My blessing,” meaning that it does not even depend on the “M’KABEL,” “recipient,” as the case would be when one gives something, but the blessing will be given at all times as long as the Jews will be in Israel. Therefore, when the Jews did not observe the law of SHMITAH, they were driven out of the land, as it is said: “Then shall the land be paid her Sabbaths, as long as it lieth desolate, and ye are in your enemies land. Even then shall the land rest, and repay her Sabbaths. As long as it lies desolate it shall have rest, even the rest it had not in your Sabbaths when ye dwelt upon it” (Lev. XXVI:34-35). Rashi comments: “The seventy years of the Babylonian exile exactly correspond to the seventy Sabbatical and jubilee years which Israel failed to observe while in their land.”

The Sabbath of man is on the seventh day, the Sabbath of the land is on the seventh year (SHMITAH). After seven SHMITOT comes the Sabbath of the world, the jubilee year. On the Sabbath of man, we are told, “The rest is not for you alone but even your slave, your ox . . .” During SHMITAH your field belongs to everyone. “Let the poor man of your land eat from it and the remainder allow the beast of the field to eat.” During the jubilee year, we are commanded that “even land and property that one has paid for, goes back to the original owners.” Following such an economic system, one could not forever remain poor. One could not become totally disillusioned and fail in his trust in God.

Every small detail pertaining to human welfare is being dealt with in the Torah. Yes! One may be able to find good scientific reasons for the Sabbaths commanded therein, or any other of its laws. But in the final analysis, one must admit that no logic or scientific reason could have given the state of “KEDUSHAH,” that Sinai has given it.

Science has not yet found a way to solve the struggle between the possessing and non-possessing classes and the strife between these classes is ruining countries and peoples. But, when we examine the way our Torah deals with this question, we find that the precepts of the Sabbath of man, the Sabbath of the land and the Sabbath of the world are the real remedy for the economic disease; and that if humanity would follow the rules laid down by Moses, we would not be afflicted with these very problems.

And this we find in all the Sabbaths commanded in the Torah, that of all of them the Torah says “SHABAT L’HASHEM,” “a Sabbath to God.” All of them must have a degree of “KEDUSHAH,” “holiness.” The “KEDUSHAH” of the Sabbath of man is maintained through sharing his own rest with the people about him and even with the animals. As it says, “On the seventh day is Sabbath unto the Lord thy God, in it thou shalt not do any work, thou, nor thy son, nor thy daughter, nor thy man-servant . . . nor thy ox . . .” (Deut. V:14). So it is with the Sabbath of the land. The “KEDUSHAH” of the land is also expressed when in the seventh year the land becomes charitable and allows its crop to everyone who needs it and even to the animals of the field, as it is said: “and the Sabbath produce of the land shall be for food for you; and for thy servant . . . and for thy cattle and for the beasts of thy land” (Lev. XXV:6). On “YOVEL,” the year of jubilee too, the “KEDUSHAH” is expressed by the willingness to relinquish one’s possession and allow it to be returned to the original owner, as the Torah says: “In the year of Jubilee ye shall return every man unto his possession” (Lev. XXV:13).

Instead of giving scientific reasons to these laws, the Torah beclothes them with “KEDUSHAH.” “KI LI KOL HAARETZ” (Lev. XXV:23), “The Land is Mine.” Rashi comments: “Therefore do not resent these laws, the land not being really the property of any man.” — The knowledge that we gain from the law of “SHMITAH” helps us overcome our scepticism as to the authenticity that the Torah is from Sinai. The law of “SHMITAH” well proves that it is not a man-made law, but it is set up for us by the Eternal. Through this law we gain greater confidence and faith in God, and that helps us in the performance of the other laws and regulations of the Torah.

The second sidrah of this week begins: “If you walk in My statutes and keep My commandments, and do them —” (Lev. XXVI:3-5). Rashi comments: “SHETIYU AMELIM BATORAH,” “Ye shall study the Torah.” The meaning: “ ‘Ye shall walk’ (the Hebrew word ‘Telechu’) refers to one who goes in a definite direction. Therefore,” Rashi says, “it must mean ‘SHETIYU AMELIM BATORAH,’ that ye shall study the Torah, that which leads to the observance of the laws of the Torah.” This alone will give humanity a definite direction that will solve peacefully and satisfactorily the economic problems for every class concerned.

The Torah has particularly chosen the law of “SHMITAH,” including all the minute details, and beclothed all with a garment from Sinai. Why? Because there are many other laws about which it is best not to talk in details. For instance, the law “V’NOKEV SHEM HASHEM MOT YUMAT” (Lev. XXIV:16), “And he that blasphemeth the Name of the Lord, he shall surely be put to death.” Though it is a cardinal sin to deny God, why does not the Torah give us the details of this law? When the Torah talks about the profaning of God’s Name, the less said about it, the less profanity there is. However, with the law of “SHMITAH,” by listening to the many details, one gains a great amount of faith and trust in God. And as the law of “SHMITAH” was ordained on Sinai with its general rules and details, so it is with all the commandments of the Torah.
