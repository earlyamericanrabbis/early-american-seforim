SERMON FOR NEW YEAR’S EVE

Esteemed Congregation:

On this solemn occasion, the commencement of the New Year, I find no more suitable sentence as my topic sentence than the significant words of the Psalmist:

"*I thought on my ways, and turned my feet unto thy testimonies.*" (Psalm 119:59)

Dear Friends, happy is the man who is willing and capable to understand that there is but one God who is the sole creator, ruler, and sustainer of the universe. Happy is the man who understands that all the occurrences in the world are determined and limited by God's holy will. This man comprehends that: "*God surely made man right,*" and that there is a purpose which men shall achieve in this earthly life. To ascertain the moral aim in life, one must, however, associate with men of knowledge and reverence. He must also carefully examine and analyze at all times all of his ideas and intentions before they are spoken or carried out into practice. He who lives such a noble life fulfills daily:

"*I thought on my ways, and I turned my feet unto thy testimonies*."[^1]

This man lives up to the advice of the wise King:

"*Remember now thy Creator in the days of thy youth, while the evil days come not, nor the years draw nigh, when thou shalt say, I have no pleasure in them.*"[^2]

Concerning such a man our sages remarked: "*Happy is the man who lives a noble and sublime life when he is still at the vigor of manhood.*"[^3]

Yes, such a man deserves the title <span dir="rtl">איש</span>, a title of angels, as it is said[^4]: <span dir="rtl">והאיש גבריאל</span> and even of our Eternal God, as it is said: "*The Lord is a man of war.*"[^5] Men of this mental capacity and nobility of soul are called in the prophetic language "Angels of the Lord".[^6] Of such a man says the Thorah: "*For in the image of God made he man.*"[^7]

People of that kind are called by the rabbis, Masters, who always examine the accounts of their life. These men achieve the highest degree of morality.

There are people who are not capable of attaining this high degree of spiritual felicity. However, they also devote most of their time indulging in the Thorah, in service of God, and in practice of loving-kindness. They are men in whom the spirit of God and of mankind find delight.[^8]

Every Friday is for these men like the eve of the day of atonement. They prepare themselves through spiritual and physical purity to obtain the additional spiritual endowment which comes with the arrival of the Sabbath.[^9]

These men, my friends, understand how to cleanse themselves from the defilements of everyday life and how to become befitting of a spiritual exultation.

My friends, many of us will recall how on the day of Sabbath the Divine Majesty dwelled in every corner of our houses. The candles lit by our dear mothers radiated a certain divine and glorious brightness. I may say that it was likened to the "*light which the Lord hath hidden for the righteous ones in the future world*."[^10] The table in the dining room was covered with a snow-white tablecloth. The challes baked by our mothers were to us likened to the shew-bread which was set before God upon the golden table in the sanctuary. All the dishes of Sabbath were holy, for they had been sanctified by the preparations of our mothers, who used to utter many a prayer while cooking the meals of Sabbath.

Is there, my friends, an orator or writer who could explain in words how much of devoted and divine love, happiness, and contentment hovered in every Jewish home? How impressed we were when receiving the blessing of our parents on <span dir="rtl">שבת ויום טוב</span> Eves?

Oh, friends, will this felicity ever be re-established? Are there words to explain the sacredness, chastity, and modesty that rested upon the holy and lovely faces of our dear mothers and sisters when they listened to the song of <span dir="rtl">שלם עליכם</span> performed by the males of the family? When our fathers and brothers came home from the Synagogue, their faces shining from the divinity of the Sabbath, the women were envious, considering the males more fortunate to serve the Lord more than they could. When our fathers uttered the words: "*Forgive and pardon all our sins and iniquities,*" words included in a prayer after <span dir="rtl">שלם עליכם</span>, how solemn was this moment; with how much of spiritual exaltation were these words offered? What a cry of regret and repentance lay in them! These moments were like those which the religious Jew has on <span dir="rtl">יום כפור</span> Eve, at <span dir="rtl">כל נדרי</span> Thus did our parents fulfill every Friday night:

"*I thought on my ways and turned my feet unto thy testimonies.*"

And there were, and thank God, there still are, Jewish people to whom every last day of the Jewish month is day of <span dir="rtl">יום כפור קטן</span> a day of repentance, of prayer, and of giving special charity. To them it is a little Yom Kipur. You and I surely remember how the less fortunate and needy ones used to come on Rosh Chodesh for charity, and how they used to bless the donators with a happy Chodesh, as a season of forgiveness. These people, my friends, have their day of: "*I have thought on my ways and I turn my feet unto thy testimonies,*" on every <span dir="rtl">ערב ראש חדש</span>, on every eve of the month.

My dear friends, need I remind you what the month of Elul meant to Jews in former days, when Jewish people were more religious? The entire month was a month of penitence and preparation for the holy days of Rosh Hashonoh and Yom Kipur.

Yes, my friends, they were prepared and fit to receive a <span dir="rtl">כתיבה וחתימה טובה</span>; they were fit that God shall bestow upon them His blessings from above; they could say: "*Look down from Thy Holy Habitation, from the Heavens, and bless Thy people Israel*."[^11] They could pray thus, for they could also say: "*I have done according to all Thy commandments which Thou hast commandeth.*"[^12]

My friends, permit me to put up this question: How many of us have even given it a thought to prepare oneself in this month of <span dir="rtl">אלול</span> for the days of Rosh Hashonoh and Yom Kipur? How many of us returned to God with all his heart and soul to beg Him for forgiveness for all the sins and iniquities committed toward God as well as toward our fellow men?

Let us, therefore, my dear sisters and brothers, stand up with reverence and devotion before our Heavenly Father. Let us pray to Him from within our hearts:

"Lord of the Universe, it is surely true that we have committed many a sin during the past year, that we have not failed only once to fulfill your Holy Will. Have mercy on us and spare us, for Thou art He who knows the mysteries of the human heart.[^13] Thou knowest that in reality we would like to do Thy Will; we are only detained, to our greatest regret, by our evil instinct.[^14] Thou knowest also that our needs and necessities in life are very great and our understanding limited.[^15] We, therefore, beseech Thee, O graceful and merciful Father, Look at our suffering and not upon our sins. Look, O God, how the best of our people have been slaughtered by the thousands and millions for the sanctification of Thy name.[^16] O, say to the earth: 'Earth thou shalt not cover their blood.' O, Lord, do that for Thy Holy Name's sake. Let our grief, pains and torments be an offering of forgiveness. Satisfy us with Thy goodness and gladden us with Thy Salvation; purify our hearts to serve Thee in truth. Our Father, our King, nullify all evil decrees against us, let a happy year begin for us. Remember us in the book of happy life, in the book of redemption and salvation, in the book of maintenance and sustenance. Give us, O Lord, an everlasting peace and happiness to all the world (and let all our children come back victoriously, safe and happy from the different battle- fields). Let, merciful Father, the past year end with all its misfortunes and catastrophes, and let the commencing year bring happiness and welfare to all men."

Amen
