Preached on Rosh Hashanah, 5717

Judaism is very sparing in its discussion of the afterlife. Being a this-world religion, Judaism leaves to the Almighty the destiny of the soul once it has made peace with its Maker. The Talmud, however, offers a few insights into the last Judgement.

According to our rabbis, the interrogating angel on high poses two pointed questions to the departed soul of man, “Did you deal honestly? Did you hope for salvation?”

Can you think of any better questions that we can ask ourselves as we stand before God and seek His beneficence? If there is one time of the year that we must seek to be loyal to the royal in ourselves, it is today. If there is a justifiable reason for self-introspection, it is on this day when man pleads for his life.

Before the defense presents its side of the litigation, it must be wholly familiar with every facet of the case in question. What is the position of the American Jewish community? An unusual incident, which occurred several thousand years ago, defines the situation of the American Jew.

The setting is the Temple Court where tens of thousands of Jews had gathered to witness the formal dedication of King Solomon’s Temple. When they proceeded to offer the sacrifices brought by the well-wishers, it was soon obvious that the altar was too small to receive the burnt, the meal, and the peace offerings. As an emergency measure, the King proceeded to hallow the middle of the court and it was there that the sacrifice were offered.

Too many sacrifices for an inadequate altar - this succinctly describes the spiritual condition of the American Jew. If only called upon, the American Jew will offer sacrifice; never has the American Jew been known to shirk responsibility when called on to do his utmost. It is not he who has been found wanting but rather the Solomons – the leadership – which has failed to erect adequate altars. It has made the altar too small; it hasn’t provided the necessary stimulus, the strident incentive to create the proper climate.

In one of Lewis Carroll’s fantasies, there is the incident of a padlock with arms and legs which went about saying to everyone it met, “I am looking for a key to unlock me.” The American Jew is going around looking for a key, seeking for leadership to map the way to spiritual liberation.

On this day of Judgment, the Jewish leadership of this country can rightly ask itself: “Have we dealt honestly? Have we hoped for our people’s salvation?” What kind of altar did we erect in this Temple of the Jewish Community? How many must plead guilty at the bar of history? How many have sold out their birthright for the proverbial mess of pottage? How well they know that they have never had the courage to speak out. They have lulled the people to sleep with a false dose of make-believe faith.

As a child I had a fancy for fables and one which comes to my mind at this time is titled, “The Emperor’s New Clothes.” It is the story of a naïve emperor who had a fancy for new and beautiful raiment. At the slightest opportunity he would change over and parade in his new garb.

One day two rogues who called themselves weavers appeared. They announced that they knew how to weave cloth of the most beautiful colors and elaborate patterns. Among other things, the clothes which they could manufacture, they claimed, had the power of remaining invisible to anyone who was stupid or unfit for public office.

“These must be unusual clothes,” thought the emperor to himself. “It will help me to determine those who are unworthy of their high station.” He ordered the weavers to proceed, and gave them a very large sum of money. The two weavers set up looms and appeared to be working very busily, though in reality they did nothing at all. They asked for the most delicate silk and the purest gold thread which they then stored away in their knapsacks and continued with their imaginary work at empty looms.

Day in and day out they asked the king for additional yarn. At long last the knaves announced that on the following day they would present the king with his new outfit. That night they sat up, supposedly working at their looms, and had sixteen lights burning so that everyone might observe his clothing and try on the new suit in front of the mirror. The emperor undressed and the rogues went through the motions of fitting on the supposed suit in front of the mirror.

Although he could see nothing but his nakedness, the emperor kept silent lest he be accused of being stupid. As he walked-about the palace, everyone praised his new attire lest they too be accused of being unworthy of their office. The lords of the bedchamber who were to carry his majesty’s train, felt about on the ground as if they were lifting up the ends of the mantle, pretending to be carrying something, for they did not wish to betray their supposed stupidity. As the king proceeded through the streets, he was applauded and lavished with praise. Not one person would openly admit that he was unable to see the truth much-publicized garb.

Just that moment a child happened to be passing by and seeing the emperor, he cried out, “The king is naked, the king is naked!” It was then that the king was filled with remorse and resolved thereafter to devote more time to the tasks before him instead of expending his energies on new and more appealing attire.

When I read this fable as a child, I was greatly amused. But today, when I recall this tale, I am no longer amused. I see in this incident a description of the sorry position of Jewish life in America. Like the emperor, the American Jew has expended his energies in emphasizing superficial appearances. Although he is naked of true faith, the leadership is inducing him to believe that minimal Judaism is quite adequate.

Capitalizing on the ignorance of the masses, the knaves have exploited the coffers of American Jewish life, they have taken the sild and the gold for themselves. What is more tragic, they have deprived us of our youth who seek secular outlets for their innate ability. Yes our leaders are going through the motions; they make plenty of noise; their names are in the headlines; the candlelight power which they exude is blinding.

The make-believe game is gaining momentum. But the tragedy of it all is the silence, the complete inertia of those who know that the king is naked. What is disconcerting is to see the better-informed Jews climb onto the bandwagon, sing the praises of this surreptitious faith. They rationalize their waywardness by replying, “This is America.” They don’t want to be left out of the crowd; they don’t want to be dubbed stupid, narrow-minded or old-fashioned.

Yet, if we really want to know the truth, let us ask our children. Are they motivated by its beauty? Does Jewish lore and do Jewish values pulsate through their hearts? Are they thrilled with their Jewish association? Speak to them – and you will observe that the king is naked, that all the improvisations on the theme of Judaism have accomplished very little.

The altar is too small in the Jewish community. The average Jew hasn’t been asked to really bring sacrifice for his faith. Since the discipline of Judaism does not motivate our lives, the stress of secularism has taken over.

An old saying has it that you cannot walk backward into the future. All the beautiful houses of worship will not convert one Jew to Judaism unless there is an exacting sense of duty to faith present in the services and on the pulpit. All the fancy educational programs will not realize the genuine Jewish child unless the teacher is genuinely educated and inspired by a spiraling sense of love for his faith.

The finest furniture, the most exclusive neighborhood, the best of man’s mechanical genius will not produce the better family life unless the parent is dedicated. And exercises his rights. The finest Jewish Community Center with all its trimming will not expand the survival of Jewish life if Jewish values are not stressed and if the leadership refuses to make Judaism the motivating force of all endeavor.

It is still the good, old-fashioned Jewish home that you and I have experienced which produces genuine Jewish leadership. It is still the traditional synagogue which is acting as the transmitter of our heritage. It is still the old-fashioned parent who, perhaps none too conversant with the best that modern psychiatry has to offer, knew the fine art of guiding children along the paths of righteousness. It is still the old-fashioned *Melamed* who, through a sense of unstinting dedication, produced a generation of Jews stimulated by the beauty of our rich and affluent lore.

Unfortunately, such words as “dedication, sacrifice, devotion, sincerity and loyalty,” are absent from the working language of the modern Jewish leader. How else can we explain the cheap pageantry and vulgarity which pervade too many houses of worship? We lack leaders who are genuine shepherds.

The pre-fabricated make-shift substitutes which parade under the guise of faith will not produce faith in our leaders, and much less faith in the Almighty. Appraise the offerings of the modern synagogue and determine for yourself whether they can inspire men to greater religious observance.

The tragedy of it all is the sorry lot of him who rejects such mockery of our time-honored traditions. Such a leader is immediately condemned as incapable and anti-social. Nurtured at the breast of banalities, the American Jew expects his leader to minister to the whims and fancies for the membership.

One of the most intriguing comments of our sages involves the sudden demise of the matriarch, Sarah. After her passing we are told that, “*Vayovo Avrohom lispod leSoro velivkoso*- Abraham came to eulogize Sarah and to weep at her bier.” If you will refer to the text, you will observe that the “*chaf*” in the word “*livkoso*” is smaller than the rest of the letters. And why is this letter smaller than the rest? Asked the rabbis.

They offered two explanations. Abraham didn’t cry too much when Sarah passed away because she was already old in years. The other reason was because “*shehaimis atzmo lodas*-she committed suicide.”

Although the first reason appears plausible, the second is most surprising. How could such a pious woman commit the grave sin of taking her own life? I feel that there is another explanation implicit in the words *shehaymis atzmo lodas*. *Das* here means learning. She gave her carefully planned life for the cause of learning, for spreading the faith; that is why Abraham felt that it was unnecessary to eulogize her. He knew that such an individual could never die, for her life remained emblazoned on the hearts of those she inspired.

This is why she could have produced an Isaac who was prepared to die for his faith. That is why we study her life on this day of judgment. From her eternal resting places she cries out to you and to me. “Have you dealt honestly? Have you hoped for salvation?”

Is the altar which you have built adequate?
