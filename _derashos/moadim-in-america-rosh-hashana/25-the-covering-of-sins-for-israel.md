*“Blow the shofar at the new moon, at the hidden time for our feast day, for it is a statute for Israel, a judgment for the God of Jacob.”*

(Tehillim 81:4-5 - In the Maariv prayer of Rosh Hashanah)

*“Which is the festival in which the moon is covered? You must say this is Rosh Hashanah.”*

(Beitzah 16a)

The idea hidden here in this wonderful saying is according to what is explained in the commentators, regarding that which is brought in the Pesikta: (Tehillim 32:1) "*Happy is he whose transgression is forgiven, whose sin is covered.*" For He forgives our sins every year and year. As it is written: (Vayikra 16:34) "*And this shall be for you an eternal statute, to atone for the children of Israel, once in a year.*"

That is to say, once in a year the Holy One, Blessed be He, covers over our sins, and finds for us merit so that the mouth of the Satan and the accuser against us shall be closed. This is in a manner according to what is brought in the words of our Sages of blessed memory: (Rosh Hashanah 18a) "*On Rosh Hashanah all inhabitants of the world pass before Him like Bnei Maron. What is Bnei Maron? In Babylonia, they translated \[it\] like young sheep. Resh Lakish said: Like the ascents of Beit Maron. Rav Yehuda said in the name of Shmuel: Like the legions of the House of David. Rabbah bar bar Chana said in the name of Rabbi Yochanan: And all of them are scanned in a single scan. Rav Nachman bar Yitzchak said: We also learned it likewise: 'He who fashions their hearts together, who understands all their deeds.'*"

For seemingly it should be said, regarding that which Rabbah bar bar Chana said in the name of Rabbi Yochanan: "*And all of them are scanned in a single scan*"—behold, is it not a contradiction to the words of the Mishnah (ibid. 16a) that said: "*On Rosh Hashanah all inhabitants of the world pass before Him like Bnei Maron?*"

However, it can be said that the intention is, that if each and every one would be judged on Rosh Hashanah like *Bnei Maron*, meaning according to his individual deeds, who could stand in judgment and be vindicated before Him because of the accusers?

Therefore he said: "*And all of them are scanned in a single scan,*" meaning when the Holy One, Blessed be He, looks upon all Israel in their deeds in a single scan, and covers over the sins of each and every one . . . it is not in the ability of any accuser to accuse anymore, for truly, who is like Your people Israel, one nation in the land? And as a whole they are very good and upright. As it is said: (Shir HaShirim 4:7) "*You are all beautiful, my beloved, and there is no blemish in you.*" Is there then any more covering of sins than on this day? . . .

And this is what he said: "*Which is the festival in which the moon is covered? You must say this is Rosh Hashanah.*" Meaning, Rosh Hashanah is a festival on which day the Holy One, Blessed be He, covers Himself, in order not to look upon all the sins of each and every one . . . He only looks upon all Israel in their deeds in a single scan . . . then certainly the person has merit so that the Satan will not be able to accuse, and understand this.[^1]

In yet another manner the idea in this can be said, that which the moon is covered in it, and the moon is hidden from the eye of the seer. It is according to what is explained in our holy literature, behold the mercies of the Lord are very great even upon idolaters, to conduct Himself with them with compassion and with grace.

And behold our Sages of blessed memory said: (Berachos 7a) "*At the time when the sun shines, and all the kings of the East and the West place their crowns on their heads, and prostrate themselves to the sun—immediately the Holy One, Blessed be He, is angry.*"

Behold it is that on the day that the nations of the world sin, then the Holy One, Blessed be He, is angry, but at night when they are sleeping from transgressions, He is not angry. However, what shall He do for those who would also worship the moon? Behold it is that also at night the Holy One, Blessed be He, would be angry at them.

Therefore the new moon is covered in it, and the moon is hidden from the eye of every living thing, so that it shall not be in the possibility for worship; then the Holy One, Blessed be He, will not be angry at all, and also for the nations of the world "*His throne was prepared in kindness*" . . . and understand this.

According to what was stated above, the idea in this can be said in yet another manner. According to what is brought in the books, behold truly, how did the Holy Torah hide its day? That is to say, in all the festivals mentioned in the Torah it is written regarding the festival of Pesach: (Vayikra 23:6) "*And on the fifteenth day,*" etc. On Shavuos: (Bamidbar 28:26) "*And on the day of the first-fruits,*" etc. And on Sukkos: (Vayikra 23:34) "*on the fifteenth day,*" etc. But on Rosh Hashanah it was not said "*day*," but "*on the first of the month,*" etc. (Bamidbar 29:1); is it not a significant matter?

Rather the reason is, because of this it was not said on Rosh Hashanah "*day*," as it was in the rest of the festivals, because the nations of the world are judged at night and Israel in the day, so that the burning of anger shall not increase.

As it is brought in the Midrash: (Tehillim 9:9) "*And He shall judge the world in righteousness*"—at the time when the Holy One, Blessed be He, judges the nations, He judges them at night, at the time when they are sleeping from transgressions, and at the time when He judges Israel, He judges them at the time when they are occupied with Torah.

Which because of this Rosh Hashanah is two days, so that He shall judge Israel—on the first day and after it on the night of the second day for the nations of the world, in order that the judgment should not precede for the nations of the world and the burning of anger shall increase, as is known. (See also in Rosh Hashanah 8b).

And this is what it said: "*Blow the shofar at the new moon,*" and its time is on Rosh Hashanah, "*at the hidden time for our feast day,*" that is to say, on that same day that our feast day is hidden in the Torah, and "*day*" was not said regarding it. And the reason for the matter is, "*for it is a statute for Israel, a judgment for the God of Jacob*"—also for the nations.

As it is brought in the Gemara there: "*For it is a statute for Israel*"—I have only the rule for Israel; for the nations of the world from where is it derived? The verse states "*a judgment for the God of Jacob,*" for they are judged at night; because of this "*day*" was not said regarding it, and understand this.
