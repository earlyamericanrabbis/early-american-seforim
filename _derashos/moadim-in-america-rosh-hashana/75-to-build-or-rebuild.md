*The Rosh Hashonah sermon reprinted from the RCA Manual 5709-1948*

It is always a joyous experience for young people who start life together to of building their new home according to all the detailed specifications of their plans and ideas, their hopes and expectations. It is different, and yet in a sense even more inspiring when a person, who saw destruction and experienced the collapse of his dream home, resolutely sets forth to rebuild from the ruins and from the remnant of his once glorious home a new and possibly even better structure to house his loved ones. Similarly in the story of nations the tale of building countries and fashioning empires is always a fascinating one, but the ability of some few people to rise from the ashes of destruction and rebuild their life anew has been vouchsafed to but a few; among these chosen few we can surely count our people Israel.

That our greatest task for today is that of rebuilding our people’s destiny will be challenged by few. The means of this reconstruction are not so easily attained. Yet our sacred scriptures provide an account of events which for timeliness has hardly its equal. Once before when Israel was in exile, Jerusalem in blackest ruins, and our Torah hardly practiced there, a man, who was in fortunate circumstances…being in the high employment of the Babylonian kings, although one of the exiled Jews, chanced upon some men who came o the metropolis from the conquered province of Judah. This man, Nehemiah, inquired about the Jews and Jerusalem, and was told this tragic tale! “ And they said unto me: ‘The remnants that are left of the captivity there in the province are in great affliction and reproach; the wall of Jerusalem also is broken down, and the gates thereof are burned with fire.’” It was but natural that this God fearing man should break down in bitter weeping, rend his garments in mournful lamentation, and seek solace in fast and prayer. These alone temporarily assuaged the searing pain of his sensitive heart, but soon he determined that to fight the battle of the Lord he must go forth with unyielding determination and dedicate his finest efforts in an irresistible resolve to build where others had destroyed, and to aid where others has despoiled, and to consecrate where others had indulged in desecration.

Were I to skip formal preachings this morning and do nothing but read before you the thirteen brief chapters that constitute this book in the Scriptures, every one of you would clearly understand that the tasks before us, although differing in size and magnitude,are in no way dissimilar from those that confronted Nehemiah, and that our solutions of the same will be surest of success if we carefully and devotedly follow his design and pattern.

*Ours is not only to weep and cry*

*Ours is the task to build on high*

II

The problems with which this master builder in Israel had to grapple where three in number, of which the first was the need to reconstruct the gates of the city and walls of Jerusalem. Surely, in our times if we were to classify in importance the numerous responsibilities with which we are faced today, it would be agreed by all right thinking people that first amongst them comes the all-embracing and all-inclusive task of rebuilding the nascent state of Israel and strengthening the foundations of Zion. Now, when we mournfully consider the lost opportunities when so much of constructive nature could have been applied to the advancement and realization of our fondest dreams for the Holy Land, we begin to realize that no Jewish problem, however significant, can take precedence over the demands of a Jewish Palestine. Both because of the transcending importance of the Yishuv as a nucleus for a Jewish future as well as a potential source of permanent security for the homeless, I declare in the Holy hour before this assembled Holy congregation and standing in this sacred pulpit, that anyone who fails to do his utmost for the hallowed cause of Israel, no matter how devoted he may be to other Jewish causes, cannot in his heart feel that he discharges his responsibilities towards his people in these historic times.

Our generation indeed has a rendezvous with Jewish destiny. Ours is the privilege to witness the emergence of ancient Israel in a modern garb. We too, like those who returned from Babylon must be inspired and exhilarated with the prospect of transmuting the tragedy of yesterday into the glory of tomorrow and it depends largely on our devoted efforts whether those who sowed with tears, with blood, with sorrow, and with stupendous grief shall by the grace of the Everliving God reap with joy, with hope, and with song.

I need not dwell on the difficulties attendant upon this stupendous task of nation building. Enemies from within and from without are only too keen to thwart our efforts. It was no different then, when those who scaled the heights had a wicked enemy and a cunning adversary to contend with, so much so that we read “They that builded the wall and they that bore burdens loaded themselves, every one with one of his hands wrought in the work, and with the other held his weapons; and the builders, every one had his sword girded by his side, and so builded. And he that sounded the horn was by me.” We who are privileged to assist the builders of Zion are still compelled to battle a small but disturbing minority within our own ranks and uphold the hands of those inspired warriors who offer their life and limb for the protection of Israel. History indeed is repeating itself when the builders of Israel are once again surrounded by hostile forces and once again the hand that builds for the future must protect itself at the same time from the vicious forces of the darkened present.3

Many of us are only too smug in the feeling that our aid and assistance make possible the progress on the ancient soil, but before we go too far in arrogating unto ourselves a self-satisfied attitude of contentment we should value the achievements of today as against the possibilities that might have been had we given o the cause of Zion rebuilt, more of our devotion, more of our interest, and more of our loyalties in the recent past. Especially we in the synagogue who singlehandedly kept aglow the hope in a Jewish renascence throughout the ages should have been more responsive to the call of the ancient homeland and should at least now determine without fail to follow in the footsteps of pioneers who twenty-five centuries ago retraced their steps from alien Babylon to the banks of the Jordan

III

Another and equally impelling task that faced the rebuilder of Palestine was the fact that the people were in great poverty. Hunger stalked the earth, and the need of the stricken populace was unspeakable in its extent. This, too, is no strange phenomenon in our midst. We too, are called upon to extend a helping hand to tax our resources to the limit for those who are still in the land of their tormentors and are waiting with anguished eyes the help and assistance that will deliver them from their degrading status and restore them to dignity and honor amongst their fellow men. While it is true that under the challenge of cataclysmic events, American Jewry has done magnificently, yet, in line with the enormous needs and in view of the apparent and cold-blooded indifference of so-called civilized society, the extent of our aid must be broadened and expanded far beyond that of the present. It is equally true that the multiplicity of problems assailing us from every side provide an additional drain on the resources and strength of American Israel. Yet, the harrowing condition of our brethren, still permitted to rot in their former dungeons, dwarfs our local problems by comparison and parades in anguished intensity the overwhelming need of our Eurpoean wards.

Surely, we too are beset with numerous problems and assailed by the tensions of post war and “cold war” days. We should give serious consideration to our plans for the future, but even with our most pressing personal needs most dramatically displayed, we are still kings in comparison with those whose outstretched hands are in a sense visible in this hallowed synagogue. The personal sack-cloth that we may be wearing as a sign of our sympathy and feeling of kinship does not eliminate the undeniable fact that our sons and daughters are far better off than those whose record of martyrdom is unparalleled in history.

If the plans of Nehemiah included help to the afflicted, while rebuilding the walls of Jerusalem, we surely can do no less as we prepare in his spirit to build were others have destroyed and to help in a most unprecedented manner the campaign of destiny by the United Kewish Appeal.

IV

Parallel with the physical revival that the pilgrims from Babylon so successfully inaugurated went a spiritual awakening, the like of which is unequalled in the long and glorious history of the Jew. Even a cursory perusal of the eighth chapter of Nehemiah must fill one with a rare sense of exultation as we read that twenty-five hundred years ago, on this very day of Rosh Hashonah, Ezra the Scribe brought out the Book of Laws and read to the people the ancient rules that guaranteed their survival as a nation. That there exists a compelling need in our times for such an articulation of the Law of God, will surely be granted by all observers. Never perhaps was there such a large Jewish community so much in need of spiritual revival as American Israel finds itself today. One cannot point to a single area of our religious life where satisfactory conditions prevail. However, if we are to emulate the pattern so clearly established in the Biblical days to which we have made repeated allusion, we cannot possibly conceive of our task well done without a most spectacular effort to strengthen the life of American Jewry and to intensify its endeavors for a revival of Torah living.

It might be felt by some that in addition to the responsibility for European Jewry and the upbuilding of Palestine, it would be too much to expect the Jews of America to choose this moment for a religious rejuvenation. In truth, however, the Torah clearly established such a precedent. When Moses was charged with the immense task of freeing his brethren and liberating his people, he was reminded not to neglect his household and to complete all religious rituals and practice with his own immediate loved one before proceeding to the all-encompassing task of breaking the Egyptian bondage. We too, in our times, must similarly apply ourselves most diligently to cleanse our own household simultaneously with out other equally urgent obligations.

V

That the accomplishments of the aims set forth today call for a supreme effort and singular devotion is readily granted, but as in all times of historic import, we again turn to Israel’s noble women, tired and tested in a thousand trials, to help us achieve these important aims. Both Biblical and the prophetic selection of this hallowed day are centered around Great Women to indicate beyond doubt that national life and religious rejuvenation are only possible because of their devotion and inspired cooperation. The tasks outlines above can only be realized if they lend us their guidance as they so reliably did at every critical juncture of history. The noble daughters in Israel who have done so valiantly in the cause of Zion, have also in recent years taken on added and significant responsibilities in the numerous drives for overseas relief. It will be through them and only with their inspiring help that we can hope for a renaissance of religious observances here in America. The sanctification of our Sabbath must receive its impetus from the home, the education of our young ones is in the hands of our trusted helpmates, the maintenance of the hallowed standards of Kashruth as well as countless other manifestations of religious living can only come through the help and inspiration of our women.

Little wonder then that a Talmudic sage comments that when Ezra read from our ancient laws in that exalted moment, to impress the people anew with the import of religious observances, this act of reading the law took place in the special area of the Temple reserved for Israel’s women.

We are indeed asking much from American Israel when we appeal for the solution to our problems on a three fold scale. Truly we base this plea on the assured conviction that the tragic events of the recent past did not extinguish every ray of hope in heavily laden Jewish hearts. All of our dreams for a better world are predicated upon an unshatterable faith that speaks comforting words in the darkest nights of disaster. We cannot believe that the record of our people’s martyrdom inscribed in imperishable letters in crimson shall have been in vain, and that nothing can uproot or destroy the unconquerable faith that looks to an equitable solution to the problems of the day. It is true that all we can offer and our sole guarantee of better days to come issue from an implicit trust in the worthwhileness of our lives and the worthiness of our cause. Trust indeed, is what God asks of us and the interpretation of our Sages of a beautiful selection in the very book of Nehemiah clarifies this point. It is not too much to suggest that we who put our trust in princes and faith in the meagre source of human assurance, should now turn back, retrace our steps and place our reliance on Him who is the source of all blessings and fountainhead of all salvation. With their unerring instinct the sages of the Talmud gently guide us to this basic prerequisite which is the surest path to serene happiness.

Commenting upon the text, “*For the joy of the Lord is your strength,*” our Rabbis say: “*Thus speaketh the Holy One to Israel, My children borrow for Me and have faith in Me, sanctify the day and I shall make companion for you.*” God asks us to trust Him, to sanctify His day, His law, and His Name and have faith that those who believed in Him when the clouds overhead were heavy with despair will see and behold the glory that will rise upon us when the sun of redemption be our unconquerable strength.
