*"And we shall ascribe holiness to this day..." etc. "and angels shall hasten, and terror and trembling shall seize them, and they shall say: Behold, the Day of Judgment..." etc. "and all who enter the world shall pass before You like the sons of Maron."*

My masters and my teachers! The great market day occurs once a year to grant each and every person life, sustenance, and all his needs for the entire year. And it is understood on its own that they will not give all this for free; rather, with full money they purchase all this. And what is this money? Charity and good deeds. But to come to this great market day to purchase all good—life and sustenance, and this is not just ordinary sustenance, but a life of wealth and honor—and to come empty-handed like destitute paupers without a penny to a penny, without Torah, without purity, without charity, without good deeds, as we testify with our own mouths: "*What are we? What is our life? What is our righteousness?*" "*Not with kindness and not with deeds did we come before You; like poor and destitute ones we knocked on Your doors.*" And with this, shall the Congregation of Israel enter before the King with a request that everything she says should be granted to her—from the King's palace?

And in my book *Matok MiDevash*, part 1, I explained the matter with a beautiful parable—that it is a custom among the wealthy that in the dining room, an electric bell is found on the table, and when the time for eating arrives, they ring the bell and they hasten to bring them the needs of the meal and all the delicacies. One time, a poor man saw the action of the wealthy man and rejoiced exceedingly. He ran to his house and he too made such a bell, thinking that when he became hungry, he would ring the bell and the meal would be set before him. And when he desired to eat, he began to ring the bell without cessation. His wife, upon seeing this, rebuked him with a loud voice and said, "Are you crazy? The worries of life you do not have, and you only say to ring?"

Her husband answered her and said, "Do not imagine that I am performing acts of foolishness, for I saw in the house of the wealthy that when they desire to eat, they ring and the meal is set before them. And at this time, I am hungry for bread, so I made this bell and I will ring, and they will bring before me something to eat."

"You fool," his wife rebuked him, "neither the bell nor the ringing will serve the food before you. The wealthy prepared beforehand, by means of the servants, all the needs of eating there in the kitchen, and the ringing is only a signal to bring the delicacies to the table. But you are a poor and destitute man, did you understand anything beforehand, that you now ring to the maidservant that she should bring the food before you? Even if you ring all day and all night, it will not avail you, for you will remain hungry."

Yes, my masters, the requests and the voices, such as "*Remember us for good life,*" "*Write us in the book of sustenance and livelihood,*" "*The book of forgiveness and pardon*"—they are like the ringing of the bell to bring that which they prepared, to remind, as it were, the Holy One, Blessed be He, that He should give them on Rosh Hashanah from the readiness which they prepared all year through good deeds, and what they meditated upon in Torah, and what they gave charity to all the yeshivas, and what they did for the sake of hosting guests, visiting the sick, bringing in the bride, and the like. But one who did not prepare anything during the entire year, as he himself testifies, "*like poor and destitute ones we knocked on Your doors*"—and at the time a poor man comes to him and begs for mercy to help him, he answers him with disgrace and contempt, and says about him that he is a "schnorrer", why can he not do some matter to sustain his soul and not go around to the doorways? And the poor man, with his staff and his knapsack, goes from his house, and just as he came, so shall he go—how are you not ashamed now to request and say, "*Not with kindness and not with deeds did we come before You, like poor and destitute ones we knocked on Your doors.*"

And there is yet another trouble greater than this: aside from the fact that he did not come with kindness or with good deeds, but rather like a poor man at the doorway with empty hands, it would not have been so bad, for he did not come to request from a stranger, but from our Father in Heaven. But to our heartache, we have brought a lot of merchandise, but alas? The merchandise that is in our wardrobe, woe...? The merchandise is unkosher, contraband, which makes the face redden with shame: also all types of haughtiness and filth, public desecration of Shabbos, forbidden foods, the cancellation of family purity, our sons and our daughters without an education of Judaism, baseless hatred, evil speech, and it is better not to multiply words.

I request of you, let each one search within himself in the innermost chambers... there he will find everything I have thought of and much more than all that the mouth is able to speak and all that the ear is unable to hear. And the small amount of good that is with us is also mixed with dross. And when they take our small amount of good merchandise to flatter and to sacrifice it upon the supernal filter above, therefore it cannot be brought near to the Omnipresent in a mixture of filth and piggul-abomination, because of "*Offer it now to your governor*" (Malachi 1:8).

And may God protect us, or we can estimate by ourselves how much will remain there after there evaporates from it a small amount for the sake of beauty, a small portion of hypocrisy, a small amount of flattery, a small amount of cunning, and a specific measure of ulterior motives, all according to the place and according to the time. Since there is no righteous man on earth who does good and does not sin in the good itself, from all that we have thought of, there will remain a minute particle, a minute, tiny particle. And see regarding this at length in the essay *Daloti VeLi Yehoshua*.

And in this manner, the commentators explained the verse in Job (1:6): "*And it was the day, and the sons of God came to stand before Hashem, and the Satan also came in their midst.*" That day, according to the words of our Sages, of blessed memory, was Rosh Hashanah. The "*sons of God*", they are the mitzvos, for as is known, from every mitzvah an angel is created. But together with those "*sons of God*" who were created from the mitzvos, the Satan also came in their midst, which is the blemish in the mitzvos, such as the mixture of a speck of pride or some other ulterior motive.

And regarding this, I explained in another place the expression in the words of the liturgical poet: "*In the absence of a defender against the teller of transgression...*" For the defender who was created from the mitzvah is a weak angel, lacking limbs, because at the time of giving the charity or another mitzvah, he gives without a heart; if so, the angel created from this mitzvah also lacks a heart or has a weak heart, who needs to travel (to Denver, Colorado). But the evil angel who was created from the sin, which he performs with a burning flame of passion, is created as a strong angel. And this is "*In the absence of a defender against the teller of transgression,*" for there is no strength in the defender to stand against the angel who tells of transgression...

And according to this idea, the preachers explained: "*In the place where a master of repentance stands, completely righteous men are unable to stand*" (Berachos 34b). For at the time he performs repentance out of love, all the transgressions become merits; that is to say, the transgressions that he performed intentionally and with the intent of the heart will be inverted into mitzvos, and automatically they will become mitzvos performed with great perfection without any blemish, just like the transgressions from which the mitzvos were made. And a completely righteous man is unable to stand there, because in the mitzvos of the righteous man, one can always find some speck of honor or of pride or of another ulterior motive. And with this, it is well understood in "*And we shall ascribe holiness...*": "*and angels shall hasten, and terror and trembling shall seize them,*" meaning, the angels that were created from the mitzvos shall hasten in panic because they will not be vindicated before the throne of judgment, since the mitzvos were not performed with perfection, for there is no righteous man who does good and does not sin in the good itself.

But despite all this, my masters and my teachers! Do not fear anything and let your heart not fall, for we have brought with us a precious offering at the time to appease our Father in Heaven. And just as at the time the Holy Temple was standing, the Kohanim offered sacrifices, meal-offerings, and libations, and through this they atoned for all the sins of the Jewish nation, so my dear brothers, each one of us has brought with him a sacrifice, a meal-offering, and libations to offer here in the minor sanctuary on this holy and mighty day. And if you ask: Where are our sacrifices and where are our Kohanim?

My masters! Each and every one of us is the *Kohen Gadol* of his entire family. "*The sacrifices of God are a broken spirit*" (Tehillim 51:19), and the libations are the tears. And it is upon each and every one at the time to offer his heart to Heaven, to pray with all his heart, and also to give libations, which are the tears. And upon this, the sweet singer of Israel testifies: "*For He has not despised nor abhorred the lowliness of the poor, nor has He hidden His face from him*" (Tehillim 22:25).

And also the Prophet Yeshayahu said so: *"…but to this one will I look, to the poor and broken-spirited man"* (Yeshayahu 66:2).

Therefore, my masters and my teachers! You came today on Rosh Hashanah to the minor sanctuary to receive from Hashem, may He be blessed, a good inscription and sealing for yourselves and for all your families—all this—depends this time in your own hand, through the service of oneself and the desire of his heart. For let us assume even one whose actions of the entire past year caused him, Heaven forbid, the final word "*liable*". As is the custom of the world, one who sinned a sin of capital punishment and was caught in his iniquity, does it avail him if he weeps before the judge and pours out tears until he melts in his tears, and supplicates before the judge to the truth that he regrets with complete regret and will not return again to foolishness? Will all his claims, all his ink, and all his tears avail him? But on this day, before the Supreme Judge, it will avail and it will avail! For the sake of this, act and offer your heart, which is a broken heart, and also give this—tears. And we are certain that Hashem, may He be blessed, will hear our prayers and our salvation, and will write us immediately for a good life, Amen.
