*"In the Gemara Rosh Hashanah (16b): Three books are opened on Rosh Hashanah; one of absolute tzaddikim, one of absolute reshaim, and one of beinonim. Absolute tzaddikim are written and sealed immediately for life; absolute reshaim are written and sealed immediately for death; beinonim remain suspended and standing until Yom Kippur. If they merit, they are written for life; if they do not merit, they are written for death."*

One should note here several questions: (a) Why were the days of teshuvah established at the beginning of the new year? For they are days of teshuvah for the avonos that they sinned during the past year. According to the perspective of intellect, it would have been appropriate to establish them at the end of the past year, so that the year would conclude with teshuvah for the avonos that they sinned therein. And what connection is there for the days of teshuvah at the beginning of the new year? (b) What hint of moral instruction does the name "Rosh Hashanah" hint to us? Just as the names of all the festivals hint—meaning Shabbos, Yom Kippur, Pesach, Shavuos, Sukkos—for the names themselves hint to a moral instruction, each one according to its matter, as will be explained in our words, each one in its place. But what hint does the name Rosh Hashanah hint, which indicates only that it is the beginning of the year? (c) For what reason did the Sages call this day Rosh Hashanah, while the Torah called this day by the name "*Yom Teruah*" (Bamidbar 29)? (d) What matter is there in that which the beinonim remain suspended and standing until Yom Kippur? If they did not merit through their deeds which they performed in the past year to be written for life, for what reason and line of justice should the Heavenly Court wait for them until Yom Kippur?

I have not seen any of the holy authors who noted these questions. And it appears correct in my humble opinion to resolve these questions with good reason and logic.

Behold, the Gemara (Bava Batra 78a) comes to teach the good and upright path in which a person should walk all the days of his breath of life, and it said: What is the meaning of that which is written, "*Therefore the rulers say, 'Come to Cheshbon'*" (Bamidbar 21)? This means: Those who rule over their evil inclination say, "Come and let us calculate the accounting of the world." The explanation is that a person is forced to calculate the accounting of the soul—eternal life—just as he calculates all the days of his life the accountings of businesses of the life of this world—transient life. And regarding the accountings of transient life, it is not repulsive to him to calculate them time after time, because he fears lest he err in his calculation and come to a loss; so too, he is forced to calculate with an exact calculation the accounting of the life of the soul, eternal life. Just as they said (in Avos Chapter 2): "*And calculate the loss of a mitzvah against its reward.*"

And the matter is that there are four times for teshuvah. The first time, which is the most preferred for teshuvah, is immediately on that very day when he committed the transgression. And the second time for teshuvah is every Erev Shabbos. And the third time for teshuvah is every Erev Rosh Chodesh. And the fourth time for teshuvah is during the Ten Days of Teshuvah.

And the truth is that the most preferred time for teshuvah is immediately on the day when he committed the transgression. This is comparable to if a stain fell upon a garment, it is much better to wash it immediately and clean it; for if several days pass over it, the stain becomes deeply embedded and impressed into the garment, and then it will be difficult to clean the garment from the stain.

And after the teshuvah of the day, there comes as a lower level than it: the teshuvah of the week on every Erev Shabbos. And after the teshuvah of the week, there comes as a lower level than it: the teshuvah of the month, meaning on every Erev Rosh Chodesh. And after the teshuvah of the month, there comes as a lower level than it: the teshuvah of the year, meaning during the days of teshuvah.

And in truth, the matter of teshuvah is very easy, as the prophet Hoshea teaches us: "*Return, O Yisrael, unto Hashem your God, for you have stumbled in your iniquity. Take words with you, and return to Hashem*" (Hoshea 14). The explanation is: You, Yisrael, return to Hashem, because everything that you sin is inadvertent and a stumbling, just as a person stumbles on the path of his walking. And lest you say that teshuvah is in any case difficult—upon this he said, "*Take words with you and return to Hashem*"—I request from you only words, meaning regret over the past and acceptance for the future.

And this refers to the tzaddikim, who are swift and zealous to make the accounting of the soul, and they fulfill in themselves every day the statement of Chazal in Avos: "*And calculate the loss of a mitzvah against its reward.*" And they are masters of wondrous accountings with an exact calculation, and they count all the days of their lives, each and every day as a number on its own, just as one counts the days of the Sefirah! And when they complete seven days, then they count this as one week; and when they count four weeks, they count this as one month; and when they count twelve months, then they count this as one complete year.

And they are like the poor people[^1] who go and circulate after donations to gather perutah coins. Behold, the wise poor person will minimize the perutah coins and guard them to make from them an enduring principal: he exchanges the perutah coins for a silver coin, and afterwards he exchanges the small silver coins for a full silver shekel, and afterwards when he has several silver shekels, he exchanges them for a gold coin, and behold, now there is in his hand a gold coin as an enduring principal. But the foolish poor person, when he receives a few perutah coins, will not guard them to make from them an enduring principal, but rather he expends them immediately.

And the tzaddikim are exactly comparable to the wise poor people, who make from the few perutah coins gathered every day a great enduring principal; for perutah to perutah combines into a great accounting. So too the tzaddikim, from the individual days, they make a great enduring principal of many years, like the statement: "*And Avraham was old, coming along in days*" (Bereishis 24). The explanation is: At the time of his old age, he brought with him all the days that he lived upon the face of the earth, for not even one day was missing to him wherein he did not perfect his soul.

But the lazy people, who do not want to trouble themselves to calculate the accounting of the world, and they destroy and lose the time of their lives in the vanities of the world, are exactly comparable to the foolish poor people, who do not want to economize a few perutah coins, and they expend every day all the perutah coins they gathered, and due to this they will not have an enduring principal, even a small matter.

Therefore, Chazal called the name of this day Rosh Hashanah. And the intention is that from the beginning of the year, a person is forced to count each day on its own, just as one counts the days of the Sefirah, and to make from them weeks, and afterwards from the weeks months, and afterwards from the months a year. And at the end of the year, he will have an enduring principal of one complete year in the service of Hashem and in precious character traits.

And now the name Rosh Hashanah is well understood. For the matter of teshuvah is regret over the past and acceptance for the future, that he shall sin no more. And behold, regret over the past is an easy thing, for all the reshaim are filled all the days of their lives with regrets; but the main thing is to fulfill the condition of acceptance for the future. And therefore, the name Rosh Hashanah hints to us a moral instruction: meaning, from the beginning of the year, accept upon yourself with absolute determination to walk in the good path, and then when the year concludes, you will have an enduring principal of one pure and clear year.

And now there is also resolved what the Torah established the days of teshuvah at the beginning of the year; for the main thing is the acceptance for the future and not the regret over the past, as we have said. And the acceptance for the future is from the beginning of the new year, after the regret over the avonos that they sinned in the past year.

And also there is resolved what Chazal called this day by the name Rosh Hashanah, whereas the Torah called this day "*Yom Teruah*"; for they have one and the same idea. For the shofar will remind a person that today is the beginning of the new year, therefore you are forced to accept upon yourself with absolute determination not to lose the days that are coming toward you in the vanities of time, and you are forced to begin to count the individual days to make from them an enduring principal of one complete year, pure and clear.

And now it is well understood, and so too the passage which we placed at the opening of the gateway of the essay: for the three books themselves testify to the desire and the yearning of the people who are inscribed in them. For the book of the tzaddikim testifies about him that he is one who desires life, and the proof is that the tzaddik brought with him the past year—complete, pure, and clear. And even if the past year has already passed and is no more, yet in the boundary of the tzaddik, it is alive also now, because he did not destroy it or lose it in the vanities of time. And due to this, because he is one who desires life, therefore he is written immediately for life.

But the reshaim who destroyed and lost the past year in the vanities of time, they themselves testify that they desire the death of time, and the proof is that they themselves put to death the past year in the bonds of time, and it has already passed and departed from the world; therefore they are written immediately for death, because they themselves desire death.

But the beinonim, who—even if they did not put to death the past year, because they did not destroy and lose it—yet they also did not enliven it, and they did not bring the past year in life, pure and clear; therefore they remain suspended and standing until Yom Kippur. The intention is: if they will accept the acceptance for the future with absolute determination. For regret over the deed of the past year alone is not enough, since all the reshaim are filled with regrets; rather, the main thing is the acceptance for the future—if it is truly with absolute determination. Therefore they remain suspended until Yom Kippur, for then it will be recognizable if they accepted the acceptance for the future with absolute determination or not. For the deeds of the days of teshuvah, they testify whether the acceptance is with absolute determination or not. Therefore, if they merit, they are written for life; if they do not merit, they are written for death.

And everything, with the help of God, is resolved with good reason and logic.

[^1]: Man in his few days is compared to a poor person in the phrase "I am poor and expiring from youth" (Tehillim 88), as is explained in our words in the essay "Hakamath Matzevah."
