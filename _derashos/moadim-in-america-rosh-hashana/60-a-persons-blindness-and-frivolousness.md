*"And Moshe heard. What rumor did he hear? Rabbi Shmuel bar Nachmani said in the name of Rabbi Yonasan: He heard that they suspected him of violating the prohibition of a married woman, as it is said, 'And they envied Moshe in the camp,' which teaches that each and every individual was envious of his wife from Moshe, as it is said, 'And Moshe took the tent, etc.'"*

(Sanhedrin 110a).

*"Rava expounded—and some say it was Rabbi Yitzchak who said: What is the meaning of that which is written, 'The sun and the moon stood still in their habitation'? What were the sun and the moon seeking in the heaven, etc.? This teaches that the sun and the moon ascended from the firmament to the Zvul, and they said before Him: 'Master of the Universe, if You execute justice for the son of Amram, we will shine, and if not, we will not shine.' At that moment, He shot arrows and spears at them. He said to them: 'Every single day people bow down to you, and you shine for My glory, yet you did not protest; but for the glory of flesh and blood, you protest?!', etc."*

(Nedarim 38b).

One of the unique phenomena of its kind in the life of Yisrael in the wilderness is certainly the business of the dispute of Korach with Moshe, whose consequences were so bitter and sad. And the most wondrous thing in this matter is the despicable slander regarding the moral character of Moishe Rabbeinu.

Indeed, we can understand the various complaints that the Children of Yisrael complained against Moishe Rabbeinu many times—even though it is certainly a great sin not to place absolute trust in the most wondrous man of all, Moishe Rabbeinu—but nevertheless, those complaints were about possible matters. That is to say: the Children of Yisrael were thirsty—and they raised a voice of crying out; they longed for meat—and they complained; the emissaries of the nation, who were sent also with the consent of Moshe, returned from spying out the land of Canaan and brought forth an evil report about it, describing it in black colors and its inhabitants as giants, mighty men, and warriors—the nation feared and wept. It is understood that it was not fitting for the Children of Yisrael in that generation, who saw all the mighty hand and all the great terror which Hashem wrought before their eyes at every step and turn—it was not appropriate for them to be of little faith and constantly fearful. But all these were possible things, and we can understand them. However, to be so wild and lawless as to suspect Moishe Rabbeinu, the choice of humanity, in a sin so terrible and awesome as this, is an impossible thing, and the intellect of man cannot reconcile it.

If at least those who suspected were only a small part of the nation, members of a specific faction, then the matter would be somewhat understandable. But according to the words of Chazal in their clear statement: "*This teaches that each and every individual was envious of his his wife from Moshe,*" the matter is strange and incomprehensible.

Also another exceptional event we see in this dispute, in which the sun and the moon also took part (even if in an allegorical sense). The two great luminaries departed this time from their neutrality, which they guarded even at a time when danger of death hovered over the heads of Moshe and Aaron; they too took part in the dispute, by standing on the side of Moshe and coming before the Holy One, Blessed be He, to complain about the injustice that was done to Moishe Rabbeinu.

One of the greatest shortcomings that exists in man is his frivolity; a shortcoming that brings on its account always bad consequences. And what is most saddening is that this shortcoming causes such evils that were not at all in the intention of anyone to bring about. The evils come on their own without the intention of a designer; frivolity leads to this.

Most felt is this shortcoming in the domain of "speech." There is nothing easier for a man to waste more than "speech." That precious human talent with which he was graced, and by virtue of which he was crowned with the title "the crown of creation," the talent of speech is minor in his eyes and he wastes it in vain. We do not intend here one who utilizes the talent of his speech in order to do evil to his fellow with prior intent, for that is a double-edged sword to do evil without limit. We intend here only speech that comes out of frivolity itself, without prior thought to do evil and to harm anyone whosoever, and even in such speech, there is the power and the strength to destroy worlds and to bring disaster upon the greatest and holiest people.

Such an example we find in the incident of the sons of Eli, Chofni and Pinchas, about whom Chazal testified that: "*Anyone who says Chofni and Pinchas sinned is nothing but mistaken, etc. And how do I fulfill the verse, 'that they lay with the women'? Rather, out of that they delayed their bird-nests, so that they did not go to their husbands, the Scripture accounts it to them as if they lay with them*" (Shabbos 55b).

And ostensibly, how is it possible that the Scripture would write a matter which in truth was not in reality at all? And if there was found a fault in the order and the discipline of the offering of sacrifices, and that by means of this the women were held back at the entrance of the Tent of Meeting for a long time waiting for their turn to sacrifice their offerings—but in any case, it would not ascend upon our minds to compare this minor fault of lack of order to the great sin that was written about them in the Scripture: "*that they lay with the women*"?

However, the absolute truth is that both the words of the Scripture which accuse as mentioned above are sincere and upright, and also the words of Chazal who testify that Chofni and Pinchas were clean of any sin. In this is reflected in a bright manner the frivolity in man regarding his speech. And this frivolity, it and it alone, was the primary cause for the accusation that the verse accuses them of.

If in general it is a minor thing in the eyes of a man to waste with his words, this matter is even more minor in the eyes of women: "*Ten kabs of speech descended to the world, and nine of them were taken by women.*" This human weakness is expressed with even greater intensity and greater strength when a person sits idle, without any work to occupy himself with. And based on this assumption, picture in your souls the following: in the city of Shiloh, the place of the Tent of Meeting, the designated place for all Yisrael for the offering of sacrifices, there was gathered a great multitude of the women of the Land of Yisrael for the offerings of their sacrifices. The work of the offering proceeds slowly—whether because a structured system was lacking in the work, or other reasons like showing favoritism and the like—and with no choice, the women have to sit there and wait weeks and months until their turn arrives to sacrifice. During the entire time of their stay there, they go about idle without any work, and who can estimate all the conversations without end or purpose which the women conversed, sitting idle and bored by the entrance of the Tent of Meeting?

It is understood that the conversation revolved around the personalities of Chofni and Pinchas, the chief administrators in the order of the work of the sacrifices. All their pain and grief of heart they expressed in their conversations about them, stretching harsh criticism over their conduct and the order of their lives.

And now picture in your soul: a congregation of women waiting, standing together and conversing, the content of the conversation is approximately like this: "How much time have you been waiting here?"—asks one woman the second—"Almost three weeks," answers the second, "and I do not see yet an end to my waiting here." "I have been staying here for more than a month of days," complains the third, "and I do not know yet when I will be able to return home." "What shall I tell you, my dear friends," relates a fourth, "for everything a person needs mazel and success; just now I saw that one wealthy woman, who not long ago arrived, and apparently they will already offer her sacrifice on the morrow." And another woman answers to this: "Certainly for everything a person needs mazel! You should have seen the radiant and laughing faces that Chofni and Pinchas showed her at the time of their speaking with her with pleasantness and gentleness!" And another woman answers opposite her: "A short time ago I saw that one woman of the daughters of the wealthy came to the Kohanim and they received her face with open joy, and spoke with her a few hours in a private room, and if it interests you to know who was that one who conversed with her with such great friendship in the private room? Come and I will reveal to you: it was Pinchas himself!" And another woman notes, with a smile that can be interpreted both ways: "*Indeed so, there are no angels upon earth...*" And in conclusion, the women look one upon the face of her companion, winking an eye to say: there is substance to the matter...

And what were the consequences of such idle conversations? The consequence was: "*that they lay with the women, etc., who assembled at the entrance of the Tent of Meeting.*" These are the consequences of idle conversations spoken in frivolity among those who go about idle. Each one adds a little by the power of his empty and idle imagination, without intention to do evil to anyone whosoever, and from all these "additions" there accumulates material to tell a story whose power is to bring disaster upon the one spoken about. And all this came "*out of that they delayed their bird-nests,*" and there remained for the women a long time for idleness wherein, having nothing to do, they spoke with frivolity what they spoke.

And now it is understood that the truth is with both of them, with the words of the Scripture and the statement of Chazal. The sin of Chofni and Pinchas was only "that they delayed their bird-nests," but this sin caused a greater sin—to hold back the women in Shiloh and to cause them to waste their time in idleness and to increase idle conversation whose consequence would be "*that they lay with the women,*" etc.

This trait of frivolity, especially in the domain of speech, was the primary cause of the great sin that Yisrael sinned in their stamping such a despicable stain upon the personality of Moishe Rabbeinu, which is expressed in the words "*And they envied Moshe in the camp.*" Only a lack of character traits like this could bring to such a terrible suspicion upon the Holy of Holies.

Such an incautious step, to speak superfluous words without any intention to do evil, was performed first by a woman—and even if a great and highly elevated woman—for ultimately a great woman is also a woman, and it is the nature of women to increase in words. And in this step of hers, she brought evil also upon herself. And this woman was our acquaintance Miriam the Prophetess. And in her sitting once with her brother Aaron and conversing with him in a pleasant and friendly manner, as is the way of a sister with a brother, she spoke also about her brother Moshe. Behold, Moshe is a brother to her and she has the permission to speak regarding him. "*And Miriam and Aaron spoke against Moshe because of the Cushite woman whom he had married, etc. Hath Hashem indeed spoken only with Moshe? Hath He not spoken also with us?*" etc.

At first glance, the words appear as a simple conversation between a brother and sister regarding their brother without any intent to do evil, and we do not see any fault in these words of theirs, even in the speeches that Miriam expressed against Moshe in her emphasizing "*for also with us did Hashem speak.*" And nevertheless, we behave in the manner of human beings regarding derech eretz. In these words of hers, she complained about Moshe for separating from his wife Tzipporah. And ostensibly there is no fault in them, but these innocent words that went out from the mouth of a woman, who brought them out from that frivolity of hers, fell in the camp of Yisrael like thunder on a clear day, and brought about a revolution in many minds. And the practical outcome of this was the statement of the verse: "*And they envied Moshe in the camp,*" which teaches that every individual was envious for his wife from Moishe Rabbeinu.

And like this was the order of the chain of events:

"*And the anger of Hashem was kindled against Miriam*" for her harsh complaint against Moshe, and she was punished with a bitter punishment: "*and behold, Miriam was leprous, white as snow.*" Miriam, the elevated woman, the first in her nation, was punished with a punishment so disgusting and despicable as leprosy, which brings on its account also distancing and separation outside the camp of Yisrael. And this incident happened at the time when all Yisrael were prepared and ready to set their paces to the way, to travel further to their destination, and she needs to be shut up lonely to her soul outside the camp.

Certainly this information cast a commotion in the camp of Yisrael. And upon her being sent outside the camp, all Yisrael ceased from the work of their preparations and looked after her until her disappearance from their eyes. And it is understood that their conversation all seven days of the confinement was only regarding the matter of this incident.

And we can picture to ourselves that these few words that Miriam slipped from her mouth—"*Hath Hashem indeed spoken only with Moshe? Hath He not spoken also with us?*"—whose intention is that the prophet too is in the category of man, and also the man whom Hashem speaks with him will not lose his natural feelings, expanded and grew in their quantity and quality, in their passing from mouth to mouth in a camp of three million souls, men and women sitting idle without work and waiting to go out to their path. They chewed these few words, deepened their focus in them for seven days, and each and every one added a little from his imagination, until these words received a completely different form and content: the sister of Moshe, who herself is a prophetess, testifies with her own mouth that the prophet is like one of mankind for all his feelings and physical needs—and if Moshe separated himself from his wife Tzipporah for a long time... and the consequence was: "*And they envied Moshe in the camp.*" And from our aforementioned perspective, this matter is natural and understood. For even greater evils could have grown from this, that a nation so numerous sits idle and its mind is frivolous. And idleness brings to boredom.

Yet despite all the slander, lashon hara, and complaint, they had to be blind not to see the great light that radiated from the face of Moishe Rabbeinu. Only those groping in the darkness could not see the immense difference that is between Moishe Rabbeinu and another man, even if this "other" is also a prophet.

And with justice Chazal described to us in a very beautiful allegorical way the weight of this sin of casting suspicion upon Moishe Rabbeinu—where the first ones who began in this were Korach and the members of his congregation—until even the sun and the moon could not bear the humiliation of Moshe and their patience snapped, saying to the Master of the Universe: "*If You do not execute justice for the son of Amram, we will not shine.*" That is to say: if human beings are so blind as not to see the truth of Moishe Rabbeinu; if human beings are found in such darkness as not to feel the difference between Moishe Rabbeinu and another man; if their eyes are smeared over from seeing the light that shines from the personality of Moishe Rabbeinu—why do they need then the sun and the moon in the world?! Why do they need the great luminaries, if in any case they are smitten with blindness? Why do they need the bright light that shines if fog is their swaddling band?!

Upon this He replied to them, as it were: "*Every single day people bow down to you and you shine, for My glory you did not protest, but for the glory of flesh and blood you protest?!*" A logical, deep answer the Holy One, Blessed be He, replied to them in this—even if also in an allegorical manner—He said to them, as it were: Is it only just now that you saw that human beings are blind? Is it only just now revealed before you that human beings wander in the depths of darkness? Every single day man makes for himself new gods, generation after generation creates for himself new idols. And all this, behold, is because human beings are smitten with blindness and do not see the infinite light of the Godhead. And you, luminaries of the Upper One, upon all this that is done daily you did not protest, and only now when human beings could not see the light of Moshe, you recognized that man dwells in darkness!!!

And true and established are these words and they point to the target. The wandering of human beings in the fog is the source of all the evil that happens to them. The eyes of all the nations of the world are smeared over from seeing the eternal worldly light of the Godhead; blind are all the nations of the world from seeing the truth and the uprightness which needed to be the rulers in the world; dark are their paths and they will not see and will not feel the great and terrible injustice done to the nation of Yisrael over the course of thousands of years. And smitten with blindness they are also from seeing to amend their ways for their own benefit, until there will arrive the true Illuminator, King Messiah, who will open their eyes to see all the truths and the correct things, and only then for all the children of man there will be light in their dwellings.
