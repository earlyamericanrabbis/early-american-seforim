*“Rabbi Kruspedai said in the name of Rabbi Yochanan: Three books are opened on Rosh Hashanah: one of the completely wicked, and one of the completely righteous, and one of the intermediate. The completely righteous are written and are sealed immediately for life; the completely wicked are written and are sealed immediately for death; the intermediate are suspended and standing from Rosh Hashanah until Yom Kippur; if they merited, they are written for life; if they did not merit, they are written for death, etc.”*

(Rosh Hashanah 16b)

Many find difficulty in this Aggadic saying: seemingly, why is a special book necessary for the intermediate? Behold, is it not possible that when they merited, they shall be written in the book of the righteous, and if they did not merit, they shall be written in the book of the wicked? If so, why yet a third book[^1]?

However, it can be said the idea in this is according to what is explained in our holy literature, that the matter does not deal here with books upon which the deeds of men and their actions have not yet been written. Rather, they are the books from each and every man according to his deed, that which is upon him to give an account every night by the signature of his hand, regarding his deeds and his works, day by day, as is known.

That is to say, the righteous for their pleasant deeds; and the wicked for their ugly deeds; and the intermediate for their deeds, neither pleasant nor ugly, so that in general, by means of this, a special book is necessary for them.

For in the book of the righteous, it is impossible to give their account, for there only pleasant and beautiful deeds are written; if so, when their deeds were also written there, behold does it not appear as if they are like the righteous in their own eyes. And in truth, are they then righteous?

And so too in the book of the wicked, it is impossible to give their account, for there only evil and ugly deeds are written; if so, when their deeds were also written there, behold they appeared as if they are like the wicked in their own eyes. When in truth, are they then wicked? Therefore a third book is necessary!

And this is what he said: *Three books are opened on Rosh Hashanah, etc.* That is to say, these books, upon which the deeds of men and their actions have already been written, each and every one according to his excellence and his level, namely completely wicked, completely righteous, and intermediate. And it is not necessary except to record their recompense there.

Meaning, completely righteous \[are\] written and sealed—in those same books—immediately for life. Completely wicked \[are\] written and sealed—likewise in such a manner—immediately for death.

Intermediate are suspended and standing from Rosh Hashanah until Yom Kippur; if they merited, they are written—in those same books—for life. If they did not merit, they are written—likewise in such a manner—for death.

And now it also comes out well, for what reason when they merited, why should they not be written in the book of the righteous? And so too when they do not merit, why should they not be written in the book of the wicked? Rather, since they also have a special book for their deeds, why should their recompense be written in the book of others? And understand this.

According to what was stated above, the idea in this can be said in yet another manner: why is a special book necessary for the intermediate? And also for what reason regarding the righteous and the wicked did Rabbi Yochanan say "*written and sealed,*" while regarding the intermediate on Yom Kippur he said "*written*" alone,

And he did not mention regarding them sealing at all? It is according to what is explained in the commentators[^2], that therefore intermediate ones are suspended and standing specifically until Yom Kippur, for then He inclines toward kindness, whereas on Rosh Hashanah, which is the Day of Judgment, He does not incline toward kindness.

As it is brought in the words of our Sages of blessed memory[^3]: "*Ilfa raised a contradiction: it is written*[^4]*: 'and abundant in kindness,' and it is written: 'and truth'? At the beginning 'and truth,' and at the end 'and abundant in kindness.*'" Meaning, on Rosh Hashanah, which is the Day of Judgment, as is known, then He conducts Himself with the attribute of truth, and does not incline toward kindness; but at the end, on Yom Kippur, which is a day of forgiveness and favor to the Lord, then He inclines toward kindness[^5].

Therefore, those who remain intermediate on Yom Kippur, and is written in the book for life according to the attribute of "*and abundant in kindness,*" he is written in a special book for himself, and not with the righteous who merited life according to the attribute of truth, and for this reason a third book is necessary.

Therefore it comes out well, why he did not mention, regarding them, sealing at all, because according to the instruction of our Sages of blessed memory[^6]: "*The seal of the Holy One, Blessed be He, is truth.*" And since they could not merit according to this attribute, how shall they be sealed?

Whereas the righteous, who merited to be written for life also according to the attribute of truth, therefore it is written regarding them "*written and sealed,*" etc., and understand this.

And again, in another manner there is to note here, according to what the commentators[^7] find difficult regarding that which the liturgical poet said in the *Musaf Shemoneh Esreh* prayer: "*On Rosh Hashanah they shall be written and on the Day of the Fast of Kippur they shall be sealed*"—according to whom does he follow? For according to the aforementioned Gemara, behold the righteous and the wicked are written and sealed on Rosh Hashanah, and the intermediate are suspended and standing until Yom Kippur and on it are written for life, etc. And how did he say that the writing is on Rosh Hashanah and the sealing is on Yom Kippur? And if the liturgical poet holds like the one who says that all are judged on Rosh Hashanah and the verdict on Yom Kippur, that is to say the sealing, if so, why do we say all the ten days of repentance until *Neilah* "*Write us,*" it should have been for us to say from Rosh Hashanah and onward "*And seal us*"?

Rather, it can be said the idea in this is according to what is explained in the commentators, that sometimes the letter *Bet* is used in place of *Mem*, as it is written: (Vayikra 8:32) "*And that which remains of the flesh and of the bread you shall burn in fire.*" It is as if it were written: "*from the flesh and from the bread,*" etc. And if so, we see the intention in "*On Rosh Hashanah they shall be written,*" meaning from Rosh Hashanah they shall be written until Yom Kippur, and then they shall be sealed at its end at the time of the closing of the gate; and since the intermediate have a time for the beginning of judgment which is called here "*writing*" from Rosh Hashanah until Yom Kippur at its end, therefore we pray all these days for "*writing*."

However, according to what appears, also regarding the righteous it is relevant to pray "*Write us*" all the ten days of repentance, for behold Maimonides ruled in Chapter 3 of the Laws of Repentance, that the country and all the world in its entirety are judged according to the majority. And if so, granted that for individuals the judgment is finished on Rosh Hashanah, nevertheless if the country and all the world is intermediate, the individual too is suspended and standing until Yom Kippur, since man is a social being, and his life and his sustenance and his needs are all mixed with others . . .

And according to this, it can be said that the liturgical poet follows the method of the aforementioned Gemara, and the matter refers even to the righteous, and that is what he said: "*On Rosh Hashanah they shall be written,*" meaning even those who are written on Rosh Hashanah, namely the righteous, even so on Yom Kippur they shall be sealed in the completion of their judgment in several matters that depend on the country and on all the world . . . and understand this.
