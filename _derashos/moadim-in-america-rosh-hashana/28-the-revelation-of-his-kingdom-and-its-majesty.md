*"Our God and God of our fathers, reign over all the entire world in Your glory."*

(In the Shemoneh Esreh prayer of Rosh Hashanah)

It is brought in the *Levush* and in the *Minhagim* that one should not say "*all*", for behold, afterward he says "*all of it*", see there. And in truth, in *Seder Rav Amram* the word "*all*" is not found. However, in Maimonides and in Abudarham and in *Mahzor Vitry* and in the *Tur*, both are found: "*all*" and also "*all of it,*" see there. And see in *Turei Zahav*, because we find that the word "*all*" does not imply "*all of it*", as in Gemara Yoma 74a, where we include from "*any*" even a part, even a minimal amount. And so too in Gemara Sanhedrin 78, the dispute of Rabbi Yehuda and the Rabbis: "*any soul*", one says until there is an entire soul, and Rabbi Yitzchak ben Bezalel thinks "*any soul,*" even a minimal amount.

And it can be said: if so, why is the word "*all of it*" not also said below? Like "*and every fashioned thing all of it shall know that You fashioned it,*" and "*every creature all of it shall understand that You created it.*" For here too it could be understood so, that not all fashioned things shall know that He fashioned them, and not all creatures shall understand that He created them.

However, it can be said the idea in this is according to what is explained in the commentators, regarding that which our Sages of blessed memory said: (Bava Batra 8a) "*Rabbi Yehuda HaNasi opened storehouses in years of famine and said: Let masters of Scripture enter, etc. Jonathan ben Amram pushed in and entered. He said to him: Rabbi, sustain me! He said to him: My son, have you read Scripture? He said to him: No. Have you studied Mishnah? He said to him: No. He said to him: If so, with what shall I sustain you? He said to him: Sustain me like a dog and like a raven! He sustained him. After he went out, Rabbi sat and was distressed and said: Woe to me, for I have given my bread to an ignoramus.*"

For seemingly it should be said: why did he regret that which he sustained him with? Behold, is he not obligated to sustain him like a dog and like a raven, as he said to him at the beginning? It can be said that to a dog and a raven they give from the inferior food that is not fit for human consumption. However, he was concerned that he gave him bread that is fit for human consumption. From this fact that he did not say simply bread, but is precise to say *my* bread, meaning the noun with the suffixes which according to the rules of grammar is to indicate that the thing is attributed to the owner of the noun, like a worker "*He who works his land shall be satisfied with bread*" (Mishlei 12:11), because he works in this land every year and year, therefore it is called by his name "*his land.*" Likewise, "*and in his Torah he meditates day and night*" (Tehillim 1:2), therefore it is called by his name "*his Torah.*" So it is here: "*my bread*", that is to say, clean bread that was baked for me and is fit for consumption; over this he regretted.

And this is also the intention of the author of the Haggadah who begins to say: "*This is the bread of affliction that our fathers ate in the land of Egypt*", for it was not clean bread; "*all who are hungry*", even an ignoramus, "*let him come and eat*" <span dir="rtl"></span>like a dog and like a raven.

And likewise we find also in the Holy Torah, this is what is written: (Bereishis 4:3) "*And it was at the end of days, and Cain brought from the fruit of the ground an offering to the Lord.*" Rashi explained: "*From the inferior sort,*" etc. From where did Rashi know to say so? Rather, from the fact that the Torah said simply "*from the fruit of the ground,*" it implies those fruits that are ownerless and inferior; for had they been good, it would have needed to say "*from the fruit of his ground,*" from the fruits that grew in his ground that he worked and plowed and sowed himself. Therefore the Holy One, Blessed be He, did not turn to his offering. Whereas Abel brought from the firstlings of his flock, that is to say from those sheep that he fattened himself, and they were significant to him to sacrifice this to the Lord; therefore the Holy One, Blessed be He, accepted it.[^1]

Likewise also here, we pray to our Father in Heaven first: "*reign over all the world,*" let the glory of His kingdom be revealed in the world; afterward "*the world, all of it,*" that is to say, it shall be called by His name because it is His, as it is said: "*to establish the world in the kingdom of the Almighty,*" etc.; there shall not be any more any man who doubts at all that You created Your world from of old.

And now it comes out well why also further in the prayer the word "*all of it*" was not said with suffixes. For then it will no longer be implied that "*all*" sometimes means not "*all of it*", for then all the inhabitants of the world shall recognize and know that the Lord is God in the heavens above and on the earth below, and understand this.

[^1]: See on this also in my book "Nechmad VeNa'im" Part 1, The Offering of Cain and Abel
