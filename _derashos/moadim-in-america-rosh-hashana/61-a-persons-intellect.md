*"Write this for a memorial in a book, and rehearse it in the ears of Yehoshua: for I will utterly blot out the remembrance of Amalek from under heaven, etc. And he said: 'The hand upon the throne of Hashem, Hashem will have war with Amalek from generation to generation'" (Beshalach 17). And the explanation comes in the words of Chazal: "The Name is not whole and the Throne is not whole until the seed of Amalek is blotted out, from generation to generation—until the generation of the Messiah"*

(Mechilta).

*Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel said: "It is a tradition in our hands that Amalek falls only by the hand of the children of Rachel, etc."*

(Yalkut Shimoni, Shoftim 51).

*"Remember what Amalek did unto thee by the way as ye came forth out of Egypt; how he met thee by the way, and smote the hindmost of thee, all that were enfeebled in thy rear, when thou wast faint and weary; and he feared not, etc."*

(Teitzei 25).

The absolute urgency that we see in this commandment—the urgency of remembering, a matter which we do not find in any other commandment except for the commandment of Shabbos: "*Remember the Sabbath day, to keep it holy,*" whose explanation is: do not forget even for a moment the sanctity of Shabbos; and so too here: "*Remember what Amalek did unto thee,*" whose explanation is: do not forget even for a moment that which Amalek did, and in any event remember to blot out his name—the commentators and expositors spoke a great deal regarding this, and we will mention here a few of the most primary questions, which are: a) This commandment—blotting out the remembrance of Amalek—is absolutely against the character of the holy Torah, for each and every commandment therein is filled with love and mercy for all the world's creatures; and over every word and every letter hover feelings of kindness and grace, yet in this commandment the Torah placed upon us such an obligation which is ostensibly very cruel and is in contradiction to all the commandments of the Torah besides it? b) If it is truly a commandment upon us to eliminate after all those who did evil to us, this commandment would have been more appropriate against the nation of Egypt, which afflicted, shattered, and humbled us, and its cruelty was so harsh that it cast our children into the Nile; against such a nation it would be fitting for us to be commanded: "*Remember what Egypt did unto thee, blot out the remembrance of Egypt.*" But not against Amalek, whose entire evil was in this—that he began to fight with us, but in truth did not do so much evil to us, for ultimately "*Yehoshua discomfited Amalek*" and we defeated him. And after all these we see that regarding the cruel and murderous nation of Egypt we were commanded: "*Thou shalt not abhor an Egyptian, because thou wast a stranger in his land*"; and regarding Amalek: "*Thou shalt blot out the remembrance of Amalek.*" Is this not a wonder? So too, how did the Torah obligate us in such a commandment which is impossible to fulfill, since we do not know who this Amalek is, for even before the destruction of the First Temple it is accepted by us that "*Sancheriv came and confused the entire world*"? c) An open contradiction we find in this commandment: in our parashah, the Torah wrote "*thou shalt blot out the remembrance of Amalek*"—placing this work upon us to do it; and in Parashah Beshalach, the Holy One, Blessed be He, said to Moshe, "*Write this for a memorial in a book, etc., for I will utterly blot out the remembrance of Amalek*"—it appears from this that the Holy One, Blessed be He, Himself takes upon Himself to blot out the remembrance of Amalek? d) And especially wondrous is the immense war that the Godhead fights against Amalek, to the extent that in His fierce anger He uttered such an oath that "*the Name is not whole and the Throne is not whole until He blots out Amalek*"—a wonder unlike any other! The Godhead, as it were, has a world war with a clod of earth!!! We are astonished to read such an expression in the relation of the Godhead to human beings!!!

It is an accepted assumption in human society that under the category of the concept of "evil" enter the actions of: theft, robbery, fraud, and the like, and it goes without saying murder, which is the greatest evil. But this assumption is a mistake in a certain measure. True, such deeds are certainly bad, and there is no person in the world who would dare to decide that they are good deeds.

But the mistake is in this—that these deeds are not the "essence of evil" or "absolute evil"; even murder does not enter under the category of evil in essence. All these deeds are only *bad deeds*, *bad actions*, but not the evils themselves. For greater clarification: for example, if a man steals a thing from his fellow, about such a thief we cannot say that he is bad "in essence," "absolutely" bad, for he did not do this evil for the sake of the purpose of evil itself; he did not steal the money or the movables of his fellow in order to diminish him and to pain him—no, and no! He performed this evil in his desire to attain money or an object. And instead of choosing the good path—to work and earn all these from the toil of his hands—he chooses for himself the bad path to steal from his fellow. And this action is a *bad action*.

And not this alone, but even plunder and robbery, and also murder—which is the greatest and most terrible evil—they too do not enter under the category of absolute evils, for the murderer too does not murder for the sake of the purpose of murder itself, but rather in order to cover and hide another bad matter which he performed and fears lest it be revealed to the eye of all; or another cause brought him to perform this bad deed, because the slain person stood on his path and did not let him fulfill his gain or his desire, and therefore in the abundance of his agitation and anger he killed him. And had all these causes not driven him to this, then he would not have done any evil to the slain person. These deeds we call *bad deeds*, but not absolute evil in essence. And likewise, if a man robs the freedom of his fellow and works him as a servant against his will, this too is a bad deed, but not the evil in essence. For he does not intend for the evil of his fellow, only for the benefit of himself.

What is it then, *evil in essence* or *absolute evil*?

The sign of absolute evil is: if the doer of the evil does not bring to himself through this bad action of his any tangible benefit, neither money nor honor, and nevertheless he performs his deeds only *in order to pain* fellow; he performs his bad deed for the sake of *evil in itself*! This is absolute evil!

In a similar sense to this we find in the words of Chazal: "*The Holy One, Blessed be He, called the evil inclination 'evil,' because it is evil in itself, without any other purpose.*"

And this assumption we can assume also regarding a good deed. "Good" in its primary concept is not the philanthropist or the performer of other good actions. These are only *good deeds*, but not the *good in itself*, the *absolute good*. And this is because in his good actions he brings benefit for himself, such as honor—one of man's strongest aspirations—or he satisfies the desire of his soft heart, which is a pleasure to man like all pleasures. And therefore we cannot call the action by the name "good in essence," only a "good deed," which is only a good and upright means to attain his request, such as honor and the like.

What is it then, "good in essence," "absolute good"?

The good action which does not bring to a person any benefit or satisfaction, but rather the opposite—he inherits pain and affliction in exchange for it; such as, for example, if a man gives to his poor fellow all that he has, and he remains hungry; or a man gives a precious thing, the delight of his heart, for a good purpose, and he remains in pain over the loss from him of this precious thing—this is the "good in essence." He performs the good for the sake of the good.

This is one assumption.

And there is also a second assumption, and it is: for example, there is in the world a disease called by the physicians "the Asiatic disease," or "the Indian disease." All the civilized world takes measures to fight against this disease and to defeat it. It goes without saying that the intention in this is not to fight the Indians or the Asiatics. The disease is only called by their name because among them the disease broke out initially.

There is a third assumption, and it is: in our desire to cure any disease, we assault it with a power opposing it; like, for example, in the diseases of diphtheria, smallpox, and the like, we fight against them with a fluid (serum) that is processed out of the body of a person sick with this disease.

Not the personality of Amalek was the primary cause that the Torah should place an obligation upon every man of Yisrael to blot out his remembrance. The primary intention here was for a known idea, whose bearer of the idea happened to be Amalek; and therefore the Torah intended by the word "Amalek" not the personality of Amalek, only the *Amalek-ness*—if we are permitted to express such an expression—exactly like known diseases which were seen initially among known nations, the diseases are called after the name of the nations that were bearers of the diseases initially. And therefore, if we come to fight the diseases, not upon the nations do we assault, only upon the diseases. So too it is in the commandment of "blotting out Amalek." Amalek was the first nation in which there was seen initially the dangerous disease called by the name: *absolute evil* or *evil in essence*, and man must fight with this disease and assault it with great power until its destruction. All the heat of human vigor must serve as weapons to destroy and remove from humanity the terrible sin of "absolute evil." For there is power in the hand of this sin to lower humanity to the nethermost pit. This disease can bring humanity to the lowest level.

And this small nation, the nation of Amalek, was already entirely poisoned from this disease. And as a proof for this, the Torah described the signs of this terrible disease, which was not found in another nation besides Amalek, in its fighting with Yisrael. And the Torah says: "*Remember what Amalek did unto thee*"—remember the bad signs of "absolute evil" which were seen in Amalek. Many nations fight, but when nation fights with nation, the purpose is only in order to expand its boundary—like the world war that exists between the French and the Germans—but not the war itself is the purpose. And the Torah says: see that the cause in the war of Amalek with you was not for this purpose, for "*how he met thee by the way*"—a wanderer you were and you did not have yet a land which they would desire, and disputes of boundaries were not yet possible.

There is also another cause for war, and it is old political calculations, and when an opportune time comes, a nation aspires to take its vengeance from the nation which did evil to it in the past. Upon this the Torah says: "*as ye came forth out of Egypt*"—you were not yet a nation then, according to the meaning of this word, for just now you went out from Egypt and you did not stand until that time in your own domain, and there was no possibility that there would be with you old calculations, since you did not encounter any other nation until now.

There is also a nation that goes on growing and the nation dwelling upon its boundaries fears lest it grow greater than it and compete with it, and in its anxiety for the future it fights with that nation in order to weaken it. Upon this the Torah says: "*and thou wast faint and weary*"—weak and weary you were in your being a wanderer in the wilderness, and there were not recognizable then your greatness and your might, that they should be a cause for a warrior against you.

And there are also wars between nation and nation surrounding religious zealotry, if a nation wants to force its view upon its neighbor nation; upon this the Torah says: "*and he feared not God*"—he was very far from religious zealotry, because he had no religion altogether. But what urged him to fight with you? *Absolute evil*, which was in the nature of this nation; one single and unique turning it had—to pain the nation of Yisrael! And this is *evil in essence*!

And because Amalek was the first nation in which this disgusting disease was seen initially—and it is the disease of "evil for the sake of evil in itself"—therefore the Torah, in its desire to designate the disease, called it by the name "Amalek-ness."

Not so was the nation of Egypt. This nation was indeed a cruel nation, which was fitting for a severe punishment, and nevertheless this nation was not a bearer of "absolute evil," for its bad deeds were always for the purpose of its own benefit, such as the building of Pithom and Raamses, that the Egyptians enslaved the children of Yisrael to build them; even the most cruel commandment—to cast the newborn sons into the Nile—was not "absolutely bad," for for a patriotic purpose Pharaoh decreed this decree, in his believing the words of the astrologers.

But to our regret, the "disease of Amalek-ness" is highly widespread in humanity, and it is planted in the heart of every man, in every place and at every time, without difference of gender and race. The difference between nation and nation is: that in one nation there are five percent Amalek-ness, and in a second nation there are more percent, and in a third nation there are still more, and so on. And so too at every step and turn we encounter people who bear within them feelings of hatred toward others. Even if the hated person did no evil to the hater, and also the hatred that the hater bears toward the hated does not bring him any benefit, and nevertheless his eye is narrow in the life of his fellow. And though there is not in him Amalek-ness in a full measure so as to fight with him, in any case there is in him Amalek-ness in a decent measure so as to do him evil, or to spread a bad rumor about him, and the like.

And only one means there is whereby we can defeat and uproot this bad disease which became rooted in humanity, and it is: a power opposing it, the disease. And just as with physical diseases one fights against them by means of a power opposing them, so too in this disease. And the power opposing "absolute evil" is only "absolute good"; the "essence of evil" can be defeated only by means of the "essence of good."

And the materialization of the essence of good we encounter initially in the personality of our mother Rachel; she was its first bearer of absolute good. And as we explained above, the sign of the essence of good is that its doer does not expect to attain benefit through his action, and conversely, the good action brings to its doer *evil*, and he performs it only for the sake of the "good" alone. And this is the *absolute good* of our mother Rachel, who—according to Chazal—surrendered the delight of her heart and the beloved of her soul to her sister in that she surrendered the signs to her sister, even though she herself suffered greatly through this action of hers. This holy personality is it, the true essence of good.

And an action like this we encounter a second time in one of her children, in Yonasan son of Shaul, who in his love for David was ready to surrender the crown of kingship to David, saying: "*and I shall be to you for a second,*" even though to him belonged the judgment of kingship.

And therefore—only her children, a branch from the lineage of "absolute good," were able to defeat "absolute evil"—Amalek, which bears within it the full measure of Amalek-ness. And therefore we see that only upon her children was the obligation placed to fight against Amalek: initially Yehoshua son of Nun of the tribe of Ephraim, a son of Rachel; and afterwards Shaul from the tribe of Binyamin, and at a later time: Mordechai, a Binyaminite man.

But Amalek-ness in a small measure which is found in every man, the Torah placed the obligation upon every man of Yisrael to fight against it. At all times and in every place a man shall fight against this abominable disease called by the name Amalek-ness, and this is the intention of the Torah in its saying: "*thou shalt blot out the remembrance of Amalek*"; not Amalek himself, only the "*remembrance of Amalek.*" And this remembrance is found in every place and at all times.

And great and heavy will be this war, and a long time it will last. Generations will go, generations will come, and the battle array of the burning war of good with evil will not cease. And humanity will not reach the hoped-for destination if there is found yet in the world even a small measure of the abominable absolute evil of Amalek-ness. All the sins of human beings do not hinder human happiness like this sin of "evil in essence." But also in that measure, the war of the bearers of the "true good" against the bearers of the "true evil" will not cease.

And this is what the Torah recorded for us in the words "*a war to Hashem*"—the symbol of the source of good, the true good—with Amalek, the symbol of absolute evil. And this war will last a very long time—"*from generation to generation.*" And therefore "*the Name is not whole and the Throne is not whole,*" meaning the breathing space of *absolute good*, which then the Name of the Holy One, Blessed be He, and His Throne will be whole. And nevertheless, says the Holy One, Blessed be He, the final decisive war of good with evil—in which the evil will be defeated and completely cast down—will come only by means of My messenger, and he is King Messiah. This is the intention of "*I will utterly blot out*"—only he, in his Godly power, "*with the breath of his lips shall slay the wicked*"; only he will bring the dominion of absolute good in the world. And this is what Chazal explained in their statement: "*from generation to generation—until the generation of the Messiah.*" But until that time, the obligation is placed upon every man to fulfill the commandment "*thou shalt blot out the remembrance of Amalek,*" and he shall not cease even for a single moment from fighting the wars of good against evil, until the final purpose arrives with the coming of King Messiah to destroy it with a definitive destruction.

And by this will be understood the Aggadah which says that before the coming of Messiah son of David, there will appear first Messiah son of Yosef—a branch from the lineage of the personality bearing within it the symbol of absolute good—but he will fall in the war; meaning, he will not completely defeat the *absolute evil* of Amalek-ness. And afterwards will come Messiah son of David, who was prepared from Providence to bring the great light to humanity in that he will completely eliminate the *absolute evil* called by the name *Amalek-ness*. And this is the secret of: "*and rehearse it in the ears of Yehoshua: for I will utterly blot out the remembrance of Amalek.*"

Beis Medrash Shmuel
