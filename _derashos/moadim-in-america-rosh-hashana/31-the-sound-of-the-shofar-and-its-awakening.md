*“Rabbi Isaac said: Why do they blow the shofar on Rosh Hashanah? Why do they blow? The Merciful One said: "Blow." Rather, why do they sound the teruah? Why do they sound the teruah? The Merciful One said: "A remembrance of the blast." Rather, why do they blow and sound the teruah when they are sitting, and blow and sound the teruah when they are standing? In order to confuse the Satan.*

*The Tosafos explained in the name of the Jerusalem Talmud: When he hears the sound of the shofar one time, he is frightened and yet not frightened; and when he hears it a second time, he says: "Surely this is the shofar blast that shall be blown with a great shofar, and the time has arrived for him to be swallowed up," and he is confused, and he has no leisure to perform an accusation.”*

(Rosh Hashanah 16b)

Yehuda the son of Rabbi Nachman opened: (Tehillim 47:6) "*God has ascended with a blast, the Lord with the sound of a shofar.*" At the time when the Holy One, Blessed be He, sits and ascends upon the seat of judgment, He ascends with judgment; what is the reason? "*God has ascended with a blast.*" And at the time when Israel take their shofars and blow before the Holy One, Blessed be He, He stands from the seat of judgment and sits on the seat of mercy, as it is written: "*...the Lord with the sound of a shofar*" (Tehillim 47:6), and He is filled with mercy for them, and has mercy on them and transforms for them the attribute of Justice into Mercy; when? In the seventh month. (Vayikra Rabbah 29:3).

Many of the commentators have sought a way to illuminate these words of the Yerushalmi: what is the distinction between the first time and the second time, such that he did not say at the first time: "*Surely this is the shofar blast\] that shall be blown with a great shofar,*" etc.? And they also find difficulty in the Midrashic saying: "*And at the time when Israel take their shofars and blow before the Holy One, Blessed be He, He stands from the seat of judgment,*" etc.—why specifically with a shofar? See in their words what they shall say, but I said that I too shall answer my part in this.

Behold, you have no sound that will awaken the heart of a man with holy tremors so much as the sound of the shofar! This is a wondrous sound, a heavenly sound of the might of the Lord, a magic sound of the eternity of Israel, which is drawn from generation to generation, to awaken the people of Israel in all its events with the Divine quiver, to lead it to its happy end, to the Divine idea for faith in the Lord.

Like at the giving of the Holy Torah, upon the mountain that the Lord desired for His dwelling, there was a very strong shofar sound, "*and all the people who were in the camp trembled*" (Shemos 19:16).

Meaning, it was not a sound of the response of might and victory over the hater! No sound of revenge upon the enemy! Only the sound of the Divine idea and faith in the Lord, which shook their hearts with quivers and Divine tremors, to break out in the exaltation of the soul and the spirit, upon the great preparation for the chosen status, for the receiving of the Torah and the yoke of the Kingdom of Heaven, which made them into an eternal nation! Into an everlasting nation!

And so it was also in the rest of its events: in a time of trouble, or in war and the like, this wondrous sound would awaken his heart in holy trembling and with spiritual strength over his status, like in the war of Yehoshua (Yehoshua 6), and in the war of Gideon (Shoftim 7), and so David and all the House of Israel bring up the Ark of the Lord with a blast and with the sound of a shofar (II Shmuel 6).

And so in a time of trouble, that he shall return to the Lord and He shall have mercy on him, as is brought in Tractate Ta'anit (Ta'anit 15b). And so it is also on the Day of Remembrance, when they would blow the shofar, it was to awaken the people to repentance, as it is written: (Amos 3:6) "*Shall a shofar be blown in a city, and the people not tremble?*"

However, regarding the essence of repentance, there is a point of contention to be raised, which many find difficult: how can repentance be effective? Behold, when a man sins, he harms the honor of the Holy One, Blessed be He; even when he returns and confesses before Him and He forgives him—behold, the Holy One, Blessed be He, is King over the entire world, and the law is: "*A King who forgives his honor, his honor is not forgiven*"? (See in the commentators what they shall say regarding this).

But it can be said the idea in this is that it is a necessary thing that repentance be accepted, for without repentance, the world could not exist; because the human species, for whose purpose the world was created, would have turned into an evil and predatory beast, after being caught in the snare of the Satan, to be sold to his desires, with no one looking at all before his eyes at hope and resourcefulness to be uplifted from his lowliness; then he would have descended in his soul until the lowest pit, and with the destruction of his soul, the world too would be destroyed. Does the Holy One, Blessed be He, then desire the destruction of His world? Or the destruction of the work of His hands?

As is brought in philosophical works, this is what is written: (Bereishis 6:5-6): "*And the Lord saw that the wickedness of man was great on the earth, and every inclination of the thoughts of his heart was only evil all the day. And the Lord was consoled that He had made man on the earth, and it grieved Him to His heart.*"

Rashi of blessed memory explained: *And the Lord was consoled that He had made*: It was a consolation before Him that He created him among the lower beings, etc.; *And it grieved Him to His heart*: He mourned for the destruction of the work of His hands, etc.

That is to say, that all the creation of the living beings and likewise the man was from the earth, but the difference is that all the living beings were created through the intermediary of the spheres, which give power to the earth to give birth and to grow.

Whereas man was created from the earth only by the Holy One, Blessed be He, Himself, without an intermediate cause. Behold, regarding his perfection, it is certainly a great thing that he was created by the Holy One, Blessed be He, Himself; but regarding his lack of perfection and his sins, there are two aspects here:

1.  For it can be said: perhaps if he were created by an intermediary, his punishment would not be so great as it is for being created by the Holy One, for the flaw is greater in the work of the hands of the Holy One, Blessed be He, Himself.

2.  And it can be said also a reasoning to the contrary: that the Holy One shall not loathe also the work of His hands, as it is written: (Iyov10:3) "*That You should loathe the labor of Your hands?*" If so, if man were created through an intermediary, without the labor of His hands, certainly the Holy One, Blessed be He, would loathe him to destroy him entirely when he sinned. But since he is the creation of the hands of the Holy One, Blessed be He, Himself, He has pity on him and conducts Himself with him in kindness like a father over his son.

And this is what the verse said: (Bereishis 6:5-6) "*And the Lord saw that the wickedness of man was great on the earth, etc. and the Lord was consoled,*" meaning that there was a consolation for Him, because He specifically made man on the earth—meaning that He Himself made man on the earth, not through the intermediary of the spheres like the rest of the living beings. And the reason for this, "*and it grieved Him to His heart,*" as Rashi of blessed memory explained, that He mourned over the destruction of the work of His hands, and as the claim of Iyov: "*That You should loathe the labor of Your hands?*" Behold, for the Holy One, Blessed be He, does not desire the destruction of His works, and from the necessity of the situation to conduct Himself with them with compassion.

And so it is further explained in philosophical works, that for this reason we do not find the virtue of repentance among the angels, who in truth are also visited in judgment, as it is written: (Yeshayahu 24:21) "*to visit judgment upon the host of the height in the height,*" and if there is no sin, what is the visitation, and what is the judgment? And so it is written: (Iyov 4:18) "*Behold, in His servants He puts no trust, and in His angels He places reproach.*"

The reason is that the existence of the world does not depend on them; therefore the Holy One, Blessed be He, did not give them the virtue of repentance. Whereas man, for whose sake the world was created, and through whom it exists, therefore He gave him the virtue of repentance, which is necessary to be accepted, except for a decree that cannot be turned back.[^1].

As is brought in the Midrash Rabbah: (Devarim Rabbah 11:9) "*Behold, your days draw near to die*"; since Moses saw that the decree of judgment was sealed against him, etc., at that hour, what did Moses do? He donned sackcloth and wrapped himself in sackcloth, and wallowed in ashes, and stood in prayer and supplications before the Holy One, Blessed be He, until heaven and earth and the orders of creation were shaken, and they said: perhaps the desire of the Holy One, Blessed be He, has arrived to renew His world?

A Heavenly Voice came out and said: The desire of the Holy One, Blessed be He, to renew His world has not yet arrived, but "*in Whose hand is the soul of every living thing and the spirit of all flesh of man*" (Iyov 12:10), and "*man*" is none other than Moses, as it is said: (Bamidbar 12:3) "*And the man (Moshe)...*" What did the Holy One, Blessed be He, do at that hour? He announced, etc., that they should not accept the prayer of Moses, etc., because the decree of judgment was sealed against him.

That is to say, even this Midrashic saying indicates that repentance is for the existence of the world, and were it not for it, the world would be void. And this is the proof: since heaven and earth and all the orders of creation saw how Moses, the faithful shepherd, donned sackcloth, etc., and was not answered, they said: Perhaps the desire of the Holy One, Blessed be He, has arrived to renew His world? Meaning, since the repentance of Moses was not accepted, it is of necessity that the world should be void, and to renew it again a second time?

To this a Bas Kol went out and said: The desire of the Holy One, Blessed be He, to renew His world has not yet arrived, etc. Meaning, there will not be a voiding of the world here, because it is only like a decree that cannot be turned back.

And this is also the intention of the Midrashic saying at the opening of our words: "*And at the time when Israel take their shofars and blow before the Holy One, Blessed be He, He stands from the seat of judgment and sits on the seat of mercy, etc.*" Meaning, since the sound of the shofar will awaken their heart to holiness and purity, to refinement and exaltation from the descent of their soul, He is filled with mercy for them to forgive and pardon, because this purpose is the virtue of repentance, so that there shall be existence of the world.

And now, after the introductions of our words, the saying of the Yerushalmi standing at the opening of our words will turn correctly on its hinge: "*When he heard the sound of the shofar one time, he is frightened and yet not frightened,*" meaning because he is in doubt how the matter will fall—if the Holy One, Blessed be He, will accept their repentance or not, although repentance is of necessity to be accepted, but what is their repentance in His eyes? ...

However, "*when he heard it a second time, he said: Surely this is the shofar blast that shall be blown with a great shofar, etc.*" Meaning, here he understands that this is no longer the sound of the shofar of Israel—that they improved their deeds and the Holy One, Blessed be He, will forgive them—for they already blew for this, and certainly the Holy One, Blessed be He, was appeased by them. Rather, the second shofar sound is that which is said: (Yeshayahu 27:13) "*And it shall be on that day, a great shofar shall be blown.*" For this he is frightened and cannot accuse, in his opinion, since He was appeased like the first time with the sound of the shofar, He will be appeased by them also a second time, that He shall sound further the sound of the shofar of redemption! May it thus be His will, and understand this.

In another manner the idea in this can be said, that in these words of Rabbi Isaac, a wonderful portrait also stands out regarding the character of the essence of Israel our people in their exile among the nations. That the "*stain*" (*shemetz*—often referring to the forces of impurity or the accuser) and all its company. . . there is almost no disgraceful thing in the world that they do not attach to its character[^2] . . . and likewise they claim that the people of Israel have no scientific preparation at all.

And the proof they claim is, that throughout its long history, it created nothing . . . but it is a people that has before it "*only four cubits of coarse materiality*" . . . that all its existence is but upon criminal iniquity, and a sin against the laws of human life.[^3]

But in truth, if they deepen the investigation into the matter of the character of the essence of the people of Israel, they will see that it excelled specifically in this: that all the great visions that appeared in the spiritual world were only its portion that rose higher above them.

And not only that, but for it appeared all the great visions in the spiritual, even at a time when it lacked all the necessary conditions for creation[^4], and was standing on the brink of destruction. For it is possible to surmise how dry it was—perhaps it was like stones of sand and wilderness that the storm brought into the desert of the nations? ...

Which likewise the idea can be dressed in the "*blasts of sitting*" and the "*blasts of standing*": that is to say, that the character of Israel shall be recognized in this, that they will sound the *teruah* for Him and for His faith in all the periods equally.

"*Sitting*"! It is hinted at regarding the time of their sitting in tranquility and rest . . . for the word "*sitting*" (*yeshivah*) is a borrowed term for tranquility, as is known.

"*Standing*"! It is hinted at regarding the time when they lack all the necessary conditions for faith. Only they stand on the brink of destruction, without knowing at all where to turn? . . .

And this is what he said: "*Why do they blow and sound the teruah when they are sitting, and blow and sound the teruah when they are standing? In order to confuse the Satan.*" Meaning, to hint by this that when the Satan and all his company . . . see that Israel has a scientific intellectual understanding to uplift themselves in their souls in all periods equally, to create all its great visions in the spiritual, and not to abandon their faith . . . then he is confused, and does not know how to hide his failure further, for his mouth has spoken falsely in claiming that Israel is but a people that has before it "*only four cubits of coarse materiality*" . . . that all its existence is but criminal iniquity, and a sin against the laws of human life. And the proof is: for if it were a people with such a character, could it then uplift itself in its soul in all periods equally? . . . and understand this.

And again, in another manner the idea in this can be said, that in these words of Rabbi Isaac, there is also hidden the manner of confusing the Satan from accusing, and it is according to what Rashi explained there: "*To confuse—so that he will not accuse; when he hears Israel cherishing the commandments, his words are blocked.*" (Rashi, Rosh Hashanah 16b)

Because the matter is considered as if they did repentance out of love, and according to the instruction of our Sages of blessed memory: (Yoma 86b) "*Anyone who performs repentance out of love, intentional sins are made for him like merits.*" See there.

(Yoma 86b): "*Anyone who performs repentance out of love, intentional sins are made for him like merits.*" Therefore he is confused from accusing regarding the sins of Israel at all, so as not to transform them into merits. And understand this.

According to what was stated above, a moral idea can also be said regarding what our Sages of blessed memory said: (ibid. further 26a) "*All shofars are valid except for that of a cow, etc.*" Ulla said the reason of the Rabbis is because an accuser cannot become an advocate.

Like the statement of Rav Hisda, for Rav Hisda said: For what reason does the High Priest not enter in golden garments into the innermost sanctum to perform the service? Because an accuser cannot become an advocate. And is it not so? But behold, there is the blood of the bull? Since it has changed from a live animal to blood, it has changed, etc. But behold, there are golden garments outside for the service in the courtyard? We are speaking only of the inside. Is the shofar not also outside? Since it is for remembrance, it is considered like being inside.

That is to say, after our Sages of blessed memory said regarding the shofar: "*Since it is for remembrance, it is considered like being inside,*" therefore every man at the time of the blowing of the shofar needs to be awakened with a great awakening over his soul and his spirit, to show by this that he fears from the dread of judgment, at the time when his remembrance ascends to the innermost sanctum, and they examine his deeds.

In order that there shall not be an accusation against him, saying: "*A borrower and a denier of the debt,*" since he does not tremble or fear from the dread of judgment at all? However, when he is awakened in his soul from the dread of judgment, the mouth of the accuser against him shall be closed.[^5] And understand this.
