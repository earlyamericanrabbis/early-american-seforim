*"Eternal light in the treasury of life, lights from darkness He said and it was."*

(In the Shacharis prayer of Rosh Hashanah)

The commentators have already noted here wondrously regarding the expression "*He said and it was,*" for it should have said: "*and there were lights from darkness*"? In the manner that is written in the order of Genesis, as is known. And also, what is its matter to Rosh Hashanah?

Rather, the idea in this can be said, according to what is explained in our holy literature, that it deals here with two kinds of lights: one is that which was created at the beginning of creation, with which a man would gaze and look from the end of the world until its end, and which was hidden for the time to come; and one is that which remained afterward to illuminate the darkness.

Which according to the view of our Sages of blessed memory: (Vayikra Rabbah 11:7) therefore it was said regarding it: (Bereishis1:3) "*And God said, 'Let there be light,' and there was light.*" That is to say, with the expression "*and it was*" which is used for distress and not for joy, because even it is not a joy, since the world did not merit to use that same light. For Rabbi Yehuda bar Simon said: The light that the Holy One, Blessed be He, created on the first day, a man would gaze and look with it from the end of the world until its end; since the Holy One, Blessed be He, saw the deed of the generation of Enosh and the deed of the generation of the Flood, that they are corrupted, He stood and hid it from them. This is what is written: (Iyov 38:15) "*And from the wicked their light is withheld,*" see there. Behold for you from here that the light that is used now, it remained only to illuminate for us from darkness and gloom.

And this is what he said here: "*Eternal light in the treasury of life,*" that is to say, the light that was created at the beginning of creation, it was hidden in the treasury of life, for the world did not merit to use it.

"*Lights from darkness,*" meaning that there did not remain for us except light, to illuminate for us from darkness and gloom. Therefore he said "*and it was,*" meaning that for this reason it is written in the verse: "*And there was light,*" because the expression "*and it was*" is used for distress and not for joy . . . that is to say, that there was no joy, since the world did not merit to use that same light . . . which because of this it is also to be surmised, what its matter is to Rosh Hashanah, because on this day we arouse mercy upon His kingship and upon His future, we also mention regarding the hidden light, that the world shall merit to use it. . . and understand this.
