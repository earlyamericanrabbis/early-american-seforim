*"For You hear the sound of the shofar, and listen to the teruah, and there is none like You."*

(In the Musaf prayer of Rosh Hashanah)

At first glance, it can be said: what is this "hearing" and what is this "listening", and why this double language? And also, what is the meaning of "and there is none like You"? For in general, who can be likened or equated to Him? As it is said: (Yeshayahu 46:5) "*To whom will you liken Me and make Me equal,*" etc. And also regarding the hearing of the shofar sound and the listening to the *teruah* specifically—there is no wonder in this; what is this "and there is none like You" regarding the hearing of the shofar sound or the listening to the *teruah*?

Rather, it should be said according to what is known: behold, when Rosh Hashanah falls on a weekday, then He hears the actual sound of the shofar. However, on Shabbat, there is no sound of the shofar. Only then He understands and listens to the remembrance of the verses of the shofar and the *teruah*, and it is as sweet to Him as an actual *teruah*. And this is what he said: "*listens to the teruah*" . . .

However, a king of flesh and blood—if he should enact some statute and judgment in a book, and his servants who are under the staff of his rule do not fulfill his role in action, even if they honor him with their mouth and their lips, and all day they read from the book the statute and the judgment which was cast upon them on behalf of the king—would they not be called transgressors of the king’s commandment, and would he not hold them liable for their heads?

But not so is our King, our Father in Heaven. Behold, He set for man a statute and judgment to blow the shofar in the month, a blast on the day of the hidden moon. And behold, when it falls on the Sabbath day, then our Sages of blessed memory established to nullify the commandment of the shofar on this day. Yet the remembrance of the verses of the shofar and the blast He shall listen to, as if they performed it in action.

And this is what he said: "*And there is none like You,*" meaning, what king of flesh and blood is like this—that the reading of the statute and judgment from the book shall be considered for him as an actual action? . . . and understand this.

In another manner the idea in this can be said, according to what is explained in books: that the *Teruah*, which is like "*groaning a groan*" and "*wailing a wail*", indicates a *Ba'al Teshuvah*. And the *Tekiah* indicates a simple righteous person. (See also in the holy *Shelah*).

And behold, it is brought in the commentators \[regarding\] this which is written: (Devarim 32:1) “*Give ear, O heavens, and I will speak,*" etc. Moses, who was close to the heavens, said "*Give ear*", for it belongs to one close to him to speak with the language of *Ha'azanah*. And the earth was at a distance to him, so he said "*and let it hear,*" a language of hearing which belongs to a distance. But Yeshayahu the prophet was close to the earth, so he said: "*Give ear, O earth.*" (See also in *Ohr HaChaim HaKadosh*). Behold, that for one standing close, the language of *Ha'azanah* (listening closely) belongs to him, and for one standing far, the language of *Shmiah* (hearing).

And this is what he said: "*For You hear*"—from a distance—the sound of the simple shofar, which is the Righteous person. "*And listen closely*"—from up close—to the *Teruah*, which is from the Penitent. For in the place where penitents stand, perfectly righteous people cannot stand, as is known.

In which matter "*there is none like You*"—that is to say, to pay the sinner good in exchange—his evil, and to elevate him to a sublime height, where a righteous man cannot reach . . . and understand this.
