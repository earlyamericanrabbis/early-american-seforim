Preached on Rosh Hashanah 5717

“Dibrah Torah b’loshon *b’nei adam*”-In order to bring home their poignant reflections, Judaism and the Torah speak in terms of human experiences.

Hence, when endeavoring to capture the stirring solemnity of this inventory period of the year, the rabbis resorted to the bookkeeping system as their vehicle of expression.

Thus we read-“*Sholosho s’forim nif-tachim b’Rosh Hashana*-Three ledgers are opened up by the Heavenly Tribunal on Rosh Hashana.” “*Echod shel zaddikim germumim*-another is the ledger of the wicked;” “*v’echod shel baynonim*-and still another is the ledger of the average people.”

“*Zaddikim nichtavim l’alter l’chaim*- The pious are immediately inscribed in the Book of Life. *Reshaim* *nichtavim l’alter l’misah* -The wicked are immediately inscribed in the Book of Death. *Bayonim teluim v’omdim* – The average individuals are temporarily inscribed in the Book of Life. If, however, they refuse, they are then transferred to the Book of Death and their doom is sealed.”

When describing the trial period granted the average person, the rabbis make use of an unusual idiom. It reads “*teluim v’omdim*” – which freely translated, means they are hanging and standing. This unique idiom caught the fancy of one rabbi and he proceeded to explain it in the following manner.

In olden times it was customary procedure to hand a condemned criminal in the following fashion. The condemned was first made to stand on a high stool; afterwards, a noose was strung around his neck. Once secure, the executioner would quietly step forward and kick the stool from under the condemned man and he would fall to his death.

During these crucial Ten Days of Penitence, he continues, the average man figuratively speaking, is stood on a stool with the noose around his neck. If he repents he is spared; but if he continues his waywardness, the stool is kicked from under him.

AT first impact, this rabbinical appraised of man’s precarious stance during the trial period embracing Rosh Hashana and Yom Kippur appears to rule out any further exegesis. However, the more observant student of modern international affairs can visualize a broader area of analysis. Not only does the rabbinical comment reflect on the position of individual man per se, but it also delineates the position of the various governments who comprise the family of nations.

There are some whose position of strength and positive philosophy leads one to feel that they will be inscribed in the Book of Life. There are others whose philosophy of government and negative proclivities leads one to believe that ultimately they will be destroyed. There is, however, one country in particular whose position may be correctly defined as “*teluyah v’omedes* – handing and standing,” the gallant State of Israel.

A noose is being tightened around Israel’s neck and the stool which still assures its uncertain survival is being rocked and buffeted.

Israel’s Arab neighbors are not alone in this mission of destruction. When Prime Minister Eden publicly announced that Great Britain has no differences whatsoever with the Arab world, that Nasser alone is the bone of contention, what is he telling the world? When Israel, who has been denied the use of the Suez Canal, the very life-line of its existence for many years, is not invited to the London meeting, what conclusion can we reach?

I do not believe that the American people are opposed to Israel. I feel that America is Israel’s ally and that, God forbid, if America were to abandon Israel, there is no question that Israel’s chances for survival are dangerously slim. However, one does believe that our State Department policy, vis-avis Israel, has been seriously misdirected. Does it not seem that if our State Department had made a peace treaty with Israel as the price for the Aswan Dam, that much of its present embarrassment could have been avoided?

If Nasser would have rejected such a proposition, then the burden of guilt would have been his to bear. If our country would have sent arms to Israel when Russia made her recent bid to invade the Middle East by sending Egypt millions of dollars worth of supersonic jets and bombers, sixty-ton Stalin tanks, rockets capable of scorching everything, and a reinforced navy to blockade the ports of Israel, there is no telling how Nasser would have conducted himself.

True, we cannot condemn the American people, but mistakes have been made in the higher echelons which have placed Israel in an awkward position. Of late, there is too much talk, in intimate circles, that the umbrellas of appeasement soon will be brandished in high places to placate the Arab world at the expense of Israel. The noose is being tightened and the stool is being kicked from under Israel’s feet.

In the realm of atonement, Judaism regulates that forgiveness by proxy is wholly unfeasible. A man must win his own atonement. Tis regulation is especially applicable to Israel’s present crisis. If Israel is to be inscribed in the Book of Life, she can only look to Jews for help. The Israelis themselves have long ago accepted this primary obligation. They are giving and sacrificing. Their meager pay-checks are constantly being depleted by newer and heavier taxation. Jews, they keenly understand, must resolve and salvage their own destiny.

It was the Kotzker *Rebbe* who made similar observation some while back. When a Chasid came to him and related that his *Rebbe* made it a habit to pray to the Almighty God to hasten the coming of the Messiah, the Kotzker remarked, “Instead of asking God to send the Messiah, why doesn’t he instead ask his disciples to undertake the task themselves.” When Moses stood before the waters of the Red Sea and both he and the children of Israel cried to God for help, the Almighty retaliated with the command-“*Dabayr el b’nai Yisroel v’yis-u*-Wherefore do you cry unto me, speak unto the Children of Israel and command them to go forward.”

Our brethren in Israel have gone forward and they will continue to surmount every Red Sea of difficulty. Would you not call it going forward when you consider that since 1948 Israel has tripled her population and that in two or three years the population count may reach the two million level?

Are they not going forward when we consider that since last Rosh Hashana alone, 45,000 new immigrants have settled in Israel, that 20 new agricultural settlements have been established and that a 66 mile pipeline has been started to bring water from the Yarkon River in the north to the Negev region in the south?

They certainly have gone forward when we consider that since 1950 industrial production has more than doubled and that today Israel produced some 70% of all its food requirements and that exports have more than doubled in the past few years.

There is a unique way in which we can help. Our Israeli brethren are not asking us to pack bag and baggage and settle in Israel. They are not asking us to fight their battles. All they ask is that we help them to help themselves. When one invests in Israel Bonds, he is actually securing for himself a sound return for his investment. There is not a single instance where Israel has reneged on its obligations. Those of us who have purchased Israel Bonds in the past have clipped our coupons and deposited our dividends.

Buying a Bond means more than helping Israel secure its survival and giving yourself a profit of 4%, it proves to them that we have not defaulted. Is there anything more tragic than being left alone to experience friendlessness?

Our Israeli brethren want the security of knowing that we are all in this fight for Jewish survival, that for the five million Jews who are in danger, in Israel, in Morocco and behind the Iron Curtain, there are five million American Jews who want to stand up and be counted.

An Israeli Bond is more than a financial link, it is a bond of survival, an artery which carried life to an undernourished blood supply. Certainly we are not going to abandon Israel at its Valley Forge. When America first began to close ranks after the Revolution, it sent Benjamin Franklin to France for only one purpose-to sell American Bonds.

If we are going to be disturbed about all the other minorities, we certainly should be concerned about this valiant minority which is bringing the light of democracy to that area of the world which has been thriving on feudalistic corruption. If for no other reason, we should purchase Israeli Bonds to prove to the American public that we American Jews are interested in Israel’s survival. If we Jews indicate a half-heartened approach toward Israel, then what can we expect from the American public-at-large?

Nasser has told the Western nations that they can go “choke.” He has defied the might of Britain, France and the United States. What is stopping him from defying the people of Israel who have little enough in weapons, not nearly enough water, only the beginnings of industry and are only long on courage?

I visualize in the State of Israel a unique opportunity which comes to people very rarely. It is situation very well described by a remark made by one writer who was commenting on the recent rescue at sea of the Andrea Doria passengers. It had been called an “Act of God.” This particular writer asked her readers “Why do we raise our voices to the Almighty in gratitude when we are spared from evil, why do we not herald the pleasant experiences in life as an ‘Act of God?’ Daily, hundreds of ships ply the treacherous seas, yet when they arrive at port, we fail to thank the Almighty for their safe passage. Instead we attribute the ship’s safe arrival to man’s technical genius.

Our sages long ago noted, “*Keshaym shem’varchim al horo mevarchim al hatov-o*-It is the duty of man to bless the Almighty for the bad and for the good.” The Almighty is as capable of performing miracles as He is of causing catastrophes.

In my humble estimation, the establishment of the State of Israel is an “Act of God.” It is no less a miracle than the partial salvage of Europe’s Jews from the Nazi maelstrom. We gave generously to save Jews from death; by the same token we should now give to save Jews for life.

The Lubliner *Rebbe*, when commenting on the different reactions toward the great experience of Revelation on Sinai, noted, “When our ancestors beheld the wonderous sight of lighting and thunder, ‘vayiro haam vayonu-u-they grew fearful and withdrew.”

However, about Moses we are told, “*Umoshe nigash el hoarofel*-Moses disregarded the searing flames and the raging thunder; he strode forward and took his place before God.”

Today there are heavy clouds hanging over Israel, the air is dangerously charged. In the face of this grandiose experience let us shun the attitude of our ancestors, let us not withdraw shaking and trembling. Instead, like Moses, let us enter the very arena of danger.

Let us show the world that like the Torah, Israel holds within its boson the promise of a greater day for suffering Jewry. Who will be the first to step forward and announce “*Hineni*-Here I am, you can count on me.”
