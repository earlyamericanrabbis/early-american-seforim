*"Today is the birth of the world, etc. If as sons, have mercy on us like the mercy of a father on sons. And if as servants, our eyes are hanging fixed upon You until You shall be gracious to us and bring out our judgment like light, Awesome Holy One."*

(In the Musaf prayer of Rosh Hashanah).

Many have been precise here in the language: what is the meaning of that which is said: "*And if as servants, our eyes are hanging fixed upon You,*" specifically?

However, it can be said the idea in this is according to what is explained in the commentators. It is brought what our Sages of blessed memory said: (Sanhedrin 19a):

*"A king does not judge and they do not judge him, etc.; it is not different it applies only to the kings of Israel, but the kings of the House of David judge and they judge them, etc." What is the reason why not? Because of an incident that occurred, for the servant of King Yannai killed a person. Shimon ben Shatach said to the Sages: "Set your eyes upon him and we shall judge him."*

*They sent to him the king: "Your servant killed a person." He sent him to them. They sent to him: "You too, come here, for 'And it shall be testified to its owner,' the Torah said (Shemos 21:29)—let the owner of the ox come and stand over his ox."*

*He came and sat. Shimon ben Shatach said to him: "King Yannai! Stand on your feet and let them testify against you, and not before us do you stand, but before the One who spoke and the world was created do you stand! etc." He said to him: "Not as you say, but according to what your colleagues say."*

*He turned to his right, and they pressed their faces to the ground; he turned to his left, and they pressed their faces to the ground. Shimon ben Shatach said to them: "You are masters of divergent thoughts; let the Master of thoughts come and exact punishment from you." Immediately, Gabriel came and struck them to the ground and they died. At that hour they said: "A king does not judge and they do not judge him, he does not testify and they do not testify against him."*

And so it is also here, when the Holy One, Blessed be He, comes to judge Israel, and sits and ascends upon the seat of judgment (see in *Vayikra Rabbah* 29:3), and the Court on High judges them; behold their claim "*And if as servants,*" is intended to say: if You judge us in the category of servants, we have no existence of our own for the purpose of judging us, but rather our eyes are hanging fixed upon You for You are our Master, and the Master must stand to be testified in with the servant . . . and this is impossible to say of the King of Glory, and if so, the judgment is voided, until You shall be gracious to us and bring out our judgment like light, Awesome Holy One! (See also in *Beis Yisrael*, Month of Tishrei, Article 2), and understand this.

In another manner the idea in this can be said, according to what is explained in the commentators, regarding that which our Sages of blessed memory said: (Kiddushin 11a) "*There is no legal cancellation clause regarding servants."*[^1] If it is revealed defects, behold he sees them; if it is because of hidden defects, what does it matter to him? He requires him for labor, so he does not care, see there.

Likewise also on the Day of Judgment, when the Holy One, Blessed be He, comes to judge Israel, whom He acquired as servants, and found them full of defects, revealed and hidden . . . behold their claim is also: there is no *simpon* regarding servants . . . and specifically regarding the Holy One, Blessed be He, before Whom all secrets are revealed, certainly there is no thing that nullifies the purchase of a servant . . .

And this is what is said: "*If as sons, have mercy on us like the mercy of a father on sons, and if as servants, our eyes are hanging upon You,*" etc. Meaning, in whatever manner it may be, You must have mercy on us; if as sons—is it then the way of a father to see defects upon his sons? And if as servants—behold, there is no *simpon* regarding servants . . . and understand this.

According to what was stated above, it is worth noting regarding that which our Sages of blessed memory said: (Berachos 5a) "*Afflictions cleanse the iniquities of a man, by a kal v'chomer from a tooth and an eye,*" which are one of the limbs of a man that a servant goes out to freedom by them, afflictions that cleanse the whole body of a man are all the more so.

For seemingly it can be said here, behold this is only regarding a Canaanite servant, but a Hebrew servant does not go out to freedom by the loss of tooth and eye, and what *kal v'chomer* is this regarding Israel, for it is possible to explain that a Hebrew servant shall prove the contrary, for he does not go out by tooth and eye? (See also in *P'nei Yehoshua* and in the *Rif* there).

However, it can be said that the idea in this is according to what is explained in books, for behold we maintain that a servant of two partners does not go out by tooth and eye, for we require "*his servant*" who is unique exclusive to him. (See *Bava Kamma* 90a, and in Rambam, *Laws of Servants*, Chapter 5).

And behold in Rashi: (Shemos 21:6) and it is from the Talmud *Kiddushin* (22b): "*An ear that heard on Mount Sinai at the hour when I said: 'For the children of Israel are servants to Me,' and not servants to servants, and this one went and acquired a master for himself—it shall be bored with an awl,*" etc., see there.

Behold it is explained that Israel are servants of the Lord, and if so if one man of Israel sold himself to a master, behold he is a servant of two partners, for behold he has two masters, the Holy One, Blessed be He, and also the master to whom he sold himself. Therefore it is the reason that a Hebrew servant does not go out by tooth and eye, because he is a servant of two partners, and we require "his servant" who is unique to him, whereas a Canaanite servant who has but one master, therefore he goes out by tooth and eye, and behold this is regarding a servant who sold himself to a master, he is a servant of two partners and does not go out by tooth and eye.

However, with Israel whom the Holy One, Blessed be He, afflicts with afflictions, behold he is not a servant except to the Lord, and such a servant who has but one master properly goes out to freedom by tooth and eye, and if so, there is properly a *kal v'chomer* that afflictions cleanse the iniquities of a man by a *kal v'chomer* from tooth and eye, for behold if the Hebrew servant were not a servant of two partners, he would go out to freedom by tooth and eye, and the difficulty of the commentators is properly settled, and understand this.

[^1]: Explanatory note: a matter that nullifies the purchase of a servant.
