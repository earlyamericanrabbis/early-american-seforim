For Rosh Hashana That Falls On Shabbos

*In Parashas Emor it is written (Vayikra 23): "In the seventh month, on the first day of the month, shall be a solemn rest unto you, a memorial of blowing of trumpets..." And in Parashas Pinchas it is written (Bamidbar 29): "it shall be a day of blowing the horn unto you." How can this be? When it falls out on a weekday, it is "a day of blowing the horn"; when it falls out on Shabbos, it is "a memorial of blowing"—we mention it but we do not blow*.

(Yerushalmi)

And the difference is understood: it makes sense that when we blow the shofar, the *teruah* will make an impression on our souls, as the prophet said (Amos 3): "*Shall a shofar be blown in a city, and the people not tremble?*" But what impression will the remembrance of the blast alone make upon us? And how beautiful in this are the words of our master Azariah, of blessed memory, (in the book *Me'or Einayim*), for on the day of Shabbos Kodesh when we cannot blow the shofar literally, its replacement will be by lengthening a bit more than customary with words of submission and reproof, and to expound upon the Torah of our God regarding the integrity of our hearts and the cleanliness of our hands, for this is truly an authentic blowing, relating to the ultimate intention—so that through this, the very essence of the desired themes and reproofs will be corrected within us as if we heard the literal sound of the shofar, as the prophet said (Yeshayah 58): "*Cry aloud, spare not, lift up thy voice like a shofar, etc.*" Therefore, we will let our brothers hear today the secrets hidden within the remembrance of the shofar, which will act to engrave upon our hearts a replacement for the blowing.

My brothers! Yesterday during the Selichos of Erev Rosh Hashanah in the prayer of *"Eleich Azkerah Ve-eshpechah Alei Nafshi"*, we requested mercy in the merit of those Ten Martyrs who were killed *al Kiddush Hashem*. And today we will take one holy martyr from among them, and he is Rabbi Akiva ben Yosef, as the foundation of the Drush. And we will first give a concept to the listeners to know the history of the days of his life, his activity, and his service for his people, for the holy faith, and for the Torah of Yisrael, and the great impression which his death made upon the nation in general.

Yosef his father was a *ger tzedek* from the descendants of Sisera, and he married a daughter of Yisrael, and she bore to him Rabbi Akiva his son in the year 4781 (or 3781) from the creation of the world. And likewise, 47 years he was at the time of the *Churban*, and he was killed for the Sanctification of the Name in the year 4901, 53 years after the Churban. According to this, the days of the years of his life were 120 years. He was imprisoned on the 5th of Tishrei by Hadrian, and killed for the Sanctification of the Name on Yom Kippur.

In his youth he was a shepherd (or the chief of the shepherds) of Kalba Savua. And he was modest and excellent, and the daughter of Kalba Savua saw him and betrothed herself to him (Nedarim, Chapter *Ha-Nedar min ha-Mevushal*, and in Kesuvos, Chapter *Af Al Pi*): "*Rabbi Akiva was a shepherd of Kalba Savua. His daughter saw that he was modest and excellent. She said to him: 'If I betroth myself to you, will you go to the house of study?' He said to her: 'Yes.' She betrothed herself to him secretly, and she sent him to study under Rabbi Eliezer and Rabbi Yehoshua. His father-in-law heard and drove her out from his house and vowed to prohibit her from benefiting from his property.*" And at that time, Rabbi Akiva was forty years old, and he studied for 12 years under Rabbi Eliezer and Rabbi Yehoshua, and he brought with him 24,000 disciples, and his father-in-law\`s vow was annulled and he gave him half of his property (Nedarim 50a). And he married his wife when he was 64 years old (the opinion of Tosafos). And he became exceedingly wealthy, as is explicitly stated there (in Nedarim 50): from six things Rabbi Akiva became wealthy...

And he was the leader of the generation and remembered the poor. And in the war of Beitar, he was an armor-bearer to Bar Kochba. And his famous disciples are Rabbi Meir, Rabbi Yehuda, and Rabbi Yosi. And he lived in Bnei Brak (this is a place adjacent to Petach Tikvah today). And he was called "*Rabbi Akiva, a full storehouse,*" since he learned everything in a mixture and afterwards clarified each matter on its own, as is explained (in Gittin 67a). His statements are found in the Shas Mishnah 261 times, besides what is found in the Talmud and Midrash. On the day Rabbi Akiva died, Rabbi Yehuda HaNasi was born. From the time Rabbi Akiva died, the glory of the Torah ceased (Sotah, Chapter 9, Mishnah 15). An unattributed Mishnah is Rabbi Meir; an unattributed Sifra is Rabbi Yehuda; an unattributed Sifrei is Rabbi Shimon; an unattributed Tosefta is Rabbi Nehemiah; and an unattributed Seder Olam is Rabbi Yosi—and all of them are in accordance with the view of Rabbi Akiva, for they were his disciples (Sanhedrin 86a). Rabbi Akiva authored Mechiltin and Sefer Yetzirah (Seder HaDoros: Rabbi Akiva).

And now, honored listeners, incline your ears to the words of the mouth of the holy Tanna, Rabbi Akiva ben Yosef (Berachos 61b): "*'And thou shalt love Hashem thy God.' It was taught in a Baraita: Rabbi Eliezer the Great says: If it is said 'with all thy soul,' why is it said 'with all thy might'?... Rather, if you have a person whose body is dearer to him than his money, therefore it is said 'with all thy soul'; and if you have a person whose money is dearer to him than his body, therefore it is said 'with all thy might.' Rabbi Akiva says: 'With all thy soul'—even if He takes your soul.*"

Our Sages taught in a Baraita: *One time the Roman government decreed that Yisrael should not occupy themselves with the Torah. Pappos ben Yehuda came and found Rabbi Akiva publicizing gatherings in public and occupying himself with the Torah. He said to him: "Akiva, are you not afraid of the government?" He stated to him a parable: "To what is the matter comparable?..." there in Berachos. They said: Not many days passed until they seized Rabbi Akiva and imprisoned him in the prison house, and they seized Pappos ben Yehuda and imprisoned him next to him. He said to him: "Pappos, who brought you here?" He said: "Fortunate are you, Rabbi Akiva, that you were seized for words of Torah; woe to Pappos who was seized for empty matters!*"

At the hour when they took Rabbi Akiva out for execution, it was the time for *Krias Shema*, and they were combing his flesh with iron combs, and he was accepting upon himself the yoke of the Kingdom of Heaven (reading Krias Shema). His disciples said to him: "Our master, this far?!" (Meaning, for they held according to the opinion of the Rambam, Chapter 5, Law 4 of *Hilchos Yesodei Ha-Torah*, that whoever is killed for a mitzvah for which one is not obligated to be killed, behold this one is liable for his own life). He said to them: "*All the days of my life I was distressed over this verse, 'with all thy soul'—even if He takes your soul. I said: when will it come to my hand that I may fulfill it? And now that it has come to my hand, shall I not fulfill it?*" (Rabbi Akiva held according to the opinion of Tosafos in Avodah Zarah 27b that for a pious individual it is permitted).

He was lengthening the word "*Echad*" until his soul departed with "*Echad*." A Heavenly Voice went forth and said: "*Fortunate are you, Rabbi Akiva, that your soul departed with 'Echad.*'" The ministering angels said before the Holy One, Blessed be He: "*Is this Torah and is this its reward? 'From men, by Your hand, O Hashem' (meaning, from Your hand he should have died, and not by the hand of flesh and blood)!*" He said to them: "*Their portion is in life.*" A Heavenly Voice went forth and said: "*Fortunate are you, Rabbi Akiva, that you are designated for the life of the World to Come.*"

And in Menachos 29b, Rav Yehuda said in the name of Rav: At the hour when Moshe ascended on high, he found the Holy One, Blessed be He, sitting and tying crowns to the letters. He said before Him: "*Master of the Universe, who holds back Your hand (Rashi explains: who holds back Your hand from what You have written, that You need to add further crowns upon them)?" He said to him: "There is one man who is destined to be at the end of several generations, and Akiva ben Yosef is his name, who is destined to expound upon each and every thorn of the letters heaps upon heaps of laws." He said before Him: "Master of the Universe, show him to me." He said to him: "Turn backward." He went and sat at the end of eight rows...*

*When he arrived at one matter, his disciples said to him: "Rabbi, from where do you know this?" He said to them: "It is a law given to Moshe from Sinai." His mind was settled, and he returned and came before the Holy One, Blessed be He... He said before Him: "Master of the Universe, You have shown me his Torah, show me his reward." He said to him: "Turn backward." He turned backward and saw that they were weighing his flesh in the butcher market (Berachos: that they combed his flesh with iron combs). He said before Him: "Master of the Universe, is this Torah and is this its reward?!" He said to him: "Be silent! Thus it arose in thought before Me." (And the feeling is clear, for He, may He be blessed, should have said "thus it arose in My thought," and further it is a wonder: did not Moshe with justice ask, "Where is the God of justice?")*

My brothers and friends! Now we will speak a little of the matter of the day, and with this we will arrive at the aforementioned statement of Chazal.

"*Blow the shofar in the new moon... for it is a statute for Yisrael, etc.*" What is it that a day was designated for judgment—"*a law of the God of Yaakov*"? For how can it be pictured by Him, may He be blessed, to designate for this a limited day to sit on thrones for judgment in the manner of human judges—He about whom it is written: "*And that You should visit him every morning, and try him every moment,*" "*He who fashions their hearts together, and they are all scrutinized in a single glance*"? How can it be pictured to designate by Him, may He be blessed, a limited day for judgment? Rather, because so decreed His blessed wisdom—"*a law of the God of Yaakov.*"

And upon whom did He place the obligation to designate the day? Upon those being judged (as it is brought in the Yerushalmi, Rosh Hashanah Chapter 1), on the verse*: "For what great nation is there, that hath God so nigh unto them." Rabbi Yochanan said: When the ministering angels gather before the Holy One, Blessed be He, to say: "When is Rosh Hashanah? When is Yom Kippur?" the Holy One, Blessed be He, says to them: "Why do you ask Me? I and you, let us go to the court of below."*

My brothers and friends! Let us contemplate, please, the kindness from On High, that He placed in our hands to designate and establish the Day of Judgment and Law on a day when we are found to be good. And the answer is double and multiplied: a) In order that we should know in advance to improve our deeds and to eliminate the evil from our midst before we approach the judgment, before the searchers come to our houses to inspect and check our deeds—before the collectors come to collect and to take pledges, Heaven forbid (as the Tanna says in Avos Chapter 3: "*The shop is open, and the shopkeeper extends credit... and the collectors go around continually and exact payment from a person with his knowledge and without his knowledge*"). And this is: "*For You bring a statute of remembrance to inspect every spirit and soul; from the beginning of such things You made known, etc.*" b) From His designating a day to judge the universe with righteousness, we learn how to judge with righteousness one his fellow—for we too shall be gracious and merciful to those liable in judgment when we learn His blessed attributes.

Moshe Rabbeinu, peace be upon him, when he gave praise to the Holy One, Blessed be He, opened in the Torah and said: "*The Rock, His work is perfect... a God of faithfulness and without iniquity.*" The feeling is clear: is it with this that the Holy One, Blessed be He, is to be praised—in that He does not commit iniquity?! The verse cries out and says: "*The earth is full of Your kindness, O Hashem,*" "*And the kindness of Hashem is from everlasting to everlasting,*" "*The world is built on kindness.*" And Moshe praises Him in that He does not commit iniquity? I am astonished! Is it pictured to praise even a court excellent in its generation, such as Rabbi Akiva Eiger or Rabbi Leyb Chasid, ztz”l, in that they do not commit iniquity, because they are not perverters of justice? Behold, we already know in the wisdom of logic that anything in whose absence there is a deficiency, there is no virtue in its presence. (For example, if you praise a maiden before the groom because the maiden has two eyes and a nose, it will not be considered among virtues, since a young woman without eyes and a nose is considered a deficiency). And anything in whose absence there is no deficiency (for example, the young woman does not speak French, the young woman does not play upon the harp and violin) there is a virtue in its presence (the young woman understands the French and German language, plays on the violin). And if so, behold, to commit iniquity is considered a deficiency for a human being, and what is this that Moshe glorifies the Holy One, Blessed be He—"*a God of faithfulness and without iniquity*"? But after contemplation, we will understand that the praise and the glory that Moshe praises Him, may He be blessed, in his saying "*and without iniquity*"—you have no greater praise than this, and it is fitting only for the Holy One, Blessed be He, and not for a human being.

And now, hearken and hear, O Yisrael! My dear brothers, by a human being in a court below, a judgment and law cannot be pictured without some iniquity. And even if the judge is a lover of justice and uprightness, being righteous in all his ways and perfect in his deeds, and will not deviate by a hair's breadth from the book of laws—in all these you will find in his judgment which you judge upon the judged person also cruelty and iniquity. For example, one who rebelled against the kingdom or lay in wait to kill someone with guile, and became liable in judgment to be executed; the judgment that went forth from the judges according to the law—they judged a true judgment, for a judgment of death belongs to that man. But behold, his wife and his children are not to blame for his deeds, and his parents, behold, did not sin, and why should they be punished because of him? For through the punishment of the accused, behold, his wife and children will starve, and shame and disgrace will cover the face of his parents and his family who are not to blame for his sin. And even if the judge is a faithful lover to his family, behold, he does to them evil to grieve their soul over the fruit of their womb, through his children, with a disgrace that will not fade. But the Holy One, Blessed be He, is "*the Rock, His work is perfect*" (and from every side "a God of faithfulness and without iniquity"). For when He sits upon the Throne of Judgment to judge the man, He will look and see that he became liable in judgment—if he has the merit of the ancestors he will emerge innocent; sometimes they suspend it for him in the merit of his wife; sometimes in the merit of his children, as it is written regarding the sons of Aaron: "*and children they had not,*" and Chazal said: behold, if they had children they would not have died. For He, may He be blessed, weighs in balances of justice the pain of all those who grieve in his death, whether they are fitting for that pain. And such a judgment is not in the power of a human court to judge, and only regarding the Holy One, Blessed be He, is it pictured to say: "*The Rock, His work is perfect*"—from every side, "*a God of faithfulness and without iniquity*"—which is impossible for any flesh and blood. And this is what we say in the Mussaf prayer for Rosh Hashanah: "*You remember the deed of the universe and review all the creations of old; You remember the entire enterprise... everything is revealed and known before You, Hashem our God, watching and looking until the end of all generations, for You bring a statute of remembrance to inspect every spirit and soul.*" Then You are watching and looking until the end of all generations—perhaps there will emerge from them a generation of the upright who will live for their sake, just as we found that for the sake of two noble women, Ruth the Moabite and Naamah the Ammonite, the Holy One, Blessed be He, spared two great nations and did not destroy them (Bava Kamma 38b).

We must further awaken the ears of the listeners regarding a wondrous matter found in the character of the nation of Yisrael, and whereby many and great researchers of the heart are astonished, as it is brought in the Midrash Rabbah on the verse: "*And he smelled the smell of his garments, and blessed him*"—the smell of his garments, such as Yosef Meshisa, and Yakum of Tzeroros (names of men who were transgressors among Yisrael and ultimately sanctified the name of the Holy One, Blessed be He). Yosef Meshisa—at the hour when the enemies desired to enter the Temple Mount, they said: "*Let one of them and from their own midst enter first*" (one of Yisrael shall enter), let him enter first and stretch forth a hand into the vessels of the *Beis Ha-Mikdash*. And they said to that Yosef Meshisa that he should enter, "*and whatever you bring out shall be yours.*" He entered and brought out a Menorah of gold. They said to him: "*It is not the way of a commoner to utilize this; rather, enter a second time, and whatever you bring out is yours.*" And he did not want to enter anymore. Rabbi Pinchas said: They offered him the tax revenue of three years (so that he would enter a second time), and he did not accept it upon himself. He said: "*Is it not enough that I angered my Creator one time, that I should anger Him a second time?!*" What did they do to him? They placed him inside a carpenter's saw-horse (a bench of a thick board wherein they saw planks) and they were sawing him. He was groaning and saying: "*Woe! Woe that I angered my Creator!*"

And in the Yalkut to Shir Ha-Shirim: "*'What is thy beloved more than another beloved, O thou fairest among women?' Rabbi Akiva said: This is the praise and glory of Him who spoke and the world came into being. The idolaters ask Yisrael, saying: 'What is thy beloved more than another beloved... that you are thus killed for Him?' as it is said, 'Therefore the maidens (alamos / al-maveth—unto death) love Thee,' for 'for Your sake we are killed all the day long.' And Yisrael reply: 'My beloved is white and ruddy' (ruddy all the days of the year, as it is written 'Wherefore is Thine apparel red'—for the nations), but 'white' to me on Rosh Hashanah, and my garment is white as snow.*"

The remembrance of the blast of the shofar of a ram—which reminds us of the Binding of Yitzchak our father who stretched forth his neck upon the altar for the Sanctification of the Name—makes an impression on our souls to surrender also we our lives and all that is precious to us for the sanctity of His blessed Name. And with this we operate and awaken the mercies of Heaven upon ourselves and upon our infants, and for this "*my beloved is white,*" my garment is white as snow; He is a creation full of mercies to have mercy upon us. And this power of self-sacrifice for the Sanctification of the Name we inherited from our holy ancestors who surrendered their souls for the Sanctification of the Name. And therefore, we see many times among our people who had already turned away from the path of the Torah, and at the last they surrendered their souls for the sanctity of His blessed Name, as is known. And as we find here in the Midrash in the incident of Yakum of Tzeroros, the nephew of Rabbi Yosei ben Yoezer, who said about his nephew: "*In a short moment, this one preceded me to the Garden of Eden.*"

And these words of ours will open a light to understand the response of the Holy One, Blessed be He, to Moshe: "*Thus it arose in thought before Me,*" and the response of Rabbi Akiva to his disciples: "*All my days I was distressed over this verse... and now that it has come to my hand, shall I not fulfill it?*"

As we said, for the power of self-sacrifice for the Sanctification of the Name is an inheritance to the Congregation of Yaakov from their holy ancestors. And Rabbi Akiva, who was a son of converts, feared all his days lest he not be able to stand in the test to sanctify the Name of Heaven, and he was highly distressed over this lest this power be lacking to him and it be the end of his children in a time of distress during a decree of destruction, Heaven forbid. Therefore, all the days of his life he pictured in his soul the Sanctification of the Name during the Recitation of the Shema, and he longed all the days to sanctify the Name of Heaven in practice, and all his days he requested from the Holy One, Blessed be He, that he should merit to die the death of the upright for the Sanctification of the Name. (Therefore, when his disciples asked him: "*Our master, this far? Torah study is after all a mitzvah and not among those mitzvos for which one is killed, as mentioned above?*") He said to them: "*All my days I was distressed over this verse, 'and with all thy soul'—even if He takes your soul. I said: when will it come to my hand (that I merit to this), and shall I not fulfill it?*"

And with this we explain the response of the Holy One, Blessed be He, properly to the complaint of Moshe Rabbeinu, peace be upon him, who asked: "*Master of the Universe, is this Torah and is this its reward?*" He said to him: "*Be silent! (Is this not his aspiration all the days of his life?) Thus it arose in thought before Me*"—every time that he read the Recitation of the Shema, only within this was his thought, that He should merit him to die the death of the upright for the Sanctification of the Name (and this is the highest level above which there is none, as is known from the scholars of truth). Therefore, also the remembrance of the blast of the shofar of a ram will make a great impression upon us today, for it will operate upon us to remember the Binding of Yitzchak our father who surrendered his soul for the Sanctification of the Name. The remembrance of the blast will remind us therein of thousands of thousands of our holy ancestors who spilled their blood for the holy faith.

Therefore, my brothers and sisters, let us hold fast also we to the flag of faith as long as the breath of the Almighty enlivens us; let us guide our sons and our daughters in the path of the Torah and the mitzvah, and then we will all merit to a good and crowned year, a year in which Yisrael is saved with an eternal salvation, a year in which we will ascend to Tzion with joy, Amen and Amen.
