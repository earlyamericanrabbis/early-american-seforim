*"And in the seventh month, on the first of the month, a holy convocation shall be for you; all work of labor you shall not do; a day of blast shall be for you."* (Bamidbar 29:1)

(In the reading of Maftir of Rosh Hashanah)

*"Rabbi Levi bar Chama said in the name of Rabbi Chama bar Chanina: one verse says: (Vayikra 23:24) "A rest, a remembrance of the blast," and one verse says: (Bamidbar 29:1) "A day of blast shall be for you"? It is not a difficulty: here is on a festival day that occurs on the Sabbath; here is on a festival day that occurs on a weekday."*

(Rosh Hashanah 29b)

In this instructional saying, there is also hidden in it a sublime and moral idea regarding the character of this awesome holiday, despite that the merit of this day itself causes for them, for Israel, that their sin shall be atoned.[^1]

Even so, a man also needs to know that he should take to heart that not only on the merit of the day alone does the matter depend, but he also needs to return in complete repentance for his deeds, and to recognize his perverseness, and to shake himself from his dustiness, and to purify his soul and wash it from the stains of his physicality. And only with such repentance does the law dictate that his sin be cleared! And only after this great soul-revolution does the law dictate that transgression and also wickedness be wiped away! And also that the year shall be for him for a blessing and not for a curse.

But without repentance for his deeds, the danger is reflected for a man in all ways... and especially for one who profanes the holy Sabbath, and shall despise all that is dear to the Lord, but instead indeed comes on this day to the courts of the Lord only to fulfill his obligation before the creatures... certainly the danger is reflected for him, that the year shall be for him for sighing and wailing...

And this is what he said: "*A rest, a remembrance of the blast*"—on a festival day that occurs on the Sabbath, meaning when the holy Sabbath too is in the account of the holiday, that is to say, when a man shall keep the Sabbath according to the law refraining from profaning it, and likewise when he shall not despise all that is holy and dear to the Lord, just as he shall not despise the day of Rosh Hashanah . . . then there is no need for a literal blast (*teruah*), that is to say, to groan a groan for it . . . or to wail a wail for it . . . but rather his deeds alone shall cause that the year shall be for him a year of blessing!

But on a festival day that occurs on a weekday, meaning when only the festival day alone is in the account, without the holy Sabbath, and without any cherishing of what is dear to the Lord, but rather everything is mundane to him . . . then "*a day of blast,*" meaning a literal blast, that is to say, that the danger is reflected for him that the year shall be for him a year of "*teruah*", to groan a groan . . . or to wail a wail . . . because his entire intention is only to fulfill his obligation for the sake of social standing . . . and understand this.[^2]

According to what was stated above, there is to note regarding that which is brought in the *Book of Commandments* of Maimonides of blessed memory, Section 170: It is a commandment to hear the sound of the shofar on the first day of Tishrei, and that is His, may He be blessed, saying regarding it: "*A day of blast shall be for you,*" etc. (Sefer HaMitzvot of Maimonides, Section 170).

And seemingly it should be said: why does he not bring the commandment of the blowing of the shofar from the order of the festivals in *Parashat Emor* (Vayikra 23), where the commandment of the shofar is also written: "*A rest, a remembrance of the blast,*" but rather brings from *Parashat Pinchas*: "*A day of blast shall be for you*"?

Rather, according to the explanation of the Gemara above: "*Here is on a festival day that occurs on the Sabbath, here is on a festival day that occurs on a weekday,*" it comes out well why Maimonides brings thus; since he comes to teach us the obligation of the blowing of the shofar, therefore he brings this commandment specifically from *Parashat Pinchas*, because here it is said: "*A day of blast,*" for the essence of the law of the blowing of the shofar we learn from here, that it deals with a weekday.

Whereas in *Parashat Emor*, where it is written: "*A remembrance of the blast,*" that is to say—...When Rosh Hashanah occurs on the Sabbath such that it is forbidden to blow, he was not able to learn from there the obligation of blowing the shofar; therefore he brings from *Parashat Pinchas*, and understand this.

And according to what was stated above, there is yet more to note here: what did our Sages of blessed memory see to lead them to cancel these commandments: such as shofar and lulav, for which a time is fixed for all Israel together, because of a distant concern for the future? As is brought in the words of our Sages of blessed memory: (Rosh Hashanah 29b) "*And it is the Rabbis who decreed regarding it according to the reasoning of Rabbah, for Rabbah said: All are obligated in the blowing of the shofar and not all are expert in the blowing of the shofar; a decree lest he take it in his hand and go to an expert to learn and carry it four cubits in the public domain, and this is the reason for lulav, and this is the reason for Megillah.*"

Even though in other commandments they decreed thus? As is brought also in the words of our Sages of blessed memory: (Yevamot 90b) "*Uncircumcised, sprinkling, and a scalpel; a linen sheet for tzitzit, and the lambs of Shavuos, etc.*" (See Rashi's commentary there).

However, it is not similar to all these, because regarding sprinkling and a scalpel the rabbinic prohibition is already present before them, and also regarding the uncircumcised and the linen sheet for *tzitzit*, it is only for the individual and the entire commandment of Pesach is not uprooted (see Rashi's commentary on "uncircumcised"), or *tzitzit*. And even the lambs of Shavuot which is a commandment encompassing the collective of Israel, but even it is only in a case, if it occurred that he slaughtered them not for their intent (see Rashi's commentary there). But shofar and lulav which are general commandments for all Israel together, and also their postponement is fixed according to the order of the festivals, almost fixed every two or three years, certainly it is difficult to understand why they decreed thus?

Rather, it can be said the idea in this is, according to what is explained in our holy literature, behold the remembrance and occupation with any commandment, behold it is as if he performed it in practice. As is brought in the words of our Sages of blessed memory: (end of Menachot 110a), Rabbi Isaac said, for it is written: (Vayikra 6:18, 7:1) "*This is the law of the sin-offering,*" and "*this is the law of the guilt-offering*": anyone who occupies himself with the law of the sin-offering it is as if he sacrificed a sin-offering, and anyone who occupies himself with the law of the guilt-offering it is as if he sacrificed a guilt-offering.

Therefore, because of the decree of Rabbah, they relied on the remembrance of the shofar at least in the ten verses of Shofros verses of the shofar blasts that our Sages of blessed memory established for us, and it has support also from the Holy Torah, as it is brought in Rosh Hashanah (Rosh Hashanah 32a): From where \[is it derived\] that we say Malchuyot, Zichronot, and Shofrot? Rabbi Eliezer says, for it is written: (Vayikra 23:24) "*A rest, a remembrance of the blast, a holy convocation.*" *Shabbaton*: this is the sanctity of the day; *Zichron*: these are the *Zichronot*; *Teruah*: these are the *Shofrot*; "*a holy convocation*": sanctify it by refraining from performing labor. If so, the commandment of the shofar was not canceled entirely.

And so it is regarding the matter of lulav on the Sabbath, that they mention in any case in the Torah reading to the entire public the matters of the lulav and the four species; behold, the thing is also close to those commandments as if he performed it in practice. And behold, besides this, Moses already established for Israel that they should be expounding upon the matter of the day... as is known. (See the end of Megillah 32a).

And with this will also be settled that which they said above: "*And this is the reason for Megillah*"... from that which they said: (Megillah 4a) "*Regarding Purim that occurs on the Sabbath, they ask and expound upon the matter of the day. Why specifically Purim? Even a festival day too, for it was taught: Moses established for Israel that they should be asking and expounding upon the matter of the day: Passover on Passover, Shavuot on Shavuot, etc.*" The mention of Purim was necessary for him; lest you say, we should decree because of the reason of Rabbah, it teaches us that it was not decreed. Behold, in this too, the commandment was not canceled... and understand this.
