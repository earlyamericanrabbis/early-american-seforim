SERMON SECOND DAY OF ROSH-HASHONOH

Esteemed Congregation:

Today we celebrate the advent of the Jewish New Year. There is a great distinction between our celebration of the New Year and that of other peoples on earth. While others ceremonize the beginning of a new year with gay parties, we Jews commemorate and solemnize the occasion with <span dir="rtl">תשובה</span> penitence, <span dir="rtl">תפלה</span> prayer, and <span dir="rtl">וצדקה</span> charity.

Subsequent to the performance of the emphatic and significant prayers of <span dir="rtl">ונתנה תוקף</span> and <span dir="rtl">בראש-השנה</span> in which we find an impressive description of the momentous importance of the holy day of <span dir="rtl">ראש-השנה</span> as a day of trial, for all mankind and every living soul, by the Lord; as a day of judgment, a day when the Lord, our God, reviews all our activities of the past year and decides upon the continuation and duration of our life, and upon the very conditions of our life, after the completion of these prayers in humble devotion, after tears have moistened our eyes, we shall cry out:

"<span dir="rtl">ותשובה ותפלה וצדקה מעבירין את רוע הגזירה</span>".

"*Repentance, prayer and charity avert an unfortunate decree.*"

These words restore within us new hope and happy expectation.

Repentance, prayer, and charity are the means to effect that our Father in Heaven shall grant us a favorable verdict, and to bless us with a happy and prosperous new year.

Let us then, my friends, endeavor to comprehend the full meaning and significance of these three words <span dir="rtl">תשובה תפלה וצדקה</span> and also how to apply them properly.

<span dir="rtl">תשובה</span> penitance, realistically interpreted, means the complete perception of the range, of the importance, and the consequences of our sins, as well as the awareness of our neglect and disregard of our duties towards God and our fellow-man.

Friends, the proper acknowledgment of one's faults, together with true confession and sincere regret, is the first step towards amelioration and self-improvement.

The Wise King says: "*For there is not a righteous man upon earth that doeth good, and sinneth not.*"[^1]

The fear of the Lord is the beginning of wisdom, and the knowledge of the All-Holy is understanding."[^2]

According to the great rabbi and philosopher Maimonides, <span dir="rtl">תשובה</span> is the complete recognition and admission of wrongs committed; <span dir="rtl">תשובה</span> it is to express an honest confession, as it is also to correct and make good our evil doings and it is for us to decide not to repeat them; it is, finally, to do what is right, good and pleasant to God and man.[^3]

Esteemed Congregates, the Psalmist says: "*Depart from evil and do good; seek for perfection and pursue it.*"[^4] May the sacredness and importance of this day serve us as a great spiritual exaltation to make us unmistakably determined and resolved to live a sublime life filled with fine, noble, and righteous deeds. It shall be our endeavor never to cause harm to anyone, never to make anyone feel ashamed, or to create confusion of mind in our neighbor or to cause him to fall into pecuniary difficulties. It shall be the doctrine of our life, our principle, to have compassion, sympathy, affection, and a true spirit of neighborliness towards every- one, to adjust ourselves continually to be “<span dir="rtl">סור מרע ועשה טוב</span>“ one who departs from evil and strives to do good — “*One who seeks perfection and pursues it.*”

A severe verdict may be averted by <span dir="rtl">תפלה</span> prayers. My friends, we must pray in order to establish our spiritual contact with the Lord. Our prayers shall help us lift our- selves from the depth of the evil instinct, from egotism and demoralization; our prayers shall exalt and inspire us and make us capable to perceive the <span dir="rtl">שכינה הקדשה</span> Divine Majesty at all times. The service to our Lord shall give us the right understanding of how to purify our character and soul. Our Sages call <span dir="rtl">תפלה</span> prayer, divine service of the heart. May, therefore, the loftiness and significance of this day's prayers bring us to the heights of spiritual exaltation, so that we may serve and worship the Lord with all our heart and soul, in humility, devotion and reverence, and may we thus entreat mercy from God to fulfill all our wishes for good.

The best evidence for the reality of our good intentions and our strong resolution to exert all our mental and physical strength and to observe and keep all the noble virtues and deeds, as they are prescribed in our holy Thorah — is with- in the fulfillment of the third remedy, namely <span dir="rtl">צדקה</span> charity. Yes, charity — but charity with its Jewish interpretation. According to our conception of the word <span dir="rtl">צדקה</span> it means nothing else but to discharge one's human obligations towards one's fellow man. We are to practice charity in a very congenial manner, in order that the less fortunate may not feel humiliated or degraded, but rather, encouraged, com- forted, and uplifted, with a new hope for a better life.

Sisters and Brothers! Let us, in true appreciation of the eternal spirit of this day of Rosh Hashonoh, resolve to live a new life, a life of greater achievements. Let us return to our God with all our heart and soul, with the strong resolution to make good all our wrongs, to be, at all times, kind and righteous towards our fellow men. It is only then that our repentance, prayer and charity will save us from the severe sentence above. And it will be then, and only then, that we may deserve that the Lord grant us a happy and prosperous New Year, a year of the <span dir="rtl">גאולה שלימה</span> the liberation of our people.

Eisan Shmuel
