*In the Gemara Berachos (16b): Rav, after his prayer, would say this: "May it be Your will, Hashem our God, that You grant us long life, a life of peace, a life of goodness, a life of blessing, a life of sustenance, a life of physical vigor, a life in which there is fear of sin, a life in which there is no shame and humiliation, a life of wealth and honor, a life in which there shall be in us love of Torah and fear of Heaven, a life in which You will fulfill all the requests of our heart for good, Amen Sela."*

*(In this prayer of Rav, the word "life" is found eleven times, corresponding to the eleven months of the year that we bless—excluding Rosh Hashanah, which we do not bless. And corresponding to Tishrei, it is found in the prayer of Yechadesheu Ha-Kadosh Baruch Hu, etc., le-chayim u-le-shalom).*

We must understand why he mentions and repeats the word "life" for each individual request. It is obvious that all these we request while one is yet alive!

We will explain this according to the commentators, and first we will introduce the explanation of the descriptive noun "life" as it is found in every place. Know that "life" is said regarding anything—even regarding the inanimate and the vegetative—as long as it performs its specific function unique to it, the description of "life" applies to it. But as soon as it ceases from performing its unique function, the name "dead" is applied to it.

Regarding the inanimate, we find that regarding iron vessels in every place, "*their breaking is their death.*" And regarding the vegetative, it is found in every place (Iyov 14): "*Though its root grow old... yet through the scent of water it will bud.*" A tree, when it dries up from producing fruit, is called dead, even though it is fit for the consumption of fire or to make vessels from it.

So too man, who is separated from the rest of living creatures by the power of speech and intellect which Hashem, the Fashioner of man, graced him with—if he turns away from the path of the Torah and the mitzvah, he is called dead. And this is what we say, "*for they are our life, etc.*" And it is written in the Torah: "*But you who cleaved unto Hashem your God are alive, all of you, today.*" And how much more so if he acts wickedly to perform a strange deed. And this is what Chazal said: "*The wicked, even in their lifetimes, are called dead,*" as it is said, "*And thou, profane wicked prince of Yisrael*" (Yechezkel 21:30)—is he literally slain? Rather, because he did not fulfill his destiny and nullified his specific function unique to him, he is called a slain one, dead.

And this is what David said: "*I shall not die, but live*" (while I am alive)—and the remedy for this is: "*and declare the deeds of God*" (with this I shall live eternally). And the wicked, because they do not prepare themselves for the life of the World to Come, are therefore called dead. As are the words of the Tanna (Avos Chapter 4): "*This world is comparable to a corridor before the World to Come, etc.*" Thus, the life of this world is nothing but a place of preparation for the life of the World to Come, wherein man will then arrive at the true, blissful life eternally. And if a man will be of those who continuously walk in the path of Hashem, his days will be lengthened, as it is written (Mishlei 10): "*The fear of Hashem adds days, but the years of the wicked shall be shortened.*"

And this is what we request today: "*May it be Your will... that You grant us long life*", just as we say in the blessings of the Shema: "*And enlighten our eyes in Your Torah... and unite our hearts to love and to fear Your Name, etc.,*" for through this we will merit length of days.

And we will understand with greater explanation according to the words of the Psalmist (Tehillim 50): "*He calls to the heavens from above, and to the earth, to judge His nation.*" (The verse speaks in metaphor and riddle), for the heavens and the earth are not judges! But indeed, know that there are two timelines for the life of man:

1.  According to the capability based on the measure of natural life and the radical moisture customary within him.

2.  The time given to him based on the uprightness of his path by the judgment from Above.

And the two measures are designated, one to the heavens and one to the earth (meaning, natural life is designated to the earth, and uprightness is designated to the heavens). And this is: "*He calls to the heavens from above, and to the earth, to judge His nation*"—meaning, both of them judge the man and conclude his judgment, whether for life or for death. If from the cause of nature he dies, it is because the sustaining power has already ceased, and the marrow of life that preserves him is completed; or if from the cause of the judgment of Heaven, which was thus decreed according to justice and uprightness whose dwelling is in the heavens above.

Truly, the tzaddik—his righteousness is decisive to increase his days beyond his natural powers, as it is written, "*The fear of Hashem adds days.*" And conversely, the wicked—even if according to his nature he is yet in his prime, and the days of his life could yet continue for mighty days, but when the judgment from Above has decreed his sentence that death is better for him, because as long as he exists he will corrupt further, therefore "*the years of the wicked shall be shortened,*" and he will not complete his years estimated by the power of his customary nature—unless he repents, then his soul shall live, and his righteousness is decisive to increase his days like the sand, beyond the power of nature given within him.

And in this judgment will be the decree from Above upon one who subjugates his soul to raise his sons for the study of Torah and his household to guide them in the paths of righteousness—the judgment from Above grants to lengthen the days of his life, as it is written: "*And you shall teach them to your children to speak of them... and you shall write them upon the doorposts of your house, so that your days and the days of your children may be multiplied... like the days of the heavens upon the earth.*" The explanation is: that as it is viewed from the heavens beyond the power of the earth, one who is careful in the mitzvah of tefillin lengthens days, as it is said, "*Hashem, upon them they shall live.*" One who occupies himself with tzedakah and acts of lovingkindness—"*tzedakah saves from death*" and he merits length of days, as it is written, "*He that follows after tzedakah and chesed finds tzedakah, life, and honor.*" And this is mixed into the prayer that we request: "*May it be Your will, Hashem our God, that You grant us long life*"—give us yet, Hashem, that we may walk in the path of the good and merit length of days.

A LIFE OF PEACE: Man, who is composed of body and soul, requires two types of perfection: the perfection of the body, that there should not be found within it any destruction through the strife of the elements and the temperaments of the body and their separation; and he must further endeavor to attain the perfection of the soul. As Chazal said: "*Regarding beinonim, both the good and evil inclinations judge them.*" "*Woe is to me from my inclination, woe is to me from my Creator.*" Because the soul and the body fight one against the other all the days, as is described before us, in *Sefer Akedat Yitzchak,* with his beautiful words, and this is his language: "*And all the days that the king's daughter associates with this stricken body, what a commotion are her thoughts! How great is her pain! For the law of lack of agreement is between them. Woe to the house that bears the yoke of strife between a man and his wife; all their days they anger one another... a strife of saturation and affliction all the days of their being together in the fortress. This one loves the hunt, and this one dwells in tents; this one cleaves to Hashem, sanctifying herself from the defilement of the world which is placed in her heart, and this one fears for the shame of the path of the vineyards,*" (And this is what the prophet Yeshayah says, 57:19): "*Peace, peace to him that is far off \[the soul\] and to him that is near.*" When they are bound and connected one with the other, it is an agreed matter from the Sages of Yisrael that through avonos and transgressions, which are the illnesses of the soul, the body also becomes sick; and with the atonement of the avonos, the body returns to its strength, as it is said, "*Who forgives all your iniquities, Who heals all your diseases*" (Tehillim 103).

And with this we will understand the words of David (Chapter 30): "*And I said in my prosperity, etc.*" The explanation is: I thought that life and health are only natural, to the extent that as long as the body is healthy, nature dictates that I should not fall sick. And this is: "*And I said in my prosperity, etc.*"—meaning, as long as I am prosperous and there is not found within me any destruction through the strife of the elements and the temperaments of the body and their separation, then "*I shall never be moved.*" But now it is known to me that life is double; it depends only upon the desire of Hashem and His blessed will. And this is: "*Hashem, by Your favor You settled strength for my mountain*"; now I am convinced that it depends only upon Your will, and "*when You hid Your face, etc.*" (then this mountain—the body—began to falter). Just as we said in the explanation of "*He calls to the heavens from above, and to the earth, to judge His nation... Unto You, Hashem, I call, etc.*" And this is why we request from the Holy One, Blessed be He, a life of peace—peace in the body, peace in the soul, in the manner of: "*My covenant was with him of life and peace.*" While yet in his living life, he merited true peace.

A LIFE OF GOODNESS: As is known, the pleasant, the beneficial, and the good are three distinct matters. And these three together you will find in our holy Torah and its mitzvos, as it is written (Tehillim 19): "*More to be desired are they than gold, yea, than much fine gold*"—this is the *beneficial*, which benefits a person in his time of distress; "*sweeter also than honey and the honeycomb*"—this is the *pleasant*; "*moreover by them is Your servant warned*"—meaning, even Your servant who does not pursue after imagined things, the beneficial and the pleasant, nevertheless is warned by them; because "*in keeping them there is a great reward*" (the ultimate *good*). And this is what we request from Him, may He be blessed: a life of goodness—meaning, that all our toil should be for a good purpose.

A LIFE OF BLESSING: Two types of life exist in the world, as it is written (Parashah Nitzavim): "*I have set before thee life and death, the blessing and the curse.*" The explanation is: there are times when life and health are nothing but a blessing, and life can be a curse. When Hashem does good to the good, his entire days will be according to the Torah and the mitzvos, occupying himself with tzedakah and acts of lovingkindness—this life is for a blessing. Truly, the man to whom God grants wealth and possessions, and he fills the hollow of his belly, and in everything he turns he acts wickedly, a sinner who causes others to sin, using the horns from his hand to strike down everything holy in Yisrael—you have no life of a curse greater than this, as Chazal said, "*The wicked, even in their lifetimes, are called dead.*" And this is: "*Behold, I set before you this day a blessing and a curse; the blessing, if you shall hearken*"—meaning, this world will be to you for a blessing when you prepare your heart to hearken; "*and the curse, if you shall not hearken.*" And for this we request from Him, may He be blessed, a life of blessing—that we should live life according to the Torah and merit blessing.

In yet another manner, a life of blessing in the manner of: "*And in thee shall all the families of the earth be blessed.*" As Chazal explained in the Midrash Rabbah, Rabbi Chiyya bar Abba said: "*The praise of a matron is not when she is praised by her relatives, but rather when she is praised by her rival wives.*" And this is what we pray: "*May it be Your will that we merit to a life of blessing from all the nations,*" as it is written, "*Yisrael, in whom I will be glorified.*"

A LIFE OF SUSTENANCE: As is known, many people cause themselves to die before their designated end allotted to them by nature, because day and night they do not rest upon their stations and they toil far beyond their strength, or they endanger themselves on dangerous paths to bring prey to their house to satisfy the soul of their children. It turns out that they cause themselves death from the sustenance which they seek. And this is why we pray for "a life of sustenance"—that He should grant us sustenance that brings life.

And in a second manner, in the way of the Tanna (Avos): "*This world is comparable to a corridor before the World to Come; prepare yourself in the corridor so that you may enter the banquet hall.*" And as is known, a person is forced to seek sustenance from labor or from some business, and sometimes the sustenance causes spiritual death through the desecration of Shabbos and Yom Tov, Heaven forbid, or in businesses forbidden according to the Torah, as regarding this Yirmiyah already raised a lamentation: "*With our souls we bring our bread.*" And by way of hint in the Toras Moshe: "*Thou shalt not eat the soul with the flesh.*" And fortunate is the man who merits and is sustained from the toil of his hands in the manner of the Rambam: that a man should endeavor in all his deeds that they should be for the sake of Heaven, so that even matters of permission and necessity—his intention should be for the service of the Creator, or for a matter that brings to His service. And this is what Shlomo said: "*In all your ways know Him,*" and one who conducts himself thus serves his Creator constantly (in Orach Chayim, Section 231). And in this manner, behold, also his toil in his seeking sustenance also brings him to eternal life. And upon this we pray and request from Him, may He be blessed: "*Grant us a life of sustenance*"—that we should merit life from the sustenance.

A LIFE OF PHYSICAL VIGOR: In the manner of that which the prophet said (Yeshayah 58): "*And Hashem will guide you continually, and satisfy your soul in drought*" (the explanation is that the spiritual bliss will be very great, for He will satisfy your soul with the brightness of the Upper Light); and corresponding to the perfection of the body, he said: "*and make your bones strong*"—meaning, He will arm them with structural moisture and the strength of youth.

Further hinted in this is a prayer for the nation in general. And this is (the prophet Yechezkel 37) describing the nation at the time of exile as a valley filled with human bones. And upon this the lamenter said: "*He has worn out my flesh and my skin; He has broken my bones.*" And David said: "*When I kept silence, my bones wore away*" (Tehillim 32), and he prayed for the generality of the nation (ibid. 38) and said: "*O Hashem, rebuke me not in Your wrath, etc. There is no health in my bones because of my sin.*" (Ibid. 141): "*As when one plows and cleaves the earth*" (where the clods are scattered here and there in the expanse of the land), so "*our bones are scattered at the mouth of the grave.*" And in this hollow exile we are scattered to all corners of the earth. And upon this we pray for a life of physical vigor—that there should be with the strength of youth the generality of the nation, in the manner of: "*Renew our days as of old.*"

A LIFE IN WHICH THERE IS FEAR OF SIN AND FEAR OF HEAVEN: Behold, Chazal already said, "*Everything is in the hands of Heaven except for the fear of Heaven.*" And here Rav let us hear a new thing—that also with Heaven there applies "fear" by way of metaphor. This is comparable to a father who saw his only son holding a sharp butcher knife in his hand, or holding the barrel of a rifle, and the father is afraid lest he harm himself; or when his son holds a cup filled with the poison of death and the father is afraid lest the son place it, God forbid, into his mouth and die from this. By way of this, fear is found in Heaven; for the 613 mitzvos, according to the Zohar, are the 613 counsels of the holy Torah for the children of Yisrael to hold onto the paths of Hashem—"*Just as He is merciful, so too you be merciful, etc.*" And they are warned for their own good not to become defiled with forbidden foods, distancing them from creeping things and swarming things, from all types of defilement that defile a person, understanding them to conduct themselves in family life for their happiness and the happiness of their children who will be born, as it is written: "*and by this thing you shall lengthen days.*" And this is why we request a life of fear of Heaven: "*May it be Your will that we live before You in a life of fear of Heaven,*" in the manner that they paved for us from Heaven.

And "*fear of sin*"—this is a great level, where a person is afraid of the sin itself, not specifically out of fear of the punishment, but rather that we should merit to fear of sin, that we should be afraid of the sin literally as a person is afraid of a venomous snake, a fiery serpent, and a scorpion; and just as he flees from the tail and the venom, so he flees from a transgression.

A LIFE IN WHICH THERE IS NO SHAME AND HUMILIATION: In the manner of: "*And please do not make us dependent, Hashem our God, neither on the gifts of flesh and blood, etc., so that we should not be ashamed nor humiliated forever and ever.*" The explanation is: that we should never come to an internal shame; for when a person stumbles in one of the sins, the shame will cover the face of his soul internally during the performance of the bad deed. Even if the matter is not known to any person and no scorn will move him from them, nevertheless he will be ashamed in himself within the chambers of his heart, because he lowered the glory of his soul and behaves with degradation toward the image of God given within him. And the humiliation will cover his face because he finds himself lacking the perfection that was in his hand to achieve, and to bend his inclination, or to be careful that he should not come to this obstacle (in the path of repentance). And this is what we request: a life in which there is no shame and humiliation.

A LIFE OF WEALTH AND HONOR: Because there is wealth that comes to a person through disgrace and shame, as it is written in Yirmiyah (17) regarding this, calling out: "*As the partridge that sits on eggs which she has not laid, so is he that makes wealth and not by justice; in the midst of his days he shall leave it, and at his end he shall be a fool.*" And upon this we request: a life of wealth and honor—that we should merit to two tables: to wealth along with the honor of the Torah, Torah and greatness together.

A LIFE IN WHICH THERE SHALL BE IN US LOVE OF TORAH AND FEAR OF HEAVEN: In this we request from Hashem that we should not make the Torah a spade wherewith to dig, nor a crown wherewith to magnify oneself, for then he would not love the Torah, but rather its exchange—because he is sustained from it. And this will be what is recognizable only from the love of the Torah for its own sake, and from that the purpose of Torah is repentance and good deeds. As Chazal said: "*Woe to the one who has no courtyard, yet makes a gateway for the courtyard!*" And through this we request that there shall be in us love of Torah and fear of Heaven.

And further we can explain according to the author of the *Akedat Yitzchak*, that the love and the fear are nothing but one thing, not two. For the fear is bound with the thick cords of love, and the love is gripped in the ropes of fear—two joined together and they become one thing. And as the author of the *Sefer Ha-Bris* explains it with a parable of a father's love for his son: upon seeing the dear son, a child of delights, jumping upon the table and leaping over the vessels, behold, the father standing outside is afraid lest his son fall from the table. And what place is there for this fear? Behold, the father is certain that no punishment will arrive to him from the child if he falls, and the language of fear is not applicable here! Rather, it is because he loves the child very, very much, and he is afraid lest pain and damage and blemish arrive to the child from the fall—not that he fears for his own body. And if so, what is the matter of this fear and what is the cause for it? You must say: this is the love, that he loves the child with a great love. Therefore, it is the way of a father that he is afraid to do a thing that he knows is against the will of the child—even to speak a word that is against the child's will—but the father endeavors always to perform before him everything that gladdens the soul of the child. And this itself is the matter of the great love and fear of the fear of exaltedness for His Name, may He be blessed. For when he loves Hashem with the love of truth, he is afraid for Him always lest the sin cause a blemish in the honor Above. Behold, when a man fears Hashem in this way, it is a sign of truth that he loves Hashem with a fierce, true love, and this fear is the perfection of the love. And through this we pray: "*and grant us a life of love of Torah and fear of Heaven.*"

A LIFE IN WHICH YOU WILL FULFILL ALL THE REQUESTS OF OUR HEARTS FOR GOOD: Because in truth, a man does not know what is good for him in life; for both might, both wisdom, and so too wealth are kept sometimes for its owner to his detriment. Therefore we find in the prayer of Shlomo at the dedication of the Temple (Melachim I 8) that regarding the gentile he says: "*And also to the stranger, etc., You shall hear in heaven... and do according to all that the stranger calls to You.*" But regarding Yisrael he requested: "*and give to every man according to all his ways, whose heart You know.*" And so too David prayed for Yisrael and said: "*prepare their heart*" (first that they should understand what to request, and afterwards) "*You cause Your ear to hear.*"

And in this way we pray today: "*May it be Your will that You fulfill the requests of our heart for good*" (as You find to be good before You), and bring us to a good year, a year in which we are written for a good life, and good sustenance, and for the complete redemption speedily in our days, Amen!
