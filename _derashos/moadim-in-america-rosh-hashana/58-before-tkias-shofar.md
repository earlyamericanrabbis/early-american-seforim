*"Rabbi Yitzchak said: Why do they blow a straight blast on Rosh Hashanah? Why do they blow? The Torah said, 'Blow!' Rather, the question is why do they sound a broken blast? The Merciful One said, 'A remembrance of a broken blast!' Rather, the question is why do they blow a straight blast and sound a broken blast when they are sitting, and blow a straight blast and sound a broken blast when they are standing? In order to confuse the Satan"*

(Rosh Hashanah 16b).

Great questions pick at my mind currently, honored masters! We stand now to receive the face of a new year. Yet I will ask you: by what did it merit to be called a "new year"? How is this day different from any other day? What is changed this day from the day of yesterday? To the day of yesterday it is called an "old year," and to this day it is called a "new year"!

Behold, yesterday the sun rose and the sun set, and today also the orders of Creation have not changed—the same sun and the same moon and all the host of heaven—and no change is seen in the Creation. And just as the day of yesterday fell into the abyss of the past and it was not known that it had entered its midst, so too this day and the coming days will fall into the abyss of the past and will not leave behind them any change in the Creation. And if we, who are formed of clay, divide the days into weeks, and the weeks into months, and the months into years, it is only to make the counting easy for us, lest we need to count the days by the thousands and tens of thousands.

But we shall ask: why did our holy Torah command to give a special value to a specific day to call it the beginning of a new year? Behold, a thousand years is considered like a single day in the eyes of the Master of all, and even this is only by way of metaphor, since He has no beginning and He has no purpose or end. And if so, the question stands in its place: why do they blow? Why did our holy Torah command us to blow and to announce that from this day a new year begins, since there is nothing new under the sun, today being just like the day of yesterday?

And a still greater question is to be asked: if ultimately we blow according to the commandment of the Torah, it should be enough for us with straight long blasts alone with simple sounds, with sounds that are pleasant to our ears, with sounds that gladden hearts, and why do they sound a broken blast? The groans and the wailings, why do they come? Are the groans that go out from our hearts all year long too few for us, that we must seek a place to sigh? Are the wailings and the weepings of the entire year too few for us, that on the day of the festival—the day designated to be called a new year—we must utilize them for a strange pleasure?

It is enough for us to utilize groans and wailing at a time when we have no way to be saved from them, at a time when the heart is full so that it cannot contain within its midst all the tragedies of life, at a time when the heart bursts to go outside, at a time when the brain will not perform its work from the abundance of its toil, at a time when we are found in a state of despair, at a time when we find ourselves at a loss for counsel, at a time when life stands before us like a fiery pit wherein all the constraints of the netherworld fall to our portion, at a time when all the bad and turning mishaps of time—angels of death and wrath—play with us, at a time when the raging waves of the sea of life pass over us, to the extent that if we pay attention to all these, our hearts will not suffice for us to sigh, and our eyes—even if they become a fountain of tears—cannot express even one out of a thousand of our bitter feelings. And why slap our groans and our tears at a time when they are not necessary for us, at a time when we could utilize musical instruments to gladden our souls, so that we might forget a little bit the bitterness of life? And the question stands in its place: "*Why do they sound a broken blast?*"

And though we, the short-sighted, cannot ask deep questions about our holy Torah, and we must walk after it with closed eyes, without being able to penetrate to its depth to understand its hidden secrets, but why did we add also upon the commandment of our Torah, and "*why do they blow a straight blast and sound a broken blast when they are sitting, and blow a straight blast and sound a broken blast when they are standing*"? It should be enough for us to fulfill the order of the Torah and not to add upon it, since we cannot understand the purpose?...

But, honored masters! A man who travels to stroll, to enjoy the glory of Creation and the beauty of nature—this man will not count nor number with a discerning eye the number of parasangs that he traveled and that he has yet to travel, since he has no specific destination for his journey to which he carries his soul, because the journey itself is his purpose and he enjoys it, and his entire wellspring is only that the journey should cause him an expansion of mind and gladness of heart, for this is his entire desire from this journey of his.

And so too, the man who travels on a distant path, who must spend many weeks and months on the road until his arrival at the place to which he travels there for a specific purpose, for a specific goal, for a known aspiration—upon his standing at the beginning of his journey, he will not begin to number and count the number of parasangs that he traveled and that he has yet to travel, since it is but a little while since he began to travel and he has yet to travel much; of what account is it if he went further to travel by one parasang more or less. And he only seeks means of how to improve his journey during the time that he must travel in a wagon or on the railroad, lest the time grow long for him in his journey, lest he feel the loneliness, and lest he know lack, since he must be a long time on his way.

Not so the man who travels to a specific place for a specific purpose of his, and his journey is exceedingly short, and in just a little bit he will arrive at his desired place—this man will not take to his heart, and it is all one to him, whether his short way will be pleasantness or pain, whether he will hunger or thirst a little, whether his sitting in the wagon is good for him or difficult for him, since his journey is not his purpose, and it is also exceedingly short, and what of it if during a short time he suffers a little, behold, in just a little bit the end will come to his journey. In this manner, the traveler will not suffer grief from his journey and his heart is good upon him, even at the time when the way is not as his soul would love...

Yes, honored masters! Our path—the path of life—is not hedged with roses; rocks of stumbling and stones of obstruction stand always as a hindrance to us. In everything we turn we are always stumbling, beaten and afflicted; the measure of punishment is greater than the good measure, the tragedies are too awesome, all the constraints of the netherworld are our portion in life, and we waste away in our poverty, and like melting wax we are turned from the emotions of our hearts and from the anxieties of our souls. Every bad happenstance, every obstacle, and every quarrel, and all kinds of bad diseases that pass over us, rob from us our peace. And a great value we give always to all the bitterness of life, and all the bad happenstances make a permanent place and great impressions in our hearts, and deep wounds, because we always forget that "*the days of our years among them are seventy years, and if with strength eighty years,*" and our path—the path of life—is a short path, and it itself is but a corridor to eternal life. And it is only a journey to a specific destination, and it is all one to us how this short way passes.

And a voice from On High goes out and calls to man: Son of man, short of days, why do you seek a good life, and why do you get agitated by a bitter life, since life is exceedingly short? And it is only the Satan—which is the evil inclination in man, which is the Angel of Death in life—that drives you from rest over nothing, and you are pursuing and driven, and you are killing and killed, and you do not take to your heart that you—what will it give you if you sit in serenity in your small and short world, or if life turns away from you and the world becomes narrow for you and you do not find in it a place to sit. Between this and that, the short way will pass, and in vain are you pursuing after the vanities of time, and for their sake you sin against the uprightness of your heart, and in vain do you grieve your heart when you pursue and do not attain...

And therefore our holy Torah said: make your days into weeks, and from weeks months, and count the months into years, and then you will know the shortness of your way: "*Blow the shofar in the month,*" to remind you that you have passed one station out of the seventy stations that you must travel, and you are closer by a station to the place where there you will separate from life. And this remembrance will remind you of the remembrance of the shortness of life, and you will no longer pay attention to the bitterness of life, to the pokings that are in life, and to the weepings and the wailings with which life is rich, and you will not be drawn in the greatest measure to the vanity that is in life—to the cruel Satan that binds you to life in order that you should feel even more the negative that is in life; rather, you will be happy with your lot and laugh at all the mishaps of time, and you will not sell the uprightness of your heart for the vanity that is in life...

And this is the intention of their statement which we placed at the opening of our words: Rabbi Yitzchak said, "*Why do they blow a straight blast on Rosh Hashanah?*" Meaning, why did the Torah say to blow, to place boundaries to life and to establish limits on a thing that has no end, and to divide between the years? "*Rather, the question is why do they sound a broken blast?*" Meaning, the greater question in this is why did the Torah say to meet each year with a sighing groan and a wailing sob—it would have been better to receive each and every year with a sound of gladness and a sound of joy.

And behold, upon these questions there is to answer a simple answer: the Torah said so, and we have no right to seek into things that are hidden from us. "*Rather, the question is why do they blow a straight blast and sound a broken blast when they are sitting, and blow a straight blast and sound a broken blast when they are standing*"—which this the Torah did not command us, but rather from ourselves we conduct so? "*In order to confuse the Satan*" who binds us to life, and because of this we feel even more the disasters of life, and for the sake of life we surrender our souls to the Satan.

And therefore, we must blow a straight blast and sound a broken blast both when we are sitting and both when we are standing, to tell us: both when we are sitting in serenity in the world and in life, and both when we have no place to sit in life and we are suspended and standing in the world, with no place to find rest for our weary soul, we must know always that only seventy stations we have to pass. And every time that we pass one station, one year, we are distancing ourselves further from the present life, and we are closer to the end of our journey to eternal life. And therefore, let our hearts not grieve excessively from the negative that is in life, but rather we must be ready to give a judgment and accounting for our deeds that are in life.

Indeed, we must endeavor also for the sake of this short life to sweeten it with everything possible, and even more so to sweeten the life of our fellows; but God forbid to grieve our souls from the bitterness that is in life, since this too will pass in a short time. And when we understand the value of life, then without much toil we will feel peace in our souls, and our hearts will not grieve, and we will make peace between us and our Father in Heaven, and we shall merit to the end of days and we shall see in the consolation of Tzion and Yerushalayim, Amen!...
