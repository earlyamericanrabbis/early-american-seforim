*The year 5670 (1909) for us in our request, according to the abbreviated era*

"*And say before Me on Rosh Hashanah: Sovereign Kingship verses, Remembrance verses, and Shofar Blasting verses. Malchuyos so that you shall crown Me over you; Zichronos so that your remembrance shall ascend before Me for good. And with what? With the shofar.*"

(Rosh Hashanah 16a)

And this requires explanation: in what way will the benefit be greater from the verses of *Malchuyos* than from the recitation of other songs and praises?

"*And who is a great nation that has God close to it, etc.*" (Devarim 4:7).

*"As is the custom of the world, a person who has a legal trial wears black garments, etc., because he does not know how his trial will turn out. But Israel is not so; they wear white garments, etc., and they eat, drink, rejoice, and are confident that the Holy One, Blessed be He, will perform a miracle for them."* (Jerusalem Talmud, first chapter of Rosh Hashanah).

And this is difficult: behold, we do not rely on a miracle! And with what are Israel confident regarding this?

Is a trial before the Holy One, Blessed be He, easier than a trial before flesh and blood? And behold, the rationale is simple: for there are two types of inspiration: intellectual inspiration and sensory inspiration. And it is known that sensory inspiration exists in every person. For example, if someone comes into a Jewish home and says that it has been two days since he tasted anything at all, the homeowner will certainly hasten to feed him. And likewise, if he sees that his fellow suffers harsh afflictions and cries out from heartache, he will certainly be inspired and hasten to his salvation. But if he does not see it through a sense, but they only relate such a thing to him, the average person will not be inspired by this, unless it is a conviction whereby through his intellect he pictures the matter as if he were seeing it through a sense.

And with this, I explained that which Rabbi Yochanan ben Zakkai said to his disciples: "*May it be the will that the fear of Heaven be upon you like the fear of flesh and blood.*" For they wondered at this, that you should be inspired by the fear of Heaven, which is an intellectual matter, as it is written (Tehillim 111:10): "*The beginning of wisdom is the fear of Hashem,*" just as you are.

And beforehand, let us speak a little regarding the coldness of spirit that reigns in our midst on the Day of Judgment. For behold, if someone had a trial before a king of flesh and blood, and if it furthermore pertained to capital punishment, he would certainly not be quiet and would not rest from seeking advice and tactics to be vindicated in the judgment. And why is a trial inspired by the fear of flesh and blood, which is a sensory and visible matter? This is a great level that only select individuals achieve. But average people are not inspired except by sensory fear. And for this reason, if we were to see with a sense how they sit in Heaven on thrones for judgment, and the Holy One, Blessed be He, is at their head, for then we would be in dread and fear. But since we do not see this with a sense, there is no inspiration within us, just as we are not inspired by the great Day of Judgment after passing away, when seven judgments pass over a person, as is written in the holy Zohar, Parashas Naso, and these are its words:

"*And in what ways does the Rabbinical Court pass judgment over a person when he departs from this world? One, that upper judgment when the spirit departs from the body. And one, when his deeds and his words go before him and herald and announce concerning him. And one, the judgment when he enters his grave. And one, the judgment of the grave. And one, the judgment of the worms. And one, the judgment of Gehinnom. And one, the judgment of the spirit that goes and wanders in the world and finds no rest until his deeds are completed,*" up to here are its words. And nevertheless, we are not inspired, and everything is for this reason: because we do not see a thing with our senses.

And behold, in order that we should be inspired by the Day of Judgment, I found a tactic, and it is to let the listeners hear a clear depiction of our trial in the Rabbinical Court of Above, and through the sense of hearing, the son of man will also be inspired. And I will open with a parable:

To a mighty king, there were two types of intimates: (A) his relatives, (B) his ministers. And it was in the days of his kingship, he recognized some of his relatives whose love for him was faithful, and who were ready for him through fire and water. And his soul was bound with their soul, and as a sign of love, he gave them four things: (A) he gave them permission to enter into his palace without permission; (B) whenever he made a feast, they were among the heads of the invitees, and he anticipated seeing them, and at the time of the conclusion of the feast he said to them, "*Stay a little longer, for your parting is difficult for me*"; (C) he always praised them and was also regular to say, "*I am a relative of theirs,*" and not that "*they are relatives of mine*"; (D) that he engraved their forms upon the throne of the king.

And behold, a certain minister envied them, and he disguised himself as one of their friends, and began to entice them that upon their entry into the palace of the king, they should take the printing press and make forged bills. And it was as he spoke to them day by day, they were enticed by his words. And the day arrived when they agreed to do their work, and the minister hastened to inform the king of the deed of his friends. The king entered into the inner room and found his friends with a machine in their hand to forge the bills. The king was astonished and they too were terrified before him. And the king understood that they regretted this exceedingly, and the king said to them: "To clear you entirely is impossible, even though I know that my minister enticed you. But this I will do, that I alone will judge you, and I will also adjust the day of the trial to the day of my Coronation." And it was when the designated day for the trial arrived, the army men led the accused to the house of law. And when the king saw through the window that they were walking in great pain, sighing and wailing, he cried out with a voice: "Do not make them like the tramping of my threshold! And let them not see my face." Immediately, one of them was aroused and bitter with weeping and said: "This king, who was always anticipating to see us, today does not want to see our faces!" And they heard a voice calling, "The witnesses have not yet come." And the king said, "Why do you need witnesses? I myself am the witness." Another was aroused to weeping and said, "If the king himself will be the witness, I have no hope." And when they saw that the prosecutor was sitting next to the king, another aroused weeping and said, "If the prosecutor general is here, how shall we stand in judgment?" And they began to argue with one another and said, "Woe, what has happened to us! Are we not even today faithful to our king, and how did we perform this abomination?"

While they were yet speaking, they hurried them to the place of judgment. And it was as they saw the king, they remembered the first days, how the king would always receive them with a pleasant countenance, and now he looks at them with a loathing of soul; they all moved as one in weeping until there was no more strength in them to stand on their feet. The king, seeing that they were filled with regret, signaled to the prosecutor to leave his place. And despite all this, the accused were weeping without cessation. And as they were standing and weeping, behold, suddenly a sound of bells ringing with resonant cymbals, for the honor of the day of dedication, and the chazzans and the singers sang the song of the day, An'im Zemiros, and the sound of the shofar was heard in the city: "*May the king live, existing forever and for all eternity!*"

The accused, who were always accustomed to being among the heads of the invitees, forgot the travail that befell them out of their abundant love even now to the king; their faces brightened from abundant love and joy until they almost broke out into dance. The ministers grew heated over this, but the king understood from this that even now they are faithful in his love, and only the hand of the inciter was in this betrayal. And he thought in his heart that he must forgive them, but in order that they should not continue to do such a thing, the king did this thing, which is: when in the afternoon he made a great feast in honor of the day, the king commanded that the accused be called to the feast.

And it was upon their arrival at the feast, he seated them alone in a narrow room, and upon the table he spread a soiled tablecloth, and upon it pieces of dry bread, speckled. And when they saw their feast, they sat solitary and were silent. And at the conclusion of the feast, they brought before them programmatic notices with sealed envelopes. And it was when they opened the envelopes and read the notes, they saw that there the punishments of each and every one were written: Who by stoning! Who by burning! Who by decapitation! Who by strangulation! Who by fire and who by water! Then they could not restrain themselves, and they wept with a bitter, crying voice, with an awesome voice, and wept until the voice was heard in the house of the king. And the king entered to the sound of their outcries and asked, "Why do you weep?" And when they showed him their notes, he said: "Behold, do you not see that I have not yet signed upon this? You still have time to annul the decree."

And the moral is so: The King is the Holy One, Blessed be He, and He also has two types of intimates: (A) under the name of ministers, they are the supernal ministers; (B) relatives, they are the children of Israel whose souls are carved out from under the Throne of Glory. And when the Holy One, Blessed be He, saw that Israel love Him with all their heart, and with all their soul, and with all their might, He transmitted to them five signs of love and honor: Torah with all your heart, Divine service with all your soul, acts of lovingkindness with all your might. And permission was not given to the ministers to enter inside and to say song on their own without the authorization of the King, until the relatives threefold describe the holiness to the Holy One. And the angels do not say song until they hear the lips of Israel. And they are the masters of the house, the great righteous ones, and they bring inside the sons of the palace.

\(B\) On all the festivals, we are among the heads of the invitees to fulfill, "*And you shall rejoice in your festival... before Hashem your God*" (Devarim 16:14-15). And on Shemini Atzeres, the Holy One, Blessed be He, says: "*Hold yourselves back for Me one more day, for your parting is difficult for me.*" (C) The Holy One, Blessed be He, praises Himself through us always, as it is written (Yeshayahu 49:3): "*Israel, in whom I will be glorified.*" And the Holy One, Blessed be He, says that He is a close neighbor, as our Sages said in Midrash Rabbah, Devarim, chapter 2: "*Which has a nation close—it does not say karov, rather 'Which has God close to it.'*" (D) The form of Yaakov our father, peace be upon him, is engraved upon the Throne of Glory, as it is written (Yeshayahu 49:16): "*Behold, upon the palms of My hands I have engraved you.*" And they said (Taanis 4a): "*A matter that is visible to the eyes.*"

And through these signs, we became bound with the Holy One, Blessed be He, with an intense love, until they entered through fire and water for the sanctification of the Divine Name. And behold, the Satan incited within us and enticed us to perform foolish acts of high-mindedness, which is to perform transgressions for the sake of a mitzvah. And at that moment a person performs a transgression, it ascends and prosecutes before the Holy One, Blessed be He. And the Holy One, Blessed be He, seeing that his words are correct, becomes filled with wrath against them, and says as mentioned above. But seeing our regret—for behold, even the wicked are filled with regrets—to clear them entirely is impossible, only this He did: that He alone will judge us, and the day of trial will be on the day that we prepare to celebrate the festival of coronation, which is Rosh Hashanah.

In *Malchuyos*, this is the *tekiah* of the Torah which Hashem God drove as a faithful stake in our heart. *Shevarim*, this is Divine service, because "*a broken and contrite heart, O God, You will not despise*" (Tehillim 51:19). *Teruah*, this is acts of lovingkindness and "*love your fellow as yourself*" (Vayikra 19:18). And when the day of trial arrives and we gather to request for our soul, behold, a Heavenly voice goes out through the prophet: "*When you come to appear before My face, who requested this from your hand, to trample My courts?*" (Yeshayahu 1:12).

And this aroused Rosh Hashanah read as Rabbi Yochanan to weeping, as it is found (Chagigah 4b) that Rabbi Yochanan was weeping when he came to read the verse: "*He shall appear.*" And upon their hearing the voice from the prophet: "*And I will be a swift witness, etc.*" (Malachi 3:5), woe to us from the day of judgment! And also over this, Rabbi Yochanan wept, that the Attribute of Judgment prosecutes against Israel. And Israel, with all this in their hands, regret exceedingly and say, "*Woe, what has happened to us! Are we not faithful servants to Hashem? How many arrows have they shot into us, of us they killed, of us they slaughtered, and despite all this, the banner of the Torah we did not abandon from our hands, even though we listened to the voice of the inciter.*"

And with this thought, they burst into a *Teruah* which is an allusion to a groaning groan and a wailing wail. And the Holy One, Blessed be He, seeing the *Teruah* of Israel, removes the Attribute of Judgment, as it is written: "*God ascends with a Teruah, Hashem with the sound of a shofar*" (Tehillim 47:6)—the explanation is that it was removed from the house of law because of the *Teruah*.

And behold, when we are so grieved from the terror of the Day of Judgment, behold, suddenly a sound of *Tekiah* is heard, upon which is the appearance of the *Tekiah* of the giving of the Torah, a day of coronation to the King of kings of kings from the Holy One, Blessed be He. And then Israel forget their pain and sing with a song of joy: "*Is Ephraim a precious son to Me, or a child of delights?*"

Therefore, it is good to declare and to remind throughout the entire world that He is the Supreme King. Until the Holy One, Blessed be He, who examines and checks the hidden treasures, knows that we are faithful even today in His love, and only a miscalculation was in this betrayal, and the cause of the times is for our sake. Therefore, the Holy One, Blessed be He, desired to forgive us, because repentance always avails. Only in order that we should not continue to defile His Name, the Holy One, Blessed be He, does so:

And that is when the Holy One, Blessed be He, makes a feast for the entire world on this day, and as our Sages said (Beitzah 16a): "*The sustenance of a person is allotted to him from Rosh Chodesh until Yom Kippur.*" For it is a decree for Israel, and a decree that resembles death. We too are among the invitees, only that which we sat formerly in ancient years upon the table of the King, and upon it was from all good, and now He has seated us in His cellar, darkness in narrow rooms, and upon our table is dry bread—from this we understand that the Holy One, Blessed be He, is angry with us, and because of this, our sustenance was diminished. And also delicacies they bring before Him for the dessert of the feast. My masters and my teachers, do you know what they are? It is the prayer of *Unesanneh Tokef*!

We hear them distributing notes to each one: Who to life, who to death, who by fire and who by water, who by stoning and who by strangulation. And upon our seeing these notes, our heart leaves us from the fear of weeping without cessation with a bitter voice. And at the time of the recitation of the aforementioned prayer, the doorposts move from our voices. But the voice of Hashem from on high roars, *Here* in truth, and there is still that with which to be comforted. For behold, there it is said: on Rosh Hashanah it is written, and on the Day of the Fast of Kippur it is sealed. If so, his final decree is not yet sealed, therefore we can still turn back the decree by means of repentance, prayer, and charity, which remove the evil of the decree.

And with this, the verse and the aforementioned Yalkut will explain: "*Blow the shofar in the Chodesh*"—renew your deeds; "*Shofar*"—improve your deeds, for the sake of these two things: (A) "*For it is a statute for Israel,*" and it is upon us to request of Hashem that He make us a feast upon His table as before; (B) "*A judgment for the God of Yaakov,*" through repentance our judgment will emerge like righteousness. And that which it said beforehand "*Israel*" and afterward "*Yaakov*"—I saw that the Holy One, Blessed be He, is abundant in kindness, therefore when giving us sustenance, He considers us as important people whose sustenance is according to the custom of the wealthy in the states of Israel; but when He executes judgment, He considers us under the name "*Yaakov*," as simple people who are punished more leniently.

My masters and my teachers! Behold, to weep on Rosh Hashanah is a great question among the Halachic Deciders, and ostensibly there is an explicit proof (Nehemiah 8:10) that when the people were weeping, he said to them: "*Go, eat fat foods and drink sweet drinks... for the joy of Hashem is your stronghold.*" Nevertheless, it can be said that the Holy One, Blessed be He, will not reckon it to us as a sin if we weep today, for there is a great difference between these years and the years of Nehemiah.

And I will bring a parable: In a certain city, a stone house was built for a prison house. And it was when the house was completed, they sent from the city council tickets to the ministers of the king, and also to the wealthy of the city, and they were invited to the dedication of the house. And it was upon their arrival close to the new house, they saw and behold, tables were arranged with drinks and delicacies, and a band of singers sang songs in honor of the day. The assembled cheered their hearts with wine until the governor of the province arrived. And when the governor arrived, they all went to the old prison house to call the prisoners and to lead them in the procession of the *Tveika* \[the transfer march\] into the new prison house. And all of them rejoiced that they merited to be among the invitees.

But the prisoners walked with a broken spirit, their faces buried in the ground, and most of them wept. A young son asked his father, "Why are all the assembled rejoicing, while you, who are dressed in white garments and a green discharge behind them, are angry and weeping?" And his father answered him and said: "My precious son! What are you saying? Behold, they are the prisoners for whom they built the prison house, there it is darkness and the shadow of death, and there they have bitter beds! Look and see that they walk in collars, their hands are bound, and their feet are presented in brass chains. They hoped that when the old prison house grew weathered, then they would go out to freedom, and now they see their tragedy! For they built for them a large stone house to imprison them anew—how shall they not weep?"

So, my masters and my teachers, it is true that this day is the day of the dedication of the house of the world, as we say in the liturgical poem: "*Today is the birthday of the world.*" And for this reason, in the days of Nehemiah, when they repaired the wall of Jerusalem, and Israel were ready to go out from the joy to the Land of Israel, therefore it was the obligation to rejoice in honor of the dedication of the house and the day of coronation together. But in these days, when the world has become for us a prison house in the land of Russia, Sarako, and furthermore we are shut as if under a lock in a narrow room! Our hands are bound... our feet are presented in brass chains... and we hoped that for the next year we would go out to freedom and return to our land, but in vain! Furthermore, we must sit in custody, and not enough for us is the old prison house, but rather they prepared for us a new prison house, in which also our souls and our flesh are imprisoned.

Where are our children? Where is the holy faith upon which our spiritual life depends? Our children are afraid in all the lands, in the paths of prisoners they walk, and upon the walls only their photographs are hung like the monuments of a cemetery. Alas! The curse was fulfilled within us: "*Sons and daughters you shall beget, but they shall not be yours*" (Devarim 28:41)—the explanation is: the pain of raising children you will have, but to receive spiritual satisfaction from them, that you will not—because they will walk in a narrow path of the internal and external oppressor, and there they will do what their heart desires, until we will have from them disgrace in this world and in the World to Come.

And how shall we not weep? How can we restrain ourselves from bringing down tears by the third? On the contrary! It is upon us to weep without cessation and to supplicate before Hashem that He take us out from the bitter exile and bring us to our land. And when we perform the dedication of the house upon our Holy Temple, then we will exult and rejoice. And may it be the will that this shall be speedily in our days, Amen Sela.
