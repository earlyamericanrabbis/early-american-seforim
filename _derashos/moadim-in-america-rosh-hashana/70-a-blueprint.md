Yesterday we analyzed a problem, and suggested some pictures with which to describe the religion of tomorrow that we would like to see come true. Today, we would like to go a step further and outline in specific detail, a blueprint which should serve for an effective, inspiring, elevating religion of tomorrow.

The three steps suggested for attainment of this are actually the same as are suggested by Rabbi Simon in the Mishna in describing the three foundations of Jewish life; the study of Torah, kindness, worship, and the practice of charitable deeds. In a long and allegorical story, the Rabbis of the Mishna, commenting on this morning’s Biblical portion, tell us that when leading Isaac to the Akedah, Satan appeared unto hm dressed as an old man and asked him where he was going. The Patriarch responded that he was going to pray to God; then the evil one re-appeared, this time masquerading as a young man and inquired of Isaac where he was going. Isaac’s response was that he was going to learn Torah. And finally he came before Sarah and in teasing fashion asked her if she knew where her husband and only son were going, and her answer was, “To do the will of God.”

Whenever we are on the threshold of historic changes or problems assail us, and we want to turn to a sure, secure and confident source of advice, whenever anything of great import occurs, these three fundamental lessons confront us directly to teach us that within them resides the answers to all our queries and the solution to all our problems.

Abraham did not philosophize much about the purpose of his mission. Facing a great challenge and confronted with an extraordinary situation, he knew that the times called for prayer. Is there anything that the world needs more today than prayer and the power and value of prayer? This is surely no time to question philosophically and inquire quizzically as to the nature of prayer, how effective is it? Will we really expect that our prayers will change the Will of God?... and other shibbeloths that were served up by an unprayerful era. For we have passed the time when Man hypnotized with his success, progress, and achievements, though of himself as a self-made man who worshipped his maker. There has been a drastic change in the spiritual habits of our generation…our security of yesterday has turned into the insecurity, the anxiety, the anguish and sheer terror of atomic tomorrow.

Our political forces seem merely to desire survival for Man has become to feel small again, and when Man feels small, he knows that God is great. The mood of our prayers should not be that of a child whose supplications express themselves in the words “Give me, O Lord,”… but rather in the more mature, more sanctified, “Help me, O God.” Help me utilize the progress of Technology, the achievements of science, the advancement of politics and the march of economics to bring more happiness into the world.

The prayer that the world needs today and which the religion of tomorrow should emphasize, is not so much one of philosophic speculation, nor of intellectual groping, but one which displays a deepened spiritual content, and which would in turn spiritualize all of our endeavours. This is no age where verbal men and caustic minds will want to seek out the nature of God, but rather the times call for expressing our query in the words of the ancient Prophet, “What doth the Lord require of me?”

Tomorrow’s religion should feature the prayers that concern themselves not alone with our needs and wants, but with us, with ourselves, with what we want to do with our lives and what we are needed for.

There were times, indeed, in the history of mankind, when study preceded prayer, and with our Jewish people, study in fact is a mode of worship. But in line with the Midrashic story, a new approach and a new strengthening of prayers is the first requirement of the age, which is closely tied up and should be followed by the study of Torah, as Isaac answered. Not alone because learning Torah will give meaning to our lives and substance to our striving, but also because this eternal font, this everlasting sources of wisdom has the answers which the world seeks and has the “knowhow” to cure our ills and help our sorely distressed beings.

Much has been said, but never enough about long periods in Jewish history when without there was terror, anguish, pain, persecution and sorrow… but within the forcibly enclosed confines of the Jewish world, there was calmness, serenity and rocklike faith, because of the strength that grows from prayer and philosophic reassurance inherent in the study of Torah.

Last, but not least, the answer of the Matriarch will complete this Golden Triad for doing the will of God implies doing good deeds, and compels imitating His attributes and assures the perfectibility of Man and Godlike actions.

The one unavoidable and irreplaceable prerequisites for the successful attainment of this three-pronged approach to Tomorrow’s religion is a basic re-allignment in our thinking, away from the world and facing towards God. In this fashion, we will be enabled to repair the broken lines of communication between ourselves and our Maker, which have suffered extensively during periods of smug, self-satisfied materialism. It will help deepen our consciousness and widen our awareness of the tremendous holding power of learning, studying, and living God’s Torah, and is a road of radiant illumination when properly evaluated.

Finally, it will make man the higher and more Godlike being, by practicing those deeds which make him resemble the image of his Creator, and in that patter and for such purposes Man was created.

All these three emphatic attributes of Tomorrow’s Religion require a certain selflessness and indicate a need of servicing the Lord with joy, with no direct selfish motive in view. This thought has been so beautifully expressed once again by the wise men of the Midrash, who told us that the term Lech Lecho, occurs twice in Genesis in two succeeding weekly portions, one of them we read today. They inquire quizzically which of the two is greater:

<span dir="rtl">א"ר לוי שני פעמים כתיב לך לך אין יודעין אזו חביבה אם הראשונה או שנייה מן מה דכתיב אל ארץ המוריה הוי שנייה חביבה מן הראשונה</span>

“The second one”, they tell us, because that is free of selfish connotations. The First Lech Lecho, as Rashi comments, was for Abraham’s benefit, for the advancement and the enlargement of his name, and for the improvement of his family status. The second one includes Moriah, which has many meanings. It means service and reverence of the Lord; it means learning and teaching the Law, it means having mercy and compassion for a fellow human being. It is for all of these that in the summation of our sages, the second one is so much higher and holier.

The Chassidim are fond of telling a story about one of our greatest saints, who implored God that Redemption might come to his people. He continued in this stubborn prayer, after he was told that this was not the time and he should cease his supplications, but he persisted stubbornly until a Baskol was heard saying that because he insisted in his plea, though warned not to continue, he would forfeit his share in the world to come. Thereupon, the Tsadick exclaimed with joy “Now I can truly serve the Lord for His sake and His sake only. Now that even Olem Habo has been forfeited I can’t even be accused of serving the Almighty for these purposes. Now I can sevre for His Name only, and only for His Name’s sake.”

This spirit, this devotion, tomorrow’s religion will need to have, if it is to bring to us, to a sorely tried world, the peace, the tranquility, the joy and the hope for which we pray and which agitates our minds and hearts on this day of Rosh Hashonah.
