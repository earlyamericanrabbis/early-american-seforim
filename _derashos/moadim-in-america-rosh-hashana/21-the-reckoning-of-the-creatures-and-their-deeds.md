“*You are standing today, all of you, before the Lord your God: your heads, your tribes, your elders, and your officers, every man of Israel. Your little ones, your wives, and your stranger who is in the midst of your camp, from the hewer of your wood to the drawer of your water, etc.*” (Devarim 29:9-10)

“*Rabbi Elazar opened: (Job 2:1) "And it was the day, and the sons of God came to stand before the Lord, and the Satan also came among them." "And it was the day"—this is Rosh Hashanah, for the Holy One, Blessed be He, stands to judge the world, etc.*” (Zohar HaKadosh Shemos 202)

The commentators have already noted that here too the verse refers to Rosh Hashanah, for seemingly it should be asked: what matters concerning the subject of Rosh Hashanah are found here, that they expounded: "*You are standing today,*" etc.—this is Rosh Hashanah? And it is further difficult: since it said "*all of you,*" why did it afterward specify in detail, "*your heads, your tribes,*" etc.[^1]

However, it can be said the idea in this is that in these words themselves, where the verse stated a general rule and a detail: "*all of you, etc., your heads, your tribes,*" etc., it is hinted that this day is when the Holy One, Blessed be He, stands to judge the world—this is Rosh Hashanah! For in general, it is from the love of the Holy One, Blessed be He, for His people Israel, that on this day the mouth of the Satan and the accuser shall be closed.

Therefore, the matter is from the beginning in a general manner, that is to say, that the Holy One, Blessed be He, does not judge each and every person according to his individual deeds, at a time when his standing is poor and empty, but rather through the unifying together of all Israel; then no accuser has the ability to accuse anymore, for truly, who is like Your people Israel, one nation in the land!

As the view of our Sages of blessed memory indicates regarding this, in their wonderful saying[^2]: "*On Rosh Hashanah all inhabitants of the world pass before Him like Bnei Maron. What is Bnei Maron? Here \[in Babylonia\] they translated like young sheep. Resh Lakish said: Like the ascents of Beit Maron. Rabbi Yehuda said in the name of Shmuel: Like the legions of the House of David. Rabbah bar bar Chana said in the name of Rabbi Yochanan: And all of them are examined in a single glance. Rav Nachman bar Yitzchak said: We also learned it elsewhere: 'He who fashions their hearts together, who understands all their deeds*[^3]*.*'"

For seemingly it is a contradiction between that which Rabbah bar bar Chana said in the name of Rabbi Yochanan: "*And all of them are examined in a single glance*," to the words of the Mishnah, what it said: "*On Rosh Hashanah all inhabitants of the world pass before Him like Bnei Maron*[^4]"?

Rather, the intention is according to what is brought in the books, that therefore the Men of the Great Assembly established the version of the *Shemoneh Esreh* prayer in the plural language, that is: "*Heal us*," "*Bless upon us,*" and the like, and not in the singular language, because when a person stands to pray before the Holy One, Blessed be He, only for himself, then he shall arouse an accusation against himself for there is no righteous man on earth who does good and does not sin.

However, when he stands to pray in the plural language, that is, for the collective of Israel, then he will not arouse any accusation against himself, for Israel as a whole, their deeds are pleasant; therefore, in their merit, he too is able to pray.

And this is what Rabbah bar bar Chana said in the name of Rabbi Yochanan: "*And all of them are scanned in a single scan," that is to say, when each and every one would be judged on Rosh Hashanah "like Bnei Maron,*" according to his individual deeds, who could stand before Him and be vindicated in judgment? However, that "*all of them are examined in a single glance,*" meaning when the Holy One, Blessed be He, looks upon the deeds of Israel as a whole, then who is like Your people Israel? And as a whole they are good and beautiful, as it is written[^5]: "*You are all beautiful, my beloved, and there is no blemish in you.*" Therefore they are able to stand in judgment before Him, which, similar to this, is also explained in the commentators, regarding that which is proven in the words of our Sages of blessed memory[^6]: "*Rabbi Yonatan raised a contradiction: It is written*[^7]*: 'And the number of the children of Israel shall be like the sand of the sea,' and it is written*[^8]*: 'which cannot be measured*'" *"...and shall not be counted*"—it is not a difficulty: here is a time when Israel do the will of the Omnipresent; here is a time when they do not do the will of the Omnipresent.

For seemingly it should be said: how is it possible to say, that at a time when they do the will of the Omnipresent it "*shall not be measured and shall not be counted*"? And behold, at all times it could be that they would be doing the will of the Omnipresent, and if so, how is it possible that in one moment they would multiply so much?

Rather, the intention is: "*if there shall be a number of the children of Israel,*" meaning that if we calculate each and every one by himself, in the manner of the order in counting where everything has a number alone, then they are called: "*not doing the will of the Omnipresent,*" for regarding each and every one alone according to his private deeds, his deficiency can be seen, and who shall say "*I have purified my heart*"?

But if it "*shall not be measured and shall not be counted,*" meaning that if there is no number for each and every one alone, and He sees the deeds of Israel in general, then through this they are called: "*doing the will of the Omnipresent,*" for in general they are good, and there is no blemish in them[^9].

However, the Holy One, Blessed be He, also feared, that at this time the mind of a person would be at ease so that he would turn away from the path entirely, after it would be in his power to mix with the collective: that "*all of them are scanned in a single scan;*" if so, for what reason shall he not do all that his heart desires? Therefore the Holy Torah warned also regarding the individual: "*your heads, your tribes,*" etc., meaning, let him be who he may be, the responsibility is upon him that he shall not do all that his heart desires, at the expense of his fellow[^10].

And now it comes out well, that which they expounded at the opening of our words: "*You are standing today, all of you,*" etc., "*Today*": this is Rosh Hashanah, for the Holy One, Blessed be He, stands to judge the world, etc. Since in the general and the detail of the verse, matters from the day of judgment are embodied in it, and understand this.

According to what was stated above, it can also be said that the idea in this is that which the Mishnah said "*like Bnei Maron,*" and it is according to what Rashi explained there: *Maron*, an expression of "*authority and lordship,*" that is to say, like dominion.

That is to say, even though it is the Day of Judgment, for it is the way of the world that on the day of judgment of the nations of the world, they are saddened and melancholy, and the world almost darkens for them.

But not so on the day of judgment of the King, Kings of Kings: all inhabitants of the world pass before Him like *Bnei Maron*! Meaning, before Him they are able to come in judgment like the children of princes and nobles of the land, with joy of the heart, for "*the Rock, His work is perfect and all His ways are justice,*" and we are certain that He will not do injustice in their judgment, and understand this.
