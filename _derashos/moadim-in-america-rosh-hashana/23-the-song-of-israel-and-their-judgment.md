*“The ministering angels said before the Holy One, Blessed be He: Master of the Universe! For what reason do Israel not say Song before You on Rosh Hashanah and on Yom Kippur? He said to them: Is it possible \[that\] a King sits on the seat of judgment, and books of life and books of death are open before Him, and Israel say Song?”*

(Rosh Hashanah 32b)

More than one of the commentators turned for himself a way to illuminate this Aggadic saying; see in their words what they say. But I said to myself, I too shall answer my portion in this. For the intention of the ministering angels is to ask: behold in every festival it is a day of judgment, as we say: (ibid. 16a) "*At four times the world is judged: on Pesach for the grain, on Shavuos for the fruits of the tree, on Sukkos they are judged for the water,*" etc. Even so, they say Song on them, and if so, for what reason do Israel not say Song on Rosh Hashanah and Yom Kippur? . . .

However, it can be said that the idea in this is according to what is explained in the books, that therefore on the other festivals they say Song, because a man knows in his soul, however much he may have done evil in his deeds, and he returns in his spirit to the Omnipresent . . . even so he is certain that the world will be judged in general to merit, because of the righteousness of his fellow, or because of the righteousness of the righteous in the generation, and upon the merit of the collective, why shall he not say Song?

Whereas on Rosh Hashanah and Yom Kippur, for all inhabitants of the world shall pass before Him like *Bnei Maron*, as they said (ibid. 18a): "*What is Bnei Maron? In Babylonia, they translated it like young sheep; Resh Lakish said: Like the ascents of Beit Maron; Rabbi Yehuda said in the name of Shmuel: Like the legions of the House of David,*" etc. That is to say, that each one is judged individually, as Rashi explained there: "*They count them and they go out one after the other.*" If so, there is no possibility that he should rely on the righteousness of his fellow . . . for the righteousness of the righteous shall be upon him . . . and the wickedness of the wicked shall be upon him . . .

As we say (ibid. 16b): "*Three books are opened on Rosh Hashanah: one of the completely wicked, and one of the completely righteous, and one of the intermediate,*" etc. If so, he has no benefit in the righteousness of his fellow in the individual judgment, for even if his fellow shall be written in the book of the completely righteous, even so he himself could be written in the book of the completely wicked, and if so, how can he say Song?

And this is what the Holy One, Blessed be He, said to them: "*Is it possible that a King sits on the seat of judgment, and books of life and books of death are open before Him,*" etc. Meaning that each and every one is judged by himself: this one shall be written in the book of the completely righteous for life, and the other could be written in the book of the completely wicked . . . if so, how shall they say Song?

Whereas on the other festivals, although they also are days of judgment, even so— ...They say Song on them, because the world is judged then in its entirety, and not in detail . . . why shall a man not say Song? . . . and understand this.

According to what was stated above, the commentators have already noted here wondrously: regarding what they said, "*and 'books of life and books of death'*" etc.—the dead, what are they seeking here, to stand in judgment on Rosh Hashanah regarding what they did in their lives? Behold, they already received the judgment of Heaven in the first year, as the Sages say, of blessed memory: (Eduyot, Ch. 2) "*The judgment of the wicked in Gehenna is twelve months.*" And if it is regarding the dead who died in this year, behold in all twelve months He judges them every day, except for Rosh Hashanah?

Although it can be said that this refers to the Sages' saying, of blessed memory: (Rosh Hashanah 16b) "*Three books are opened on Rosh Hashanah, one of the completely wicked, and one of the completely righteous, and one of the intermediate . . . the completely righteous are written and sealed immediately for life, the completely wicked are written and sealed immediately for death*" etc. That is to say, that those of the completely wicked were called "*books of death,*" because they are written there for death.

However, according to Rashi's explanation there, "*Three books*": "*books of remembrance of the deeds of the creatures.*" That is to say, that they are not books of the verdict, but rather books of remembrance of the deeds of the creatures, that after the inspection they shall be divided into three groups, etc. Regarding how to sustain them for the World to Come, as the explanation of the Tosafos there: "*And all this that is called here regarding the wicked 'death,' and regarding the righteous 'life,' meaning for the life of the World to Come.*" If so, the dead—what are they seeking here? . . .

However, it can be said the idea in this is according to what is explained in our holy literature: behold, at the time when a man is judged in the Heavenly Court, the Holy One, Blessed be He, demands an account for all the souls that perished in our sins, may the Merciful One preserve us. Like the children who are dying for the sin of their fathers, and likewise the righteous who are seized for the sin of the generation. Regarding all these, the Holy One, Blessed be He, demands judgment and an account from the living.

As the view of our Sages of blessed memory indicates also regarding this, in their wonderful saying: (Avos 4:29) "*Those who are born are destined to die, and the dead are destined to be brought to life*" etc. That is to say, that there is— ...Several people who die, may the Merciful One preserve us, in order to bring life to others. Like the children dying for the sin of their fathers, and likewise the righteous who are seized for the sin of their generation.[^1]

Therefore, "*and the living shall take it to heart,*" that is to say, the living need to take it to heart regarding this, that people died for their sins, and to say: "*Our hands have not spilled this blood, atone, please, for Your people*" . . .

And this is what he said: "*and books of life and books of death are open before Him,*" etc. Meaning that the living are judged regarding those written in the books of death, who died, may the Merciful One preserve us, for their sins; if so, how shall they say Song? . . . and understand this.

[^1]: See also in my book "Or HaMidot", The Goal of Man and His Responsibility.
