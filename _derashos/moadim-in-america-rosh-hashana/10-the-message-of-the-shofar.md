Sermon for the first day of ROSH-HASHONOH 1951 — (5712)

Esteemed Congregation:

People well versed in the teachings of our holy religion know that we are requested to fulfill the commandments of the Lord intelligently. Our sages say, however, "*By all means let a man engage in the study of the Thorah and in good deeds, even when lacking the proper conception of the purpose of the Mitzvah, for through the practice and the repeated exercise he will arrive at the stage of doing the good and noble deeds with the full perception.*"[^1] This idea, however, is applicable only when the inadequate intention or understanding of the will of the Lord does not abrogate and annul the purpose of the Mitzvah, as in the case of charity and social activities, where the work in itself suffices to achieve the desired end; the needy are helped, the synagogues, the thalmud thorahs, the hospitals and all the other fine and very essential institutions are maintained, even when the donors give their contributions out of selfish motives, like the glorification of their names or similar reasons. It is surely not so when the physical part of the Mitzvah, when the whole action shall serve only to awaken within us a fine and noble idea, the proper intention which is the Mitzvah itself, like the reading of the SHEMAH ISRAEL, or like the BLOWING OF THE SHOFAR which we are about to fulfill, because these Mitzvoth are to remind us that there is but one omnipotent God and also of His sovereignty as the creator of the entire world; Mitzvoth of that kind request the concentration of the mind without fail, and for the fulfillment of such Mitzvoth thinking is peremptory and indispensable.

Let us, therefore, my dear friends, at this moment of spiritual elevation, try to understand the MESSAGE OF THE SHOFAR to the very best of our ability.

The first of the seven sentences which we are about to recite in responsive reading, prior to the blowing of the Shofar, indicates emphatically that this ceremony must be performed with concentration of mind, must come from within our heart, from within our soul.

"*In my distress I called upon the Lord; The Lord answered me by setting me free.*"[^2]

Yes, my friends, this sentence is an earnest reminder that lip services remain ineffective and do not procure the desired results.

The initials of the first words of the following six sentences compose the words <span dir="rtl">קרע שטן</span> which mean that we pray God that He in His great mercy shall free us from the power of Satan, the symbol of all evil inclinations and to disprove and reject all the accusations and indictments against us. We read[^3]:

> "*Thou dost hear my cry;*
>
> *Do give ear to my plea.*
>
> *"Thy chief word is truth;*
>
> *All Thy just commands are forever.*
>
> *"Be Thou my gracious protection;*
>
> *Let not the arrogant oppress me.*
>
> *"I delight in Thy promise,*
>
> *Like one who attains great wealth.*
>
> *"Teach me good judgment and knowledge,*
>
> *For I believe in Thy precepts.*
>
> *"Accept my offerings, O Lord,*
>
> *And teach me Thy laws.*"

On which day, my dear friends, is it more befitting than on this day of ROSH-HASHONOH, the day when the Lord sits in judgment upon all the world and decrees upon the destiny of all flesh, on which day more than on this shall we pray to our Heavenly Father that He shall accept graciously all our supplications? On what day more than on this shall we pray for purifying the mind and heart from all profane thoughts and evil inclinations? On which day more than on this shall we pray for a perfect perception of the Thorah and the Mitzvoth?

My friends, the sentences we just quoted shall remind us to pray intelligently and with devotion for all these great and noble things. These sentences shall create within us the appropriate inspiration of the Thekiath-Shofar, the blowing of the Shofar. And now, my friends, in conclusion let me explain to you briefly The Message of the Shofar itself.

According to the great religio-philosopher and codifier of the Jewish law, Rabbeinu Moses Maimonides, the blowing of the Shofar serves primarily as a very ernest call for mankind, saying:

"*You who are asleep, wake up!*

*You who are in trance, arise!*

*Search your doings and repent;*

*Remember your creator!*

*You forget constant truth,*

*And indulge all year in trifles,*

*Which cannot profit or save,*

*Look rightly into your souls!*

*Amend your ways and your deeds;*

*Let each one of you give up*

*His evil course and purpose!* "[^4]

According to Rabbeinu Saadya Gaon, of the tenth century, the blowing of the Shofar is based upon ten reasons:

1\) Rosh Hashonoh is the anniversary of creation, and the THEKIAH of the Shofar proclaims the sovereignty of the Lord its creator.

2\) On Rosh Hashonoh, the first day of the ten days of THESHUVAH, of repentance, the Shofar warns and stirs men to amend their life.

3\) The Shofar reminds us of the revelation on Mt. Sinai, when the trumpet blast grew louder and louder "*and the people said, 'We will do and obey.*'"

4\) The Shofar brings to mind the warnings and exhortations of the prophets.

5\) The Shofar reminds us of the battle-alarm in Judeah during the destruction of the Temple, this by the Shevarim and Theruah, the sounds of the sigh and grief.

6\) The Shofar reminds us of the AKEIDAH, the attempted sacrifice of Isaac.

7\) The Shofar shall inspire the heart with awe and reverence.

8\) The Shofar shall remind us, through the Shevarim and Theruah, of the day of judgment.

9\) The Shofar reminds and inspires us with hope for the restoration of Israel, as it is said: "*And it shall come to pass on that day a great Shofar shall be sounded.*"[^5]

10\) The Shofar shall remind us of the resurrection which will take place at the time of the Messiah.

Endowed and provided with all these noble ideas and hopes and intentions, we can now solemnize the blowing of the Shofar; and may our Heavenly Father grant us His blessings for a very happy and prosperous New Year.

Amen
