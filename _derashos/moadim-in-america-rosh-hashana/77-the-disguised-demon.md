Preached on Rosh Hashanah, 5713

When the Almighty first created man, He elected him as His co-worker in the universe. Little lower than angels, man was considered the high point of Creation.

He reigned supreme over the beast in the field and over the fowl in the sky. Man was given freedom to roam at will and nothing, save the Tree of Life and the Tree of Knowledge, was forbidden him.

Man, however, faltered; he threw discretion to the winds and transgressed the will of his Creator by partaking of the forbidden fruit. Summarily, punishment on punishment was heaped on him. God exiled him from Paradise. The animal in the field no longer was subservient to his domination, and his bread, he now earned, by the sweat of his brow.

Man’s waywardness which led to his ultimate downfall is classed as the Original Sin. Now scholars from time immemorial have sought the reason for man’s gross ineptitude. Their explanations have varied from the plausible to the fantastic. Yet, a casual examination of the text will readily reveal the true reason for man’s backsliding.

The serpent, the Bible tells us, was envious of man’s claim to superiority. According to our sages, the serpent, at the time of creation, possessed both arms and legs, he walked upright and was blessed with the power of speech. His height was that of a camel but, above all else, “*vehanachash hayah orum*-he was shrewd and cunning.” He knew when to strike, when to capitalize on man’s weakness. In his quest to dethrone man, he figured that Eve would be easier to conquer.

“Eat from the Tree, Eve,” he craftily urged, “and you shall be like the Almighty.” He came, supposedly, as a friend and confidante. All he seemingly wanted was to elevate man. “Just was He is omniscient, so you too shall be omniscient.”

Knowing that the Tree had been forbidden them, Eve went as far as to say that they weren’t even permitted to touch the Tree, let alone share of its fruit. It was then that the serpent snatched at the opportunity to drive a wedge. He brushed her against the Tree. “See,”
he said, “you touched the Tree and you did not die.” Determined to exploit his advantage, he shook the Tree, and partook of the fallen fruit. “Now,” he continued, “just as I did not die so you too will not die.”

Unable to withstand the gnawing temptation, she cried out, “My husband Adam lied!” She ate of the fruit and afterwards enticed her husband to follow her example. She even fed the animals of the field the forbidden fruit, making them all subject to the sting of death.

The “Original Sin,” was the result of a devilish snake in the guise of an angel of mercy. And when we examine the course of human transgression, in most instances, we discover, that the pattern follows the “Original Sin.” It is not usually a case of premeditation but rather the handiwork of some devil who appears camouflaged as an angel of mercy.

Consider why, this, our world is so bitterly divided. At no time was it in greater danger of destruction than it is now by push-button war. Is it not because the devil of communism comes arrayed as an angel? It exploits man’s weakness, enticing him with hollow dreams and unfounded hopes. Some of our most intelligent American citizens are victimized by it colorful disguise.

For some time, I have sought to understand the reason for man’s nervous state of existence. Despite our manifold appurtenances, we are still unhappy. Man has exceeded the speed of sound, his inventions are myriad and magical and, yet, he suffers a gnawing sense of inadequacy. He feels hopelessly tormented. His greatest quest is peace of mind. Undoubtedly, there are many reasons for man’s undoing but towering above many is one-the devil who comes garbed as an angel.

Who can deny that most of us live for others and not ourselves? Instead of turning our gaze inward, we prefer to turn it outward. We live to impress others. As a result we undertake a vicious cycle of activity and effort to outdistance our neighbors. We drive and we drive until our bodies crumple and our minds shrivel. Everything becomes a legitimate effort as long as it brings the desired results.

We modern exhibitionists recall to mind Alphonse Daudet’s, “The Man With The Golden Brain.” This masterpiece is concerned with a boy who, instead of having a human brain, was born with a brain of gold. He discovers this abnormality during an accident when he hits his head against a hard object and fins that instead of blood, liquid gold flows from the wound. The story pictures him near the end of his life gouging into the recesses of his skull to fling the last melted nuggets of his brain for the objects of his pursuit.

We, too, have gold on the brain. In our quest to gain the material advantages of life, we literally cast our lives away to the nether-winds. Alas, the devil of envy knows how to bait his trap; his matchless guise is difficult to detect.

When pondering our subject, one cannot disregard the realm of faith. Often we hear people rationalize about their religious feelings. They excuse their inadequacy with the curt offering that what counts is how you treat your fellow man. They point out to those who pray thrice daily, who observe the Sabbath and the Holidays but who have no scruples about deceiving their fellow-man in business. In their estimation, all one has to possess is a good heart; one must be charitable and considerate.

If this isn’t the devil parading as an angel, what is? Who ever contended that Judaism is fragmentizing faith? Did Judaism ever postulate that sanctifying the Sabbath is more important that doing righteousness? He who has studied his Judaism knows full well that bereft of a sense of justice and righteousness, ritual piety becomes a lame mockery. Both are indispensable for Jewish survival.

The Ten Commandments embrace both relationships-that of man with God and that of man with man. Flattery, hypocrisy, slander, and deception are as contrary to the tenets of Judaism as failure to serve God adequately. Cardiac Jews, who are concerned solely with human relations, are in reality devils posing as angels. The truly pious man is an all embracing personality. His concern is not limited to one phase of human experience. Starting with the first Jew, Abraham, and examining the lives of those who were genuinely pious, you will find no instance where the man-to-man relationship was depressed and demeaned. On the contrary, these men of God were known for their sense of unstinting compassion and generosity.

When exploring the subject of the devil-in-disguise, one cannot disregard the institution of the Traditional Synagogue.

It has become vogue to streamline the service and rearrange the ritual. To beautify the services, if the effort does not veer away from the directed sequence, deserves our commendation and praise. However, when we are told that the sole salvation of the Orthodox Synagogue is contingent on basic changes, even though the supposed soothsayer may be sincere in his intentions, we say, beware! Unwittingly you are being taken in by the devil who poses as an angel.

We have had too many situations where this well-meaning effort was climaxed in abandonment of everything basic and genuine. A Traditional Synagogue means only one thing-a Traditional Synagogue. If there are some who seek innovations, there is ample room for expansion elsewhere.

I would like to cite the difference between the Catholic and the Protestant church. The Catholic church is still one united entity, whereas the Protestant church can cite more than 260 denominations within its orbit.

Like atoms which split, sop it is with traditions once you start demeaning them. What is *not* needed is a change in the service; what is needed is to convert Jews to Judaism. The misinformed Jewish masses cry out for instruction and inspiration. But a facsimile of faith is *not* the answer.

Our modern innovators may recall the ancient tale about a thief who vowed that he would never steal anything insaide a synagogue. One day, when he was at his wit’s end, he rationalized as follows: “I vowed that I would not steal anything inside of the synagogue, but that doesn’t prevent me from stealing something which is attached on the outside of the synagogue structure.” With that, he climbed onto the synagogue roof. Observing a heavy bolt protruding in the center of the roof, he proceeded to dislodge it. The moment he loosened the bolt, a thunderous crash resounded inside the building. The tumult attracted the townspeople. On entering the synagogue they discovered that the main lighting fixture lay shattered on the floor.

The bolt which the thief had worked loose supported the fixture. His seemingly insignificant action had brought disaster. Is this not true of our faith? Very often a supposed minor change here, an insignificant innovation there can dislodge the candelabra of our faith and send it crashing to earth.

Our sages ask, “*vehaychon ho-yo odom be’oso sho-o?* – What was Adam doing while the serpent was carrying out his deception?” They significantly note, “*yoshayn lo*-he was fast asleep.”

Consider what happened to the first man while he slumbered! He suffered exile and humiliation. Such is usually the case with men who fall asleep at the rudder of their responsibility. They bring upon themselves and their descendants untold despair.

God grant that we be ever alert to the devil that comes deceptively adorned. God grant that we be blessed with that vanguard of dedicated men and women who will stand watch against all intruders.

Often I hear people ridicule Jews for having the tendency to motion with their hands when they converse. I see this supposed redress as one of the greatest compliments which our people could be paid. The Jew speaks with his hands, he uses his hands, he believes in doing, in creating, in giving of himself.

Constructive accomplishment has been the philosophy of Jewish survival. God grant that we will continue to build an impregnable fortress of Jewish accomplishment that devils, despite their magnificent and colorful garb, will never be able to penetrate.
