SECOND DAY OF ROSH-HASHONOH

Esteemed Congregation:

We stand now at the end of the past and the beginning of the new year. With a heart filled with apprehension and deeply bent from sorrows we think of the many and various sufferings and humiliations which our fellow Jews in Russia and all its satellites had to undergo in the past year. We think of the six and a half millions brutally killed and also of the extraordinary tragic conditions in which the remaining Jews in all these countries live and even of those in the land of Israel which are surrounded by tenacious and barbarous enemies. We think of the deplorable discovery of many anti-Semitic and reactionary organizations in our beloved free America. Dark, stormy, and dangerous clouds are gathered over our heads. We are asking: What will the new year bring for them and us?

Worthy Friends, let us try to understand the past and find out what our obligations are.

The prophet Habakkuk calls out: "*His ways are ever- lasting.*"[^1] Our sages have given a wonderful interpretation to these words by saying: "*Whoever studies (traditional) laws every day is assured of life in the future world, as it is said: "His ways are everlasting.*"[^2] Read not Halichoth ways but Halachoth (traditional) laws.

My dear friends, let us try to understand the words of our sages and find thus an answer to the above question.

The Almighty created the entire universe and all that fills it; the Lord has given to everything in the world its own particular law of development and existence. Every violation of these laws is consequently detrimental, sometimes immediately and sometimes later. Some mistakes are remediable and some cannot be corrected. Some evil actions cause unimportant and some very serious harm. Mistakes of the average individual may bring damage only to himself, and the same usually in proportion to that particular error, but some to very large circles, yes, some to the world as a whole; some damages are only of an economic nature; others are also of a spiritual and moral nature. The ways of the world or nature, the results of all activities and actions are determined by God and so are the "<span dir="rtl">הליכות עולם</span>" the ways of the world identical with <span dir="rtl">הלכות עולם</span> cosmic laws.

Friends, the higher one elevates himself above the standards of the average man, the more serious are the results of his failures. A great orator or writer can, for instance, spread a wrong idea among uncultured masses, among frivolous people who are unable to draw logical conclusions and thus mislead them. Such a man causes very great damage. A very clear example are the demagogical and crazy ideas of a man like Hitler. His theories proved to be disastrous to millions of his own country, race, religion and fellow-men; and also for the world as a whole, causing the Second World War with all its terrible consequences for many, many generations. Similar results can also be caused by the megalomania and chauvinism of a man like Mussolini.

Esteemed Congregation, the natural development of man is:

1\) To endeavor to acquire knowledge as much as possible, to develop his mind and understanding in order to have proper judgment and refined and good manners, and to enable oneself to achieve the heights of the real cultured man.[^3]

2\) To study some profession or trade by which one shall be able to secure his livelihood.[^4]

3\) To take care and provide for his wife, children, and immediate relatives.[^5]

4\) To work for the welfare of the community,[^6] of the state and for humanity as a whole.[^7]

The great leader is destined by God and nature to take care of himself, his family, his people, and mankind.[^8] The Jewish High-priest had to pray and offer sacrifices for the welfare of all mankind. Any violation of this law brings personal and general harm and disaster.

My dear friends, as it is with the individual, so is it also relatively with nations. Each and every nation must spare no efforts for its own development and independence, in every direction; namely, intellectually, economically, politically, etc., but must indeed, without fail, work in the same way for social justice and welfare in the world all over. For as no one man can be happy alone, so does the welfare of a nation depend on the welfare of all other peoples. Isolationism was and will remain dangerous at all times.

Friends, the Jewish people proclaimed the fundamental principles of humanitarian ideals. The principle of "*Love thy neighbor as thyself*", was propagated by Israel while humanity was still divided into casts, into patricians and plebeians, into masters and slaves; when brutal force, despotism and barbarism were dominant, when ignorance was prevailing and brutal force ruling; as early as that time, Israel propagated liberty and equality of all mankind.

It is very remarkable that this Jewish people, because of its enthusiasm to serve the world, forgets itself, its characteristics, its peculiar and sublime culture and philosophy, its own history, its happy and auspicious past, its right to live and exist as an independent nation; and all this happens specifically in the countries where it is enjoying a so-called emancipation.

The Jews of Babylonia, Persia, etc. did not want, because of their devoted patriotism to the aforementioned countries, to return to their national homeland under the guidance of Ezra and Nechemia, as King Cyrus of Persia had given them the permission to do. The Jews that lived at that time in Spain and Portugal also rejected with pride the call of the above-mentioned great leaders, to return to the Jewish homeland by saying: "We have our national country here."[^9]

My friends, while the first ones of Persia, Babylon, Egypt, etc. namely, the Jews have been lost to us and disappeared together with their conquerors, the peoples of ancient history, the latter, the Jews of Spain and Portugal, have become for nearly two thousand years a football of the capricious favors or disfavors and disgrace of the people of the Diaspora.

In spite of all the great and splendid contributions which our people have made to the development of culture and science, to financial and general economic conditions in all the countries of the exile, Israel has been in normal times and in progressive countries, at best tolerated, but as soon as a depression arises, the Jew is always the scapegoat. Yes, my dear friends, our people always pay dearly for their mistakes. Can there be better evidence for this than the current events? The assimilated German, English, and French Jewry has betrayed to a certain extent its own people and deserted it. They claimed to be Germans, English, Frenchmen of mosaic faith, but not Jews. Thus they have torn and damaged the unity of the Jewish people.

Barbaric and cruel Hitlerism which spread the poison of racial hatred and prejudice the world over has scarcely aroused a part of these assimilated Jews from their lethargic slumber and revived their conscience.

Friends, even in religious circles, as a matter of fact, children were given opportunities to learn anything except what concerns their own national culture, history, and philosophy. Our children achieve the highest marks and dis- tinctions in worldly knowledge but are to our greatest regrets ignorant of matters which deal with Judaism. How far away are they from things sacred and dear to us!

Friends, we deprive our youth of every opportunity to become acquainted with Judaism and wonder at the same time about their lack of understanding for and interest in Jewish ideals. Oh, how happy were our parents when their children shared a common way in life with themselves! When they could call out: "*And both (father and son) walked together,*"[^10] as we read this morning in the Holy Script about our ancestors Abraham and Isaac. What a glorious time was it when our people understood that the importance of studying the Thorah surpasses that of many other commandments. When they conceived that knowledge of the Thorah is very important because it leads to a sublime way of life;[^11] when they understood that we cannot abolish or stop the Jewish public schools even for the purpose of building the Holy Temple.[^12] Not so at the present time. One hears often enough that even people who are not well- to-do are striving to erect in their section a beautiful and modern synagogue, but very seldom is it known that they try to build a parochial school where their children should have a proper opportunity to study Hebrew in proportion to worldly knowledge. Is it therefore a wonder, my friends, that a great part of our youth is estranged from the synagogues and Jewish environment? That many of them are even ashamed of their parentage? They mingled with the others and learned many of their vices[^13]. Can we call out with the same pride as in former times: "*Our hands did not shed this blood*"[^14] Have we at the present time the sacred, pure, and beautiful family life as in the former times? We must call out, to our greatest regret, with a mighty: No!

We shall learn therefore from the past of our own history and try to make good, to correct this terrible blunder of ours. We must go back to our God, our wonderful and sublime Thorah, to our peculiar ideals and philosophy. We must learn to have self respect again, to strive to attain our independence. Then, only then, will we be accepted as real teachers in the world; namely, to teach mankind the lofty conceptions of life; then will we be able to educate men to practice real justice and loving-kindness, to lead them to the pacifism proclaimed by the great prophet Yeshayahu: "*And they shall beat their swords into ploughshares, and their spears into pruning knives. Nation shall not lift up sword against nation, and they shall not learn any more war." Then will come that time of supreme happiness of which Yeshayahu speaks: "And there shall come forth a shoot of the stem of Jessie, and a sprout shall spring out of his roots. And there shall rest upon him the spirit of the Lord, the spirit of wisdom and understanding, the spirit of counsel and might, the spirit of knowledge and of the fear of the Lord; and he shall be animated by fear of the Lord; and not after the sight of his eyes shall he judge, and not after the hearing of his ears shall he decide; but he shall judge with righteousness the poor, etc. and decide with equity for the suffering ones of the earth, etc., and the wolf (meaning the exploiters) shall dwell with the sheep (meaning the poor workers) and the leopard shall lie down with the kid; and the calf and the young lion and the fatling shall be together, and a little boy shall lead them. And the cow and the she-bear shall feed, together shall their young ones lie down; and the lion shall, like the ox, eat straw (meaning, according to the interpretation of Maimonides, the rich ones shall live as simple a life as the poor ones and poor as comfortable as the rich), etc. They shall not hurt nor destroy on all my holy mountain; for the earth shall be full of the knowledge of the Lord, as the waters cover the sea.*"[^15]

Then humanity will be united into one people, to serve one God, as we pray in the main prayer[^16] of today. "*And that they may all form a single band to do Thy will with a perfect heart.*"

Amen
