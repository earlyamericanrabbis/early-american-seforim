*"The One Who opens a gate for those who knock in repentance."*

(The Musaf prayer of Rosh Hashanah)

It can be said the idea in this is in a witty manner, along the lines of what is brought in the words of our Sages of blessed memory: (Niddah 16b) "*A man shall not enter the house of his fellow suddenly.*" Rather, before he enters, he must knock on the door. Behold, the man who understands the spirit of men and their nature can understand from the sound of the knocking who is the one knocking . . .

- If he is the city guard, he knocks with strength and with impudence, because he comes with the power of authority.

- If he is a messenger, to collect a debt for commerce, he knocks with etiquette, because he profits from the debtor.

- And if a moneylender with interest comes to collect...

"*...before Me, as if today I created you as a new creation, this is what is written: (Yeshayahu 66:22) 'For as the new heavens and the new earth...'*" (Vayikra Rabbah 29:10)

The idea hidden here is according to what is explained in the commentators, regarding that which is brought in the words of our Sages of blessed memory: (Shemos Rabbah 35:6) "*If you have kept the Torah, I consider it for you as if you made yourselves,*" see there.

Because there is no thing that completes a man and teaches him the way in the paths of life like our Holy Torah, which is truly a teaching for man in wherever he turns—in his lying down and in his rising, in his walking on the way, in his commerce, in his food, and in his family life, and the like. For by the power of his heart's culture alone, man is not yet perfected such that he can be compatible with life, or that he can make peace with others. As we see in this time of ours, that even for the "cultured" nations, they lack the professions in the perfection of life required to be able to take a step toward making peace with one another. Rather, even at this time, they hate one another with an ultimate hatred, and their blood is spilled in the streets, as is known; because their culture has not yet developed to its full necessity, like our Holy Torah with its exalted and lofty concepts, which are ordered and built upon good and upright traits which develop and make the man into an exalted essence.

As our Sages of blessed memory said: (Makkot 24a \[text: 83b\]) "*613 commandments were told to Moses, etc., and David came and established them upon eleven, as it is written: (Tehillim 15) 'A Psalm of David: Lord, who shall sojourn in Your tent? Who shall dwell on Your holy mountain? (1) He who walks uprightly, (2) and works righteousness, (3) and speaks the truth in his heart. (4) He does not slander with his tongue, (5) nor does he do evil to his fellow, (6) nor does he take up a reproach against his neighbor. (7) In whose eyes a vile person is despised, (8) but he honors those who fear the Lord. (9) He swears to hurt and does not change. (10) He does not put out his money at interest, (11) nor does he take a bribe against the innocent. He who does these things shall never be moved.'*"

The understanding is a wonder: how did our Sages of blessed memory permit making David—the anointed of the God of Jacob—as one who "*cuts the plantings*" of the Hebrew religion, not to leave from the 613 commandments more than eleven? (<span dir="rtl">עי׳ פירש״</span> See Rashi's commentary).

Rather, it should be said that their intention in this is to say, that before a man reaches to the perfection of the Torah, David said that a man should adorn himself with these 11 spiritual ornaments: "*He who walks uprightly, and works righteousness,*" etc. For these eleven traits, they themselves are the souls of all the 613 commandments. But one should not, Heaven forbid, subtract from the count, for each one has a certain unique quality to make a man an exalted being.

And so too is the intention of what the Sages said: (ibid.) Yeshayahu came and established them on six, etc. Micah came and established them on three, etc. Habakkuk came and established them on one, as it is said: (Habakkuk 2:4) "*The righteous shall live by his faith.*" That is to say, each one made general principles according to his view and estimation, through which a man can achieve the most correct level to perfect himself in the commandments of the Lord. But Heaven forbid thinking that they uprooted the faith![^1]

For in truth, even the observance of one general principle from our Holy Torah can perfect the essence of a man, through which a noble and God-fearing character from His Name, may He be blessed, shall be fixed in his soul, and he will achieve the deed which God has made!

As is brought in *Ikkarim* (ibid.): A story of a certain wise man to whom an armed bandit and master of transgressions came, and said to him: "I want to return in repentance and keep the commandments of the Blessed Name, but I cannot load upon myself the multitude of commandments in the Torah, for I cannot carry that burden."

And the wise man said to him: "Accept upon yourself one thing alone to keep it properly."

And the bandit said to him: "Yes!" The wise man said to him: "If so, accept upon yourself to tell the truth," and he accepted it upon himself and went on his way.

After some time, that bandit was going to rob a man and kill him, and a man met him and said to him: "Where are you going?" And that bandit remembered "what he had accepted upon himself to tell the truth," and he told the man where he was going. And so another man met him and asked him where he was going? And he told him. Immediately that bandit pondered in his heart: "These two will be witnesses against me and they will kill me."

And so it happened to him many times, in everything he wanted to do, until he was prevented because of this from his evil deeds.

Behold, it is explained that by keeping one general principle, or two general principles, or more, it is possible for a man to be able to keep most of the commandments of the Torah, or a large part from it, that through it he shall merit the highest level, and also through it he shall find to take his portion in the order of the world. For the perfection of man will not be completed, except through true ideas and upright traits—for a man in himself, and also in his conduct with his fellow.[^2]

And this is what is brought in the Midrash Rabbah there: "*If you have kept the Torah, I consider it for you as if you made yourselves.*" That is to say, if you are diligent to walk and to conduct yourselves only according to the way of the Torah, I consider it for you as if you made yourselves—meaning, into an exalted and perfect essence; for were it not for the instruction of the Torah, what advantage would man have over all created beings?

Likewise also here, regarding what he said: "*In all the Musaf offerings it is written: 'And you shall sacrifice', and here it is written: 'And you shall make' a fire-offering,*" etc. Meaning, through the repentance of Israel and their *teruah* to the Lord, they merit as if they were also made into an exalted essence . . . as the Midrash Rabbah said above: "*I consider it for you as if today you were made before Me," etc., and understand this*.

According to what was stated above, the idea in this can be said in another manner: that which in all the Musaf offerings it is written "And you shall sacrifice", and here it is written "*And you shall make*", etc. It is according to what is explained in our holy literature: for what reason is its name called *korban*? It is because through the sin of man he was distanced from the Holy One, Blessed be He; however, through his *korban* he is brought close again to the Holy One, Blessed be He . . . as is known.

And because of this, in all the Musaf offerings it is written "*And you shall sacrifice*", meaning: through the sacrifice you shall be made close to the Holy One, Blessed be He . . .

Whereas on Rosh Hashanah, since this day was established as the Day of Judgment when all who enter the world pass before Him like a flock of sheep. . . consequently it is not the sacrifice that shall bring the man close to the Holy One, Blessed be He, but rather that which he shall examine his deeds . . .

Therefore it is written "*And you shall make*", meaning that only through your deeds shall you be made close to the Holy One, Blessed be He . . . and understand this.
