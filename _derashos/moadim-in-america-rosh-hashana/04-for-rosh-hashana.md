The Sages of our people showed a thorough acquaintance with human nature, when they wrote the following maxim. Let the right hand always draw back again to the fold him that the left hand had thrust aside. Expressed in our usual manner of speaking, that maxim would read so: "If you are in duty bound to reprove your neighbor be gentle and sympathetic. The occasion which elicited that Rabbinical teaching, was a discussion on Elisha's treatment of his servant. Gehazi had been guilty of a sin, which we Israelites consider most offensive to God <span dir="rtl">חלול השם</span>. With falsehood on his lips, he had asked of a distinguished Gentile presents which his high-minded Master had refused. The provocation was great. A prophet in Israel and his religion lost very possibly by that act in the estimation of strangers to our faith. But according to a tradition, the curse uttered against the transgressor, extinguished in him every spark of manhood. While it made his body leprous, it caused also the ineffaceable leprosy of the soul. Gehazi died impenitent. Yes: Elisha was austere. Men far above temptation, will be apt to look with an eye of unyielding severity on those who are the sport of unruly passions.

But... Dear fellow believers! In this assemblage none is so saintly pure, so incorruptible that he will be scandalized by our backslidings.

We have met on this memorial day of the creation not with the fear
that we shall receive a withering rebuke from some spiritual
superior, but with an ardent wish and a prayer that the solemn
communion of individuals equally fallible, if not equal
degree sinful, may profit all. Each recalling his own foibles
may carry out the nobly conceived aphorism of the Rabbis. The right-hand kindly extended to the brother-sinner will draw
back again to the sacred fold, him that the left-hand had
thrust aside. Persuasive words and not imprecations will effect that
sublime end.

Oh! blessed the eloquence which softens the callous. Thrice blessed that eloquence of the heart which stirs up sentiments of
religion in creatures shunned by the saints because sunk in worldliness. Step forward thou brother whom the Almighty has
endowed with that soul-elevating gift, and tell us how to live
in godliness, that we may die the death of the righteous. Could we
now be filled with that knowledge, this day of memorial would
create for us a new heaven upon earth, delighting our spirits
even until they be taken to higher glories.

Have you, beloved hearers! formed an idea of that terrestrial heaven? In my mind it consists of harmony perfect and unalterable. Rising within the centre of each man's household, it swells and reaches the social family. In such a household religion,— the religion of Abraham ever appealing to our finest instincts— strikes the chords which produce that harmony. The happiness of all is there the effort of each individual. No undutiful Son causes a father's pillow to be moistened with tears. The Daughter makes the home redolent of joys; fragrant with the sweet scent of filial virtues. See them brothers realize the grand ideal of the inspired poet. They dwell together in unity. Soft like the dew of Hermon which watered the hills of Zion; precious like the oil which anointed Aaron is their affection. In such a household the morning dawns in a cloudless horizon. At sunset fresh pleasures are ushered in. The wife leans gently on her husband's arm for support. Never will she crush it by the weight of discontent and ambition. The plighted companions of a lifetime pour out their heart-stores of their love on their offspring, and these give that earthly heaven intensity of brightness. Shining forth in full splendor, it casts light beyond the narrow compass of a home circle. From a household, whose every member worships God in the devotion of love, will issue forth Charity, that charity which hides under the folds of a broad mantle a neighbor's failings. Nothing is too loathsome for its touch. Nothing too defiled for its approach. To rescue man, and not suffer him to sink in the mire of corruption, the right hand of fellowship is extended. It brings back again to the sacred fold, him that the left hand had thrust aside.

If any vows now can so teach us to live in uprightly, then will indeed this day have created for us a new heaven. Wherever we dwell it will spread over our heads a pavilion of peace. We the children of the covenant bred up in that pure atmosphere, cradled in domestic affection, will carry out the lessons imbibed in our intercourse with our fallible fellow-mortals. In none of them will the dignity of manhood be blotted out by a blasting curse. Not by austerity and acerbity of temper. By kindness may the sinner be converted. But... he whom we would crown prince of knowledge does not come forward armed from this assemblage to teach us how to live in uprightly that we may die righteously. Aye! we all feel the truth enunciated by a Sage that scarcely one will anymore be met, who can claim spiritual superiority.

Do Thou O Lord! with whose breath the heavens above were stretched forth, and the earth beneath was peopled with intelligent beings, do Thou mould a creation in which our hearts may expand, and our souls be elevated. Cause us to see there the joys which spring forth from religion, and slake our thirst in that limpid water. May the year we have greeted in solemn convocation, open to us a sphere of actions, where love will spiritualize our homes, and charity like the sun, illumining other orbs without obscuring itself, may warm, lighten and reanimate all creatures. Amen.
