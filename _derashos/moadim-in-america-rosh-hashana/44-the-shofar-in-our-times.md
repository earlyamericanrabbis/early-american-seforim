It is a positive commandment of the Torah to hear the blast of the shofar on Rosh Hashanah, as it is said: "*It shall be a day of blowing for you*" (language of the Rambam, z"l).

What are the reasons for the blowing of the shofar? Many of the great men of Torah wrote and strived to make it understandable to the ear \[literally: break the ear\] why we blow on Rosh Hashanah. And I too chose as the topic of the sermon the matter of the shofar, because it has many outcomes; it proclaims and warns, and as the select words of the prophets, it holds an important place — and before we explain its matter for today, let us contemplate the action of the shofar across the span of times, what it accomplished and what was done through it in Jewish history.

The Shofar in the Days of Moshe

When the first shofar appeared at the time of the Giving of the Torah, and there it is said: "*And Hashem said to Moshe: Go to the nation and sanctify them today and tomorrow, and let them wash their clothes*" (Yisro 19).

From this we understand that the nation was still in a simple state, and possessed soiled garments, and it was necessary to cleanse their heart and purify them, and they lived in simple life and in slavery, and were not yet worthy to receive the Torah. It was necessary to prepare them and purify them for the great and elevated day, to blow into them holy thoughts and elevated concepts to transform their present life — and the proof is that they did not permit them to touch the mountain, for the mountain was holy, for the light of the Torah shone upon it. And those who departed Egypt were still closed and sealed to receive the rays of splendor; and behold, during the Jubilee, "*they shall ascend the mountain*" (ibid.), for the blast of the shofar created a new creation within them, to transform from the mundane to the holy, like the thunders after the winter, a preparation for the spring, its progressive power. And the sound of the shofar was very strong, and all the nation that was in the camp trembled, because it broke the old ways and created new ones. "*And it was that the sound of the shofar grew progressively very strong; Moshe would speak, and God would answer him with a voice.*" The shofar clears a path and prepares one to hear the word of Hashem for one born of a woman.

The Shofar in the Days of Yehoshua

And Yericho was locked and closed; no light of intellect penetrated there, and it was also closed before Israel, because at that time, they and only they were the bearers of Hashem's Torah to spread it and teach it to the wild nations. And like the walls of China \[literally: the Chinese walls\] that do not allow a European man to set foot in their land, so that he will not spread knowledge and cease the darkness and ignorance — so too, certainly at that time, the people of Yericho did not allow a foreign man entrance to dwell among them, because he was a civilized person. And what did they do, and what means did the Bnei Yisrael find to fell the wall, the iron barrier between the light and the darkness? — And behold, seven Kohanim bore seven shofars, and the Kohanim blew with the shofars, and the nation shouted and blew with the shofars, and it was when the nation heard the sound of the shofar, the nation shouted a great blast, and the wall fell in its place (Yehoshua 6). The shofar is a great damager, for an elevated power exists in the bent giant \[i.e., the curved ram's horn\]. With its voice, it fells the wall of fortresses with a force greater than what was received from the Krupp factory.[^1]

The Shofar in Eretz Yisrael in the Days of the Sanhedrin

From Rosh Hashanah until Yom Kippur, slaves were not released to their homes, nor were they subjugated to their masters, nor did fields return to their owners. Rather, slaves would eat, drink, and rejoice, with their crowns on their heads. Once Yom Kippur arrived, the Beis Din blew the shofar, slaves were released to their homes, and fields returned to their owners (Rambam, Laws of Shemittah 10:14).

*And so, three things delay the fulfillment of Jubilee: blowing the shofar, releasing slaves, and returning fields to their owners* (ibid.).

And so, two things are done and recalled through the shofar: they release slaves and forfeit land ownership.

How much blood was spilled, how many sermons were preached regarding the matter of slaves! The holy prophets chastised and cried out: "*Release your slaves!*" From the day of man's creation on the earth until today — the question of slavery and freedom does not give rest to the men of the kingdom to find some scheme to reach a compromise between the slave and the master; when this one falls, this one rises, and each one in his place shows his harsh arm. And likewise, an eternal dispute between the estate owner and the farmer, regarding the forfeiture of land — is this a small matter? And once again, a son of a sheep, a ram's horn as it is in its true appearance — and if he plated it with gold, it is invalid — performs miraculous deeds, bringing freedom to the world.

From all the aforementioned things, we see that the shofar in the days of Moshe Rabbeinu made a splendid revolution into the uncircumcised hearts, preparing the soil to sow within them the Torah of life. In the days of Yehoshua, it broke through the wall that divides and obscures the luminaries. The shofar in the days of the Beis Din and Yisrael on their land proclaimed freedom to slaves.

And behold, on the day of Rosh Hashanah, the Jew prays a pure prayer and requests: "*Create for me a pure heart, O God, and renew a steadfast spirit within me*" (Tehillim 51:12). For he conducts an accounting of his soul — not like the entire year when he conducts the accountings of others from before, lowering their honor, while he in his actions is complete, upright, and clean of any trace of defect. Suddenly, a great revolution occurs in his life; he descends into his inner heart and finds many deficiencies and a terrible abyss in his deeds and thoughts. He is at a loss for advice and finds no other way except to request from the Creator a new heart — for the heart from the entire year is soiled. And so he found it correct to request "renew a steadfast spirit within me," for even regarding matters touching upon the spirit, the spirit has not yet ascended above, but only in a crooked path; it goes from top to bottom. And he also found an iron wall he built during the course of the year, which barrier did not allow the cry of the downtrodden to penetrate him then. Therefore, he must demolish and build, uproot and create new things. And who will assist in this? — Behold, the shofar sounds its voice, and vast strength is within it; it proclaims freedom to his desires and scatters all the walls that do not allow the sun's rays, daughter of heaven, to shine inside him, and directs his heart to the word of Hashem, and he will be at peace in his conduct as a Jewish man.

"*Fortunate is the nation that knows the shofar blast; Hashem, in the light of Your countenance they shall walk.*" And as the homily of Chazal states: "*'New'*— renew your deeds; *'Shofar'* — improve your deeds. The Holy One, blessed be He, said: Since you entered before Me in judgment on this day and emerged acquitted, I consider it upon you as if today you were made, as if today I created you a new covenant. That is what is written: *'For as the new heavens...'*" And behold, the Jew on the holy day is like a newborn child — and a lamp shines upon his head, and how elevated is his prayer! He lights lamps unto the end of the world; he will rectify all creatures under the kingdom of the Almighty and requests: "*And all shall come to serve You.*"

And how sweet are the words of Chazal regarding the designation for the two days of Rosh Hashanah — not as the "*second festival day of the Diaspora*" like all the holidays and festivals, but rather as "*one long day*", both days as one long day. For one day is too short for all the deeds performed on it, and the night is considered as nothing, for light continues without interruption, like the battlefield of Yehoshua against the kings of the Emori, or as he spoke when the spirit of song rested upon him, when their ugly deeds were cut off and wiped out, and the idols of their gods were destroyed, and he said before the eyes of Yisrael: "*Sun, stand still in Giv'on, and moon in the valley of Aiyalon... and it did not hasten to come set for about a full day.*" And because it was necessary to lengthen the day to finish his great deeds, and such deeds require light — so too, Rosh Hashanah is a great and long day, full of splendor and pleasantness. And just as on Rosh Hashanah the first man was created, so too his descendants today are renewed with a new heart and a steadfast spirit, and give to the Creator of all creations the crown of kingship.

"*Cloud and thick darkness are around Him; righteousness and justice are the foundation of His throne*" (Tehillim 97:2).

In a court of law, where matters of dispute are clarified, and the judges issue a verdict on every matter of transgression, the gates are open and permission is granted to every visitor to enter inside, so that it shall be revealed publicly that the judges issue a true and righteous judgment based on proper clarifications and established, reliable discretion. And all who turn to their crooked ways, justifying the wicked and condemning the righteous, perform their deeds in the dark in order to cover their abominations in obscurity.

And behold, we see in the Supreme Conduct that Hashem established His throne in the heavens, yet it is covered and hidden from our eyes, and His entire judgment is in laws of life that are wondrous beyond us. Despite this, we believe that His work is perfect, a God of faith without iniquity, and we bless over the bad just as over the good, for we have known Him and recognized Him that His ways are ways of pleasantness and He judges the world with righteousness.

And this is what the Psalmist said: Even though "*cloud and thick darkness are around Him,*" nevertheless, "*righteousness and justice are the foundation of His throne.*"

[^1]: The Krupp cast steel factory (*Krupp-Gussstahlfabrik*) in Essen, Germany, founded in 1811 by Friedrich Krupp, grew into a massive industrial empire and the primary armaments manufacturer for the German military through both World Wars, eventually merging into ThyssenKrupp AG in 1999.
