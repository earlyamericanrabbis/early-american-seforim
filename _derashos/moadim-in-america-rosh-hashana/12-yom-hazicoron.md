ROSH-HASHONOH

Esteemed Congregation:

The holiday we are celebrating today is called <span dir="rtl">יום הזכרון</span> the day of remembrance. It is also the day of <span dir="rtl">תקיעת שופר</span> a day of blowing the Shofar.

My friends, in yesterday's sermon before the blowing of the Shofar, I have tried to explain to you the ideas symbolized in the sounds of this trumpet, what they stand for, and what they are to remind us of. The <span dir="rtl">שברים תרועה</span> likened to the sound of the sighs and lamentations, shall remind us of our different mistakes and of their tragic con- sequences. It shall prompt us to make up for the wrongs we have done and keep us from repeating them.

Today when the day is more a <span dir="rtl">יום הזכרון</span> a day of memorial, of remembrance, we shall endeavor to com- prehend its full meaning. Everything in the universe is limited, as is commonly known, by three dimensions; namely, length, width and height (or depth). Everything in the world is also limited by a certain period of time (the fourth dimension) and has, therefore, a beginning and an end. These laws concern all the physical matters of the world and are well known to every one of us. What I would like to point out is that even mental, spiritual, and ideal things have their limitations. The human mind is limited by physical and spiritual factors. Our mental efficiency can be developed, enriched, and expounded only to a certain degree. Our parents, teachers, and environment can disclose our mental abilities. Our understanding and intelligence can be developed and enriched by association with great men, by listening to and partaking in their conversations, discussions and debates about various important subjects. However, as soon as our mental capacity reaches its climax, it cannot go beyond that limit. Since our mental acceptability, as well as our memory, is restricted, there are things which are beyond our conception and also hard to keep within our memory. The purpose of the day we are celebrating sets a limit to further definition of this subject. The fact I wish to bring to your attention is that even in our idealistic activities, we must apply the correct measure. Every exaggeration or extremity becomes derogatory and detrimental to the purpose.

My dear Congregates, it is a well-known fact that righteousness and wisdom are of paramount importance. They bring bliss to the world, but they, too, must have their limits. The wise King Solomon said:

"*Be not righteous over-much; neither show thy- self over-wise. Why wouldst thou destroy thyself?*"[^1]

Appreciation, gratitude, and patriotism are fine virtues. However, chauvinism and humility are objectionable, and beneath the dignity of a civilized and cultured man. The over-zealous patriotism of some of our people, who live in the progressive countries of the democracies and enjoy tolerance, caused a great many of them to relinquish their national individuality and to become assimilated. The very extreme wing of orthodox Jewry also brought about a great rift in the rank and file of our people, which caused much discord and strife and even bitter clashes within our people. Such behavior led many a great nation to its doom; they ceased to exist de facto and are now found only in the recorded history of ancient times.

My dear Friends, religious differences are not strong enough to preserve the individual existence of a nation which is conquered and exiled by another. We cannot deny the fact that the existence of the Jewish people through the nearly two thousand years of diaspora under the most cruel inquisitions, social and economic persecution is only due to the preservation of our religion and tradition. But we also must emphasize that this religious individuality of our people did not bring about the solution of the national problems, and that but a small part of the Jewish people has been saved. When more than ten tribes of our people — the vast majority of the people — were exiled from our land to Babylon although they were religious and observed the traditions and customs of their ancestors, they did not heed to the call of our leaders Ezra and Nehemiah to return to our native land, they disappeared in the course of time and are lost to this day. The piety and gentility of Rabbi Secharja ben Avtilos caused the destruction of our house and the burning of our temple and the exile of our people.[^2]

The day of New Year, the day of remembrance, shall therefore bring to our attention that all the Jews be united into one Israel, without any difference as to nationality, religious conception, or other standards. But that they must be united and have one goal, one ideal; namely, preservation of our religion and tradition, the re-establishment and re- construction of our land Palestine, and the return to our original glory.

Let us call out to the different groups and organizations among our people: You are welcome at any time to go with us, and even to lead us, to the revival and redemption of our people, but not to mislead us or to have us deny our individual and national self-preservation. Better, let us stand together and proclaim to the various fractions and parties of Israel, from the extreme religious Agudists and Chassidim to the extreme Leftists: Oh! Let us stand united for the welfare and preservation of our people!

Let us listen attentively to the words of the prophet Yeshayahu:

"*And it shall come to pass on that day, that a great shofar shall be blown; and they shall come who were lost in the land of Assyria, and they that were outcasts in the land of Egypt; and they shall worship the Lord on the holy mountain of Jerusalem.*"[^3]

"*That they may form a single band to do Thy will, O Lord, with a perfect heart!*"[^4]

And it will not be a prayer for this one day, but a resolution for the future, an aim which we must have before our eyes each single day of our life. And if we perform this great prayer with the right devotion, if we take this wonderful idea with us into our everyday struggle, and perpetuate it, the dawn of glory for Israel will re-awaken, never to vanish again.

Amen
