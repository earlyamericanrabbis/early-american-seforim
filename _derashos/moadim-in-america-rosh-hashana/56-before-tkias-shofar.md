*"And it came to pass, when Yehoshua was by Yericho, that he lifted up his eyes and looked, and behold, there stood a man over against him with his sword drawn in his hand; and Yehoshua went unto him, and said unto him: 'Art thou for us, or for our adversaries?' And he said: 'Nay, but I am captain of the host of Hashem; now am I come.' And Yehoshua fell on his face to the earth, and bowed down, and said unto him: 'What saith my lord unto his servant?' And the captain of Hashem's host said unto Yehoshua: 'Put off thy shoe from off thy foot; for the place whereon thou standest is holy.' And Yehoshua did so"*

(Yehoshua 5).

Yehoshua, in his walking with Moshe Rabbeinu who carried in his hands the two Tablets of the Covenant to the nation of Yisrael, upon hearing "*the voice of the people as they shouted,*" said to Moshe his master, "*There is a noise of war in the camp.*" And Moshe replied to him, "*It is not the voice of them that shout for mastery, neither is it the voice of them that cry for being overcome; but the noise of them that sing do I hear.*"

The intention in the words of Moshe Rabbeinu to Yehoshua is: That which you hear—the voice of the people in their shouting—you cannot understand what this voice hints to, and it will tell us nothing: whether it heralds the sight of victory or heralds the terror of downfall. This voice gives us only a "*voice of annos*"—a voice of the affliction of judgment, a voice of doubt, a voice of perplexity, a voice that tells something and tells nothing, a voice that lets our ears hear some rumor, but does not let us hear whether it is a good rumor that makes the marrow fat, or a bad rumor that dries up the bone. In short, it is a voice of doubt which consumes from soul to flesh like the melting away of a sick man. And this doubt is worse for every man than any definitively bad rumor.

In this situation, honored masters, we find ourselves currently as we stand ready toward the coming year which is coming upon us for good, covered in a veil of fog over its face, without knowing how it will appear before us: whether its face is the face of the sun that illuminates the earth and those who dwell upon it, or the face of a demon of destruction, scattering horror all around it. Bitter and bad is the destiny of any man who waits with eyes lifted to On High toward disaster or joy, without knowing which of them will greet his face. And worse than this is the destiny of a man whose bad mazel casts him from wave to wave constantly, who because of every happenstance coming toward him will fear a great fear lest it be a trampling for him. For he looks at himself always as a stepchild who is always ready to receive blows and beatings, and his heart will not allow him to believe that also for him—the stepchild of life—the sun will shine at some point in time, and happenstances will smile upon him. If he also is found at a moment wherein he passes from era to era, at a moment upon which depends his destiny for a long time, how agonizing and sorrowful are his thoughts, how bitter and bad are his feelings, with what do his memories pain his heart, and how much will he tremble and be dismayed toward his future.

So too are we, the House of Yisrael! On this day there begins for us, the children of Yisrael, a new year which we meet now with the sound of the shofar—the voice of the people in shouting—a voice that heralds to us that a new year has come upon us, and together with it will begin a new war, the war of life, the war of a man for his soul, for his existence, for his life, for his sustenance, which are as difficult for a man of Yisrael as the splitting of the Red Sea. And our eyes are lifted to On High to request from before the Master of all and Creator of all and Sustainer of all, upon us, and upon our wives, and upon our young children, and upon our nation.

And at this moment, there pass before us like sheep our bitter and bad memories that passed over us in the past year; all faces gather blackness, every back is bowed, all eyes stream with tears, all hearts ache. This one brings up in his memory the bitter separation that he separated from one of the beloved ones of his soul, whom cruel death separated between them in the past year. This one brings up in his memory that all his wealth that he toiled over for many years went to waste in the past year. And this one brings up in his memory that his tender young children, beloved to him in his soul, are wasting away from poverty and from lack of everything.

How numerous are the groans heard at this moment in this holy house, which break half of a man's body. And one great groan goes out from the beginning of the world to its end, from whose voice ears grow deaf and hearts shatter, a groan that breaks cedars and reaches until the Throne of Glory; and all the small and great groans that go out from us are ashamed before it to let their voice be heard On High—it is the groan of the daughter of Yisrael, the miserable nation, Knesset Yisrael that convulses between life and death, and coos like a dove over her deep wounds and over her bitter destiny. Yisrael is a scattered sheep, all that devour her are not held guilty; the treacherous deal treacherously and the spoiler spoils, with no one to demand her humiliation and no one to seek her blood; whose land slips out from under her feet, with no place to set the sole of her foot; for all our distresses and groans are but small sparks from this great groan.

And with such bitter memories, as we come to meet the future, behold, does not the hair of our flesh stand on end with doubts that find a place at this time in our hearts: who knows if the year coming before us will be better to us than her sisters that passed before us? Who knows if she has not come to finish that which her previous sisters began, to break our hearts and to bend our stature more and more? And with justice we shall ask the future, which stands before us with its sword drawn in its hand to destroy and swallow up and annihilate existence, standing in a certain measure under the Malach Hamashchis: Art thou for us, or for our adversaries?

But, honored masters! The answer that the man who had the drawn sword in his hand replied to Yehoshua, we can take also for us as an answer... Yehoshua too, the captain of the host of Yisrael, upon seeing a drawn sword, felt within his inner soul the disaster of the universe through the power of annihilation and loss that reigns in the world, through the bad and bitter happenstances that the Creator of the universe apportioned to His world, and of which nine kabs were taken by the nation of Yisrael, the scapegoat of the entire world. And with a bitter heart he asked, "*Art thou for us, or for our adversaries?*"

But the answer that he received to his question—which ostensibly is improper, not corresponding to the question, for to his question that he asked him, "*Art thou for us, or for our adversaries,*" he did not reply to him, but rather replied to him, "*I am captain of the host of Hashem; now am I come,*" which regarding this he did not ask him, when he came and whose captain he is? Truly, after deep contemplation, I see that he answered him a decisive answer, a correct and true answer, which the greatest teacher in the world—which is experience—verifies this answer.

And it is: that the sword of Hashem of Hosts, the sword of all existence that consumes and destroys and loses everything, is not like a drawn sword of flesh and blood, which is designated for a specific man and prepared for a specific purpose, so that when we see one man draw his sword, we can ask him for what it was sharpened and prepared. For the sword of Hashem of Hosts is prepared and stands ready, and anyone who comes and places his neck under it, behold this one destroys himself knowingly, and anyone who distances himself from it can be saved from it. And this is his answer: "*Nay, but I am captain of the host of Hashem; now am I come,*" that is to say: I cannot answer you on your question, "*Art thou for us, or for our adversaries?*", since I am the captain of the host of Hashem; at this moment I came upon you and at this moment I can go from you, and this depends upon your conduct: if you walk in the path of the Torah, in the path of life, in the path that every upright man and every orderly nation walks, automatically my sword will be for your side. But if you do not conduct yourselves with measure, with caution, and with etiquette, in love one for his fellow, and the individual will not desire to bring a few sacrifices from his private earnings for the benefit of the public and the collective, then the reminder of the sword, which consumes and loses everything—private people and great nations who do not know how to bring order to their deeds—comes to work upon you too. And this is what Yehoshua asked him: "What saith my lord unto his servant?", that is to say: what does He request from the nation of Yisrael so that then the sword will be for your side? And upon this he replied to him: "*Put off thy shoe from off thy foot; for the place whereon thou standest is holy.*" That is to say: cast off from upon yourself the feeling of desires, bad character traits, and excessive licentiousness, your base self-love, and everything that brings the disaster of life to every man in particular and to every living nation in general. "*For the place whereon thou standest is holy*"—since you are ready to make from servants of Egypt a nation, there is necessary for this, for the length of its days and its existence, an elevated conduct and good character traits, and then by means of the sound of the shofar the walls of Yericho will fall.

So, honored masters, let us stand ready to hear the sound of the shofar and to hope that it will be for us a voice heralding good; but we must gather our spiritual strengths, to distance from us all bad character traits, baseless hatred and our excessive self-love, base desires and all despicable pleasures of life, which consume every good trait and every pure thought. And then the good Hashem will be in our assistance, and the sword of annihilation that pours its dominion over all existence and all bad happenstances will fall to the lot of our adversaries and not to us. And we shall see the raising of the horn of our nation, Amen!...
