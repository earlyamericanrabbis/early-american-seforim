*"In the absence of an advocate of righteousness against the one who tells of transgression, tell Jacob the matter of statute and judgment, and justify us in judgment, O King of Judgment."*

(In the Musaf prayer of Rosh Hashanah)

The commentators have already noted here at this point wondrously: after there is no advocate of righteousness against the teller of transgression, what is the benefit in asking of the Holy One, Blessed be He, that He should tell Jacob a matter of statute and judgment to justify us in judgment? . . .

However, in order to come to the solution of this matter, we must cast an eye upon that which is brought in the words of our Sages of blessed memory: (Yalkut Shimoni, Yeshayahu 63)

"*This is what is written: 'I speak in righteousness, mighty to save' (Yeshayahu 63:1). In the time to come, the princes and accusers of the nations come and say before the Holy One, Blessed be He: Master of the Universe! Is there then favoritism before You? If the nations are idolaters, then Israel are also idolaters; if these are revealers of nakedness, then these are revealers of nakedness; if these are shedders of blood, then these are shedders of blood. For what reason do the nations descend to Gehenna while Israel do not descend? At that hour, Michael, the advocate of Israel, becomes silent.*"

"*As it is said: (Daniel 12:1) 'At that time Michael shall stand, who stands for the children of your people'—that he stands to teach advocacy for Israel and he becomes silent. And 'standing' means nothing other than silence, as it is said: (Iyov 32:16) 'they stood and answered no more.' And since he becomes silent, the Holy One, Blessed be He, said: Michael, you have become silent, and you do not teach advocacy for My children? By your life, I shall teach it. I speak regarding them in righteousness and save them, as it is said: 'I speak in righteousness, mighty to save.' In what righteousness? ... In the merit that you performed, that you received the Torah, for were it not so, where would My kingdom be? etc.*" See there.

Meaning, that He shall find merit in Israel, even through the acceptance of the Torah alone—even if they do not fulfill His commandments so much—because by means of this His kingdom was made known to the whole world, as is known.

As is brought also in Tosafos (Avodah Zarah 3a), regarding that which we say there: "*Heaven and earth are affected involved in their testimony.*" And if you say, behold there is a great proof that Israel fulfilled the Torah, for behold heaven and the earth exist? And it can be said that they do not concern themselves with this proof, etc. And further it can be said that they exist by virtue of what Israel accepted, as he said: If Israel accepts the Torah, see there. That is to say, that the condition was on the acceptance alone.[^1]

And this is what the liturgical poet said: "*In the absence of an advocate of righteousness,*" etc. Meaning, on the Day of Judgment when the Angel Michael, the advocate of Israel, does not want to stand against the one who tells of transgression...

We request from before the Holy One, Blessed be He: "*tell Jacob a matter of statute and judgment,*" etc. That is to say, to mention before the judges on high the merit of the acceptance of statute and judgment, which is the Torah, for the condition was on the acceptance alone, because by means of it His kingdom was made known in all the world...

Then "*justify us in judgment, O King of Judgment,*" that is to say, that they shall be convinced to know that the righteousness is with us, for only in the merit of Jacob and his sons was His kingdom made known... and understand this.

Again, in another manner the idea in this can be said, regarding what he said: "*In the absence of an advocate of righteousness against the one who tells of transgression,*" etc. It is according to what is brought in books regarding what our Sages of blessed memory said: (Sanhedrin 17a): "*A Sanhedrin that all saw the defendant as liable, they exempt him; what is the reason? Since it is learned as a tradition to delay the judgment to make for him merit, and these did not see merit for him, see there.*"

For the intention is, since the tradition is according to the Holy Torah, what it said: (Devarim 13:15): "*And you shall inquire and search and ask diligently, etc.*"

That the obligation on the court is to seek merit for the one on trial, and in a place where there was not one of the Sanhedrin who would find for him any merit to justify him, and to find in him an act that would advocate well for him against the "*teller of transgression*"... this is a sign and a wonder that they did not act according to the mind of the Torah; therefore they exempt him.

And this is the intention of the liturgical poet in what he said: "*In the absence of an advocate of righteousness,*" etc. That is to say, if there is not one among the judges on high who finds for us merit against the "*teller of transgression*"—meaning that they all judge toward liability...

We request from before the Holy One, Blessed be He: "*Tell Jacob a matter of statute and judgment.*" That is to say, to mention before the judges on high the merit of that which is a statute in the judgment of the Torah: that a court which has judged everyone toward liability must exempt the defendant.

"*And justify us in judgment, O King of Judgment.*" That is to say, that they shall be convinced to know that the righteousness is with the Holy One, Blessed be He; for they cannot judge only toward liability. What is the reason? Since it is learned as a tradition to delay the judgment to make for him merit, and these judges did not see merit for him... and understand this.
