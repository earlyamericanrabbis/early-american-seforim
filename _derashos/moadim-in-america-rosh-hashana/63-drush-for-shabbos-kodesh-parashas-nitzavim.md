(Before the Selichos, 5665/1905)

"*Ye are standing this day all of you, etc.*" And after he testified to them with the curses of the Covenant, he said: "*And it shall come to pass, when all these things are come upon thee... and thou shalt return unto thy heart... and shalt return unto Hashem thy God.*"

It has already been established from the early ones to arise at the awakening of the morning to increase in Selichos and supplications, and to request from before Him, may He be blessed, the grace of atonement for our sins, for they are numerous. This is truly an exceedingly auspicious hour, as the scholars of truth wrote, for then the Attribute of Kindness prevails. And upon it the prophet Yirmiyah said (Eichah 3:19): "*Arise, cry out in the night, at the beginning of the watches; pour out thy heart like water before the face of the Lord; lift up thy hands toward Him for the life of thy young children, that faint for hunger at the head of every street.*"

And we shall return to the order of the parashah: "*Ye are standing this day*" (Rashi, of blessed memory): like this day which is permanent, and it grows dark and illuminates, so did He illuminate for you, and so is He destined to illuminate for you. And so it shall be forever; this matter will not change until the end of all generations. Whenever, Heaven forbid, the Children of Yisrael turn away from the words of this Torah, He testified to them with blessings and curses, and this will force them to return to Him, may He be blessed, as He promised us: "*And it shall come to pass, when all these things are come upon thee... and thou shalt return unto thy heart, and shalt return unto Hashem thy God, and shalt hearken to His voice... that then Hashem thy God will turn thy captivity, etc.*"

And for this, the prophet warned the nation concerning the requesting of His kindnesses, may He be blessed, and he will guide us how to pray before Him and with what we should greet His face first, to purify the vessels of speech and the heart—the advocates of uprightness wherewith we come before Him to request on behalf of ourselves and on behalf of our nation at the time opportune for this. And this is what he said: "*Arise, cry out in the night, etc.*"

And in Chovos Ha-Levavos (Gate of Love of Hashem, Chapter 6): "*in the night,*" because the prayer of the night is purer than the prayer of the day in several ways. For a person at night is more free from the distraction of friends who would appoint him and companions who visit him, and from creditors who would demand from him. Also, the senses of a person rest at night because he does not see what would distract him, and does not hear what would interrupt him. And it is far from hypocrisy due to the small number of those sitting with him at night. And isolation is better at night, like every lover with his beloved, and one who desires with his desired object, as the prophet said (Yeshayah 26): "*With my soul have I desired Thee in the night.*" And it is said: "*By night on my bed I sought him whom my soul loveth.*"

And it has already been mentioned also in the songs of David: "*I have remembered Thy name, O Hashem, in the night,*" "*At midnight I will rise to give thanks unto Thee,*" "*I anticipated the dawning of the morning, and cried,*" "*Mine eyes anticipated the night-watches,*" "*In the day of my distress... my hand was poured out in the night and slacked not*". For this, the prophet advises the masters of repentance: "*Arise, cry out in the night, at the beginning of the watches*"—you shall arise from your sleep to cry out and to pray to Hashem, in that very watch which is the first and primary one, which is more prepared than the entire night to draw a cord of kindness, and at that hour the prayer and the cry are exceedingly auspicious before Him, may He be blessed.

But before we approach to pray, let us turn, please, to look at our advocates of uprightness! The advocates of uprightness in prayer are five: the hands, the eyes, the mouth, the heart, and the tongue.

The hands—we found (Melachim I 8): "*And Shlomo stood before the altar of Hashem... and spread forth his hands toward heaven.*" But if the hands are not clean, they will further arouse prosecution, as the prophet said (Yeshayah 1): "*And when ye spread forth your hands, I will hide Mine eyes from you, etc.*"

The eyes—"*Unto Thee lift I up mine eyes, O Thou that dwellest in the heavens.*" But also opposite it in sin: "*haughty eyes,*" "*The eye that mocketh at his father, etc.*"

The mouth—"*And my mouth shall declare Thy praise.*" And opposite it: "*and every mouth speaketh wantonness.*"

The tongue—"*And my tongue shall talk of Thy righteousness.*" And opposite it: "*What shall be given unto thee, or what shall be done more unto thee, thou false tongue?*"

The heart—"*My heart and my flesh cry out for the living God.*" And opposite it: "*Every one that is proud in heart is an abomination to Hashem.*"

And this is what the prophet said to them (Yeshayah 59): "*For your hands are defiled with blood... your lips have spoken lies, your tongue muttereth wickedness... their thoughts are thoughts of iniquity.*" Therefore, we must purify beforehand these advocates of uprightness, before we draw near to request mercy upon ourselves and upon our young children and our infants.

And with what shall we wash them to purify them before Hashem? With the tear of our eyes. For for the most part, tears will come from the feeling of regret and abandonment of sin, which comes from the depth of the heart until his eyes stream with water that is desired and accepted to purify every taint. As Chazal said: "*The gates of tears were not locked.*" This is the meaning of the verse: "*Arise, cry out in the night, at the beginning of the watches; pour out thy heart like water before the face of the Lord.*" And so he said (Iyov 16): "*My advocates are my friends; mine eye poureth out tears unto God.*"

"*Lift up thy hands toward Him for the life of thy young children.*" As it is written: "*and recompensest the iniquity of the fathers into the bosom of their children.*" Chazal said: when they are small, they are seized for the iniquity of their fathers. Therefore, lift up toward Him your hands—the vessels of action that belong to you, which are the palms of the hands. Which until now were at the lowest level—let them be lifted, give them elevation and upliftment, to raise them upward for the service of Hashem. "*Lift up thy hands toward Him*" (to pray) "*for the life of thy young children that faint for hunger*" (for sustenance) "*at the head of every street.*"

"*Behold, the days come, saith the Lord God, that I will send a famine in the land, not a famine of bread, nor a thirst for water, but of hearing the words of Hashem*" (Amos 8:11). The prophet prophesied for the distant future, that days will yet come (in our time) when they will lack nothing—even meat, even wine and strong drink they will be satiated with—but the hunger and the thirst will be to hear the true word of Hashem. For those who stand at the head of the people to open their ears to discipline will preach emptiness and foolishness in order to find favor in the eyes of their congregation.

And this is what Yirmiyah said: "*Arise, cry out in the night, etc.*" When you arise in the watches of the night to request forgiveness and atonement for your iniquities, then "*pour out thy heart like water before the face of the Lord for the life of thy young children*"—specifically upon their souls that are fainting for hunger, Heaven forbid, "*not a famine of bread, nor a thirst for water, etc.*" "*Lift up thy hands toward Him for the life of thy young children, etc., at the head of every street.*"

You will see that the soul of your young children is fainting for hunger for the word of Hashem. There you will see how they desecrate *Shabbos Kodesh* by kindling fire: children of the poor will roam on Shabbos Kodesh in the streets to sell newspapers; children of the wealthy will run on bicycles and perform every forbidden thing. "Lift up thy hands toward Him for the life of these young children of yours!" Parents! Give your eyes to guide them in the path of the Torah and fear of Heaven, and please entreat the face of Hashem that He turn back the heart of your children to Him, to love and to fear Hashem.

And without doubt, in our awakening the dawn for the awakening of the morning to pray and to supplicate the face of Hashem for the soul of our young children—and on the condition that we, from our side, guard the steps of our children and habituate them in the path of the Torah and the fear of the Almighty—then He too, may He be blessed, will awaken to hasten the morning light of our redemption and the ransom of our soul.

As the Psalmist said (Tehillim 130): "*My soul waiteth for the Lord more than watchmen for the morning.*" He means: in my soul, I returned to Hashem to be among the watchmen for the morning—meaning, from those who awaken before the dawn for His service, may He be blessed. Then we can trust in kindness, and to guard and to hope for the morning light of our redemption. And therefore: "*Let Yisrael hope in Hashem, etc. And He will redeem Yisrael from all his iniquities.*" And we shall merit to a year of life and good sustenance, to a year of redemption for us and for all Yisrael, Amen and Amen.
