"*Let thy garments be always white*".

Fellow Israelites! The goodness of Him who spoke and creation was, has permitted us to cross the threshold of another year.

If on looking back, none of us can say that the path gone over, has been perfectly smooth, that none there have proceeded without the least hindrance, and always, succeeded in reaching the haven of their desires, yet all of us have cause nevertheless to thank our invisible Guide who led us safely, from dangers, which our short sight had not perceived. How many sore trials of body and mind – frail and fallible as we are – might have been our sad lot to bear, if the Almighty had not averted them unknowingly to ourselves!

And to the reverse, how very many were the tangible blessings showered down upon us individually, and collectively. We were favored with a mild winter – inexpressibly beneficial to those who depend on their daily labor for their daily bread, and principally to the modest poor.

We enjoyed a summer almost unprecedented in its luxurious fruitfulness, and free entirely from local diseases which have often before "*Stalked in darkness, and wasted at noon.*"[^1] We have profited by a revival of commerce and confidence throughout this gloriously reunited country of our love, and for the absence of political agitations which, alas! insatiable greed on one side and semi-savageness on the other, have stirred up in the Eastern Continent to the misery of thousands of families that have suddenly been plunged into a sea of desolation.

Notwithstanding some private pecuniary losses and domestic bereavements, which must call forth the sympathies of brethren and coreligionists, we may acknowledge, here in this freemen holy habitation, and in the language of our Sacred Scripture, that the Lord was pleased to crown our year with His loving-kindness and tender mercies; that He has satisfied our mouths with the rich yieldings of the soil... and that "*He has not dealt with us after our sins, nor requited us according to our trespasses*"[^2]. But now the year 5642[^3], during which we experienced on earth the goodness of our Father in Heaven, has sunk into eternity. Another has just arisen, whose arrival we have greeted with devotional psalmody: “<span dir="rtl">תחל שנה וברכותיה</span>”.

We do not know, and fortunate it is that we don't, whether the Disposer of human events will permit us again to see the revolving of a coming cycle of months. Should He, in His unsearchable wisdom, ordain otherwise, are we ready to respond to the summons; each of us answering: "Here am I O Lord! Into Thy hand I commit my spirit"? I mean, That,—aside from the anguish natural in parting, even temporarily, with creatures we fondly love,—we feel light; that we are not oppressed by the weight of shame for duties neglected by the burden of wrongs committed, which would prevent our rising into the realms of bliss. In other words: followed the recommendations of the oldest preacher:

<span dir="rtl">בכל עת יהיו בגדיך לבנים</span>

"*Let thy garments be always white*"?

My fellow Believers, that it is far from my thoughts to sadden the hearts of the very oldest among my brothers and sisters in faith. Not because the Solemn Season which we have assembled to celebrate reminds us of the swiftness of time, do I predict a void in our midst, which, if I myself am spared, I would infinitely lament. You will agree that none dies because his legal adviser proposes that he should set his affairs in order and make his will.

I have heard of some who, whenever in the act of signing their testamentary document, dropped the pen in terror. But they died intestate, and the posthumous good deeds with which they intended to purchase redemption, remained unfulfilled. We should not act so unwisely. A future which is most certain, however deferred, awaits us. Does not prudence dictate that we provide for it early, while enjoying vigorous health, while in the full possession of our mental faculties, while we are our own selves, can map out the course to pursue, so that, though prepared to meet the inevitable in peaceful serenity, we may, still continuing to live, live nobly? The Divine Messenger may not arrive for scores of years, but when he does come he will find us in the fitting state, pictured by the Sages in one of their allegories. For the ancient teachers of Israel did not describe the passage from our existence in the flesh to an existence in spirit as painfully difficult. In their mind it was pleasantly easy, provided that our conscience is light. As an illustration of their meaning, the following parable. "*A King sent word to a certain number of his officers, that they might expect to be invited to a royal banquet. He did not specify the day nor the hour. At that the thoughtful ones dressed themselves becomingly and continued to do so in anticipation of the call. Others did not think it worth their while to get ready so soon. The summons might not come for weeks or months. "No doubt, we shall learn in time, when the King expects us", so they argued. But suddenly a herald cries: "the invited officers are wanted immediately*". Those who had kept themselves well dressed for the occasion, found the gates of the palace wide open. They were ushered in amidst universal greetings. Those who were caught unprepared, stood back bent under the weight of their disgrace. What would they not have given for a few moments that they might change garments. Too late, the order of the Ruler must be obeyed instantly.

You know, my fellow believers, how to apply the legend. Happy \[are\] the creatures who can be always presentable, who remain in attendance for the call of the King of Kings. Does it mean that we must fold our arms in complete inactivity, expecting to have soon to leave what we have long labour'd for?

Not at all. To add to our energies, to our industry and perseverance and not to disqualify man for his vocation, have the Sages instructions to by their allegorys. The idea that between life and death there may be but a step, must urge us to proceed steadily in our good works, which should never, if possible, be left to others to finish.

Of course, they who believe that the return of our perishable body to its primitive elements, is also the extinction of the mind which the body action of the soul which stirs within us ennobling sentiments, such ones, if there are any who have been so madly, pervertedly reasoned themselves into a denial of immortality, may sink into idiocy at the thought of being suddenly called away.

Not so those who feel, as I do, that earthly life is only one of the stages of man's activity, that when removed from it, we are transferred into another and higher sphere, higher in proportion to mental and spiritual powers, we have sharpened by exercise here below; those feel so, will redouble their efforts not to be found wasting away or misusing the faculties given by the Sovereign of worlds <span dir="rtl">רבון העולמים</span> given for the performance of a terrestrial mission. Those who so feel, will not allow wrong to lodge a single night with them, falsehood to abide a moment in their midst. They will not say: "Time is at our command. We shall engage it in the service of our passions. If our neighbor is injured thereby, we will make reparation for it at some future day". No, at once they will put on righteousness, and never take off that robe of spotless purity. Proceeding in their career with honesty and singleness of purpose they will enjoy here an existence which was granted in loan given us by a loving Creator, for work and its attending satisfaction, for social intercourse and its accompanying improvements, they will still be ready for the transition into another existence, where God will assign to each a heavenly task suitable to each one's ability. That task is now incomprehensible to us, as the babe newly born cannot understand the future in store for him, yet it is certain, as certain it is, that the Universe has issued forth by the will of a Being most wise and Merciful.

Oh may we who acknowledge only that adorable Being, be worthy of Him. May we who have gathered in solemn assembly to proclaim Adonai our King, our Father and Saviour, deserve His blessings this year and for a long term of years by the virtues He delights in, by probity, chasteness and truthfulness. And Thou O Maker of heaven and earth, who hast kept us alive to this day, let the memory of Thy worshippers ascend before Thee, and apportion to them from Thy exhaustless treasures health and competence, domestic joys and the capacity to ascend higher in a sphere of spiritual understanding. Grant that we all may act approvingly in Thy sight, that when the summons to Thy Royal Presence is heard, the herald Thou sendest forth may find that we had been in readiness, arrayed in snowy innocence, that agreeably to the counsel of the inspired preacher of old: “*Let thy garments be always white*”. So may it be.

Amen
