*"In this month renew your deeds; with the shofar in this month, improve your deeds. The Holy One, Blessed be He, said to Yisrael: If you have improved your deeds, behold I become for you like this shofar—just as this shofar takes in through this \[narrow end\] and brings out through this wide end, so too I arise from the Throne of Judgment and sit upon the Throne of Mercy, and I overturn for you the Attribute of Judgment into the Attribute of Mercy"*

(Vayikra Rabbah, Parashah 29).

Honored masters! We are standing currently in matters elevated toward the new year, which our holy Torah commanded us to meet with the sound of the shofar, and our hearts are filled with hope and fear, without knowing what it will bring to us—the crushed and afflicted nation, the nation whose stature is bent from the burden of its toil—whether healing in its wings for the entire nation in general and for each one of us in particular, or, Heaven forbid, breaking upon breaking which the past years brought, and wound upon our old wounds that have not yet been healed, and over which skin has not yet formed, and the opening of the blows has not scabbed over, wherewith we were struck in the house of those who love us and the house of those who hate us.

Behold, I see that after each one of us has poured out in this holy house the bitter feelings of his heart, suddenly a hush has been cast, as if each one is anticipating and waiting to hear by way of a voice from On High his destiny and the destiny of his nation for the days that will bring the future. But a voice from On High they will not let our ears hear; rather, only the sound of the shofar they will let our ears hear in just a little bit.

And what can we hear from this mute shofar, in which there is no speech? And what will this shofar teach us? "*A remembrance of teruah, a holy convocation,*" our holy Torah said. But what will it remind us of? To remind us of that which passed over us—we do not need its sound for that; the wounds of our hearts are so deep from the past years until there is no need to remind us of them, and would that we could forget them. And the future, behold, this mute shofar will not reveal to us, and just as before hearing the sound of the shofar, so too after this our futures are covered in fog, and we remain suspended and standing between hope and fear without knowing.

And how wondrous are the words of the Midrash Rabbah there: "*All year long Yisrael are caught in avonos and entangled in troubles, and on Rosh Hashanah they take a shofar and blow, and they are remembered before the Holy One, Blessed be He, and He forgives them.*" Where are the forgivenesses and where are the pardons that we achieved from the past years, during which also there was no lack of a multitude of shofar blowings? Has not each and every year from the past years brought us only illness and pain, physical pains and spiritual pains? And once again, we are waiting toward the sound of the shofar with feelings of holy trepidation, as if only upon it depends our destiny and the destiny of our entire nation, and we cherish this moment as if it is more precious in our eyes than entire years?...

Indeed, these moments are precious, and such moments—where a person gives them a greater value than entire years—we find occasionally in life. For example, at the time of the military conscription, we encounter in such districts at every step and turn: here we encounter one man who submitted a request to exempt his son from army service, as he is an only child to him, and upon him his life depends, and separation from him is as difficult for him as separating from life, and he is suspended between hope and fear to hear the results of his request, and each and every moment is considered like a year in his eyes.

And here is found one man who submitted a request to exempt his son from army service, as he is the one who feeds and sustains him, and if they take his son from him, the staff of his bread will be broken. And here there will happen before us a woman of aggrieved spirit, standing and waiting to hear the results of her request which she submitted to exempt her son, since she already brought one son as a sacrifice upon the altar of love for the homeland. And for all these people, the moments are more precious to them than many years.

And if for them those moments are precious which can bring them either great pleasure or brokenness of heart and grief, how much more so for a man who stands to be judged for some iniquity whose punishment is the deprivation of life, and by the breath of the lips of the judges his life is suspended—of what account in his eyes is the final moment of the results of his judgment? And how much more so for a man who stands to be judged in a matter whose punishment is the death penalty, and along with this, the course of his judgment was also not permitted to be revealed even to the prisoner himself, and he does not know what his destiny will be. And at the time when he is led in a black wagon, he does not know whither he is led, and if this wagon is not for him the crossing that crosses from life to death; who can estimate his inner feelings in these moments?

And how much more so how deep and pure are our feelings now, when all these descriptions are present by us, as we stand ready to meet the new year without knowing what it will bring to each one of us—whether in its pouch are hidden for us life and the sustenance of life, or, Heaven forbid, the garments of the dead and the sacks of the poor? And even more do knock our hearts, knowing that all the misfortunes of life and the mishaps of time depend only upon us ourselves, for "*the foolishness of man perverts his way, yet his heart rages against Hashem*"; and who can know the future destiny of our nation in general and the destiny of each one of us in particular, since it is a great principle that one who suspends his fate upon his own understanding has no choice. But after all these, the question stands in its place: what does the shofar let us hear?

But, honored masters, if we make our ears like a funnel to hear the sound of the shofar with deep contemplation, we will hear from it a great deal and we will understand even more. We will understand from it—if we only know how to hear—the destiny of our futures. For though the words of Chazal are sincere when they say, "*and on Rosh Hashanah they take a shofar and blow, and they are remembered before the Holy One, Blessed be He, and He forgives them,*" yet all this is if we understand the sound of the shofar which teaches us that the happiness of each and every man in particular, and the happiness of every nation in general, is according to his conduct.

And just as with a shofar—though the pleasantness of its sound depends much on the quality of the shofar in which the blower blows, and the sound of the blower who blows in a good shofar will be more pleasant to us than if he blew in a bad and ruined shofar, and the sound that a person produces by means of a shofar will be more pleasant to us than if he produced it with his mouth without a shofar, because by introducing the sound into one side of the shofar and bringing it out from the second side, the sound of the person changes for the good and pleasant—yet the main thing depends, the pleasantness of the sound, on the blower himself: if his heart is a healthy and strong heart, then the sound of the shofar is exceedingly pleasant, and if his heart is a weak heart, then his sound is weak, and the sound of the shofar will not be pleasant to our ears. So too is the destiny of our futures: it depends a great deal only upon us ourselves.

True, much depends—the happiness of each and every man and each and every nation—upon the causes and the happenstances, messengers of On High to roam the earth, and which a man of short sight and frail strength cannot remove from his path; but more than anything, the destiny of each and every man and nation depends upon his conduct, upon the orders of his life, upon his outlook on life and on the happenstances that surround him, and upon his preparation of how to utilize all the good and bad happenstances for his benefit and for the benefit of his nation.

Yes, honored masters, if we desire to know what the days coming upon us will be for us, we must learn from this shofar—whose pleasantness of sound depends upon the blower himself, in his knowledge to select for himself a good shofar, in the health of his heart, and in his knowledge of how to utilize the shofar—so too is our destiny dependent only upon us ourselves.

And this is the idea of the Midrash that we placed at the opening of our words: "*In this month renew your deeds; with the shofar in this month, improve your deeds. The Holy One, Blessed be He, said to them: If you have improved your deeds, behold I become for you like this shofar—just as this shofar takes in through this and brings out through this, so too I arise, etc.*" That is to say: just as the blowing of the shofar depends upon the blower and upon the shofar, so too the forgiveness and the pardon depend only upon you, together and equally with the side causes.

Regarding the shofar: just as a shofar whose mouth is coated with gold is invalid, so too if all our aspirations and words are only for a despicable purpose to acquire silver and gold, it is invalid. Just as a shofar inside a shofar is invalid, so too we must know that having one thing in the mouth and one thing in the heart—two voices different from each other—is invalid. Just as a shofar where one widened the narrow part and narrowed the wide part contrary to its greatness and nature is invalid, so too one who goes against the laws of life and against the laws of our holy Torah, which is compatible with life, is invalid. Just as a shofar from the horn of a cow—a straight horn, a horn that stands to gore and poke anyone close to it—is invalid, so too we must know that we need to be pleasant one to his fellow and to help his counterpart, and not to poke one his neighbor, and if he does not do so, he is invalid...

Away with dispute! Away with licentiousness! Which are like rot to the House of Yisrael; away with lack of order, away with the lack of preparation for life! Away with idleness. Let us unite, with our youth and with our elders, and let us work with one shoulder for the benefit of our nation, and then we will be able to go with a courageous heart toward the new year which is coming upon us for good, in hope to reap in joy that which we sow at this time in tears, Amen!
