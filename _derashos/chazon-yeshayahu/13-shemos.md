*“And Pharaoh said ‘Who is the Lord, that I should hearken unto His voice to let Israel go? I know not the Lord, and moreover I will not let Israel go.”* (Shemos 5:2)

*“Said Rabbi Hiyya bar Abba: When Moses and Aaron came to Pharaoh’s Court, his birthday was being celebrated, and all the kings in the world had come, each one bearing a crown to place on his head as cosmocrat. Moses and Aaron stood in line and waited. The guardsmen, seeing them, and not knowing who they were, informed Pharaoh of their presence.*

*‘Have they a crown for me?’ “No”, answered the guardsmen. ‘Then let them wait till after the celebration. ‘When they were finally admitted to Pharaoh’s presence, he asked them: ‘What is your mission?’ And they answered: ‘ The great God of the Hebrews commissioned us to convey this message to you saying: ‘Let my people go, that they may hold a feast unto Me in the wilderness.’ ‘Who is the Lord, that I should hearken unto His voice to le Israel go? asked Pharaoh. Said Rabbi Levi: Before propounding this question, Pharaoh produced a register of all the gods, and called out the name of each god, saying: ‘the god of Edom, of Moab, of Zidon I find here, but the One you speak of is not recorded here.’ And Moses answered him, saying: The gods in your register are dead, and therefore, need a register to have their names identified; but our God, is a living God, who is the Eternal.’ Jeremiah pointed at it:*

*But the Lord God is the true God,*

*He is the living God, the everlasting King”* (Yalkut Shimony, Exodus, 175)

This Pharaoh knew not Joseph, but knew how to rule an oligarchy. Of him the Agadah speaks as the cosmic or t par-excellence. There is a vast difference between ordering people to obey man-made laws, and ordering them to observe divine laws. The ancient Egyptians knew the science of government as well as the science of embalming. They also were great builders, and were jealous of any other race which ventured a building program of its own. But everything was done in the name of some deity, for the sake of religion, to please the gods, either to appease their anger, or to add to their pleasures. Every alien people, conquered by the Egyptians, were reduced to slavery, but the slaves were told that they were working for the gods who ordered them to obey their taskmasters. Religion in Egypt was not only an arm of the State, but its entire backbone.

The drama of Israel in his first exile unfolds itself upon a scene of antiquity the like of which this planet never again beheld. The attempt of Joseph to inaugurate an era free from the influence of the myriads of gods and to secularize government by giving the priests, the representatives of the gods (See Bereishis 47.22) their due of the government’s coffers, so everybody may know earthly and matter-of-fact the sources of their divine authority actually were, did not last long. Without the aid of the gods, Egypt could not hold its commanding position. Besides, Joseph was far ahead of his time.

When Joseph and his generation died, and the people forgot that the gods had nothing to do with saving the world from famine, but that it all was the work of one man, new devices to re-instate the gods in their former favor were found; the first step had a new feature, namely; Pharaoh should thenceforward be the real god of the world, in addition to being the cosmocrat over the secular affairs of mankind.

Just then Egypt was sorely in need of man-power. Perhaps, because a man-god was placed at the head of all other gods or perhaps, because of the birth of the germ of disintegration, due to an overambitious program of conquest, Egyptian sovereignty, prior to the accession of the Pharaoh who knew not Joseph suffered a death-darting blow from many directions. Open rebellion was threatened in the subjugated Canaan and in several other provinces. Soldiers were needed abroad; but men were needed at home for police and defense duty, as well as for work. Recorded history informs us that Egypt had just launched a great boom in pyramid building, in order to meet the competition of other growing peoples, who had like ambitions.

The people of Israel, meantime, had grown quite numerous, and its influence was being felt. The danger line was reached when the work of the tribe of Levi became known. Levi had succeeded Jacob to be head of the school founded by Shem and Eber, and established in Goshen by Judah before Jacob had come thither. That school had grown in all branches and among the students were men of great skill in all branches of artisanship, as well as in learning. Pharaoh the cosmocrat, who wanted to be the head of all gods, had no trouble in finding ready man-power in Goshen. He knew that the slave, by forced labor, would acquire enough ignorance to believe readily in the divinity of the one who gives him a stomach-full of food. It was a diabolical scheme under a mantle of divinity. A meretricious plan introduced as a sacrosanct formula.

Thus were the children of Israel cast into the furnace of affliction for a double purpose, first to furnish man-power for Egypt, and second, to quit reaching out their brain for heavenly knowledge. But the authorities were wise enough to enslave the tribe of Levy - the learned man of the Yeshivah. The tribe of Levi, because it had devoted itself solely to the religious needs of the people, and to study, was therefore completely separated from the rest of the tribes, in that none of its members were drafted to the army of labor.

Then means were adopted to have the Israelites assimilated by a very ingenious means; The man children were condemned to be drowned, so that the Hebrew women would have to intermarry, would be forced to it by having them nationalized. The existing make population, would, in time die out, and, in but a single generation, their program would thus be carried through in all its details.

And the Egyptians employed science and psychology in order to achieve their goal. For instance, women were employed to carry around crying Egyptian babies throughout the Hebrew neighborhoods, in order to discover hidden Hebrew babies, but it is the nature of a baby to commence to cry when hearing the crying voice of another baby. That is the reason why the mother of Moses “*could no longer hide him.*” (See Yalkut Shimony, Exodus, 166) Besides, what difference would it have made in the end, whether the male children were cast into the Nile, or into the stream of Egyptian idolatrous demoralization?

Moses and Aaron went to the court of Pharaoh when the celebration of his birthday attracted delegations from all over the world. Each delegation brought a crown, as a present to Pharaoh, a symbol that his authority was recognized by the particular country which presented the crown. Moses and Aaron were asked by the guards as to the nature of their mission, and as to whom they represented. They had heard that each delegation claimed to represent a certain god, usually the god of the nation they were delegated by. Moses and Aaron, too, must have told the guards that they represented the God of the tribe of Levi—the only people of the children of Israel who had not been thrown in the Egyptian forage of Idolatry. Pharaoh, when informed of their presence, of course, thought that they actually represented some nation. He didn’t know all nations by name, though he claimed cosmicrometric powers over all. He, therefore, merely asked: “*Have they a crown for me?*” The crown, of course, would tell the name and position of the country and nation they represented. But such credentials Moses and Aaron had not with them. The tribe of Levi never adorned Idolatry with garlands. Pharaoh considered their request and not being contrary to court procedure, they were admitted. Moses knew that Pharaoh, claiming divine powers as well as earthly dominion, would not listen to them at all if a petition would be presented to him to let Israel worship God on his territory. Moses knew that he considered every territory, where there is even the smallest number of people, his ground of propaganda for cosmicrometrical purposes. He, therefore, requested to have the permission granted to Israel to worship their God in the Wilderness—a place where none of his agents can possibly establish propaganda headquarters.

But Pharaoh minded not the territory; he wanted to know, Who is God? A god without a specific territory was news to him. Not knowing all gods by name he produced the register. No, he found Him not there.

Moses and Aaron had accomplished their mission; they were sent, and they gained admittance; Pharaoh had heard that there is a God; all that remained for them to do, is not to be silent when idolatry was praised in their presence. But Moses had the skill of diplomatic language. “The gods in your register, are dead and therefore, need a register to have their names identified; but our God, is a living God, who is the Eternal.

Wisely did the Midrashic authors quote Jeremiah:

*“But the Lord is the true God,*

*He is the living God, and the everlasting King.”*

A state supported by dead gods can only be mummified.
