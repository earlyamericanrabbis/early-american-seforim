*“And Bezalel made the ark”* (Shemos 37:1)

*“It is written: ‘Give to a wise man, and he will be yet wiser’ (Proverbs 19.9); that might be said of Bezalel. When Moses instructed him to build the Tabernacle, Bezalel asked:*

*“What purpose is the Tabernacle for?”*

*For a double purpose ‘, answered Moses; ‘to have God’s Shekinah dwell there- in, and to have the children of Israel gather there to be instructed in the Torah!’ And Bezalel asked:*

*“Wherein will the Torah itself be deposited?”*

*‘When we will have the Tabernacle ready, we shall construct an ark’, answered Moses. But Bezalel commenced to construct the ark first, even as it is said: ‘And Bezalel made the ark.’*

*“There were three crowns in the Tabernacle: (1) On the altar; (2) on the Table; and (3) on the Ark. The one on the Altar, Aaron’s merits won for him; the one on the Table, David’s merits won for him, but the one on the Ark remains the property of everybody, and whosoever desires it may attain merit and claim it.”* (Yalkut Shimony, Shemos 37:414; Yoma 72b)

*“Moses charged Bezalel to begin with the building of the Taber- nacle, and he began with the Ark. Why? Because that was the place where in the Torah was treasured, from whence light comes to the temporal as well as to the eternal worlds.”* (Midrash Tanhuma, Shemos 37:6)

There are two classes of builders; one takes brick, mortar, a trowel, a plumb-line and whatever operative skill he had at his command and builds a house; another, takes words, classifies them, makes sentences and paragraphs and delivers them in good order to his fellow-men, and in the process of time builds character. Both are builders; both deserve reward, if they do their work well; one is an operative builder, the other a speculative builder. But there is a vast difference between the two builders; the work of the operative builder can be destroyed by many causes — fire, water, wind, war, or time; the work of the speculative builder can never be destroyed; a contribution to the intellect of mankind, to the civilization of the world, to the moral standard of life, to the friendly intercourse between man and man, nation and nation, which is the edifice of the speculative builder, is indestructible, immortal.

Man, as he is, appears to be a unit; but within his physical and mental makeup there are four distinct beings constantly striving for supremacy; the dual personality of man is in reality a quadruplication of four distinctly striving elements. There are: (1) his natural state, minus any human acquisition; (2) his human acquisition; (3) his power for good; (4) his power for evil.

All that which is visible to science is given as the source of all these four elements struggling within man. All of these are acquired by training, by experience, by environment, and by reflection. The physicist does not look toward heaven for the responsibility of human conduct. The behavior of individuals springs from natural causes; therefore, do they attribute all evil to the varicolored systems of social order in the several parts of the five continents.

Out of the laboratory of science comes forth the periodic eruption in the form of revolution, or the hurricane of war, or the invasion of new movements, which cover forever bygone ideas, and bury erstwhile conceptions of life and death.

There has ever been a struggle between religion, and science, and the many upheavals of religious organizations throughout history were caused by the element of fear—fear for the infidel, for the atheist, for the materialist, for science.

But in all Jewish sacred history we find no instance where research was forbidden in religious grounds. In fact, the very foundation of Judaism rests upon knowledge. “*It is mandatory to know that there is a God.*”

However, in the final analysis, the real source of all social evil is found in the historic truth that the several dominating religions retained many elements of ancient mythology, of paganism, and of mysticism, based upon a form of belief which science cannot endorse.

That is why the world witnesses today the strangest of events in history among the one hundred and fifty millions of people in Russia. From an Oligarchy which had encouraged the actual belief in a corporeal god, in icons, in the divine right of the Czars; the Russian people changed into materialists, and are waging a war of extermination against all religious denominations.

In Judaism no such condition could ever be dreamed of. The form of belief that was evolved by Abraham is based upon knowledge; the harmony and oneness of nature proclaim the Unity of God. The commandment to “*KNOW THAT THERE IS A GOD*” throws the burden of research, of knowledge, of scientific investigation upon every intelligent man and woman. With the doctrines of Monotheism as its firmest pillar, Judaism could not go far afield in its scope of dogma, rite, faith, or formalism. All laws had to be founded upon a human basis, for human beings to understand, and to be able to apply them to daily life upon earth. The basic philosophy of Judaism is to establish the divine conception of Heaven upon an earthly, or natural, foundation, to remove from its path every unintelligible precept, and to have human life upon earth regulated on a human scale of justice.

The world is coming nearer to that conception as the centuries roll by. There are neither mistakes nor contradictions in our Torah; but there is yet a vast amount of current ignorance with respect to many parts of the Holy Writ. The fact that in all know-literary productions of the world’s genii, no book can be spoken of as even near to the sacred writings, given sufficient ground upon which to base the pure belief in Revelation.

Moses was not the founder of a new religion; he is spoke of as a law giver, nor, are any miracles attached to either his birth or his life’s work before he encountered the Flame in the Thorn Bush. But he is not believed in because of the tokens, or the signs, or the miracles he was able to perform. “*The historian needs no more evidence than the current testimony of a whole people which was present, and which saw, and which participated, and which transmitted to posterity all of his works*”, says Maimonides,- this is rightly summed up in the Verse:

“*Moses commanded us a law,*

*an inheritance of the congregation of Jacob.*” (Devarim 33:4)

The word Torah means “learning” doctrine, law. It is not a code of religious law alone; it embraces every conceivable action and behavior; man’s relation to his fellow as well as his relation to his Creator.

Moses and Bezalel differed in their opinions as to what was most needed for the preservation of the law. Moses was of the opinion that the Tabernacle, the Tent of Meeting, was the first necessity after Revelation. He, therefore, ordered Bezalel to build the Tabernacle first.

The crown of the Altar, sacrifice, Aaron inherited; the crown of the table, triumph, David inherited; both the Altar and the Table were parts of the furnishings of the Tabernacle. God’s *Shekinah* rested in the Tabernacle; the whole congregation of Israel came to the Tabernacle for instructions. Nevertheless, when Moses told Bezalel that the people would receive instructions in the Torah, Bezalel asked: “*Wherein will the Torah itself be deposited?*” The answer of Moses was: “*When we will have the tabernacle ready, we shall construct an ark.*” But Bezalel, according to the Yalkut and Tanhuma authorities, commenced his work by building the Ark for the Torah, first.

Give to a wise man and he will be yet wiser. That is indeed, to teach us that Bezalel was wise, and had his wisdom increased by commencing work on the Ark before the Tabernacle was constructed. On the Ark was the crown of the Torah; that crown no individual in history inherited; that belongs to all scholars of all generations, to every one who desires to know, to study, to perceive, to pursue and to practice.

Bezalel was wise, because from the Ark came forth a light which shows mankind the path of righteous living on earth, and of pleasing heaven where eternity is treasured.

The Tabernacle no longer exists; but the light which had been placed in the Ark by Bezalel is becoming brighter as the world’s wisdom grows. Indeed, Bezalel constructed an Eternal Edifice.
