*“And Abraham was old, well stricken in age”* (Bereishis 24:1).

*“Some man is well stricken in age, but is not old, and some man is old, but not well stricken in age; but Abraham, our father, was both old and well stricken in age. The Tanaites said: ‘Prior to the advent of Abraham there was no old age in the world; when Abraham came, he prayed to have mercy upon the human race and grant it old age; it is therefore said: ‘ And Abraham was old, well stricken in age.’ This is even as Solomon in his wisdom said:*

*“The hoary head is a crown of glory,*

*It is found in the way of righteousness” (Mishlei 16:31)*

*even as it is said of Abraham: ‘ For I have known him (loved him), to the end that he may command his children and his household after him, that they may keep the way of the Lord, to do righteousness and justice; to the end that the Lord may bring upon Abraham that which He hath spoken of him.’ (Bereishis 18:19) Moreover, ‘And Abraham was old;’ in age he acquired wisdom.”* (Pesikta, Bereishis 24; see also Baba Mazia 87a)

*“Until Abraham, there was no old age; whosoever saw Abraham thought he was seeing Isaac; until Jacob, there was sickness, and death came in the full vigor of life; and Jacob prayed for mercy, and from his day onward, sickness comes before death; until the prophet Elisha’s days, no person ever recovered from a sickness; disease was certain death, and he prayed for mercy, and cure became possible.”* (Sanhedrin, 107b)

It is not a pleasant anticipation to reach old age without visible means of sustenance, without a home and bread. Civilization has produced luxuries beyond the dreams of the most fanciful poets of all ages. People of modest means have to-day more of the comforts of life than the most fastidious rich of bygone generations could obtain with all their wealth. With but a touch of a finger on the wall of one’s dwelling, science brings him heat, light, power, music, news, sermons, orations, entertainments, drama, comedy, tragedy, or the price of quail in Japan, or the eulogy over the remains of a statesman. Distance is nothing at all now; fly, if you are in a great hurry; phone if you must know instantaneously. Science makes the earth produce everywhere, winter or summer, myriads of different fruits, vegetables, cereals, berries, herbs and grasses; electric cooking, ready foodstuffs, refrigeration, elevators, open plumbing, and innumerable other conveniences. Labor-saving operated devices which, if a 19th century baron could rise from his grave, he would believe that he was promoted to a new heaven in the spiritual, and not back in his former material world.

Rejuvenation is a subject now occupying the serious attention of great scientists. Every wasted life, every disappointed career, every dissipated body, and every one else who has no reason at all, would give his all for a rejuvenated life. Even the very poor would be willing to be young once more, although no promise of a better lot in the second course of life be held out to them.

And here we have the testimony of the Talmud and Midrash that Abraham prayed for the whole human race that the blessings of old age be showered upon it from heaven!

Is there a human being who courts sickness? The very suggestion of such an idea would be considered irrational. Fortunes are spent annually in every part of the world to prevent or exterminate disease. And now comes the Talmud and testifies that Jacob prayed that the blessings of sickness be showered upon every human head!

Elisha, with his prophetic wisdom and great spiritual power is easily understood, when he prayed that a cure be found for sickness; but who can justify the prayers of Abraham and Jacob, for old age and for sickness?

The complexities of life, and the various viewpoints of the several races and groups are constantly creating menacing problems which the succeeding generations are called upon to solve. The primitive conception of life and death, past, present and future was so narrow that it will take many more centuries to divorce even the most advanced races from the remnants of its ambiguity, superstition and barbarism. All the polish of civilization is after all, a coloring upon ancient walls, a mere calcimine upon primitive ruins, a mantle around a cave-man skeleton.

Paganism forms the greatest part of human conduct, in its fundamentals even till this day. Expression of the body, in all its ugly possibilities, is considered most modern; whereas, in reality, it is but a poor imitation of the ancient worshipers of Mercury, Baal and Astarte. For instance some dances by some dancers.

Some conceptions of morality or the lack of it, are based upon idolatrous practices of primitive peoples. Modern grounds for divorce, modern methods of punishing criminals, modern means of creating rules of conduct to differentiate between what is a crime and what is not, drawing the line between what is society, blue blood, and aristocratic and what is scum, mongrel and boorish are but printed shadows of the ancient axiom that might is right - *Kol Dealim Gebar*!

The only instance of a total departure from the primitive conception of life and things, is to be found in the life of the Hebrew patriarchate, or, rather, in the Torah, as this life is depicted therein. The fundamental laws of the Torah, when closely examined, will be found to have no connection with any of the evil practices of antiquity. The body and the soul, both, receive due and thorough attention - an entire new foundation for an entire new life.

The primitive boy and the primitive old man had the same view of life and things. Nothing mattered, as long as the sameness of life was not touched. The methods, perhaps, were changed during the earliest centuries, but the practices were the same. The father and son looked upon themselves as of one and the same generation. No progress was possible under such a system.

Under a system where the old and the young have the same viewpoint, where the father and the son look alike, education was not needed, progress was impossible, and morality non-existing.

*“The hoary head is a crown of glory!*

*It is found in the way of righteousness,”*

said Solomon. Indeed, until Abraham there was no hoary head adorned with the crown of glory, for there was none to seek the way of righteousness. When the slave became king because he was stronger, it was his day; there was no age, no wisdom, to judge, to adjust, to elevate. Abraham acquired wisdom in age; he, therefore, prayed to God to have mercy upon the human race and bless it with old age. Man is likened unto a tree; after its service is performed, its mission upon earth is done. So is man; but he must leave some of his experiences to posterity; he must get hoary and leave his crown of glory to his kindred. Abraham and Isaac looked alike; even that was not pleasing to Abraham; the father must feel that he is older, nearer to his end, than his son; the son must survive the father, carry on his worker, further the progress, increase activities and better conditions of life.
