*“Take ye the sum of all, the congregation of the children of Israel, by their families, by their fathers’ houses, according to the number of names, every male by their polls; from twenty years old and upward, all that are able to go forth to war in Israel; ye shall number them by their hosts even thou and Aaron. And with you there shall be a man of every tribe, every one head of his fathers’ house”* (Bamidbar 1:5).

*“This census was taken in order to organize them, under their respective colors, into a military force, whereas the census which had been taken before the construction of the Tabernacle was for the purpose of raising funds for the sockets. The arrangement and order of the census was thus ordered, because they were to march, under their respective flags in the order they had to be enumerated. Gad was counted in with Reuben and Simeon, because, the Holy One, blessed be He! said: ‘Reuben, with his merit as a penitent, and Gad with his physical strength, will be able to give protection to Simeon. Issachar and Zebulun were counted with Judah, because Judah was the ruler of the tribes and Issachar was the scholar of the tribes, and of the scholar it is said: ‘By me kings reign’ (Mishlei 8:15); To them Zebulun was added, because he was the equal partner of Issachar, that is to say, he was the merchant prince who supported Issachar and thereby making his pursuit of scholarship secure. Manasseh and Ephraim were enumerated together because both were destined to destroy Amalek; Asher was counted with Dan, because Dan lived in in the darkness of idolatry, as Micah who set up the graven image, was a descendant of the tribe of Dan, and no one commences to worship idolatry unless he thought of it long before; (See Shoftim 17 - 19) whereas Asher had in his inheritance much ointment, so that Dan’s habitation might be filled with light of pure ointment, of which Asher had an abundance; and, because Naphtali was full of the blessings of God, (See Devarim 33:23) Dan was placed under his banner, so that he might be blessed for his sake. The Levites were not enumerated along with the other tribes, because they were a separate legion in the service of the King of the universe; others, however, assert that the reason the Levites were not enumerated together with the rest of the tribes was, because it was clear to the almighty that all of the enumerated of the other tribes would die out in the wilderness, and that not a soul of the tribe of Levi would die.”* (Midrash Agadah, Bamidbar 1:1-49).

The world, in order to be organized in a civilized manner, must have a population able to take care of all of the needs of human life upon earth. The miner, the baker, the tailor, the cobbler, the physician, the street-cleaner, the engineer, the butcher, the farmer, the architect, the plumber, the grave-digger, the scholar, the jail-keeper, the soldier, the merchant - all are essential elements in a civilized community. Circumstances, environment, ability, or the lack of it, fate, fortune, or rather, Providence, prepares a station in life for all of these different classes, who, altogether constitute the make-up of society.

When Ben Zoma saw a multitude of people on a height at the Temple Mount, he said: “*Blessed be He who is wise to penetrate secrets; and blessed be He who created all these to serve me.*” (Berachos 58a).

One must not be considered a social slave because he makes the shoes for his fellow-beings; others make the rest of the things he needs, but cannot make them himself.

Everyone should toil in happiness and good cheer; his calling, his trade, his work, is of his own choosing. The kind of work each one does, makes no material difference as to his station in life; what really counts, is his contribution to the welfare of the rest of mankind. It is, as of right it ought to be, one great social structure, erected upon the solid foundation of work, and each worker should be content to produce the best in his line, and find happiness in the knowledge that he is a useful member of that great structure.

“*The gods, when they divided betwixt three, This massive universe, hell, heaven, and sea, Each god sat contented upon his throne, And, undisturbed, each enjoyed his own.*”

One instance of jealousy however, is recorded in Talmudic lore, upon which one *Halakah* is based, or, at least, a reason for that *Halakah*. The moon was jealous of the sun, avers the Talmud, and because it demanded an explanation from God why it was made smaller, the New Moon offering was instituted. Some, however, aver that the people of Israel bring the offering in recognition of the fact that God had to make one of the great lights to rule by night, and therefore, minimized its size, after it had been great.

Democracy is a firm pillar upon which the whole social structure of the civilized world rests. Democracy is the anti-thesis of tyranny; the cure-all for the enslaved masses. But what is democracy? Is it really a form of equality between all classes and conditions? Are all men really equal?

Life itself, yet, the natural phenomena of life, gives a negative answer to those questions. No two people are alike, either in their physique or in their mentality; they, therefore, cannot be equal in the sense that the average person understands equality. There ever were, and there ever will be, leaders and followers, educated and ignorant, misers and philanthropists, pious and profane, soul-headed and reckless, brave and cowards, wise and foolish, thrifty and idlers, because men vary in disposition, tendency, brain-power, natural ability and reasoning-sense. Thus, democracy surely does not mean physical, professional, or material equality. Since mankind must have government, the form of government can be democratic. Justice is the sole check and balance to secure democratic government. A democracy without justice, without honesty in the administration of justice, without respect for the laws created by a democratic system, cannot endure, and will ultimately become an instrument for the destruction of both government and governed.

The greatest source of democracy of and for all times, is the Torah. In the props upon which the tabernacle rested, every one had an even share; in the burden of observing the Torah, every one has an even share. The priests, the Levites, the kings, because of their added dignity, or power, or influence, or distinction, had larger responsibility to observe additional laws. Every man must have only one copy of the Torah with him; but the king had to have two copies. Every man in Israel had a share in the land of his fathers; but not the tribe of Levi, not the priests. Punishment for disobedience of any law was to be meted out alike to the most humble, and to the most high. If, for instance, the High-Priest had to be lashed, the punishment was inflicted, after which he had the right to return to his duties, if the sin was not of a character to disqualify him. Perversion of justice was considered the greatest curse in the domain of Israel.

Truly the real source of democracy, if it is to endure, comes not from the material needs of the people, but from their spiritual longing for that humanity which expresses itself in the terms of Justice. Such terms are found in the Torah. Such a democracy, based upon the equality of law, will ultimately be the firmest pillar of all society.
