*“Said the Holy One, blessed be He!, I shall not prevent them, on the contrary, let them see it (Eretz Yisrael); but my oath goes with this consent that not even one of them will enter and live in it, even as it is said: ‘Surely they shall not see the land’”.* (Bamidbar 14.23; Midrash Rabba, Bamidbar 16).

*“What did the Holy One, blessed be He!, do? He summoned the angel of Death to appear before Him, and charged him, saying: The whole earth is in thy power, save alone this nation (Israel) which I chose for Myself”* (Midrash Rabba, Bamidbar 16).

Doubt lends itself to be divided into many categories, as, for example, an even doubt, where the word of one is to be weighed against the word of another, both being of good repute; or, doubt in time, as for example, when the demise of the father and his son cannot be ascertained for the purpose of succession as to who proceed whom in death; or, doubt in law, where no expressed decision of a given act exists; or, doubt in volume, as for example, the value of an article stolen in order to establish the degree of the offence; or, doubt in identity, where no identification of an offender can be established, and many other doubts of like character. Such doubts are reasonable doubts, and because thereof, no conviction, disqualification, or transfer of property rights is possible. The status quo is applied.

A man child is born helpless, speechless, toothless; gradually a tooth appears, a sound is heard, a prattle, a leg is moved; by degrees, consciousness is developed; slowly knowledge is imparted, and - a man has grown up! He notices things around him, beneath and above him. He witnesses the birth of spring and the death of summer; heat annoys him, cold drives him indoors; he looks at the moon, hides his face from the strong rays of the sun, and attempts to count the stars; he sees the horizon, which appears to him to be the end of his world; he is charmed by the chirping of birds, fascinated by the whisper of trees, and perplexed by the darkness of night. He is told that all that which he can, and all that which he cannot see, was created by He whose Throne is in Heaven and Whose footstool in the Earth.

When the child becomes a man, he finds in his make-up, strong impulses and desires to do the very things his mother, his teacher, his father; his spiritual instructor had told him that they were forbidden to do. But he finds his inclinations much stronger than his power of restraint. A compromising medium is discovered by him in his own make-up. With the opening wedge between his conscience and his inclinations fully developed and ready, he finds a still wider gap - doubt! Geography, physics, history - The entire bundle of fagots loaded on him as material to light up his life’s journey, prove not a thing in the world to sust in the doctrine that the “*Fear of God is the Beginning of Vision*”. Fear! Why? Retribution? Who witnessed it? Reward? Who came back to testify to it? It is no more than beclouded issues consisting of Fear and Opiate. That is religion!

And, thus the man child, grown into manhood, finds it convenient to deny, to waver, to doubt, and — to sin. That is the story of every wrong-doer, of every sinner, of every criminal, of every member of society who breaks faith with God mankind.

But the fear of God is not physical fear; it is Reverence, the off-shoot of true love. Goodness is not, and should not be, dependent upon the reward which is certain to come. We do not, and we are not expected to worship God and serve him as a slave serves his master - for wages. It is not for the sake of God that we must refrain from doing evil, but for the good of ourselves. The testimony of all that which God did, and is doing, for every creature, as well as for us, is in our own bosom. We do not have to climb the mountains, ascend heaven, fathom the sea, penetrate the forest, or descend to hell, in order to fear God and love Him. We have all those in our own hearts, in our own conscience. If only we would reflect!

“*Out of the Mouth of the Uppermost cometh not forth either the good or the evil; the evil strikes the evildoer, and the good is showered upon him who doeth good.*”

An inkwell standing near a blank sheet of paper, will, of itself, not turn over, spill the ink, and make the impressions in the form of letters, words, sentences and paragraphs upon the paper. The same inference of the average intelligent human being, under the circumstances, will be that, while it is true that ink was used, there was a hand and an intelligence back of the whole process. Science may discover every last atom and the most minute molecule, but the atomism of the scientists could not, without an ever-present, Intelligence, create or be created. But why penetrate above, beneath, fore, and after, when the witnesses of God are our own brain-cells numbering in the millions, our own make-up, our own ability to span the universe with the fibres of our own thoughts.

Wavering souls are not as good, or rather, not as bad, as negative souls in the make-up of Judaism, which is a positive system for the ultimate in civilization. The atheist is against everything, and everybody in religion. The best we can do, and the worst he aims to do, is to avoid the company of the faithful. The world must have him, and if God lets him live and enjoy himself why disturb him, why do that which God Himself does not do - disturb him in his disbelief? But the wavering soul is an ever-present nuisance among us. He believes less than the atheist, for the atheist believes in himself and the waverer does not, and yet he desires a Kaddish, honor in the Temple for Synagogue, and long and noise-making eulogies at his funeral. He is afraid of the dark, and yet refuses to believe in the light of the Torah; he pays highly for a ring-seat in heaven, and refuses to believe that there is anything happening there; he does everything to kill his soul, and hides his deed beneath a mask of piety. He is the hypocritee par-excellence!

The refugees of Egypt believed, so they said; but, yet, when the test came to face an enemy in order to inherit the Promised Land, they wavered. They wanted to know ahead how true the feature of “*milk and Honey*”, was; how strong, or rather, how weak the enemy was. They wavered. They followed Moses, but they refused to believe him; they partook of the Manna and of the sweetened waters; this they enjoyed as well as the shadows of the pillar of cloud and the light of the pillar of fire; but to go to war on the strength of this belief was more than their wavering souls permitted. Of the twelve that went to spy out Canaan two came back inspired, as they went merely to pacify the rabble. The remnant few! Caleb and Joshua! The others did not count. They did not see the Promised Land. God willed it so; but not until they, by their own wavering, brought it upon themselves.

But their children were faithful! That was the strangest phenomenon among the Egyptian refugees. The parents were wavering, but the children were steadfast! Such children sprang forth in every generation, after every exodus, after every expulsion. The refugees fell victims of their own folly, whereas their children carried on the work of God!

Surely the Angel of Death can never have power over such rising generation. They were the chosen ones, the remnant few on each generation, throughout the heroic history of Israel.
