*“Comfort yo, comfort yo My people, saith your God.” (Yeshayahu 40:1) “And once again Raban Gamaliel, Rabbi Elazar b. Azariah, Rabbi Joshua and Rabbi Akiba went up to Jerusalem, and when they came near the Sanctum Sanctorum they saw a fox escaping there from, and the three of them broke down, and wept, but Rabbi Akiba laughed. \*Why art thou laughing?’ said they to him; ‘And why art you crying?’ said he to them. And they replied to him: - ‘Lo! a place whereof it is written: ‘And the stranger that draweth nigh shall be put to death’ (Bamidbar 1:51), and the prophecy concerning it that, the mountain of Zion which is desolate, the foxes walk upon it’ (Eicha 5:18) is fulfilled before our eyes, shall we not cry!’ ‘Just because of this I am laughing; for, it is written: ‘and I will take Me faithful witnesses to record; Uriah the priest, the son of Jeberechiah-Zechariah (Yeshayahu 8:2), the connection of Uriah and Zechariah, the former of the First Temple and the latter of the Second Temple, being strange, it must follow that the Ver se connects the prophecy concerning the one to the prophecy concerning the other. Now, of the days of Uriah, it is written: ‘Therefore shall Zion for your sake be plowed as a field, and Jerusalem shall become heaps’ (Micha 3:12), and of the days of Zechariah it is written: ‘There shall yet, old men and old women sit in the broad places of Jerusalem’ (Zecharia 8:4). Ere the prophecy of the days of Uriah was fulfilled, I feared lest the prophecy of Zechariah, too, will remain unfulfilled; but now, seeing the Uriahan prophecy fulfilled, I am positive that the Zechariahan prophecy, too, will be fulfilled!’ And they said to him: ‘Akiba, thou hast comforted us, Akiba! thou hast comforted us!”* (Makkos 24).

For centuries the Jewish people had many countries to die for, but none to live in; they spoke every tongue, but their own; they built many cities but not their own; they governed many nations, but not their own; they liberated many oppressed races, but not their own; they enriched many lands but not their own; they raised their voices for every just cause, but not for their own; they became part of every culture, but not of their own; they gave skill and talent to every group, but not to their own!

The philosopher is perplexed to find a reason for, and the statesman a basis, of all of the extremes which meet in such pronounced character within the folds of the Jewish people. They are the most noted individualists and yet the theories of socialism, if not its practical application as a political system, is largely attributed to them. They are the most tragic people in history, and yet, the greatest comedians are found among them. They are God’s chosen people, and yet, among them are those who never chose God. They are the “people of the book”, and yet, so many of them are strangers to the Book of the people...

But the most remarkable phenomena about the Jewish people is its survival. Living as they do under every form of civilisation, in ever clime and latitude, amidst the most advanced as well as among the most backward, traces, it is miraculous to find them almost intact, grouped within their own kindred, solving their own problems, and following their own directions, and suffering a common persecution whenever the tongue of the anti-Semite let us loose.

But the secret of their perpetuity cannot be found either in anti-Semitism or in their segregation. It will not be found on any natural or physical ground, for their existence is the riddle of the ages, the miracle of the world. Their virility, too, is out of proportion. Neither their number nor their physical resources can be cited as the reason for their prominence. One district in China or in India has more people than their total number, yet they alone figure in the daily press of the world so conspicuous, that a visitor from another planet would think that they are the leaven of the world’s human beings, the salt of the earth. Three lines, or a dozen at the most, is devoted to chronicle a revolutionary outbreak with a thousand dead in Asia, Africa, or South America; headlines on the front pages of every metropolitan paper feature an attack of a few drunkards against a few Jews in some remote section of the globe. A visitor from another planet can easily believe that the Jewish people are the earth’s most precious possession, and that every one of the inhabitants of the earth owns some share of the great value in them! It is the marvel of all denominations to find a Jewish criminal, an inmate of a penal institution, or an illiterate!

The language of the Hebrews is holy; their land is holy and they are the holy people. Anything having to do with the soul, the rest of the world must delve into the spiritual reservoir of the Jews. “*He went west*” - does not mean he prayed, he kneeled, he repented! But, “*He faced the East*” means more than all of these. One - he went West - means the grave; the other - the cradle. The world marches in cycles. It started from the east, going and continuing westward, but it reads east again. Either the early Jews were the master propagandists who discovered the secret of getting fast place in history and remaining there forever, or the fact is there because God himself brought it about. That alone perplexes the philosopher and the rationalists.

The fate of the people is also the fate of Eretz Yisrael. The children of the world devote more hours of their life to study and remember some remote hill in Palestine, or some landmark there, than they do in their study of the geography of their own countries, or districts. Tribes, of whom not a skeleton exists, are remembered just as much as if they were a neighboring people, yea, even more so. The fauna and flora of Palestine is know to civilized mankind more than that of their own native lands. From a jaw-bone of an ass to the tails of foxes, millions of children, and grown-ups too, vividly remember, and myryads of brain-cells are wasted in vain efforts to derive a moral lesson from a Palestinian lamb! The dew of the wool, the stalks of the flax, the wild honey, the birds’ nests, the fishes of the sea of Galilee, all, are treasured in the inner consciences of the believer and atheist alike. And, no one seems to ask, “WHY?”

The Jews are either hated or loved, blessed or damned, but no one remains indifferent to them. The Jews have the privacy of the proverbial fishes in a glass bowl. Whatever they do is minimized; and whatever they fail to do is exaggerated. To a vast number of people the subject of the Jews is either a study or a mania, and to the rest of the people of the world, it is a fascination. To one who studies this phenomenon objectively, it appears that unconsciously the Jews occupy the center of the world’s stage, with the whole world applauding, hissing, going into a frenzy, becoming hysterical, throwing things, and begging for more. To the believer the Jews are the living witnesses of God’s truth; to the infidel, the everlasting monument of the misfortune of religion; to the educated, the symbol of the book; to the masses, the token of miracles; to the enemy, a victim to plague; to friends, a convenience in times of need.

And, even so is Eretz Yisrael! Behind the mask of saving sacred and holy places, a path is carved leading to the fabulous treasures of the East; beneath the pretense of religion, the sharp edge of the sword became the arbiter of destinies and dynasties; beneath the hoofs of a horse lies the hidden intent of letting foxes continue to roam the place of the Sanctum Sanctorum!

Eretz Yisrael without the Jewish people is less than Hamlet without the melancholy Dame. The world cannot imagine it. History disowns such eventuality. Zion desolate is possible without its people; but Zion rebuilt is unthinkable without them. With or without the consent of the nations, or of the local tribes, or of the remnant invading irregulars, the land of Promise, will be restored to the People of Hope.
