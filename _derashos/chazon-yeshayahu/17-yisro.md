*“I am the Lord thy God, who brought thee out of the land of Egypt, out of the house of bondage.”* (Shemos 20:2).

*“Teach us, O master: If a fire break out in a house on the Sabbath, when nothing may be carried from one courtyard to another, and in that house were copies of the Torah, and other holy writings, may one save them, and carry them from one courtyard to another? Thus were we instructed by our teachers of yore: All holy writings should be saved from fire, and carried from place to place on the Sabbath, regardless of whether such writings are needed for public reading or not. The sages ruled it so, because if sacred writings be left to the flames people will be led to believe that they are worthless. Moreover, it is a fact that when God gave the Torah, everything in connection therewith was of fire, even as it is said: ‘At his right hand was a fiery law unto them! (Devarim 33:2) Said Rabbi Joshua ben Levi: ‘The Torah is of fire: the parchment is of fire: its letters are of fire; even the stiches which hold the scroll together was a fiery law unto them! Moreover, Moses, the intermediary, too, had his countenance turned into a flame of fire, even as it is said: ‘And they were afraid to come nigh him! (Shemos 34:30) The angels which descended with God, too, were of fire, as it is said:’ Who maketh winds Thy messengers, The flaming fires Thy ministers!*

*The mountains, yea, even they, were of fire, as it is said: ‘While the mountain did burn with fire.’ (Devarim 5:20) And God devours His enemies by fire, as it is said: ‘For the Lord thy God is a devouring fire, a Jealous God’ (Ibidum 4:24). Even the Word came forth from fire. When the people saw all these fires, the Holy One, blessed be He! said unto all of them: Do not fall in to the error of believing that these many sources of fire represent as many owners; I am the Lord thy God; there shall be no other gods before Me.”* (Midrash Tanhuma, Shemos 20:16)

Thus the triumph of Monotheism came about six generations after Abraham first proclaimed it to the world. And ever since all mankind trace the turning point of civilized history to the echoes of Revelation. The decalogue consists of three mandatory and seven prohibitive commandments — more negative than positive. To teach us that man’s real hardship in life is to refrain from doing evil, more so than in doing good.

Judaism, nevertheless, is a positive religion; the “*Arise and Do*” —*Kum Va’asey*, although numerically less, rather than the “*Tarry and Don’t*” —*Sheb Ve’al Taa’se* — require more effort and entail more sacrifice than its negative parallelism.

It is more expensive to honor one’s father and mother than to refrain from taking the Lord’s Name in vain. True, the positive side of Judaism numbers two hundred and sixty-eight commandments, whereas the negative side numbers three hundred and sixty-five; but it is, or it should be, as it evidently was the intention of God, that it be so, simpler to refrain from becoming a beast than to act like a man.

Numerically, the Hebrew characters, composing the words of the Decalogue, beginning with the word “*Anochi*” and ending with the word “*Lereacha*” equal the total number of the commandments in the whole Pentateuch - 613. Some great medieval authorities on speculative Kabala pointed out, that in abbreviated form, by means of letter-connecting — *Ziru’ha Otiot* —that each of the Hebrew characters in the Decalogue actually indicates the nature of each of the respective 613 commandments. Thus, if the entire Pentateuch should get lost the Decalogue itself would serve as a key to preserve the 613 commandments.

The scholars throughout the ages continued, moreover, to find in the Decalogue itself the whole structure of the Torah, that is, in so far as its legalism and moral doctrines are concerned. They built around the commandment of “*Honor thy father and thy mother*”, all that establishes discipline, respectability, and correct human conduct; around the Sabbath observance, all laws regarding labor, employer and employee; around the doctrine of Monotheism, all religious laws, dogma, rites, customs, prayers, sacrifice and faith in reward and retribution; around the law of “*Thou shalt not steal*”, all rights of property, individual, national and international, all dealings between men and men; around “*Thou shalt not kill*”, the value of life, the entire code of criminology forbidding injury to persons, through any cause whatever; around “*Thou shalt not commit adultery*”, all law aiming to elevate morality, regulation of marriage, divorce, succession, and the whole moral code; around “*Thou shalt not covet*” all ethical conduct and philosophy of social life; around “*Thou shalt not bear false witness*”, the entire structure of right and wrong, good and evil, truth and falsehood, education and ignorance; and around “*Thou shalt not take the name of the Lord thy God in vain*”, everything in life which is pure, noble, exalted, religiously-permitted and humanly possible to achieve.

The Torah, the Prophets, the Scriptures, the Mishnah, the Talmud, the Mehilta, the Safri, the Sifre, the Midrashim, the Tosefta, the Agadah, the Poskim, the Teshubot, the commentaries, the prayers, the piyutim, the hymns, the hermeneutics and the laws and commentaries derived therefrom, the Peshat, the Derash, and everything written in connection therewith must be considered sacred, each of these being a superstructure erected upon the foundation of the Decalogue. Each sage, throughout the generations, if he only contributed even one iota to the great field of sacred literature should be remembered, and his memory kept sacred by revering his writings. Even the *Halkah Dehuysh*, —non-confirmed cow — though it is not at all binding, must be kept sacred. By debate and disputation came the true light to all Israel in all ages. And everything which aided, or was instrumental in aiding, that light to appear is, and of right ought to be, sacred to all Israel.

Man’s instinct is in no manner different from the lower animal; but man is a composite being; his intellect is his soul’s child, and whatever does come from the soul is an offspring of God. It does not belong to the earthly regions; it was not nurtured on this atmosphere; it descended along with the dew of heaven, the true *Tal Hashomayim*, — Heavenly Dew.

The fruit of the soul must not be desecrated; it is the soul’s own nourishment upon which alone it thrives, by which alone, it can live within the body and remain unsoiled, unpolluted.

The Sabbath, too, is food for the soul; it is the first rest-day on record; it is holy time; it is the barrier between the animal within man and his soul. Nothing which belongs to the soul must ever be destroyed, or permit it to become worthless.

Maimonides said: “*The soul suffers from certain forms of disease as well as the body; it requires food as well as the body, and needs preventive and remedial means in order to function normally*”. The holy writings are the food and medicine for the soul’s cravings and ailments.

The Torah was given on the Sabbath day. That which we call Torah embraces all holy writings; for they all converge upon the Pentateuch. The Pentateuch and all of its oral interpretation came to us from Sinai.

Now there are certain emergencies which come to pass in the life’s history of every individual, as there were, and are, certain emergencies in the life’s history of the whole people of Israel. These emergencies are usually of a nature to endanger the spiritual life of either the individual Jew or of the whole people. Fire breaks out in our camp. Perhaps, because of economic oppression, and perhaps, because of being nearer to dangerous explosives, as is usually the case, when environment takes its course. And the fire breaks out on the Sabbath. It strikes at the very root of Jewish life. It is then the duty to remove the holy writings from the flames and save them.

Maimonides teaches: “*Likewise, if a man be living in a State where evil customs prevail, and where the people are not following the righteous ways, he should go to a place where the inhabitants are righteous, and follow the way of the good. If all the States known to him, or of which intelligence has reached him, be followers of a path which is not good, even as it is in our own times, or if he be unable to migrate to a State whose rules of conduct are good, either on account of military operations, or on account of sickness, he should isolate himself and live in seclusion, even as it is said on the subject: ‘Let him sit alone and keep silence’*” (Eicha 3:28)

By saving the holy writings, by saving the Sabbath, which is holy time, from the fire set by external influences, Israel can save himself from being consumed by the self-same fire—by assimilation.

Wisely did the sages of yore instruct us: “*All holy writings, should be saved from fire, and carried from place to place on the Sabbath.*”

Moreover, by escaping from the influences of external flames, we are sheltered by the Torah, which, at the Right Hand of God is “*a fiery Law unto us*”. The Godly fire does not consume; it purifies, it gives light, heat and power; it is the only protection the soul has against the over-present enemy. We are then placing ourselves under the wings of God’s Shekinah. Then we are told:

“*The angels which descended with Him were of fire; Sinai was on fire.*” We become the children of God: “*Who makest winds Thy messengers The flaming fires Thy ministers.*”

And, all of what we do, everything that we think, and all that we are, we can trace only to the root out of which sprang forth the greatest spiritual tree of life in all history — the Decalogue.
