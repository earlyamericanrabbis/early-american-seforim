*“And the Lord spoke unto Moses, saying: When a bullock, or a sheep, or a goat is brought forth, then it shall be seven days under its dam; but from the eighth day and thenceforth, it may be accepted as an offering, made by fire, unto the Lord.”* (Vayikra 22:26-27)

*“This is as the Verse elucidates: ‘For that which befalleth the sons of man, befalleth the beasts; even one thing befalleth them; as the one dieth, so dieth the other; yea, they have all one breath; and the pre-eminence of man above beast is worthless; for all is vanity.”* (Koheles 3:19)

*“Come and reason, and you will behold: Man is charged, ‘Thou shalt not wear mingled stuff, wool and linen together’, and concerning beasts man is charged, ‘Thou shalt not plow with an ox and an ass together’ (Devarim 22:10-11); man contaminates, as it is said: ‘He that toucheth the dead, even any man’s dead body, shall be unclean (Bamidbar 19:11) and so does the beast, as it is said: ‘And if any beast, of which ye may eat, die of itself, he that toucheth the carcass thereof shall be unclean’ (Vayikra 11:39); for a certain sin the beast as well as the man is put to death (Ibidum 20:15 - 15); of the animal it is herein said: ‘but from the eighth day and thenceforth it may be accepted for an offering’, and of the man it is said: ‘And in the eighth day the flesh of his foreskin shall be circumcised’ (Ibidum 12:3); yea, the pre-eminence of man above beast, his gift of speech, his intelligence, his power to discern good from bad, his conception of the worth of his hire, even his distinction in being buried by his fellow-beings is worthless; for all is vanity.”* (Midrash Agadah, Vayikra 22:26; Midrash Tanhuma, Vayikra 22:15).

The several systems of philosophy, which differ radically from the accepted theological philosophy concerning man’s soul, and his pre-eminence, above all creatures in the animal kingdom, will, at the first glance, find much in common with the doctrines enunciated by these Midrashic authors. Moreover, the authority of the wise Ecclesiast, Solomon, is cited as the firmest pillar for these matter-effect views of man’s being.

Not in vain did the Kabalists declare that when an animal is slaughtered for food in the prescribed manner, the animal thereby attains its real goal and serves its purpose; for even here, the Midrashic authors speak of the clean group of animals. That is their dignity of being properly buried - to even up with man’s pre-eminence.

But what of the whole kingdom of animals, of the unclean groups, of the deadly creatures, of the plague-carrying and of the disease-breeding swarms? Of course, there is a hidden purpose in all creation; but the mere idea that man is no better than the lowly forms of the animal kingdom is repulsive to the thinker who desires to see the level of mankind raised a bit in every generation.

Before deciding the position of man in the animal kingdom, before locating it somewhere in the beginning of things along with the fish-shell, sea-weed, or monkey, it would be more to the point to reach out a bit higher and use the God-given intelligence to find a more fit position, if possible.

Adam was not permitted to partake of animal food; he was ordered to eat herbs and fruit, to be a strict vegetarian. Of course, being table associates with the rest of the animal kingdom, Adam’s children and grand-children considered themselves no better than the other animals. Noah had both, the intelligence and the faith, and he was saved, and saved all species, from the deluge. Then God ordered him to eat the flesh of the animals; he only was warned not to cut a piece from a live beast and eat it; other beasts do that.

The Kabalists elaborated on that, too, by maintaining that some animals may have the breath of life, which at one time, in either the long or immediately preceded past, belonged to a human being. To them this motem - psychosis is a certainty. A mouse might have had a previous existence as a diplomat; a louse, as a politician, or a sneak as a king.

The Talmudic rational sages, however, were more scientific; they discovered the entire animal kingdom in man. Every student easily finds traces of the known animals in the men they encounter. That may or may not prove the theory of evolution, but it seems that men closely resemble the, if not allied to, the various forms of creatures; from the lobster and shrimp to the bat and owl, from the eel to the fox, from the cat to the rhinoceros.

But how much of the Man do we find in the beast, excepting in the natural expression of such phenomena as are due to the forces of this planet? Can we conceive of a beast solving any of its own life’s problems, save such as nature’s God endowed it with, as a natural law? That gulf which divides the natural laws common in the vegetation and animals and man’s intelligence has not been, and will not be bridged.

Maimonides says: “*The soul of man is the form which God gave unto him, and the high intelligence which is found in the soul of man is the form of the man who is perfectly intelligent. Concerning this form it is said in the Torah: ‘Let us make man in our form after our image’ (Bereishis 1:26). Often times this form is called soul and breath of life, and, therefore, it is necessary to take care in naming them, so that man will not err in such, but each and every name one will learn from the subject thereof.*” (Book One 1:4:8).

Man, too, has natural laws which prompt him to do certain things, in a certain manner, just as the natural laws act through a plant or an animal. That which appears highly intelligent in man, such as speech, labor, discernment between good and evil, and many like human attributes are as natural in man as is grass-eating in the elephant, of meat-eating in the lion. The beast cannot speak, not because it is a beast, but because it was not endowed with that gift by nature’s God. Fire goes upward, water downward; both mind the natural laws.

These natural phenomena appear in both man and beast, by reason of their being alive. The cannibal had not developed his power of conscience, of remorse, both of which are not natural laws, and therefore, cannot be cited as evidence that man’s evolution is based entirely upon material ground, and that all of man’s powers are mere results of education, training, acquisition, environment and breeding and culture.

The Kabalists enumerate five distinctly separate personalities which permeate man’s being, viz; (1) LIVING SOUL - Nephesh; (2) SPIRIT - *Ru’ah*; (3) SOUL - Neshamah; (4) LIFE - *Ha’yah*; (5) UNIT - *Y’hidah*. (See Midrash Rabba, Bereishis 14; and Ibidum Devarim 82).

\(1\) In Talmudic legalism man is considered an animal, a product of this earthly planet. For instance; It is forbidden to cut grass on the Sabbath - *Kotzer*; man is likened unto grass; therefore, if, on the Sabbath one will cut off another’s finger, he is guilty, besides damages, of the sin of *Kotzer*. For all material purposes, man’s being is but an animal; a living soul - *Nephesh Ha’yah*. The same name is used to designate all living creatures. (See Bereishis 1:21; and Ibidum - 24). But do not mind the English version, which translates *Nephesh* *Ha’yah* where it designates fishes, or beasts, or creeping things as “living creatures”. That is due to the richness of English in words, and the translators must be careful in placing idioms rightly. This, of course, multiplies ignorance; but when millions of divines are happy in that state, who can come and disturb them? But the real meaning of *Nephesh Ha’yah* is living soul, not living creature. Thus, in the natural and fundamental make-up of the entire animal kingdom we trace the form of natural life with all of the natural laws common to the several species and their anatomical structure.

\(2\) The lower animals are incapable, by their nature’s instincts, to practice idealism in any manner. Any form of so-called intelligence which does appear in some animals, is either reflex action, or the result of training which brings out certain instincts in an organized manner. But the savage in man is not indicative that he sprang forth from the lower animals, that he is a product of evolution, and that his make-up, when most cultured, when at the head of the highest expression of idealism, is one and the same power. Man’s awakening is not due to training only. He has in him the spirit of God - *Ru’ah*. His intelligence to discover something, to make his life more comfortable, to work intelligently, to work purposefully, is indicative that he possesses something which is not within the natural phenomena of the animal kingdom. The physical construction of some animals, such as the ruminants, the cloven-footed, or of fowl such as the dove, or of fishes, such as the squamous, have been singled out in the Torah as the clean groups of animals, birds, and fishes; and of these the people of Israel was permitted to partake. They are described as beings of a “living soul”. Life within them as within all other creatures, belongs to the natural elements common to this planet; but because the elements out of which they spring forth are inanimate, they too, are spoken of as endowed with a spirit of God. They are material beings, created by the will of God. It is because thereof that Solomon wisely asks: “*Who knoweth the spirit of man whether it goeth upward, and the spirit of the beast whether it goeth downward to the earth*”? (Koheles 3:21). In their structure, such as the lobes of the lungs, in their temperature, such as the doves in comparison to the vultures, and many like traits, the clean groups of animals, fishes and birds, are said to be best evidence that the different species of living creatures were created purpose - fully as they are, and therefore, are said to best serve their purpose when they are prepared as food in the Torah-ordered manner. Concerning this the Kabalists say that the method of slaughtering them is the proper means to their goal - *Sh’hitatan Zu’hi Takanatan*. Thus far, every link between animal and man.

\(3\) The idealism in man, the sentiments he is capable of expressing, the heights of development in social science he is able to attain, the volume of progress he is able to achieve, and the standard of living he is able to establish - all give evidence to a form of life which is not native to this planet’s nature. In these we behold a phenomenon which is as unearthly as the soul itself is said to be. With his soul, man conquers nature and spans it to his will. That power within him is his soul; it is a form and an image God bestowed upon man when He created Him. By means of this power, which is neither his spirit, nor his breath of life, man feels within himself the actual existence of an independent being; through its constant demands upon him, which, often, is in direct contradiction of what his natural instincts demand, of what his spirit of life desires most, man actually feels a struggle, and is conscious of it. That disturbs the peacefulness of his mind, the tranquility of his being; that creates pessimism; that brings forth either his best or his worst, it all depends as to what power in his make-up was developed strongest. To control the forces within him, to establish harmony, and enjoy peacefulness, man is in need of a still higher force. And, God provided that, too.

\(4\) *Ha’yah* - Life, comes to the person who, by his will-power, is able to incorporate all the three forces within him into one element of helpfulness; that is to say, that if one can make the earthly and the heavenly elements within him serve his ends in his endeavors to be completely human, he will awaken another force within himself, which had been dormant theretofore, and which cannot come to his aid before doing so. It is as much a gradual process as is the growth of the body. Life, then, becomes purposeful, interesting, and worth-while. He feels a certain elation in being; he realizes that his action is of endurable worth, that his going and coming, his sitting down and standing up, his lying down and and his rising up, have a meaning to him and to his posterity. Life unwraps itself and is revealed to him in a new form and he sees such distances which, but a short while before he had no conception of; his mind is broadened out to such proportions of which he never before dared dream of. He reaches a pinnacle, resting upon a superstructure, which gives him a perspective now to his vision; fills him with an inspiration which drags him out of his shell of clay and leaves him in a mantle of unearthly web and carries him far, far away from the desires and passions which had disturbed his tranquility and made his innermost being a battlefield.

\(5\) When he reaches such state of mind, and continues thereon, a still newer power reaches out for him and carries him completely off of his natural feet, when space, time, matter, spirit, material and ideal all combine to give him the precious life of heaven upon earth. That power, too, is dormant within him till he passes from strength to strength and becomes ready for its embrace. That is UNITY - *Ye’hidah*. Dualism, monism, and every other ancient or medieval conception fail to satisfy the soul in its craving for the food which alone can keep it alive in its dark, lowly abode upon earth. But this Kabalistic interpretation must not be accepted literally that there are as many souls, or spirits, or personalities, in the human being. It so happens that this doctrine was accepted by some of the ancient Talmudists, long before the speculative Kabalists defined any of their doctrines in the language of ordinary mortals. It is therefore necessary to point out that, according to this doctrine, the spiritual development is subject to a parallelism with that of the body. In the infant is the man. In the living soul is the Unit. But spiritual ailments will deter the growth of the soul, the expansion of life, and the approach of the Unit. Most men, therefore, live on by their Spirit only. They are just above the animal, which had been given the “soul of living” only.

The Midrashic authors found a parallelism in the happening which he befall both man and beast. And, quoting the Ecclesiast, they summed up the man who merely lives on by his spirit, and his entire pre-eminence above the beast consists of few elementary human characteristics, as of no lasting value, as all such and the like thereof is but vanity.

Verily, *Nephesh*, *Ru’ah,* *Nashamah*, *Ha’yah*, and *Y’hidah* makes the spiritual combination - make the man.
