*“And it shall be, when he sitteth upon the throne of his kingdom, that he shall write him a copy of this law in a book, out of that which is before the priests and the Levites. And it shall be with him and he shall read therein all the days of his life; that he may learn to fear the Lord his God, to keep all the words of this law and these statutes, to do them; that his heart be not lifted up above his brethren, and that he turn not aside from the commandments, to the right hand, or the left; to the end that he may prolong his days in his kingdom, he and his children in the midst of Israel”* (Devarim 17:18-20)

“*And ye shall cry out in that day because of your king whom ye shall have chosen; and the Lord will not answer you in that day. But the people refused to hearken unto the voice of Samuel; and they said: ‘Nay, but there shall be a king over us; that we also may be like all the nations; and that our king may judge us; and go out before us, and fight our battles!’”* (I Shmuel 8:18-21 q.v.)

*“When he sitteth upon the throne of his kingdom, if he does all that which is said concerning this subject, he is worthy to sit upon the throne of his kingdom”* (Safre Devarim 17; Yalkut Shimony 914).

Institutions, during the ages of antiquity, were called into being, by the desire of the people, by force of necessity; by ambitious scheming of individuals; by fear, and by brutal strength. Nimrod became a king because of his ability to conquer the beasts of the forests. This fact is chronicled in the midrashic literature not for the purpose of tracing the history of institutional archaeology, but as a warning against handing over the reign of government to the possessor of might.

But organized society had to have some form of government better than the will or caprice of the tyrant. From Plato’s republic to the Covenant of the League of Nations, the world’s literature, ancient, medieval, and modern, is overcrowded with laws, statutes, charters, constitutions, edicts, regulations and forms under which the peoples of the world should be governed, and yet, most peoples are still governed imperfectly, if not arbitrarily. In the present generation autocratic rulers have the sway in a number of civilized states, while democracies are tottering. And, even in those countries, when democracy is firmly established, no government, no fixed acts of policy endures even a half decade. The free peoples are governed superfluously by their own representatives. Modern life forced society to spend, in time and in money, the major share of its earnings and leisure in support of the government they choose.

At the bottom of all of the problems and movements in modern government is a current of fear. Each country fears its neighbor; each political party fears the other; each group fears the other. This fear-producing current is alive because it is constantly being fed by the juice of distrust jealousy, prejudice and lies, generated by machinery of ambitious covetousness, selfishness and Godlessness. The motion which drives the machinery, creates the juice and keeps the current alive, is supplied by the storm of human nature which recognizes no limitation and knows not where or when to stop. Rulers and ruled alike are harnessed to the same wild force, and recklessly chase on and on - toward where?

*“Neither shall he (the king) greatly multiply to himself silver and gold”* (Devarim 17:17) Under the laws of the Torah, a ruler in Israel, had to have two scrolls of the Torah; one, because every man in Israel was commanded to have a copy thereof (Ib. 31.19), and the other, as a ruler in Israel, he was obliged to have, so that it should remind him of his own temporal life and of the immortal life implanted in its commandments by the Almighty God (Ib. 17:18). King David, among all the kings of Judah and Israel, speaks with high exultation of this law, saying; *“The law of Thy mouth is better unto me than thousands of gold and silver”* (Tehillim 119:72). King David in this Verse gave expression to the innermost delight of his soul. Although it is the usual thing among rulers to multiply their stock of silver and Gold and subtract their stock of moral scruples, with David, in following the Torah, the situation was just the reverse; he was forbidden to multiply gold and silver and commanded to double the number of copies of the Torah, and his delight was so full that he gave expression to it in his Tehillim. “*The law of Thy mouth is better unto me than thousands of gold and silver.*”

The prophet Samuel, after seeing that Israel’s democracy lasting four centuries, was about to fall, stood out emphatically against a king. Even at best, said he, (I Shmuel 8) the king’s rule will chain the liberties of the people. And, not until God Almighty Himself sanctioned the demand of the people to change their form of government, did Samuel consent to anointing of Saul.

Wisely did the Talmudic sages say: “*If he does all that which is said concerning the subject (to rule according to the laws of the Torah) he is worthy to sit upon the throne of his kingdom.*”
