*“Now these are the ordinances which thou shalt set before them.” (Shemos 21:1) “This is as the Psalmist said: ‘The strength also of the king who loveth justice— Thou hast established equity”* (Tehillim 99:4)

*“All of the strength, might and praise belongs to the Holy One, blesses be He! King David said: ‘Thine, O Lord, is the greatness, and the power, and the glory’ (I Divrei HaYamim 29:11) Ordinarily we find that he who has might overlooks that which is right; but the Holy One, blessed be He! though the might is his, He loveth justice, even as it is said: ‘Thou hast established equity’.*

*Said Rabbi Alexander: ‘Two cameleers followed their heavily-laden camels on the same road. Being competitors neither spoke to the other. One camel fell beneath his heavy burden and the cameleer alone could not unload, and re-load. The other passed him by without a word. Then he started thinking: ‘If thou see the ass of him that heeth thee lying under its burden, tho shalt forbear to pass him; thou shalt surely release it with him’ (Shemos 23:5) This law prompted him to turn back, and release the camel from its burden, and help his erstwhile enemy re-load. Then came the turn of the other camel or to think: So, he surely was my friend, and I knew it not. Both of them became friends. Because they understood the real meaning of the Torah, peace was established between them, even as David said: ‘Thou hast established equity.’ Wisely said King Solomon: ‘The King by justice establishes the land.’ (Mishlei 29:4)*

*When God appeared to give the Torah to Israel, he commenced with justice, even as it is said: ‘There He made for them a statute and an ordinance.’ (Exodus 15.25); and here Moses, in turn, instructed Israel in the laws which had been imparted to him.”* (Midrash Tanhuma Shemos 21; Yalkut Shimony 306:1:21)

There are many sociological philosophers who contend that justice is not righteousness, and surely not love, or mercy, or charity. People love phraseology so much that they combine several principles in one sentence, in order to make the sentence appear beautiful. Particularly so are some modern preachers, who besides preaching, fail to grasp the nature of their calling, which is; mainly, not to prepare people for a decent burial, but to teach them how to live.

Judaism means nothing at all to the modern Jew, if he can get nothing out of it but a cluster of phrases which he cannot carry along either in his mind or in his pocket. Once a religion fails to influence the lives of its adherents, its existence is in vain. The modern Jewish preacher, both in Western Europe and in America, for decades, continued to preach what they were, and are, pleased to call ethical philosophy, culture, social justice and righteousness.

With the avenues of all professions open to the Jews, the lawyer, or judge, comes and contradicts the preacher, or Rabbi, not publicly, of course, by pointing out that the Courts are the real places for justice; the atheist comes, publicly, and maintains that culture has no place in the synagogue where ancient dogma still holds sway; the politician comes, and very often, from a pulpit, says: *“righteousness is what we have a government for.”*

Theological schools are annually turning out Rabbis as if the deluge of preaching is the only necessity to clear out the corruption from the land regardless of whether the students know at graduation even the names of the most important works on either *Halakah* or Agadah, the prestige of the schools go along with the “graduates” and the gradual scale of descent is low- red deeper with every graduation. Moreover these schools took upon themselves the authority of conferring *Semicha* - ordination - as if this matter was a mere formality of granting a diploma to one who is to practice a mere profession upon a suffering humanity. Without the Torah’s knowledge of what it means to be a Rabbi in Israel, some graduates go out and by showing a beautifully engraved sheepskin in Hebrew characters they are the authorities in Israel.

Of course the layman in America, not even the college men, some of whom are members of the synagogue or Temple, know anything about the history of ordination in Judaism; they do not even know of the great strife between Rabbi Joseph Karo and Levi ben Chabib about restoring the ordination. And, who cares to know what a student must know before any Rabbi is authorized to confer Semicha on him? As long as the prospective candidate knows his civics, the theory of evolution, the fact that there are higher critics, and that the higher critics did parcel out the Holy Writ in some many different manuscripts, the board of trustees of any temple or synagogue is quite sure that everything is in order, and the rabbi will “make good”.

Then comes the real work of creating ignorance, of spreading mis- information, of inventing new theories, of erecting new shadows which app- ear as mountains, of reducing Judaism to the level of a mere formalism. And — so is the successor of Moses and the prophets delegated to the ranks of professionalism.

The race in coining titles for sermons, in attracting the masses to the synagogue, or Temple, in charming the congregational bosses with long and smooth Words, and in fascinating the ladies with the gems one finds in the newest spicy book begins with every high holyday season and ends with the Feast of Weeks, when the season ends.

The soul-stirring prayers, the very sacred environment of the sanctuary, the reading of the Torah itself—everything which has to do with the souls of the worshippers is being continuously relegated to secondary positions. The main thing is the sermon, which is everything else but a “Sermon”.

According to the highest authority, the meaning of the word SERMON is “A religious discourse in which the words of God are quoted and expounded and in which the worshippers are inspired to the practice of virtue.” The *Drasha* of the Rabbi should be all that, and more, because our Torah is a fountain of life, and both the *Halakah*, and the *Hagadah* are rich in material “to excite an audience to the practice of virtue”. The *Drasha* has every right in the temple and synagogue; but it must be a *Drasha*, and not a parody; it should always be based upon the Torah, and never upon what “polite society” is prone to consider “most attractive”.

Judaism in a garment of mere words is not even a scare-crow; mere phraseology are but clouts upon a broom-stick, or upon nothing at all.

It is profane to use the pulpit, near the ark of the Torah, for any but words of Torah, which awaken the congregants to sacred action. No one but a Rabbi should ever occupy the pulpit. The many uses that the American-Jewish pulpit is put to, bring on profanity incarnate. The custom of politicians, or career-seeking professional and quasi-professional laymen who come to make appeals, or to tell the Jews how to be citizens, from the pulpit, are undermining the very existence of the synagogue, and destroying the last semblance of respect and sacredness that was yet left in the bosom of Jewry for its most sacred institutions.

Publicity-seekers, “hail-fellow-well-met” brethren and “rising stars” of all sorts, realizing the value of such an exalted platform as the pulpit, often are the organizers of synagogues and temples. Builders, contractors, office-seeking gentry, and promoters of financial schemes invade new sections in the larger sections of American Israel and establish themselves at the head of temples or synagogues. And, all with the view of self-gain. Of course, it must follow that the Rabbi must be the mouth-piece of these individuals, or his position is gone, and his sacred calling undermined. But the Torah isn’t to blame for the corruption of these individuals. It is up to the Rabbis who must not preach mere phraseology. They must stand up manfully for the Torah by expounding it from the pulpit.

In the very title, Rabbi, however, lies the cause of the evil. The public is uniformed about it; but it is a fact that historically Yeshivot did not confer the degree of Rabbi upon students. Historically the Yeshivah was never turned into a professional school. Assuredly, the students of Yeshivot were never given direct courses leading to the Rabbinical degree in condensed form. *Halakha*, in matters pertaining to strictly religious ordinances, was taken up only after the whole scope of the Talmud was studied.

Summing up the situation, it becomes clear that the Torah was neglected entirely by a number of men who occupy pulpits; therefore the great deluge of idle phrases.

The mere prattle of justice and righteousness, without the Torah back-ground as to what these principles are, means less to the future of Judaism than the ancient prattle of the Saducees about Helenism as a co-partner of our religion. The Torah does not retain any foreign substance in its bosom; it casts out both, the substance and the maker thereof.

The purpose of all social laws is to create peace and harmony, neighborly love, tolerance and universal recognition of God. That is the reason why our Torah metes out no punishment for violating religious laws, to any but the children of Israel. Order is ruling nature, law is regulating it; therefore, justice must not be looked upon as a means of revenge. For example, the serpent hisses; it is its nature; it does not do it unless some natural instinct prompts it to hiss; when it becomes dangerous, it is removed, not as a matter of revenge, but as a matter of justice.

Justice must be based upon equity. Upon such justice may a land be established. Zion will be redeemed by justice; it will be established by equity. Law is not to be created, or made, for either the convenience, or spite of individuals; it must be based upon a foundation which should sustain the whole social structure of a land. A man who refuses to save a drowning nude woman on the ground of immorality, has no conception of what morality is; a man who does not tell a fellow that his house is on fire because he never was introduced to him, has no conception of what social standards are; a man who carries his personal dislike of another man to his dumb animals, has no conception of what duty is.

We therefore are taught by the sages, that before the Revelation, God had instructed Moses in all the laws of the Torah, and Moses, in turn, instructed these laws to the people of Israel.

“*There He made for them a statute and an ordinance*”. After the fundamental laws of the Decalogue, the ordinances of every-day life were given to the people. They understood it; they were given to understand what these laws are; they constitute the sum total of Israel’s system of justice, the whole of which is based upon equity, by which their being upon earth was established and is justified.
