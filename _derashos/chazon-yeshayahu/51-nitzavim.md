*“And the generation to come, your children that shall rise up after you, and the foreigners that shall come from a far land, shall say, when they see the plagues of that land, and the sickness wherewith the Lord hath made it sick.”* (Devarim 29:21. et seq.)

*“What shall a man do in order to live? He shall sacrifice his life. The Torah does not remain permanently with any save those who sacrifice themselves for it. If he will live, it will mean he is dead, but if he will court death, he will live.”* (Tamid 32; Shabbat 83b; Yalkut Shimony)

*“To love the Lord thy God, to hearken to His voice, and to cleave to Him.” (Devarim 30.20) “We have learned, “and to cleave to him” et seq. means ‘Let no man say I will study so that people may call me Doctor- I will learn so that people may call me Rabbi; I will study so that people may call me Elder, and occupy the head chair of a Yeshivah; but study and learn because of love of knowledge, and honor will come to you in the end without solicitation and without effort.”* (Yalkut Shimony, Devarim 30:940)

The greatest problem of every generation, since time was recorded, was the “generation to come”. Parents are “afraid” of what their children will say and do in this age as the parents of every bygone generation were. In normal times, all parents did worry and are worrying about, had and has reference to their children alone, to give them better and more opportunities in life, to make them more useful members of society than they themselves are, or were. But when the “generations to come” will ask the kind of questions which are indicated in Deuteronomy 29:21 et seq. parents find themselves in a very sad plight. To point out to children their own misdeed, to confess that their own fate brought about the conditions which deny the “generation to come” every night and every opportunity in life, is a terrible situation, most horrible to contemplate and tragic to tell.

Of all the peoples on the face of the earth, the people of Israel is most worried about the “generation to come”. The confession of having lost the heritage, the home, and the national glory was made so long ago that it now rests between the covers of every prayer book to look into and give expression to during public worship, and with sad intonation parents and children alike recite it almost indifferently. The scattering of Israel, the long exile, was accepted as a punishment long ago inflicted for sins committed still longer ago. The indifference became so universal that a small but no mean wing of Israel

Fuses any longer to recognize the Diaspora as a punishment, and publicly proclaim that the Jews are scattered into a thousand fragments because they have or had, a mission to preach to the nations. That philosophy became the backbone of Modern Reform Judaism. Nationalism was set aside completely to make room for a Missionary Judaism. The “generation to come” was told to look forward - to remain when they were born, good and loyal citizens to the native lands; and consider themselves different only in their denominational affiliations. As is with the case of American of the Methodist or other denominations, or as is with the Frenchman, with Germans, or Englishmen, and their respective religious sects.

The only trouble with this philosophy was, and is, the other party to the arrangements. They looked upon the Jews not as they look upon the Catholics, the Baptists, the Greek Orthodox, or the Anglicans. The Jews to them are a different, if not an alien, people - a race destined to be distinct and alone among all the races of the earth. The “generation to come” still had questions to ask, and the parents knew not what to answer. They did not consider themselves dwellers of the *galuth*, but their children made them conscious of the many prejudices and exclusions, which at times, were smarting enough to feel real *galuth*.

To overcome these reminders of *Galuth*, new plans were adopted. Friendly relations with the Christians. The scheme found a responding chord even among the representatives of the Christian sects. The only disadvantage many find is that “good-will” movements in a community are a constant reminder of an existing cause for differences. Good neighbors, by a continuous rehashing of their old differences, will sooner or later quarrel again. Besides, the main objective of the religious elements of the other party, if not attained with kindness will end in a mass agitation against the Jews. That objective is not other than the *real missionary work*. Christian denominational representatives; even in liberal England, do not hesitate to find any arm which will support their religion.

After all is said, the fundamental difference between Jew and Christian is a religious one.

The only avenue of escape from total extinction is for the Jews to remain as they were - Jews. By living up to the laws of the Torah, they will ever be good neighbors. The liberal nations have always treated the conscientious Jew with respect. Every statesman in America, during the past forty years, was ever ready to listen to the presentation of the religious representatives of the Jews. Zionism triumphed by just such means. America supported England in its attempt to establish a Jewish homeland in Palestine.

Self-sacrifice, humility, a life of an unassuming character, will win much for the Jews in Diaspora. The political religionist among the Jews, creates more harm for the people of Israel than the anti-Semitic agitator.

A real Jewish life, to cleave to God, not to desire to outdo one’s neighbors in styles, jewelry, opera box, loudness in dress and speech, restraint in office-seeking and honesty in dealing will set up the word “praying” by example for the “generation to come.”
