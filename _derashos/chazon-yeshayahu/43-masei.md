*“These are the stages of the children of Israel (Bamidbar 33:1). The Holy One, blessed is he! said: Moses, record the places and stages of the wanderings of Israel in the Wilderness, so that they may know about the many miracles I wrought in each and every place.”* (Midrash Tanhuma, Bamidbar 33; Yalkut Shimony, Bamidbar 33:786)

The current of life, like that of a river, passes on, never to return. So much water under the bridge, so many things gone by. The movements of the stars and the planets are regulated by cycles, they reappear in the self-same manner at stated periods, and anyone versed in astronomy, can foretell where any star, or any planet will be tomorrow, next day or next year. But who can foretell where the drops of water of Niagara will be even a day hence? Who can tell what events will come to pass in the story of life’s current, even an hour hence?

Memory is all that life is, in the final analysis. Memory can be made to serve the greatest good in life. It all depends to what use memory is put.

Memory without the power of reasoning, of contemplation, of reflection, is but a cobweb. The animal can hear, but cannot reason out what it hears. The human mind, when properly trained, is able to perceive what it hears, digest it, and if it be imperative, move the individual to action. The human being excels the animal in more than speech. The animal is not unlike the inanimate things in nature, insofar as its ability to reason is concerned. There is no such thing as advancing to a higher level among the lower animals. The serpent remains a serpent, and the dove remains a dove. What the animal is at birth, it is at death. But not so the human being. Mankind cannot remain stationary. It wither moves forward, or it falls back.

Consequently, man is above the things and objects which he can see and perceive, which he can control and which he can know. Man’s purpose, in so far as his physical being is concerned, is to improve nature, whether it be himself or a field, a meadow, an animal, or a tree. But his natural self is limited only to his horizon. Not so the spirit within him. With his soul he can reach out for the hidden mysteries of the universe; he can fathom the depth, climb its height, encircle its breadth; and learn about its every particle, be it physical or metaphysical! Conversely, should man’s desires and passion carry him downward, there is no limit to the depth, extent or bottom, to his degeneration. “*He who comes to be purified, finds help; and he who comes to become contaminated, every gate is opened for him*”.

Every generation contributed to the totality of human experience. Human experience is more than human achievement. Uncontrolled forces are, and ever were, the creators of events which largely influence human history. Facts alone, do not, did not form the threads of which history is woven together. It is yet far from being conclusive whether or not facts alone, or events alone, can be called even the particles of which the threads of history were spun. The spinning-wheel which turns out the threads of history revolves only by a hidden motor; the threads of history are particles of individual souls; and the whole fabric is woven by a mysterious Hand, to which destiny is attached, and in which fates of people are concealed. It is a fact that there lived a certain race of people in Canaan; events came to pass which blotted out the memory of that people. But the fact and the events are not at all related. Another people might have lived there, and under like events - who can tell what would have happened to them?

The journey of a nation across the ages is never continuous; even that of the most powerful and outstanding nation. History records this phenomenon. There is no exception to this almost natural law. But we do find the astounding continuity of Israel to be an historic truth; from Abraham to Moses, 26 generations; from Moses to Ashi (co-editor of the Talmud) 40 generations; from Moses Maimonides, 16 generations, and who will erase the written record of Israel’s history of the last 800 years, since Maimonides?

China and Japan, too, have long and continuous journeys across the ages, but their history is a very short one. A man lived 120 years, ate up in his lifetime 25 acres of potatoes, 75 acres of corn, 60 sheep, 19 cattle, and 8000 lbs. of butter. These are facts. But not history.

History is the recorded self-expression of the soul of a people an individual, a group, a race, or the entire human family. Centuries may pass without enriching the history of mankind by one iota, and in a day history may be made which influences a thousand generations.

Moses was instructed to record every place, every stage, every movement. Miracles were wrought. How did the people take it? Human nature was not changed. In the midst of miracles, they rebelled, doubted, denied, complained. How human!
