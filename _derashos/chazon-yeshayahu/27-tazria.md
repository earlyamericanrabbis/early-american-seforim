*“And the Lord spoke unto Moses, saying: Speak unto the children of Israel, saying: If a woman be delivered and bear a man child. (Vayikra 12:1-3) “Behold what charges precede this commandment? For I am the Lord thy God; sanctify yourselves therefore, and be ye holy; for I am holy” (Ibidum 11:44) After the Lord commanded Israel to be holy, and to be separated from things contaminate, and charged them not to eat of the unclean animals and fowl, nor of the swarming, unclean creatures of the waters, nor of the creeping things on the land. He charged them concerning clean, physical living, with special reference to the purity of family life.”* (Pesikta Vayikra 12)

*The sermon of Rabbi Shimloyee: “The unborn babe in its mother’ s womb rests like a folded volume; its hands upon its temples; its arms upon its thighs; its heels upon its loins, its head between its knees; its mouth sealed; its navel open; it eats and drinks the food and drink its mother partakes of; does not eliminate, lest its mother’s life be endangered. When it is born, that which was sealed opens, and that which was open, closes, els e it would not live an hour. While in its mother’s womb, a light shines over its head, and enables it to see to the ends of the world, even as it is said:*

*‘When his lamp shined above my head, and by his*

*Light I walked through darkness’ (Iyov 29:3)*

*At this, do not marvel; one may sleep here and dream he is in Spain. The best days of the human being in his career upon earth are the ones he spends in his mother’s womb, even as it is said:*

*‘Oh, that I were as in the months of old, as in*

*The days when God watched over me’ (Ib.-2) Is there another time in man’s life wherein there are months and not years? Doubtless Job refers to the months before birth. While in that happy state, the child is instructed in the whole scope of the Torah, even as it is said:*

*‘And he taught me, and said unto me, Let thy heart hold fast my words, Keep my commandments, and live.’ (Mishlei 4:4).*

*And it is moreover said:*

*“When the converse of God was upon my tent” (Iyov 29:4)*

*At birth, an angel comes and touches its mouth, and causes it to forget all it knew, even as it is said: ‘Sin coucheth at the door’ (Bereishis 4:7) The child moreover, is sworn in; as follows: Be righteous, and not wicked; never consider thyself righteous, even the whole world will acclaim thee and say to thee that thou art so; know thee that the Holy One, blessed be He! is pure, and that his ministering angels are pure, and that the soul which He hath given thee is pure; keep it so, and enjoy the good; but know thou, that if thou wilt defile it, it will be taken away from thee.”* (Nidah 30b; Yevamos 71b; Pesikta Vayikra 12).

Purity is much more than the upkeep of hygienic rules. Even mental hygiene does not come to the standard of purity exacted by the Levitical laws.

The propagandists of birth-control and of eugenics are merely grappling in the dark, tracing the evils they seek to remedy by their teachings to the present-day social conditions, and to a conventionalism which hides the most important phases of human life beneath a mask of hypocrisy. Neither of these remedies are necessary under a regulated system of purity. But society at large can not legislate reforms which, in the final analysis, belong to and are part of, individual nature. It is hard to restrain habit; much more human nature. That cannot be done from without; it must come from within.

The most intimate self-expression, if utilized unholy, without self-restraint, in a manner to leave nature take its course, without regard of ultimate disaster, both to the individual and to society at large, must not and cannot be treated with indifference. The sages of yore, in the various classical schools, from the very inception of Hebrew civilization till the latest, Talmudic Yeshivot, at the close of the 19th century, continued to impart wholesome knowledge on the subject, and to teach the Jewish generations throughout history the principles of morality, parenthood and upbringing of children.

At no time was the responsibility, in so far as propagation was concerned, placed upon the shoulder of the woman. She is under no obligation, under the commandment of the Torah, to marry in order to bring up a family. Man must do that; the commandment is his. Under this obligation, for the fulfillment of which the Torah made him responsible, he is to select for himself a helpmeet. If his wife is normally healthy, or sound mind, God-fearing and moral, she is a fit woman to become the mother of his children. The woman commits no sin whatever, if knowing herself to be unhealthy, in danger with her life during childbirth, or unable to care for the issue, she resorts to birth-control, or remains all of her life sterile. The life of a couple where such relationship exists is as moral as that of the normal, healthy couple who become parents of a large, healthy family.

But all couples, under all circumstances, in order to lead humanity in the path of moral, upright and good conduct, have certain hygienic laws to observe, and a certain amount of restraint, politeness, and consideration to practice. In this particular instance, Judaism, for many centuries, employed the then known medical science as its hard-maid. Medical authorities to-day, might not see the elemental dangers in these matters; but practical experience throughout recorded history among the Jewish people can be cited as best evidence that as a preventive of both disease and actual family disruption, the laws of Judaism, as taught in the Talmudic schools for ages past, are scientific. They have to do with mental, if not altogether physical hygiene.

The periodic natural state of woman should be a sign-language to married couples to practice toward one mother only such politeness and necessary companionship as would be the case if they had just been introduced to one another. Menses is a natural state in women which, under the laws of Judaism, calls forth an entire set of rules of conduct toward her by her husband. The same applies to a woman in confinement. Who can tell the mysteries of nature, of God and His purposes. Perhaps, the very phenomenon, though explicit in nature, was made part of the woman’s life to afford her periodically the experience of separation, courtship and real happiness in her home life. Psychologically, it is more than justified to surmise that it was the intention of the Creator to give women just such natural blessings for her complete happiness. The rejuvenating effect upon women this phenomenon has, no student will or can deny.

This too, tests the man’s real character and fitness to be counted in among the moral, self-restraining and self-respecting members of the human family. Civilization does not consist of only the outward polish of mankind. It appears at its best under the most trying circumstances, when man must act like a man. Show me a man who eats correctly, speaks wisely, and treats his wife at all times with consideration, and I will show a really cultured gentleman.

Many treatises were written concerning the intimate, natural life of the people of Israel; volumes were filled with instructions for both men and women, relating thereto. Stated periods of separation were ordained in the Torah (Vayikra 15:19), and the sinfulness of the one disregarding the admonition is considered very serious. (Ibidum 18:19).

Seven clear days, from the time the actual menses ceases, must elapse before immersion returns to the woman her normal state. And the immersion is ordered in a specifically constructed plunge, or pool, which must be filled by water drawn from either rain, spring, ocean or river - not drawn direct from water works or vessel or tub, be it ever so clean and sanitary from the health-department view-point.

Bringing offspring into the world is the most sacred trust the human being was endowed with. Great sages, in all ages, declared: There are three equal partners in the make-up of a human, being: Almighty, father, and mother. The soul comes from God. The dwelling for the soul, the human body, belongs to the parents. No ill body can properly care for the soul. In time, civilized men will realize that crime, evil conduct, vice, viciousness and immorality can be traced to many forms of disease, and the subjects, in every instance, will be treated as hereditary sufferers.

Some eminent authorities of Judaism during all ages agreed that heredity plays the most important part in the life of the human race. Some are born that way and are like that. Environment, too, shares responsibility with heredity, or is credited with, as the case happens to be. The soul does not take away the natural inclinations of the human being; it is given as an agency to improve upon nature, to develop, to stimulate, to educate, to elevate, to purify, to sanctify and to worship. But nature will take its course, nevertheless. Thus, out of lawless, immoral, and criminal parents, society cannot expect useful members.

Judaism at its best, with its uniform candor and universal love, permits proper selection, useful control, and eugenic regulation. Present-day society might consider the means of Judaism to attain the desired end very antiquated; some medical practitioners might find the method of immersion the long period of separation, the pronouncement of sinfulness against disobedience of the dotted line, as superfluous, if not a gross superstition. But there is no psychologist worthy of his name who will not recognize in the entire procedure the grains of a happy, marital state, and of the only means by which crime, disease, ignorance and immorality can be considerably reduced, if not altogether eliminated. At least, they can use the Jewish race as a type. And, with respect thereto, the Jewish people up until the past two or three scores of years lived up to both the letter and spirit of these rules. And who can tell, perhaps therein lies the secret of Jewish immortality, of their indestructibility. In the days of Maimonides, and we have his own testimony for it, the greatest percentage of mortality was due to the lack of a system of sex control. Medical science has achieved much in the field of curing diseases due to extravagance in sex; but out of all evils, no branch of science has made much headway in preventive measures. The reason for it is simple: science can control physical ailment, and even prevent its outbreak; but it cannot dominate a field which is mainly psychological, which requires the co-operation of every individual member of society. That only the individual can do.

Purity, in its broadest sense, must be recognized by society at large through the agency of religion; and, as a self-expression of the soul, it must be in every-day life.

The Pesikta, in commenting upon the opening passage of this chapter of the Torah, rightly pointed at holiness as the sole medium by which one may hope to attain righteous living.

Then — Rabbi Shimlayee’s sermon: “*One does not have to be a believer in the transmigration of souls to comprehend the meaning thereof.*” Do not marvel at all this; the human mind travels fast; he sleeps here and dreams of Spain. More than mere words, one may find in that sermon. And, because life comes from so humble an origin it is the duty of man to elevate it as high, if not higher, than heaven. The only required means to attain it is — self-restraint. That will bring a man to the everlasting fountain of purity.
