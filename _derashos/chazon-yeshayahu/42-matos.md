*“Now the children of Reuben and the children of Gad had a very great multitude of cattle” (Bamidbar 32:1) “A wise man’s heart (understanding) is at his right (hand); but the fool’s heart (understanding) is at his left” (Koheles 10:2). “The wise man’s heart (understanding) is at his right (hand)” refers to Moses, and “a fool’s heart (understanding) is at his left” refers to the children of Gad and to the children of Reuben, who made the principal an addition and the addition a principal. Why? Because they loved their substance more than their souls, for they said to Moses: ‘We will build sheepfolds here for our cattle, and cities for our little ones’ (Bamidbar 32:16). But Moses told them: ‘Not so! Make the principal your first objective, — build your cities for your little ones and then folds for your sheep.’ (Ibid. 24) Which proves that the understanding of the wise is to his right.”* (Midrash-Rabba, Bamidbar 22)

A skilled mechanic measures the work according to the article he is making, according to the material he is using, and according to the tools he is employing. He measures the strokes, the lines, and the time. He knows before hand, the quantity of material necessary, as well as the purpose and use the thing he is making will be put to when ready. And, any mechanic who knows not any of these is a fool. The painter has no use for a hand-saw in drawing the sketch upon the canvas; and the carpenter has no use for a brush in making the frame for the completed painting. Both the artist and the craftsman are needed, but the frame is only for the preservation of the painting, and only a fool will contend that the principal things in an art gallery are the frames.

Every purposeful act aims at some goal, or the act is in vain, and not at all purposeful; so is it true with every life upon earth; it is here for some purpose. Lives are not mere accidents. Or they would not become what they are in their conscious state. The requirement of life is limited to the extent where that life happens to be, under what conditions, under what environment, under what material quantities the ages before have left behind, under what standard of living it chances to develop. Now the greatest problem is: What is sufficient to fill out a life in happiness and contentment?

The answer to this question is filled with so many complications, that it embraces almost every individual life. However, the normal human being must first consider essentials. That minimum consists of fear, shelter, and clothes. The kind of shelter, the kind of food and the kind of clothes, creates another problem for every individual, mainly depending on the standard of living set up by a given community. As civilization advances, the complications advance. The whole becomes what is commonly known as the economic problem. Temporal matters become paramount. Movement of peoples, and people’s movements, are constantly causing strife among mankind, with the resultant periodic upheavals on the battle-field, on the high seas and in the air.

The average time man, in the most enlightened countries, spends at his secular education, is about eight years; thence onward he is toiling to keep himself alive, to acquire substance, to “*build sheepfolds for his cattle*”. That is not as it should be, although it was and it is like that. The little ones are always placed at the end of the camp in the battle of life.

There is but one answer to the question, Why? Materialism! - there are no spiritual values; the spiritual within man, being of the finest web hardly perceptible to the grossness of life, finds no place in human society, no room in the scheme of things because they are things. Organized religion, in some instances not even organized, is not a force for the living. As it is now constituted, it is a mere token dedicated to the dead in its most pronounced expression, and a lotion, if not an opiate, for one’s conscience, in its shadow. With rare exceptions, this condition is the rule in society, as it is constituted today.

The Sabbath meal of a generation or two ago, is one pointed instance of how it is possible to live a full life, enjoy happiness, and be contented. The Sabbath itself is the token of all these. The Manna did not fall on the Sabbath. *“Today ye shall not find it”* (Shemos 17:25). Everything in life is as the Manna was. There should be holy time, given over entirely to the joy of the soul, as well as to the rest for the body. The principal things in life are not the ones people are building sheepfolds for. Not one fortune in history had a lasting value. The Midrash avers that no fortune lasts in a family longer than three generations, as a rule. (Midrash Ruth). The sons of Gad and Reuben showed their folly by placing a higher value on their cattle than on their little ones. Moses advised him in a correct manner, as he saw their folly.

Cities for their children! Both spiritual and temporal. Every city provides grain elevators, storage houses, and other buildings necessary for the conservation of food, goods, and other essentials for the sustenance of life. But very few cities build the places for the little ones. The *Ikkar* became a *Taphol*, and the *Taphol* became an *Ikkar*. The principal became an addition, and the addition a principal!

Indeed, the understanding of the wise man is to his right, but the understanding of the fool is to his left.
