*“And the Lord said unto Moses: Pharaoh will not hearken unto you; that My wonders may be multiplied in the land of Egypt.”* (Shemos 11:9)

*“May the Name of our God be praised?” He delivered the plagues upon Pharaoh and upon his people, in the order of a battle, organized by an earthly king. He first stopped their water-supply by turning their water-sheds into blood, even as the Psalmist says: ‘And turned their rivers into blood, So that they could not drink of their streams!’* (Tehillim 78:44)

*When they did not recant, He frightened them with the terrific noise of the frogs.*

*When they did not recant, He bombarded them with piercing gnats, striking both man and beast, even as it is said: “And there were gnats upon man, and upon beast” (Shemos 8:13) and the gnats actually pierced their bodies as would arrows.*

*When they did not recant, He caused legions to invade their country, even as it is said: “Behold, I will send a swarm of flies upon thee, and upon thy servants, and upon thy people, and into thy houses” (Ib. 17): and it is furthermore said:*

*“He sent among them swarms of flies, which devoured them.” (Tehillim 78.45)*

*When they did not recant, He smote their stock with a pestilence. When they did not recant, he caused poisoned naptha to bring on burning blans upon the skin of every person. When they did not recant, He bombarded their cities and towns with ballists in the form of hail.*

*When they did not recant, He had their land invaded by a ravaging army of locusts. When they did not recant he made prisoners of them all in their own land, by bringing on gross-darkness.*

*And, as they still did not recant, he put to death their first - born, and elders.*” (Pesikta, Shemos 11:9) See also Midrash Tanhuma, Ex.11; and She’iltot Derah Ahi Meshabha, Shemos)

The strangest thing in connection with the liberation of Israel from Egyptian bondage is the repeated assurances God gave unto Moses and Aaron that Pharaoh would not permit the people of Israel to go forth. If, on the other hand, Pharaoh would not permit the people of Israel to go forth because he had no choice in the matter, why did he deserve repeated plagues? The only reason the criminal is punished, the only reason the sinner will suffer is because he can differentiate between right and wrong, and because the choice is his. If the free-will is taken away from a man, he does not deserve punishment. Maimonides’ decision on this matter is accepted by all theologians in Israel, and he so rendered the decision.

But there are two very substantial reasons why Pharaoh drew upon himself, and upon his people, the full list of punishments: (1) his claim to be the chief god, and (2) the great example he serves to all tyrants in history.

\(1\) Pharaoh denied all divine powers, save that of his own. He had his people trained to believe that in his power alone, lied the fate of all mankind, that life and death came from him; that slavery and freedom, war and peace, famine and plenty were in his keeping.

Moses and Aaron came to him as messengers of God; he denied the existence of God, because he did not find His Name recorded in his register.

*“He who comes to be purified, Heaven lends him assistance;*

*He who comes to contaminate himself, the gates are opened for him”.*

Pharaoh, in every instance, manifested that his power is invincible. He asked Moses and Aaron, after every plague to their God to remove it from his land; when it was done, he maintained to all that he accomplished it by his will. He wanted to act as a god in every instance, and he found an open gate to the labyrinth of his troubles. Of course, all that came by direct command of God; no man can move his finger on the earth beneath, unless it is so ordained from heaven above. He knew that his position was merely that of a time-server, a successor to the other Pharaohs; that he was not divine, but a human being with all the shortcomings and failings of a man in high position. But he hardened his heart, because he feared the breaking down of the morale of his own people, and, more particularly, the loss of his own glory, as the god-head of Egypt.

The “open gate” he found ready to enter, when his action suited his inclinations, was a direct punishment from Heaven; he did not lose the free-will exercise of his faculties; but, because he desired it, it was given to him, just as any other sinner who steps out of the limits of decency, and finds the gates of everlasting disgrace open for him.

\(2\) The Torah indulges not in words which merely point an event for the sake of history alone; every word, in connection with every story as depicted therein, has a significant meaning for posterity, a lesson to be derived by all mankind of all times.

Here we have a picture of an immigrant family which contributed to the wealth and progress of a country; the pioneer immigrant of that family saved the people of that country from certain starvation during seven years of famine, and, because of his great ability, that country enriches itself fabulously. The economic independence of that country establishes it as the ranking power of the age, and it launches out upon a program of expansion in every avenue of human enterprise known to the ancient world. Its golden treasures, obtained in exchange for food from a hungry world during the days of Joseph are employed to erect monuments, pyramids, objects of art and sepulchral palaces which continue to be the envy of the ages. In addition, because of its exalted position, it starts out upon a crusade against its neighboring nationals to make them slaves. Slavery becomes the lot of the descendants of Joseph and his brothers. The Egyptians do not stop there. They frame a platform to destroy the descendants of the people which made them so great and created for them that commanding position. In order to accomplish their end, means of hellish torture are employed. Babes are torn away from their mothers to be cast in the river; taskmasters are appointed to watch out for delinquency in forced labor. The Talmudic sage, Rabbi Eleazer bar Rabbi Josse, avers, that some of the material used for the pyramids were the bones of the martyred Hebrew slaves. (See Sanhedrin 112) In addition, beheading or drowning was the punishment meted out for failing to yield the amount of production decided upon by the taskmasters.

The crime of ingratitude was augmented by the crimes of murder, torture, forced labor, and finally, threatened destruction.

In the long trail of suffering, the Jewish history, merely by changing the names of countries and tyrants, offers many similar forms of exile slavery, despoilation, murder and confiscation.

Because Pharaoh and his people were first to plot such heinous crimes against the people of Israel, God made an example of them. He set up tokens, and performed wonders to amaze all ages, to impress human history with the everlasting truth that tyranny must crumble, that slavery as an institution in society, must be abolished, and that the authors of both, tyranny and slavery, are sure to be overtaken by retribution.

God wars upon tyrants as they themselves do against their victims. The tokens and wonders, preserved for posterity to derive lessons from were delivered in a manner that every human being should be able to comprehend, to remember. Pharaoh had first to be humbled to the ground before he let Israel go. A tyrant is the most selfish, and at the same time, the most cowardly being among his subjects. As long as the trouble does not affect him personally, though his entire empire might be shaken to its very foundation, he remains obdurate. Had the last plague been given first, or had it been the only one inflicted, even though Pharaoh had been destroyed, the forces which are ever present and ready to support tyranny, or slavery, would have retained their arrogant attitude and most assuredly would have established every evil in Egypt, Pharaoh as well as his “gods” — even the gods of Egypt— had to receive their measure of justice, and, in so far as that land is concerned, no attempt to repeat what they did to Israel is on record.

Moreover, the order of the plagues was of such steady and sustaining character that it affected all classes, all ages and all sections. Their water supply, their morale, their stock, their food supply, their health, their homes, their locomotion, and finally their lives all were affected. It seems that each plague was a preparation for what followed, both strategically, and scientifically. When the rivers were polluted, the frogs had to come ashore; the heaps of exposed dead frogs brought on gnats; then a swarm of flies, then a pestilence followed by skin disease. All these were tokens. Then came real wonders; bombardment of their homes by hail; imprisonment of their population by darkness, and finally death of the firstborn and elder sin each house.

Wisely have the Midrashic sages pointed at these tokens and wonders, saying “*May the Name of our God be praised! He delivered the plagues upon Pharaoh and upon his people in the order of a battle, organized by an earthly king.*”

And it is for these two reasons that our Torah testifies, saying: “*And the Lord said unto Moses: Pharaoh will not hearken unto you; that My wonders may be multiplied in the land of Egypt.*”
