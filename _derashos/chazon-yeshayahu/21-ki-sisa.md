*“This they shall give, every one that passeth among them that are numbered, half a shekel after the shekel of the sanctuary - the shekel is twenty Gerahas - half a shekel for an offering to the Lord.”* (Shemos 30:13)

*“Life is regulated by certain laws and fundamental rules, which God made as nature’s manifestation of functioning. These laws, in so far as the body is concerned, are ten in number. They are: (1) deglutation (2) retention; (3) digestion; (4) elimination; (5) reproduction; (6) sight; (7) hearing; (8) olfaction; (9) gustation; (10) feeling.*

*The soul, too, has rules and laws by which one can recognize its manifestations as being spiritual and not physical. The five senses, which are common among all living creatures, are of an entirely different character in the human being.*

*(1) Sight in the human being becomes vision; the soul transmits to the eye the knowledge of what the object it sees really is, or what it is possible to be.*

*(2) Hearing in the human being, too, is different than in the lower animals; by the aid of the soul, man understands and perceives what he hears.*

*(3) Olfaction by the aid of the soul, becomes quite a different sense in human beings; different than that of ordinary smell; by means of its spirituality, man is able to utilize this sense in an measurably broader sense than is the lower animal.*

*(4) Gustation, under the guidance of the soul becomes of far greater use to the progress of intellectual research than physical taste.*

*(5) Feeling by the fine direction of the soul, is of a far different nature than the natural touch.*

*“All of these physical senses are directly connected by the human nerve-net to the brain, and it is from the brain that each physical sense receives the intellectual or spiritual acceleration.”*

*The soul’s manifestation of spirituality is, moreover, augmented by five intellectual phenomena, which are:*

*(1) The power to retain impressions, gathered by the sense of sight, long after such panorama is out of view, far away, or extinct.*

*(2) The power of memory, which recalls to one’s mind that which he studied, saw, touched, or heard, or tested a long time ago.*

*(3) The power of imagination, to combine two impressions in one, or to create an imaginary being by combining two different beings, for instance a winged horse.*

*(4) The power of thinking, to figure out something by applying the condition of another thing to it, as, for instance, when one thinks of an apple, he can think of either red or green.*

*(5) The power of reasoning things which are not by things which are, as for example; All men are alive. Every living thing has feeling; therefore, every man has feelings. That is reasoning out a syllogism.*

*“Thus, both the body and the soul manifest an equal number of powers. All of which total the number twenty.”* (Isaac Arama, Akedah, Thesis 52.)

*“Rabbi Johanan said: Because the transgression of the children of Israel in the wilderness violated every one of the Ten Commandments, all were told to redeem their souls by contributing half a shekel, which is 10 Gerahs. Rabbi Simeon ben Lakish said: Because they sold Rachel’s first-born, Joseph, for twenty pieces of silver, and each of the brothers took a coin, each descendant of the tribes was told to contribute a coin as a redemption for his soul”* (Midrash Tanhum, Shemos 30:10)

Rabbi Johanan, Rabbi Simeon ben Lakish, and the author of the *Akedah*, as well as a number of other Agadists and theological philosophers, both, of the ancient and the medieval schools, appear to have forced into the text of this *Sidrah*, in the passage dealing with the half shekel, much more than a mere commandment to give a minimum of half *shekel* annually to the poor, as the Verse really indicates, and is so treated in *Halakah*. The Agadists, however, base their moral structure upon that part of the passage which gives the number of coins in the shekel, by saying; *“The shekel is twenty Gerahs”*, and by saying: *“This shall they give”*, from which the inference is derived that the number of coins has to do with the commandment, and that the word “*This*”—*Zeh*—has to do with the nature of the half *shekel* itself.

And the Agadists were right. The commandment here carries along a reason for giving the half shekel: “*A ransom for his soul*”. Evidently the amount stipulated was intended as a symbol, as its intrinsic value is of no importance in comparison to what it was morally to each and every soul which was to be ransomed.

Arama, with his great analytical mind, discovered the symbol in the number of *Gerahs*. Half a *shekel* consisted of ten of the smallest coins in the realm. That is fundamental in the economic and material life of a people. As a parallelism, he set up the ten physical manifestations in the human being. Without the aid of the ten spiritual, or intellectual manifestations of the soul, the human being would be as incapable of either knowing or doing what is wrong as are the lower animals. When the people sinned, a token was given to them; they were charged to bring “*a ransom*” for their soul, half a shekel, consisting of ten *Gerahs*, which was to be symbolic to them that they should be more careful in combining the forces within them, and not use them for evil.

Rabbi Johanan saw in the coin consisting of ten *Gerahs* the symbol of the Ten Commandments. Rabbi Simeon ben Lakish saw in it the symbol of the ten brothers who conspired to sell Rachel’s firstborn son. But there is no difference of opinion between these sages; each one added to what the other pointed at. According to Rabbi Johanan, the lowermost path to which the human soul can reach is in violating the Ten Commandments.

Rabbi Simeon ben Lakish taught a still greater lesson: One may observe the commandments, and be just as the sons of Jacob, and yet, under the guise of justice, sell Rachel’s firstborn for pieces of silver. Expediency, economic necessity, political consideration, more than once in the course of history brought on a war between fellow beings, and the real underlying reason was based, not upon principles but upon pieces of silver, upon half a *shekel*.

Arama, adding his deep thought to these two great sages, penetrated the very heart of the subject. The *Gerahs* were symbolic of both the body and the soul, the physical and the spiritual, the material and the intellectual, the very composite being by which man is distinguished from the lower animals.

Wisely did the sages say: “*God Himself showed to Moses the coin that was wanted of them as ransom for their souls—Zeh Yitnu—like unto this one shall they five. — Ko-Zeh Yitnu.*”
