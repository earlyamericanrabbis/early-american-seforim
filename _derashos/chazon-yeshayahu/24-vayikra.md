*“And it any one sin, in that he heareth the voice of adjuration, he being a witness, whether he hath seen or known, if he do not utter it, then he shall bear his iniquity.”* (Vayikra 5:1)

*“And if any one sin in that he heareth the voice of adjuration: This is even as the Ecclesiast wrote: ‘Be not rash with thy mouth, and let not thy heart be hasty to utter a word before God; for God is in heaven, and thou upon earth; therefore let thy words be few’ (Koheles 5:1) Both of those passages refer to such men as blaspheme the name of God, blessed be the Holy One! Woe upon the wicked people who blaspheme the Name of God, or take his Name in vain! It is, moreover written: ‘Whoso is partner with a thief hat - eth his own soul; He heareth the adjuration and uttereth nothing’ (Mishlei 29.24) Even as the thief when caught, his accomplice too, is guilty; so is the one who hears blasphemy and comes not before a tribunal to testify against the adjurer. Let no man offer an excuse, saying: ‘What, I shall bring evil report against a fellow-being? for, God said: concerning all other matters of offensive speech one might plead that he could not tell on his fellow but not so concerning blasphemy. One will risk his life if his father’s honor is attacked; even so must one do when the honor of our Father in heaven is attacked. Moses said: ‘Is not He thy father that hath gotten thee? Hath He not made thee, and established thee?’* (Devarim 32:6; Midrash Tanhuma Vayikra 5:7)

Speech is the greatest blessing God conferred upon the human race. The tongue controls both the pen and the sword. The art of speech excels all other arts, nay it created them. There is nothing more striking in nature, than to see and hear a great man giving life to great thoughts by means of properly arranged, well chosen and clearly pronounced word. Human speech is capable of moving heaven and earth; it finds an opening in stone, a door in steel, a way in a desert, a path in the ocean, and an echo in space; it breaks fetters, shatters citadels, moves mountains, shadows the day and lights the night; it names objects, produces visions, gives wings to a horse, and music to a raven; it humbles tyrants, elevates oppressed, builds continents, and dries oceans; it educates the savage, restrains the mob, charms the babe, and frightens the hangman; it tames the lion, spans rigors, moves the Almighty and calls forth His blessings.

Human speech, moreover, instructs the philosopher, aids the scientist inspires the poet, and counsels the ruler; it adds beauty to nature, sweetness to beauty, divinity to religion, and grace to a mother; it finds delight in man knowledge in beast, friendship in flowers, and love in birds; it discovers treasures, constructs monuments, wins battles, and saves life; it ascends every height, reaches every depth, overtakes the longest distances, and conquers every element; it removes dangers, prolongs life, heals the sick, and feeds the hungry; it reveals secrets, interprets dreams, prevents catastrophes, and accumulates wealth; it makes a heaven on earth, happiness out of misfortune, liberty out of slavery, and discipline out of lawlessness; it shortens the long nights of winter, prolongs the days of summer, conserves energy, and utilizes elemental forces; it satisfies the soul’s cravings, the body’s needs, the world’s demands, and God’s commands.

But human speech has a reverse side, too. I can curse, swear, lie, malign; it brings on war, pestilence, famine and distress; it multiplies orphans, afflicts widows, chains freemen, and elevates upstarts; it deceives the innocent, gives gain to the swindler, bestows reward upon the hypocrite, and encourages vice; it dopes the sober-minded, corrupts the guards, invades privacy and breaks homes; it levels useful structures, raises dunghills, worships prostitution, and invites the devil to define piety; it bears tales, spreads slander, poisons minds and protects whoredom; it hails the murderer, enshrines the gangster, votes in office the grafter and shields the briber; it perverts justice, condemns the righteous, downs the able and gives leadership to the unfit; it forms conspiracies and calls them organizations, passes fiendish laws, and crowns folly as arbiter; it robs the poor and calls it taxation, imprisons them and calls it justice, drains their vitality and calls it industrial expediency, and legislates old-age pensions and calls it state benevolence; it graduates as lawyers, leeches, who suck the blood of the hungry, assist demons to transfer hell upon earth, and worship at the feet of injustice; it sends forth ignorant prattlers in priestly garb who call upon Satan to grace their job, they follow him truly, and turn the entire membership over to his camp; it desecrates the divine, consecrates the profane, sugar-coats poison, and poisons the sweetness of life; it hisses at prophets, cheers charlatanism, buries honesty and embraces lechery; it undermines liberalism, sets up oligarchy, strives the aged, and misleads the young; it fights for hatred, makes peace with the gallows, perpetuates lawlessness and erects monuments to chaos.

“*Both life and death are in the power of the tongue.*”

Tale-bearing, sycophancy, pen-poisoning, spying upon the innocent, groundless accusations, framing-up people - all are the strange children of selfishness, greed, vengeance, ambition, lewdness, jealousy, prejudice, perversion, cowardice and godlessness. But they all have “human soil” upon which to thrive, be they without a grain of substantial truth, or sane reasoning. It is native to the human mind and heart; in its rough, human conduct it is like that. The behavior of the most polished, under certain conditions and circumstances, falls back upon its roughness, and exposes not a finished product of God, but a half-man half-beast — a real product of the devil.

Godlessness is the root of all social unrest, crime, and chaos. The tyrant fears not; he merely carries his whims and fancies to their extreme limits; God is never thought of by him. The criminal fears not; he rarely thinks of anything save his own inclinations; God is not in his heart. The sycophant fears not; he rarely thinks of other people’s rights to live; he knows not the ways of God. The blasphemer fears not; boldly he proclaims his cult; he is loud in advocating that there is no God. You can strip the human being of everything; blind him, cripple him, throw him in a dungeon, famish him, torture him, but let God be in his heart, and he remains a human being; give a human being money, power, a palace, his heart’s full desires, but take away God from him, and you will have the devil incarnate.

Man has the inherent right of free action; he is the eternal agent of his own conscience; no one can claim the right to chain the ideas, or actions of a fellow-being; that strikes at the very root of all human conduct, of the social structure, of the very vitality of organized society. Rob the child of his God and you will raise a lawless, heartless, criminal, who will stop at nothing once the powers of good and evil will be in his own hand.

Man has moments of impulsiveness, of anger, of either just or unjust provocation; he endeavors to ease his feelings; he finds relief in swearing, in pouring out foolish senseless speech which deny the existence of either refinement or culture within his make-up. He calls upon every cell in his brain to shake out the last nasty word stored therein; he throws out the words as if he were slinging away coals of fire upon the bald pate of his antagonist; he adds meaning to every syllable, and silliness to every expression of his ugly temper; the contortion of his make-up bring out the short, nasty sentences from between his lips as if he was in a paroxysm of epilepsy. And, he usually blasphemes by taking the name of God in vain, while in such a state of beastliness. That part of the human make-up is ugliness incarnate, the embodiment of everything evil, the self-expression of a monster who broke in among human beings, through a back door. It is human to be angry, when justly provoked; it is even an admitted fact that the physiological make-up of a person changes when angry and is restored to tranquility when that change runs its course, and it usually runs its course by a physical manifestation of impatience, which brings out disorganized words of impolite character. But the blasphemer takes no account of his physiological, or other changes; his heart’s evil is poured out in the ears of his fellow-man; he even finds delight in giving his inner feelings a public ventilation.

And, worse than all is the arch-propagandist; the man who is not at all angry; he considers it a duty to “educate” the men of his generation in the doctrine of atheism; mocking at everything sacred, he makes his calling; blasphemy to him is an avocation; it usually is connected in his distorted brain with what he terms as “liberalism”; he depicts life without God as the goal of mankind; he propagates a species of “idealism” which uncovers to the human mind one gigantic grave for all spirituality, and pictures that vision as the ultimate Eden of life on this planet. He is the leper-at-large whom a whole world cannot or is unwilling to isolate.

When a stream of human passion floods the gates of tyranny and drowns both the tyrant and his system, the presence of the blasphemer is never missed; he usually urges the mob to believe that all that which they were forced to destroy was due to the belief in God; revolution is usually accompanied with such propaganda. The difference between fanaticism, superstition, hierarchy, enforced sectarianism, religious persecution, unholiness of the sword which strikes for the enforcement of dogma, and pure belief in God, and the free exercise thereof, usually escapes the mind of the blasphemer, and instead of aiding to free religion, he blasphemes with a vehemence and seeks its complete destruction.

The book of Leviticus opens with the theme of sacrificials, of atoning for sins; but it reaches its greatest height in this chapter when it brands the one who listens to blasphemy and does not oppose the blasphemer by testifying against him, as an equal sinner to the blasphemer himself.

“*Whoso is a partner with a thief hateth his own soul.*” The thief steals from a man, something which has an intrinsic value; the loss may be regained; the victim might not even be affected by the loss; but the thief who steals one’s soul who while stealing it, tells him: “I take nothing from you; you have no soul, for it only exists in your mental make-up; reaches the very pinnacle of knavery.” If he’s believed in, he replaces God; if his doctrine is accepted, he replaces religion; if his vision is incorporated in human thought, all ends at death’s door, which is his mouth. One must first hate his own soul with a deadly hate, before he ensnares himself and commits spiritual suicide.

“*Is not He thy father that hath gotten thee, Hath He not made thee, and established thee?*”

What answer does the blasphemer give to this question? Of course a negative one. The Talmudic legalism adjudges the right to claim the orphan as his begotten chilt to the one who raises him, who gives him a home and bread. Thus, when a blasphemer happens to declare to one: “you only have unearthly father, you are but an animal, of animal descent, without any spiritual part in your make-up, you are no better than a maggot; you have no more soul than a flea; “if he is believed in, then he supercedes the Father in heaven in that one’s conscience, and has every right to claim him as an intellectual child. It is more modern to call such one - “follower”, or “disciple”. It is the ancient bargain of the devil and the human being, on a more solid business basis, although in reality, one is tweedledum and the other is tweedledee.

The first line of defence against invasion is found in this passage. “*And if any one sin, in that he heareth adjuration, he being a witness, whether he hath seen or known, if he do not utter it, then he shall bear his iniquity.*”

Do not listen to the blasphemer, to the one who takes the Name of God in vain, who swears. Tell him politely, as a brother: “*Be not rash with thy mouth, and let not thy heart be hasty to utter a word before God; for God is in heaven, and thou upon earth; therefore let thy words be few.*”
