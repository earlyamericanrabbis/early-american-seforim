*“And the Lord spoke unto Moses saying: This shall be the law of the leper in the day of his cleansing.”* (Vayikra 14:1-2)

*“This is what the Verse says: ‘Let us search and try our ways, and return to the Lord.’ (Eicha 3.40) What new principle does the Prophet bring out in this Verse? Know, indeed, what he propounds and clarifies therein: All the troubles which a person encounters are but the result of his doing. The Holy One, blessed be He! is a true Judge; in his justice there is no perversion. Therefore, says the prophet, when a person of a sudden finds himself afflicted with troubles, he must not say: ‘ what harm did I to deserve this?’, but instead, let him search his ways, and finding himself wanting, let him repent. Happy is he who is stronger than his evil inclination, and does not find fault with his Creator, but trusts in Him with all his heart. Behold, these four different forms of leprosy, rising, scab, bright spot, and skin disease are not visited by the Holy One, blessed be He!, upon any person, save only if such person’s sin call forth such plagues. As example, the daughters of Zion might be cited, concerning whom it is said:*

*Moreover the Lord said:*

*Because the daughters of Zion are haughty,*

*And walk with stretched forth necks, and wanton eyes,*

*Walking and mincing as they go,*

*And making a tinkling with their feet;*

*Therefore the Lord will smite with a scab*

*The crown of the head of the daughters of Zion,*

*And the Lord will lay bare their secret parts.’ (Yeshayahu 4:16-17).*

*Nevertheless, every leper was given an opportunity to be cleansed of his dreaded disease.”* (Midrash Agadah, Vayikra 2)

Man is a free agent to exercise his will and to regulate his action as his own inclination dictates. There is no preordination of a man’s action, unless he suffers from a physical ailment which modern psychologists define as compulsion.

*“Out of the Most High proceedeth not Evil and Good.”* (Eicha 3:38), should not be made part of an interrogatory sentence, but a positive doctrine enunciated by Jeremiah. Should it be said: *“God orders man to do either Evil or Good”*, what choice has man, what reason would God have to punish man?

On the contrary, God pointed out to man the “path of life”, to aid him, if he comes to purify himself. He also created the path of death, and that too, was pointed out to man. If, under these circumstances, man sins, he alone brings punishment upon himself. Conversely, if he does good, his reward comes to him by his own merit.

This doctrine pointed out here as having been proclaimed by Jeremiah, was established firmly as a fundamental part of Judaism by Maimonides and endorsed by the entire school of medieval Jewish philosophers.

There are two forms of idealism in the firmest principles of Jewish belief; one is love, the other is fear. The Torah commands us to achieve both of these; to love God and to fear Him. But, in this connection, the terms as such, must not be accepted in their ordinary meaning. A child fears to be a- lone at night; so do nervous women; so do sick people; so do superstitious people. A child loves sweets; grown-up people love meats; some love money; others love titles. Neither this kind of fear nor this kind of love is meant by the Torah in connection with the fear and love one must practice toward God. People fear an invading enemy; so do they fear a gunman; so do they fear an informer; so do they fear a plague. People love their friends; so do they al- so love their parents; Neither this kind of fear nor this kind of love is meant in connection with the fear and love one must practice toward God.

To fear God solely because He might punish us is not a high grade of belief; He will punish us if we be guilty. To love God solely because He may reward us is not a high grade of belief; He will reward us if we deserve it. Both of these kinds of belief emanate from a selfish desire to be on the safe side; to gain and not to lose; to have and not to part with; to enjoy and not to suffer. Nevertheless, the majority of people are very apt to hold on to these forms of belief, rather than to attain a height which would eliminate all of them completely, and be filled with awe and with love for Him who spoke and the world sprang forth.

It is related: A very saintly man paid frequent visits to a border town, where some poor Jewish people made their headquarters in the course of their trade. The town had no lodging-house for the poor among them, who could not afford to pay for their meals and lodging, and support their families, too, on their small earnings. Now, the guest-house - *Hachnosat Orchim* - was a sacred institution which existed in every Jewish community. This irritated the saintly visitor. He had no money of his own, and being a stranger, he knew that his appeal to the town-folks would be put a voice in the wilderness. He, however, managed to find out that the wealthiest person in the town was, beside a miser, quite pious in his belief in a hereafter. To this miser went the saint. And here is the bargain he made, in order to establish a *Hachnosat Orchim* in that town; For the sum of two hundred dollars he sold to the miser his own share in the hereafter; all of the rewards for every food deed, all of the rewards for every precept he had observed - in short, every claim he had to a share in the hereafter, he transferred to the miser, and publicly proclaimed the sale thereof to the entire congregation, which had assembled for the occasion, on the request of the rich miser.

The saintly man, after receiving the two hundred dollars, opened a *Hachnosat Orchim*, and made a present thereof to the poor wanderers. He delivered the dedicatory prayer, which was as follows: “*Lord of the universe! I am now the poorest of all poor. I have nothing on this earth and much less in the world to come. I have no merits to ever deserve the least reward from Thee. With a heart full of love for Thee O Lord, and with a trembling body full of awe beholding Thy great works in heaven above and upon earth beneath, I pray to Thee to accept this token of Zedekah from my helpless hands. Now I serve Thee with true love and real fear. I expect no reward, and have no fear of being punished, as I am ready to suffer for my fellow beings. By dedicating this place for the poor wanderer, I beseech Thee to ever remember the helplessness of these people, to ever forgive their sins, and to aid them in their hour of need, so that wherever they go, they may glorify Thy Name.*”

It is needless to add that the saint did not remain to hear the glory showered upon his head in that town. Such is called love and fear toward God. But the story would be but half told, without telling that before long the rich miser enjoyed the hospitality of the *Hachnosat Orchim*, as he lost all of his wealth, and died cursing among all others, “*that swindler who got away with my two hundred dollars.*”

Such is called retribution. There was no fore-ordination for either the saint to have such soul, nor for the miser to have such accursed one; both were the free agents of their will.

Leprosy in all its forms, moral as well as physical, comes as a just retribution. Fatalists, gamblers, superstitious, and other kinds of abnormal persons might or might not be influenced by doctrines of pure belief, which have withstood the test of time; but the pure and the normal, and the believers in a special Providence, do see the very things which happen within their own ranks with a vision that leaves no doubt in their minds as to the wonder-working truth one gathers in a lifetime of observation.

Disease, crime, strife, and many other social evils do not descend from heaven; they are brought on by man’s own folly, by his imprudent conduct, by his ignorance, and by his refusal to learn and to know.

One’s sin might be the misfortune of his son seventy years later, (See Midrash Agadah Vayikra 14). But the punishment visited upon the son does not come about because of God’s vengeance; it is merely the outgrowth of the sin; nature’s course just takes that long before it appears in the form of punishment.

With respect to religious belief, one must be careful to define the pure, so that no impure will take hold upon the mind. Plague, pestilences, diseases, sicknesses of all kinds are subjecting mankind to an acid test. The claim that it all is a visitation from heaven, and that it all is a mystery, connected with religious belief, if religious people maintain such assertion, they defeat the very purpose of religion, and make the issues against it stronger. Every natural disadvantage man is subject to, he must not claim that it came about by the mysterious ways of God. It is almost, if not entirely, blasphemous to make such assertion, or lay such claim to Divine will, or purpose. From the very depths of man’s soul, from his innermost feelings, from his most hidden thoughts, from his most forgotten acts come forth many of the evils which, when he encounters them, he considers strangers to him, but in reality are his own true-begotten creatures.

God must not be charged with the punishment a leper’s son was inflicted with; nature took its course; the leper received his retribution; society is the poorer for having permitted leprosy to invade its domain. But “*out of the mouth of the Most High proceeded not evil and good.*”

“*Nevertheless, both the moral and the physical leper, have the opportunity of cleansing themselves.*” The moral leper, by repentance; the physical leper by treatment. But both must know that they are lepers, a menace to society. And, society, if it fears and loves God, must be ever watchful, on its guard, against the spread of such disease in its midst. It cannot blame God, if it is careless; it will have only its own self to blame, if it is derelict in its duties.

“*This shall be the law of the leper in the day of his cleansing.*”
