*“So Moses the servant of God died there in the land of Moab, according to the word of the Lord. And he was buried in the valley in the land of Moab over against Beth-peor; and no man knoweth of his sepulchre unto this day.”* (Devarim 34:5 et seq.)

*“In the long, long ago, a delegation journeyed to Beth-peor and petitioned the authorities there, saying: ‘Show us where Moses is buried’. The authorities appointed a guide who led them to the summit of the mountain. They found not the sepulchre on the summit, but they thought that they saw it in the valley. The guide took them down to the valley. It was not there, but it appeared to them to be on the summit. The delegation then divided itself-half went to the summit and the other half remained in the valley. They that stood on the summit thought they were seeing down in the valley; and they that remained in the valley thought they were seeing it on the summit. This verified the verse which states: ‘An woman knoweth of his sepulchre unto this day.’”* (Sotah 14 a; Yalkut Shimony Devarim 84:965).

*“And there went a man of the house of Levi, and took to wife a daughter of Levi. And the woman conceived, and bore a son”* (Shemos 2:1-2 et seq)

That was the birth of Moses. His death was as natural. Only his sepulchre, although the place is named, remains unseen. Between his birth and his death one hundred and twenty years elapsed. They were the most momentous years not only in the history of Israel but in the whole history of the human race. The exodus of Israel, in itself, was momentous enough; but the happening beneath Sinai stands out alone. When a race of people stepped in line and followed the trend of civilization, and when all the rest of the backward, or the forward, races will fall in line, no other starting point than Sinai could or would be seen, either by the philosopher or by the ecclesiast. Like unto blades of grass through the stone walls of a prison, the rays of Sinai pierced through the gross darkness of the ages and kindled the torches of civilization. It was Moses who went up to Sinai. Prophet and High Priest, (Zevachim 111a) ruler and lawgiver, and the most meek of all men! Endowed with a heart and head to perceive and reason all that there was to know about God (Midrash Tanhuma 90:5), all that he did he credited to others (Berachos 10b) Ascending by a pillar of cloud to receive the Torah from God (Yoma 4a), he was not ashamed to confess that there were things he did not know. (Zevachim 101a)

“*And the children of Israel wept for Moses in the plains of Moab, thirty days; so the days of weeping in the mourning for Moses were ended.*” (Devarim 34:8) “*Moses died, and Joshua led Israel toward the Promised Land.*” There was no time for lamentation. Even the thirty days’ period of weeping, was considered too long an interruption in the March toward the goal. No advantage must be given the enemy, no disheartening sigh must be conveyed to him. Moses would not want them to fail! The new generation, born in freedom dared not risk a return to slavery! Besides, hero worship was never a part of the service of Israel, which is entirely devoted to God.

Nevertheless, the Torah is called *Torah Moshe* - The Law of Moses. This not by a proclamation of the people of Israel, not by an edict of the Sanhedrin, but by the Word of God.

“*And He buried him*” - *Vayikbor Oto* - God buried Moses? The translators took special privileges with these two words.. But the words mean literally and figuratively just that - and He buried. “*Vayikobar*” should have been the verb to mean; as the translators have it, And he was buried. Even if the vocalization should be changed to make it sound *Vayikbar*, for the letters are the same, the pronoun *Oto* - him - would be entirely out of place. The attempt to disregard the masseritic text in order to rationalize the meaning of the Verse is unworthy of any Torah translator for the Torah cannot and should not be rationalized. The Talmud elaborated on this Verse by the Following: “*God, being the universal High-Priest when He buried Moses, in what water-font did He take the immersion to purify Himself from contamination?*” The question, too, was asked by a similar rationalist who could understand the Torah only as a work of man, and therefore, conceived God only with anthropomorphic attributes. It was a Saducee that asked this question of Rabbi Abuhah. The latter answered the Saducee accordingly, by telling him that God took His immersion in fire! (Sanhedrin 39c)

Almighty Himself prevented at least part of the people from making a deity out of Moses, and from worshipping his grave. He buried him, and no man knoweth the sepulchre of Moses. The Torah is the Torah of Moses. Being the greatest of man he was immortalized in the greatest Book of books. Moses was never intended to become a godhead or part of one. He was, and continues to be, a symbol of the people he so dearly loved and so faithfully served. In life and in death he stands out most high among man, and yet so humble that he appears to be only among the most lowly. High and low can find his life comparable to their own. A champion of the submerged, a fugitive, a king in Media, a shepherd, a husband and father, a trusted emissary, an enemy of tyranny, a liberator, a lawgiver, a warrior, a leader - a man under all trials and triumphs. Above him was God!

The summit and the valley both claim him, but he is in neither place when looked for to be deified. The very mighty see how humble he was, and the very submerged see how high he reached out and attained. No particular spot is the site of his sepulchre. In his death, as a human being, we see the Hand of God. In his burial we see the Hand of God. And we can see Moses only in the role of a man!
