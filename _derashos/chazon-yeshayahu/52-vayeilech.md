*“And Moses wrote this law, and delivered it unto the priests, the sons of Levi, that bore the ark of the covenant of the Lord, and unto all the Elders of Israel. And Moses commanded them, saying: ‘At the end of every seven years, in the set time of the years of release, in the feast of tabernacles, when all Israel is come to appear before the Lord thy God in the places which He shall choose, thou shalt read this law before all Israel in their hearing. Assemble the people, the men and the women and the little ones and the stranger that is within thy gates, that they may hear, and that they may learn, and fear the Lord thy God, and observe to do all the words of this law; and that their children, who have not known, may hear, and learn to fear the Lord your God, as long as ye live in the land whither ye go over the Jordan to possess it.’”* (Devarim 31:9-13)

This is called the *Parshat HaMelech* - the King’s Chapter, and it is also known as the precept *Hakheil* - commandment of Convocation. The procedure was as follows: At the termination of the first day of Succoth, at night, at the conclusion of each Sabbatical year, on a large and imposing wooden platform - *Bimah* - in the Women’s Court of the Temple, the King of the realm appeared. The Hazan then brought a *Sepher* *Torah* - Scroll of the Pentateuch - which he handed to the presiding officer, he in turn handed it to the vice-president, who handed it to the High-Priest, from whom the King received it. All, during the ceremony, remained standing, but when the King received the Sepher Torah, he sat down, and read from it the following chapters from the book of Deuteronomy; 1-6:4; from 6:4 to 11:21; he then read from 14:22 to 16:16; then he read from 28:1 to 31:30. (Sotah 41; Yalkut Shimony, Devarim 31:960)

“*What means, ‘And they had the hands of a man under their wings on their four sides*’ *(Yechezkiel 1:8)? Said Rav Kahane in the name of Rabbi Ishmael b. Rabbi Jesse, and others aver that it was said by the sages in the name of Rabbi Judah Nashi: The Hebrew text has the letter Wove after the Dalet which should be read V’ Yalo - His hand - not as it is pronounced V’Day- and the hands. This refers to no other than to Hand of the Holy One blessed be He! which are ever spread out, ready to receive the penitents before the law takes its course.”* (Pesahim 119b)

There are kinds of work that man can do; (a) useless (b) amusing, (c) purposeful.

\(a\) A tailor threads a needy, takes up cloth and makes stitches, but in the end he accomplishes nothing, the end of the thread had no knot; he forgot to make it. Or, a carpenter takes a plane and a rough board and continues to go through the motions of planning, but no shavings come forth; he had forgotten to insert the blade in the plane. Such, and similar work is useless. One may tire himself out, waste his energy, be busy all day and in the end produce nothing, earn nothing and have nothing.

\(b\) One lifts his feet, stumps with them, skip across a floor, goes through all sorts of motions with his or her body - dances. It is really non-productive work, but it amuses the audience, entertains them. The dancer may even receive his or her hire in return for the non-productive labor, but the work is, nevertheless, of some use, either to the dancers, or the audience, or to both as is often the case. This and like non-productive labor, is amusing work. It fills a certain space in the roomy makeup of the human family.

\(c\) The purposeful work moves the human wheels and advances the human march across the ages. The human family lives on the production and achievement of every individual member of society, who is contributing his or her share for what he or she gets in return from society. And, while individual effort is mandatory, the philosophy of Jewish history teaches that the individual counted little in the sum and substance of Jewish culture and communal life. The patriarchate, then the tribal system, had to yield to a centralized authority. Democracy itself, after listing for more than four centuries, finally had to submit to a monarchy. With the completion of the Temple, centralization took on a still firmer grasp. Judaism itself is tending toward centralization. The individual counts little. Community service was inaugurated during its very earliest stages of development. Groups, large or small, with a minimum of ten males over thirteen years old, were the only authorize unit to hold public worship. But the best efforts were directed to centralize worship by large assemblies at Jerusalem in the Temple.

The King, while not the ecclesiastical head, was nevertheless obliged to instruct the people at a special convocation, once in seven years, at the end of every seventh, or Sabbatical year. His “*speech from the throne*”, was ever ready for him. It had to do with the precept of *Hakhale* - Convocation. He could not add a single word to the text of the Torah! His reading of the Torah, from a Sopher Torah, was all that the multitude of people expected of him. In his honor the text where the precept of *Hakhale* appears, is called *Parashat HaMelech* - chapter of the king; but that was all. The main thing was the great assembly - the convocation, the coming together of the individuals and forming one large community. Homage was paid neither to King nor to potentate but to the Torah.

The power of great assemblies of people for good or for evil is unlimited. Every revolution had its fiery start in an unruly mob. Every movement for the betterment of the world had its birth in a similar manner. Free assembly is not an institution granted by the Bill of Rights. It is even not of a permissive character. It is a precept of the Torah - the Mitzvah of *Hakhale*.

The glory that was Israel’s he was deprived of, both by his own evil and by external oppression. It is affirmed by the sages that every nation has its angel in nation. Among the living creatures there is one called Israel. At night the symbol of Faith appears on its forehead, during the day the symbol of truth appears thereon. Truth is realization, Faith is expectation. Eretz Yisrael is Israel’s realization, *Galuth* is his expectation. The medium by which he may attain that realization, and by which he can withstand a prolonged expectation, the pillar of cloud to shield him by day from the scorching sun and the pillar of flame by night to show him the way, is the Torah.

Too many labors in vain did Israel contribute to his own misfortune during his wanderings; too much did he work to amuse others. He was, in many instances, not unlike the tailor who fails to make a knot at the end of the thread, or the carpenter who fails to put a blade in his plans. He danced, too, from one culture to another, from one vocation to another, from one country to another, to the mean and sardonic smile of amusement of his baitors. It is high time for him to engage himself in purposeful work. He can return to the ways of the Torah from every angle he may choose. Beneath the wings of the living creatures is the Hand - the might - of God - ready to receive him, if he will only repent. And, to repent collectively is the season of Convocation.
