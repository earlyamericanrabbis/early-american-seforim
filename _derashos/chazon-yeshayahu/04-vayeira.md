*“And Abraham reproved Abimelech because of the well of water, which Abimelech’s servants had violently taken away”* (Bereishis 21:25).

*“Said Rabbi Jesse Ben Hanania: ‘Reproof leads to love, even as it is said, Reprove a wise man, and he will love thee (prog.9.8) ‘He, moreover said: ‘all love unaccompanied by reproof, is not true love’. Resh Lakish said: ‘Reproof, invariably brings peace, even as the reproof of Abraham to Abimelech did bring peace’. He, moreover, was of the opinion that peace unaccompanied by reproof is not true peace”.* (Arachin 16a; Midrash Rabba, Bereishis 54:3).

Bitter experience during many centuries should have long ago convinced the Jewish people, as well as the student of the philosophy of Jewish life in Diaspora, that the greatest menace to cordial relationship and good neighborliness between Jew and non-Jew is either a lack of candor or a presence of flattery. It is very unwise to pick a quarrel with a neighbor who is capable of putting up a smiling front. He might, and usually does, strike down his neighbor, less powerful than himself, with a diabolical grin. It is worse, much worse, to flatter such neighbor. And, it is almost suicide to pose as a martyr, or indulge in praying to such neighbor. Nothing, excepting open candor, or a real rebuke, will deter him from striking in the dark.

The shaping of life upon earth belongs to the majority of every nation. So does the dominion of the respective nation belong to the majority. To the strong, the many, the voctors, were given power, government, material wealth, and rules of conduct. What they do is right. Religion, art, education, commerce, industry and the products of the soil are but arms of the governing majorities. Life’s machinery is theirs. The minority is tolerated, although outwardly, in so far as the exponents of civilized countries are concerned, vested rights are said to be enjoyed by the minority as well as by the majority. But life’s experience, even in our own times, shows a different picture. The ruling class, be it in Europe or elsewhere, is no more moral, or benevolent toward the minority than the policy of a strong government will permit.

The majority has ever been the donor and the minority, the recipient. This is as fixed a law as are the laws of nature. Fundamentals changes are not made often enough to notice the radical steps of civilization among the sub-merged carrying along the bounties which belonged from time immemorial to the governing classes. Job speaks of God as “*The peace-maker in His heaven*” (Job. 25.2), which is best evidence that there must be some inhabitants in Heaven, too, who feel the yoke of the more powerful and narrower against it.

But there are ways which point to a possibility of friendship. And that possibility is not flattery. Neither is it bribery of any kind. The first necessity to bring about a wholesome understanding between a majority ruling class and a minority ruled class is full understanding of any fundamental difference which might become the prime element in a dispute, or the main reason of a friction.

People gain knowledge by education, experience and reflection. None of these can be attained by either sham or manipulation. It must come by individual effort. In order to keep a whole city clean, every inhabitant must keep his own yard clean. So, in the final analysis, every member of the minority is a neighbor of a member of the majority. The relationship of the individual members of the two groups will count in the end. And that relationship can be turned into a practical application for the entire membership of both groups.

The good will of one’s neighbor will bring him the good will of the entire community. But such good will must be established upon a basis of reasonable understanding, which can be accomplished only by candor, by being just what one is, and by living up to a certain standard, which should not be offensive to the majority, nor inimical to its interests. Such conduct will not go against the grain of the painted surface. It will brighten the polish. It will lead to an understanding.

Flattery is a polished lie, without a hiding-place for the lie. Abraham the Hebrew became a Godly prince among his neighbors, because he spoke truly, without flattery. Abimelech was forced into a weak denial. Abraham overlooked it. His conduct was the right one. That was all it mattered with him. In due time Abimelech reflected and found himself wanting in the quality of truth, of love, of peace.

Both Jesse Ben Hanina and Reish Lakish pointed at the true path of neighborly understanding. “*No love without candor,*” said Ben Hanina; “No peace without candor,” said Reish Lakish. But Solomon said: *“only the wise will love the one who rebuke them.”*
