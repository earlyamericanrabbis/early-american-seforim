*“If ye walk in My statutes, and keep My commandments, and do them; then I will give your rains in their season, and the land shall yield her produce, and the trees of the field shall yield their fruit. And your threshing shall reach unto the vintage, and the vintage shall reach unto the sowing time and ye shall eat your bread untill ye have enough, and dwell in your land, safely.”* (Vayikra 26:3-6)

*“And the land shall yield her produce - not as it yields now, - but as it yielded in the days of Adam. Moreover, one may anticipate that in future generations the earth will produce fruit the self-same day it will be seeded, even as the Psalmist says: ‘He hath made a memorial for His wonderful works’; (Tehillim 111.4) which teaches us that when God created this planet, the power of daily production was native to the soil, which is the memorial herein spoken of. And it is again said: Let the earth put forth grass, herb yielding seed; (Bereishis 1:11) - that is to say: the earth will yield fruit daily, as it did in the days of Adam; and the tree itself will be for food, as is its fruit; and the barren trees, too, will yield fruit; and there will be no need to eat large quantities, to have enough, but a small quantity of food will be sufficient, even as it is said: ‘And ye shall serve the Lord your God, and He will bless thy bread, and thy water; and I will take sickness away from the midst of thee. (Shemos 23:25) Peradventure you will say: ‘Of what blessing is bread and water, if there be strife between man and man? Even that is spoken of saying: ‘And I will give peace in the land, and ye shall lie down, and no one shall make you afraid.’”* (Vayikra 26:7; Yalkut Shimony Vayikra 672.)

There is nothing new under the sun. Ever since man was created, he had full charge of all the forces of nature, and it is up to him to make nature’s dumb and blind forces his servants. Man’s mind must be applied to his surroundings; his vision must encircle the universe; his intellect must subdue everything which is native to this planet. Man’s powers cannot change nature; with all of his skill, he cannot create new matter; with all of knowledge, he cannot replace a natural wing for a fly, or a natural claw for a lobster. But man can discover the natural forces, and chain them to do his bidding.

All that which amazes us in the modern world was in existence ages ago; man discovered, man invented, man labored, man became master. He was intended to do just that. But there is nothing new in all that man produced; he merely perfected some of nature’s hidden forces; he merely utilized them for his own convenience.

Man was created to do just that. A font of type means nothing; but when set up in words, sentences, paragraphs, pages and chapters, when a book is produced, the work thus accomplished, is merely an application of man’s knowledge to utilize material at his command. But, fundamentally, nothing new in nature was created by either the author or the mechanic. Steam, electricity, wireless, aviation, radio, telegraph telephone, in fact every invention and discovery cannot be spoken of as “*something new under the sun*.” Man’s work merely opened up part of nature’s treasure, and dazzled the world with newness which is NOT NEW.

It is human to overlook the things we are surrounded by most of our lives; we pay no attention to them; they are common to our senses; but let some genius apply his thinking facilities to the most common thing, such as air, water, fire, earth and what possibilities there lie in and about these elements, and we all will be startled out of our wonted tranquility.

The mysteries of nature have been but partly revealed to the select few physicists in history. What the sacred minds were able to attain spiritually, what the prophets of you were able to perceive by means of their holy communion with God, was not alone of the glories of the spiritual world but even of the well-being of man upon earth, of the great good one may inherit his share while on this planet.

“*The heavens are the heavens of the Lord;*

*But the earth hath He given to the children of men.*” (Tehillim 115:16)

This is to teach mankind that life upon earth must not be a continuous process of preparation for death, must not be enveloped in a mist of pseudo-spirituality which darkens all knowledge and clouds all wisdom concerning this planet. Man is here for a purpose, and it is as sinful to refuse to live the natural life God intended us to live as it is to stoop to the things which are evil. In utilizing the forces of nature which man may reveal by his mental powers, man does his duty; and there can be no higher duty than that of making this earth a fit place to live in.

The scholar of divinity has no reason to believe that the scholars of science are his antagonists. When we encounter such antagonism, it is a safe surmise that either one, or the other, on both, are far from the last resource of their respective fields.

Religion teaches that God created everything; science combines forces and brings out useful instruments by which man’s cares and burdens are lightened. If the scientist asserts that all he finds in nature is self-constituted laws which had neither origin or control, then he errs in his judgement, and fails to penetrate the proper depth in order to behold the true light of Creation. If the divine asserts that all science is revolutionary, that all scientific theories and propositions are basically false, and that, because the language of science is not the language of the Bible, all of its doctrines are heresy; then he errs in his judgement, and fails to fathom the meaning of the Book of books.

Man’s duties are composite; he must perform them as a man. Natural laws alone, are material; spiritual laws alone are divine; man cannot do his duty by performing it in observing either one of the two sets of laws; his is a complete mission. In order to become a Unit, if such is possible, he must attain a height far above the average; he must subdue all of the natural forces within him, and unite them with his soul; then, and then alone can he be a Unit. But this is the extreme limit, and cannot be reached by vast masses of people. It is quite sufficient in itself if man does his duty, as a composite being, and feels the motion of both body and soul.

The man whose research brings out material happiness serves humanity no less than the man whose learning produces spiritual happiness; but the work of both emanate from an identical force - from the high intelligence they are able to muster as a result of giving their respective souls full sway over their bodily inclinations. Thus we are forced to the conclusion that both the scientist and the divine are servants in the House of God.

Religion has a duty to perform toward mankind, and science has a duty to perform toward mankind; the duty of one is heavenward; the duty of the other is earthward. Religion is obligated, by its own code and by its own constituted authority, to prevent man from falling into error, to restrain him from worshipping images and idols, to lead him nearer his Creator, and to show him the path which will give his soul self-expression. Science is obligated, by its clearness, foresight, accuracy, and practical application to bring out all of the hidden forces of nature, all of its marvels all of its beneficence as well as all of its dangers. A scientific dietician may well suggest certain foods for the sick, even for a sick divine, and religion can find no fault in his work. In fact he is the messenger of God while doing it. Prayer alone will not help, because the ailment is a physical one. The diseases of the soul, and it is certainly subject to its own peculiar forms of sickness, should be cured with prayers. Nature provides a remedy for the bodily ailment; religion for the psychological ailment. They are two entirely separate fields, and never can the two meet as antagonists, if both are carried on sincerely, honestly, and in the benefit of humanity.

Miracles did, and do happen before our very eyes! There is no need to work hard in order to rationalize miracles, or any other part of the Bible which points at a direct link between God and man, between the Physical and the metaphysical. It simply cannot be done. There is a gulf between the natural and the supernatural which remains till this day the impossible to bridge. Men have attained prophecy, men have conversed with God, men saw the wonder-working ways of the Almighty; but it all can neither be mathematically calculated nor physically demonstrated. Man’s soul feels it nay, knows it; but the composite man, the earthly man, cannot perceive it with his natural eyes. Nature is unconscious of its forces and of their value either for good or evil; man’s mind, discovering what such forces may mean to mankind, unearths the means wherewith to make them his slaves. And, yet, nature itself is the most miraculous of all miracles. At least so it should appear to the human mind. The color of the flowers, the sea-waves on the wheat-fields, the language of the fores-trees; the precious light discovered in jewels in the bowels of the earth, the wing of the fly, the web of the spider, the rainbow, the glaciers, the bejeweled heavens of the night, the sun-dawn and sunset, the calm music of the brink, the mechanism of man’s brain; birth, growth, and death - how miraculous it all appears to the eyes of the angels who are natives to heavenly surroundings as we are to earthly! No phenomenon in nature is fundamentally false; every fancy has a counter-part in the realms of nature. Man could not think of God, of angels, of miracles, if it was not a positive acquisition of his natural power to do so. And if it exists in man’s brain, even if he is able to think of it negatively, the being thereof is a positive fact.

Thus, with all that science is able to accomplish, it will never succeed in bringing up a Godless generation; it will never succeed to inoculate man’s mind against belief. Primitive men believed in the supernatural they believed in the idea that everything around them was supernatural. It, therefore becomes obvious that belief is a natural phenomenon. So is the organism; it is a negative force in the works of one’s brains; but it emanated from the existing fact in man’s make-up that belief is part of his being. It is the self-expression of his personality.

In forceful language as Moses had at his command, he brought God’s blessing to mankind - a most natural blessing, having to do entirely with man’s well-being on earth, with his economic condition, with his every-day living, with the very food that gives him the physical vitality to exercise his functions normally, and in good cheer. He promises, for obedience to God’s statutes and commandments, good and abundant crops, plenty of food, and safety. Thereupon the Talmudic and Midrashic authors elaborated by promising a daily yield of mother earth’s wealth. There will be no change in nature when that er will be reached; but there will be an abundance of knowledge; science will unravel all of the mysteries of nature; and - the fulfillment of these blessings, as interpreted by the Midrashic sages, will be a daily commonplace as is to-day the radio or aviation.
