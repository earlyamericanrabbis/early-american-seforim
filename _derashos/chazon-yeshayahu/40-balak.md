*“How goodly are thy tents, O Jacob etc. (Bamidbar 24:5) Said Rabbi Elazar (Johanan): From the blessings of this wicked man one may deduce what he covered behind his sweet speech. He wished that there should be neither synagogue nor schools in Israel; he employed the term “How goodly are thy tents, O Jacob”; he, moreover, wished that the Shekinah should not inhabit in their midst; he employed the term, thy dwellings, O Israel.”* (Sanhedrin 105b; Yalkut Shimony Bamidbar 23:770)

*“Behold the difference between the prophets of Israel and the Pagan prophets; the prophets of Israel continued to warn Israel against sin, whereas this prophet, (Balaam) who rose up among the Pagans, opened a floodgate (Shittim - daughters of Moab) which lost to all mankind the World-to-Come.”* (Midrash Rabba Bamidbar 20)

The bearer of evil tales, the poisoner with his tongue, kills three - himself, the listener, and the victim of the tale. When things are normal, it is possible for one to be of an evil repute only through his own misdeeds. In a society, where there exist no scruples, it is possible for the most reputable member to acquire an evil name by means of poisonous tongues, or the tongue of a single, lost sycophant. In modern times, it is called propaganda. In some States there is a cabinet pest for a professional propagandist. The greatest sufferers of evil propaganda in all history were and are the people of Israel.

Blood accusations, poisoning of wells, being lepers, the world’s scavengers, usurers, are only a few of the inventions of the ages against the Jews.

But the Jewish people were never, and are not now, afraid of one who attacks them openly. They have, to say the least, an opportunity to repel such attacks. Their own lives deny them. The bark of the dog does not at all frighten them. They have even acquired the fine art of defying pogroms. But what they do fear, and that fear has historic ground, is diplomatic praise. The Jews are able to sense danger, but being faithful, they ever believe these who pose as their friends, and when the latter utter words of praise, couched in diplomatic phrases, they are the last to detect the underlying meaning and motive. If the Jew is thrifty, it means that he is salting away all the wealth of the country he happens to live in; if he is wise, he is plotting to rule that country; if he understands business, he is conspiring to defeat competition and the tax collector alike; if he is an able member of the higher professions, he is grabbing the entire field; if he is religious, he is profaning every other religion!

Jew-baiting in the olden times was the business of the open grafter, the petty bully, the despised tyrant, or the bigoted cleric. In modern times, it has become a fine art, often wrapped in art itself, expressed in drama, on the stage, from the rostrum, in the book, on the canvas, and as a part of good government.

No one remains indifferent to the Jew. He is treated either as a villain or as the hero in the daily drama of life. The daily press of every metropolis of the world never emits an edition without spreading something new about him. He is in daily use as a pretense, or excuse, for some strange act of an unknown politician who aspired to climb to the top of his government, his profession, or his trade. Even among his own, he is used as copy by every ambitious upstart. In modern times it more than an isolated instance to see Jews being used as a pun in a game of chance between Jews and non-Jews. Jewish leadership has become a stepping-stone to high office in every democratic country. The synagogue, the Yeshivah, the Talmud Torah, the orphanage, the hospital, in fact every Jewish institution, or movement, has had its varied experiences with those who used it as a means to some selfish end. The only profession, outside of the Rabbinate, which has remained loyal to itself, and has not used the Jews as copy, is the medical profession. And, in the final analysis, when the historian will look for data of the underlying causes of modern Jew-baiting in the several democracies of the world, particularly America, Canada and Great Britain, generally, he will find the ugly tongue and nasty face of disgruntled politicians among the Jews, who did not succeed in their efforts, as the greatest contributor to that evil.

But the foundation for the actual danger, and real harm, is the overpraise of Jewry on the part of their flatterers, the successors of Balaam, who bless them, and praise them, and point their high qualities with a view of casting terror among those who fear ability.

Balaam was neither the friend of Israel nor of Amon and Moab. Balak’s demand to pronounce a curse upon Israel did not please Balaam. He knew that Israel fears not an open attack. He avoids the enemy. His natural meekness, his vulnerable position, his small number, his knowledge of appeasing an enemy, gives him the advantage over his powerful and much more numerous enemy. The enemy despises him, and fears him not. And thus, an open escape is possible. But when one blesses and praises Israel, proclaiming his spiritual might, his plans of conquest, his great future, the enemy fortifies all his boundaries, mobilizes all of his forces, and by sheer numbers and power, overwhelms him. This is the interpretation of the Talmud and Midrash of the secret meaning of the diplomacy of Balaam. History justified this interpretation.

But Balaam did more to put in effect an immediated blow. He advised open assimilation. The daughters of Moab were used as a means with that end in view. But the end was bitter, both for Israel and for Moab.

In most modern outbreaks of a similar character, diplomatic defamation was resorted to by liberal leaders, whereas in every tyrannical government, open outbreaks, glaring lies, were spread, which in the end, lost their force and the very objective sought was defeated.

The kiss of an enemy is much worse than his bite; there is an escape from his bite, but not from his kiss. Beware of an enemy who poses as a friend!
