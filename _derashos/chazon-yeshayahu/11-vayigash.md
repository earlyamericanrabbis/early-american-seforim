*“And he sent Judah before him unto Joseph” (Bereishis 46:28). “This is what the Verse speaks of, saying: ‘Dominion and Fear are with Him. He maketh peace in His high places’ (Iyov 26:2) “Dominion “refers to Michael, and “Fear” to Gabriel (the Midrash Rabba, Genesis 12, says: Dominion is Gabriel, and Fear is Michael); Michael is of the element of water, and Gabriel, of fire, and both stand before the Shekinah and do not injure one another; which implies that, ‘He maketh peace in His high places’. Said Rabbi Simeon:’ Behold, the whole of the expanse is of water and the Angels are of fire, and the ministering angels of burning flames, and yet, the water does not quench the fire, and the fire does not evaporate the water. Judah and Joseph, one a lion and the other an ox; but yesterday they were engaged in a deadly combat, and to-day Jacob sends Judah to Joseph, even as it is said: ‘And he sent Judah before him unto Joseph’; which in accord with that, it is said: ‘He maketh peace in His high Places”* (Midrash Tanhuma, Bereishis, Vayigash,6).

*“To show him the way before him unto Goshen” (Second part of Verse, Bereishis 46:28) Lahorot is here translated “to show him”, which has no meaning for, Jacob could have had the way to Goshen shown him after his arrival; Joseph would have done that anyway; what was the need of sending Judah ahead? The midrash Tanhuma, therefore, interprets the word Lahorot —to instruct, to establish the Yeshivah (of Shem and Eber).* (See Midrash Tanhuma, Vayigash,11.)

Judah’s errand to Joseph had a double purpose: (1) to establish the Yeshivah of the patriarchs in Goshen; (2) to make peace in the family.

\(1\) It is written in the Verse:

*“And all thy children shall be taught of the Lord;*

*And great shall be the peace of thy children” (Yeshayau 54:12)*

At the birth of the Jewish nation the greatest care was taken to establish it upon a solid foundation, for its builders intended to make it endure throughout the ages. Much thought was given to every principal which was recommended for its adoption. But every nation in history had an outstanding principle around which all others revolved. Greece had philosophy; Rome, art; Egypt, science; Persia, war; Tyre, commerce; Phoenicia, discovery; and so were all other countries distinguished for one outstanding feature in its make-up. Israel had to be founded upon a main principle which should be outstanding wherever he might migrate. Israel had to build a spiritual structure so that it should be easy to carry it along from place to place, and establish it, at will, the minute it had to be taken away from its rooted ground.

Abraham established that principle; he decreed that all of his descendants should be taught in the ways of the Lord. Judea became known for its religion; upon this foundation more than two billions of human beings builded faiths.

Religion to Israel, from his very first appearance upon the scene of human activities, was the first necessity of his life.

Abraham added glory to the Yeshivah founded by Shem and Eber, by establishing the principle that all of his children must “*be taught of the Lord*” and around this principle all others revolve. That kind of a foundation can be laid at will anywhere, at short notice, for it is a spiritual foundation.

Jacob and his sons knew that they were facing exile. They also knew that Egypt was “*not taught of the Lord*”, that it was neither conducive to promote the doctrine of Monotheism, nor the laws and customs of the religion practiced by the children of Israel.

Joseph was educated in the Yeshivah of which his father was the head. According to Midrashic authors, he did not forget his studies, for he remembered even the subject his father had instructed him in the day he left him; but he lived in, and was the ruler of Egypt...

Jacob understood it all; he wanted to go and see his most beloved man; but he did not want to undermine the foundation upon which the entire future of Israel was based by doing it. He, therefore, charged Judah to go as a messenger to Egypt and establish the Yeshivah, which had come to him as a heritage from his fathers, in Goshen. *Lehorot Lufanav Goshona* - to establish a place of learning before he went to Goshen. (2) After the work of establishing the Yeshivah in Goshen would be accomplished, Judah, thought Jacob, would have but little difficulty in establishing peace among the brothers.

God created every form of life, as well as every form of inanimate objects, with specific, natural, laws. Each body in bringing out God’s purpose of creating it, does not encroach upon the nature of another body, either to destroy it, or to make its power impotent. Heaven, earth, sea, star, planet and comet, each has its own specific duties to perform, and its own laws to observe. Orderliness makes nature function after its own God-given laws. There is an iron discipline in nature which man had not yet begun to emulate. When the eagle appears in the skies, little birds disappear. Rocks open themselves to permit a blade of grass to burst forth and give self-expression to its nature; the stars organize no war against the sun for causing them to fall in obscurity by day; water does not run upward to conquer fire; fire does not go downward to destroy water; the earth does not become mutinous because it was not placed among the stars in heaven; the ass never brays because the color of the radish was not given to its face; the lion never roars because he wants a barber-shop; the mouse never complains because it did not grow bigger; and the skunk is never sore because he received no invitation to join the chorus of Cherubim. From the highest forms of heavenly Intelligences to the lowest form of earthly life there is a manifestation of obedience to law and order, to harmony, to Peace.

“*He maketh peace in His high places.*”

But, not so with the human family. Here we encounter an intelligence which does not remember the laws of nature, or its orderliness. There is no peace on earth, and there is no desire for peace among mankind. There is a constant race of warriors; one continuous chain of wars; a stream of human blood flowing from the door of Eden, since the days of Cain and Abel, and reaching the very doors of hell.

Jacob feared strife among his children more than any other evil. To bring them all together in a strange land under the conditions of exile, and to have the fear lest they slay one another, was the most dreadful contemplation Jacob was confronted with.

But he knew the remedy—to teach them to know the Lord, to have them all taught of the Lord, when they would understand the respective functions of human beings upon earth, and when they would grasp the meaning of the immortal words of Isaiah:

“*And all thy children shall be taught of the Lord, And great shall be the peace of thy children.*”
