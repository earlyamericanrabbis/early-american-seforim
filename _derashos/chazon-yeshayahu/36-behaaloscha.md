*“And the people were as murmurers... (Bamidbar 11:1-3) Let the good (Moses) come, and receive the good (Torah) from the Good (God) for the good of (Israel).” In Shemos 2:2 Moses is called good; In Mishlei 4:2, the Torah is called good; in Tehillim 145:9, God is called good; in Ibid. 125:4, Israel is called good.”* (Menohot 53b)

*“Now the man Moses was very weak, above all the men that were upon the face of the earth. (Bamidbar 12:3) A man’s wisdom maketh his face to shine. (Koheles 8:1) ... Said Rabbi Yudan: The might of the prophets is great indeed, for the force of the heavenly might is likened to the face of Man even as it is said: ‘And I heard the voice of a man between the banks of Ulai’ (Daniel 8:16). Rabbi Yudah ben Syron said: This is inferred from this Verse: ‘And upon the likeness of the throne was a likeness as the appearance of a man upon it above.”* (Yehezkiel 1:26, Midrash Rabba, Bamidbar 19)

The soul of man is subject to as many ailments as is the body, if not more. The physical diseases are treated with physical remedies. The tendencies of men vary, often going to extremes. One who is suffering from the disease of arrogance becomes dangerously soul-sick if he begins to believe that honor and arrogance are synonymous. Likewise, one who considers himself no better than a dearest and believes that he is weak, is soul-sick in the extreme, and in danger of becoming a menace to society. A pathological liar, who poses as a writer of fiction, is a terrible nuisance and a bad neighbor. The sycophant who avers that he does all the harm for the sake of truth, is turning his psychological make-up into the naked skin of a serpent. The diplomat who hides his thoughts in order to enshrine a neighboring State, and considers himself a patriot and a wise man, has reached the extreme point of hypocrisy in the nethermost regions of hell. All such and like tendencies do not come from wisdom and have no connection with truth.

Maimonides (Moreh Nevuhim 1:2) wisely differentiates between truth and good, falsehood and evil. God intended man to know the truth and follow it, and to know falsehood and avoid it. Came man and made an unfair exchange. Good for truth and evil for falsehood.

In practical application of these tendencies to life, the reverse is often found. For example: the flatterer, he is to be avoided; but he seldom is. It is good to hear words of flattery. But is it true? No one to whom the words of flattery sound good and sweet, will stop to think a moment. He would discover the flattery to be utterly false; yet he considered it good. Conversely, one who was rebuked by a neighbor, a preacher, or an intimate friend, feels himself hurt; he considers a conduct of this sort on the part of his neighbor, his preacher, or his friend the very height, or depth of evil. He does not stop to think. Or he would discover that the very thing he considers evil is the absolute truth.

The serpent was first to suggest, *“And ye shall be as God; knowing good and evil.”* (Bereishis 3:5) But the Seal of God is Truth.

Moses was good; the Torah is good; God is good. Moses, being so meek, unselfish, entirely separated from the earthly things which misguide and destroy, was good. The Torah is good, for it opens all avenues of righteous conduct upon earth, and gives wisdom to all who desire to acquire it. God is good, he receives all who turn to Him with a meek and contrite spirit.

One who heals the sore feet of his slave is not good to his slave. Conversely, one who knocks a tooth out of his slave, in order to free him, is good to his slave.

There is but one route which leads to a complete understanding, to a full comprehension of these matters. It is the path of the meek. Let a man follow the path of meekness, and he will reach the goal of happiness. People have different conceptions about what happiness on this earth consists of. Some people believe wealth will bring it, others that security will attain it, still others that power, pleasure, fame, or good health will establish it. But none of these do bring happiness; even if all of them be combined. Ambition, jealousy, arrogance, defeat will undermine any one or all of these.

Meekness will accomplish it. That transcends everything. Wealth, fame, fortune, pleasure, or security are but relative terms which are acquired but do not exist within the soul of the individual affected or concerned. Meekness comes from the depths of one’s own soul. Let the other be rich, why be envious; let the others have fame, why not help him enjoy it; let the others have all the good God bestows upon him, for an I not happy and contented?

When one feels no arrogance, no envy, no hatred; when one is a true believer in God and in His justice, when one knows his own shortcomings, lives within his means, at peace with his neighbors, the enemy of no one, and the friend of all, he is ripe to practice meekness, for he is meek.

Such man’s face is worthy of being carved upon the Throne Above; the wisdom of such man, indeed, maketh his face to shine. Such a man was Moses. He was meek. Why not follow his example?
