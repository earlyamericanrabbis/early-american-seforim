*“And the Lord spoke unto Moses saying: Speak unto the children of Israel, that they take me an offering; of every man whose heart maketh him willing ye shall take My offering.” And this is the offering which ye shall take of them: gold, silver, and brass; and blue, and purple, and scarlet, and fine linens, and goats’ hair; and rams’ skins dyed red, and sealskins, and acacia-wood; oil for the light, spices for the anointing oil and for the sweet incense; onyx stones, and stones to be set for the ephod and for the breastplate”.* (Shemos 25:1-8)

*“The Mishnah teaches us: Five different persons are forbidden to heave tithes, and if they did heave, their heaving is not considered heaving; They are: a deaf and dumb person, an idiot, a minor, one who offers another man’s property, a pagan who heaves the property of an Israelite, even though the latter authorized him to do it. Said Rabbi Simeon ben Lakish (Rabbi Samuel bar Nehman) That all these are disqualified is deducted from this passage: Speak unto the children of Israel; this excludes the deaf and dumb to whom one cannot speak; “Whose heart maketh him willing”, this excludes the idiot whose heart is incapable of making him willing; ‘Of every man’, this excludes a minor; ‘That they take for Me’, this excludes one who gives not his own; ‘Speak unto the children of Israel that they take for Me’, this excludes the pagan, Said the Holy One, blessed be He! I have endowed you in Egypt with thirteen different articles, even as the prophet Ezekiel enumerates them: ‘richly woven cloths; sealskin shoes; fine linen-head gear; silk array of ornaments; hand bracelets; neck chain; nose-ring; ear rings; a crown, fine flour; oil and honey to eat”* (See Ezekiel, 16. 10. 19.). Terumos, 1.1; Talmud Yerushalmi, Terumos; Pesikta, Shemos 25; Midrash Tanhuma, Shemos 25; Yalkut Shimony, Shemos 363).

There is a vast difference between the one who makes an offering for the sake of God and the one who makes an offering for the sake of self-glory; although even the latter has a perfect right to expect a reward from heaven.

There is a vast difference between the one who gives his own, and one who gives not his own, although the latter means well, and has every reason to believe himself kind-hearted.

There is a vast difference between one who gives because his he art makes him give, and one who gives because an appeal makes him give, although the latter has every right to expect his offering to be as useful as that of the former.

There is a vast difference between one who gives because he lends a willing ear, and one who gives because he is “*ashamed of not giving*”, although the latter has every reason to believe himself as much a philanthropist as the former by counting results.

But the great trouble is when one gives as an idiot, that is, when bread for the poor is asked of him, he gives advice or direction on how to reach a country where bread is not necessarily the staff of life, or a swimming pool, wherein to drown their troubles or cool off their burning desire for food

Still greater is the trouble with one who pays no attention at all, who acts as if he were deaf and dumb, who remains indifferent. A man like he, might offer a brick instead of brick, a shine instead of shoes, a grave instead of a dwelling-place, salt instead of a drink of water.

But the greatest of all evils comes when a pagan has the authority of an Israelite to make the offerings for the sake of the Lord. A pagan understands things which are scientific, regulated on a dial, with a hand pointing at the percentage of each item; but he never can understand the *Shulchen Aruch* or *Yoreh Deah* - regulations regarding these matters of offerings for the sake of the Lord.

If these classes of people give, if they do heave tithes, their heaving is not heaving - it is insulting; they make cases out of misery; statistics out of famine, and copy for reports as dessert at annual charity banquets.

By making miseries of a homeless people known to the world, the recipients become objects of scorn; and the donors, in the end, dissatisfied individuals, who feel the stigma of belonging to the same race as the beggars.

Most modern charity, when everything is done in the open, creates beggars, puts a premium on pauperism, and helps only the lud-mouthed and impudent individuals. Many are the deserving who suffer. Between the giver and the recipient, too, are under the present system, not only the organization, but heads, hands, feet and teeth. The social workers are a very useful element in modern charity—distribution; but their attitude toward the poor is certainly not in harmony with the tenets of this Mishnah. Investigation, checking-up, inquisition and final refusal, or grant, as the case may be, is far from being in harmony with the system of Heave-offerings, or tithes-giving, or handing the poor what is really their due. Common sense more than so-called “system” is required in dealing with humanity’s problems.

Standardized charity, yardstick aid, meter-meals, or slot-machine clothes is not of Jewish origin. That system, too, will disappear, as the knowledge and understanding of our Torah will be more disseminated in the future. But the ailment is here.

It often happens that among the social workers an individual is found who combines all the five, which the Mishnah disqualifies: He certainly does not give his own; he is a minor; or undersized individual; he is deaf to the poor, and dumb in the presence of the “big boss”, he is an idiot, or desires to be one, when a discrepancy in his accounts is discovered, and, in his every act is traced the “intelligence” of a pagan.

The social service clerk of to-day is a super-man in the eyes of the community! A certain city has anti-Semitic tendencies; it treats Jewish physicians, and Jewish patients in its hospitals very badly, if not shamefully. The Jewry wants a hospital. Along comes the social service expert and decides — that that particular Jewry needs a Y.M.H.A. for health, recreation, athletics, amusement, social elevation and sports! He is the sub boss over everything and over everybody. He is the ape-par-excellence. Why should the Protestants have a Y.M.C.A. and the Jews should not have a Y.M.H.A.?

Lately everything was segregated under one roof. Swimming pool, bowling, athletics, wrestling-matches, prize-rings, dancing pavilions, restaurants, card-rooms, billiard-rooms, and, of course, *Lahavdil*, a synagogue!

The Rabbi is the general manager; he must give a Hechsher on everything; if he wants to hold his job, he must be deaf and dumb, a minor, an idiot, a pagan in order to offer everything that is not his.

And, of course, all of those come under the heading of charity. What else is there left now but to collect and distribute or gather and spend charity? The synagogue is a charity institution; so is the temple, the centre, the *Talmud Torah*, the *Yeshivah*, and *Vaad-ha-Kasharut*, whatever that means in modern Jewish life in America.

This, of course, makes the “Rabbi a schnorer-par-excellence”. If his salary is five thousand dollars annually, he must be responsible for a budget of fifty thousand dollars a year. In other words, the Rabbi receives in salary, about ten per cent of what his efforts net the synagogue or temple. Then, of course, are the many other duties mapped out for him, often by that super-individual, the social service worker, or principle, or superintendent, or whatever name or title he chooses to take when he receives the appointment.

Charity, in its modern application, has replaced every form of spirituality. It is the cure-all for every sentimental longing. It has replaced prayer; every religious act, every form of the soul’s self-expression is now to be found in the one pill - charity. The whole structure of religion was treated as if it was a disease, and the germ finally isolated. Its name - poverty; its cure - charity.

The most sacred prayers during the high holidays mean nothing at all. The appeal for charity is replacing them, and mean everything. In this great land of plenty and of prosperity, and of opportunity, the Jewish people of a sudden discovered that its greatest need is charity, and that by doing away with everything and replacing everything with charity, they will have served the God of their fathers with all their heart, and with all their soul. It is indeed, the strangest phenomenon in all Jewish history.

The up-keep of a synagogue was never considered charity; the Talmud Torah, the Yeshivah, the Rabbi, were never classified under that heading. They were necessities of life. But now in many instances Rabbis are engaged and kept because they have large families to support - for charity’s sake; Hebrew teachers are engaged who have nothing else to do, and, in fact could not earn a livelihood doing anything else; and of course, they know nothing of either Hebrew or teaching. That disease is present as an adjunct of the “charity - complex”.

In a few decades of accomplishment, American Israel can boast of at least one hundred and fifty millions of dollars collected and paid out to still the hunger of the good angel Charity. Initial payments were made on large and magnificent edifices of all kinds which give fine self-expression to charity feeling. Temples, synagogues, centers, Talmud Torahs, and “community” homes adorn every section in every large American-Jewish city. But the “neighborhood” problem brought on a situation which perpetuates the noble work of charity; mortgages and overhead, simply to keep the semi-abandoned buildings from dilapidation, fordo on the poorer brethren the Mitzvah to keep “Had above water”.

The “heaving” was done, in every section, mainly by either of the five disqualified individuals, separately, or by all of the five combined in one. Minors, deaf-and-dumb, idiots, generous-person-upon-another’s pocket, and, aided by pagans - mortgage-raiser, contractors, and publicity-seekers, erected half million and million dollar edifices in neighborhoods which can not sustain, without charity, even a self-respecting “bridge club”, and the result is: appeals, appeals, and appeals!

Of course, the only element that can be reached is the one which goes to temple or synagogue: and the only time to find them is during the High-Holiday season. Then is the real time to “touch” the heart of the “sinners” who, either must Promise to give, or be denounced by the appeal-maker as “bad citizens” — the slogan of the “bad Jews” being no longer effective.

In order to drag out a “pledge” from the deaf-and-dumb element, or from the idiotic clan, a person of a high sounding title is secured. Often a name which carries along the majesty of the law, or a threat of adversary publicity, is secured for the purpose. A pagan sits somewhere behind the Rabbi’s vestry room and writes down the names and amounts. And even on Kol Nidre night the synagogues and temples become actual marts where publicity is purchased by so much per line. The entire sacredness of the Day is gone; the atmosphere is filled not even with air of the Mitzvah of charity but with a stifling odor of cheapness which all but chokes every feeling for the divine, and glorifies the senses for earthly pride in being rich, in being able to give. That is crowning Maman in the house of God with a vengeance.

Did ever any of these types of appeal makers stop to consider the feeling of the poor, who are ever-present, of the sincere worshiper who does not need to be “sand-bagged”, who does not belong to the “Mishnah’s Five”, but who gives quietly unostentatiously, before the holiday, as was the custom of his father?

Let no man in Israel believe that a synagogue, or temple, or Talmud Torah, or Yeshivah, or a Rabbi, belong to the category of Charity. No more than the erection of bridges, dwellings, rail-roads or any other necessary institutions do. All of these come under the same category as food. Man’s earnings are just for such purposes.

“It is the nature of a man to be together with fellow-beings and to congregate in groups.” That is the back of the idea of civilized life upon earth. Without a house of worship, without a Talmud Torah, a Yeshivah, or without a spiritual leader, the rich would not be better off than the poor. What we must have for ourselves is not charity; it is our duty toward ourselves, toward our posterity.

Charity comes under a different category entirely. Bread and home for the needy; aid for the sick; care for the orphan; protection for the widow, shelter for the wanderer; support for the aged; of such is the material from which the garment of charity is made.

That is a Mitzvah; one of the 613 Mitzvot in our Torah, which makes it mandatory upon every Israelite *“to give a minimum of half shekel each year as ransom for charity”*. (Shemos 30:13) But this commandment must be understood, and reason applied in carrying out its spiritual meaning.

Tithes, heave-offerings, the shares of the poor in the vineyard, the field-corner, the growth upon the fields during the Sabbatical years, a forgotten-sheaf, gleanings, and like portions of the priests, the Levites, the poor, the stranger, the widow and the orphan do not at all come under the category of “charity”. That was the system of taxation in vogue among Israel as a nation. The obligations of all these commandments did not completely cease with the destruction of Jewish national life in Palestine.

Charity, as such, does not even exist in our Torah’s admonition, as its mandates are not to be taken as a self-assumed task for the sake of preventing disease; even the half shekel is the mandatory minimum. That even the poor who has but the price of one meal must give.

Free-will offerings, taking for the Lord of what a man’s heart makes him willing to part with, that is the highest form of duty. Then it was a question of building the Tabernacle. Thus Rabbi Simeon ben Lakish, or, as the Jerusalem says, Rabbi bar Nahman, interpreted the opening passage of this week’s Sidrah to exclude the persons as well as the means of raising funds for God’s sake in an offensive manner, saying: *“Speak unto the children of Israel, this excludes the ‘ deaf and dumb’ to whom one can not speak; ‘ Whose heart asketh him willing; this excludes the ‘idiot’, whose heart is capable of making him willing; ‘of every man’ this excludes a minor; ‘ That they take for me’ this excludes the one who offers that which is not his own; Speak unto the children of Israel that they take for Me. “*

Moreover the sages indicated, from Ezekiel’s description of what they got in Egypt that the thirteen articles asked of them are but in return for the thirteen articles given to them by Almighty. Not to glorify the rich not to serve, or crown Mamman, not to desecrate the worship in the synagogues and temples, not to promote the interests of the publicity-seekers, self-promoting career-men, was the offering of charity instituted. It was but a part of the method to instruct men to do his duty.
