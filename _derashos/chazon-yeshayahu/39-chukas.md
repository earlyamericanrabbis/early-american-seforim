*“All those who attended to the Red Heifer from beginning to end, contaminate, whereas the red heifer itself purifies. Why this paradox? Indeed, thus saith the Holy One, blessed be He! ‘I have thus decreed, and such is My edict, and thou mayes not penetrate with thy thoughts aught of My edicts’.”* (Midrash Rabba, Bamidbar 19)

*“Mount Sinai was created for the descent of the Shekinah upon it. Mount Nebah was created as a sepulchre for Moses; and Mount Hor was created as a sepulchre for Aaron.”* (Yalkut Shimony Bamidbar 20:764)

Religion cannot be rationalized. Whenever an attempt is made to interpret religion as one does science, or any branch of science, the attempt fails to impress the people who are religious because it is not science nor art nor any other earthly fact. To compromise religion is an attempt to give a physical phase to all that is not material. Man, being composite, can not conceive aught which is entirely spiritual, unless the material parts within him have been entirely conquered by the spiritual within him. Such men are but few in any generation. The Kabalists call it “*Hyspashot ha-Gashmiyot*” - Divesting the Material.

Maimonides enumerates the several pre-requisites of prophets before the “*Hand of God is laid upon them*”, a kind of preparatory and gradual divestation of the earthly grossness before they can receive the spiritual ordainment. Only in a world of the simple life, was it possible for one to divest himself from the ordinary grossness of his clay, and subjugate his body completely to the will of his soul. The mountains, the rivers, the trees, the birds, the fields and the forests by day, the clear heavens and spangling stars by night governed the mind and body in the prophet’s era, and because of it, only a limited age in all history is ascribed to the prophets, and only a limited few even then reached the height necessary to attain.

Miracles, too, are limited to a narrow space of time, according to the Bible. When a child is born, his helplessness is the sole appeal for the wonders his mother performs for his safety, his comfort and his development. That constant care of the mother, too, is limited to the childhood period. The normal child develops, grows, and becomes a man, or a woman. The mother loves him or her; he or she, in return, loves the mother. But the age of the early care of the mother is gone. So is the age of miracles gone. There is no further need of *Manna*, of the quail, of “*letting the sun stand still*”, and of all the rest of the miracles performed during the childhood of Israel.

The Torah itself is not a text book for any particular branch of human knowledge; although it embraces every conceivable branch thereof; it cannot be treated as a secular work, although its language is the language of the sons of man; to attribute to it errors and contradictions and attempt to correct them by pointing out human hands, at different periods, who wrote it, is equivalent to disrobing the world of every form of Higher Intelligence, and of stripping life itself from any Higher Purpose.

To rationalize miracles and claim that were but natural phenomena, to claim that the prophets were the preachers of the day, without having had direct communication with God, to deny the soul that it is a soul but a brain cell, to aver that death is the end, to both matter and spirit, to claim that the span of life on earth is the sum total of life, to separate man from animal only on the ground of chance development, to trace descent of man to a common evolutionary shell, is not rationalization of religion, but an attack of science itself, which can reach out, or dig down, that far or that deep, and stop for want of further knowledge.

Science is the best evidence of the Truth of the Torah. Sight, touch, taste, smell, sound — all of the five senses make instantaneous impressions upon the human make-up. Sound was utilized as an instrument of human fascination in music. Taste is an ever-present delight to the human make-up. Smell was developed, and the human being is very sensitive to its application. Touch, too, is a physical delight in human experience. Sight tops them all. But one branch of it is hardly perceptible, and seldom applied as a human agency for either good or evil, and that is, color.

Of course, all of these are natural phenomena, natural laws, although in some branches of the senses, habit, environment, training, or natural gift will play an important role as regards the sensitiveness or indifference to most of the senses, particularly taste, color, and touch.

Most men are said to be color blind. Smell is becoming less of a human and more of an animal attribute.

Things which are natural no one ever attempts, or succeeds, in giving a logical reason for their presence in man’s make-up. Natural laws are not questioned. They are taken for granted. Perhaps spiritual beings are attempting to rationalize natural laws as we attempt to rationalize spiritual laws. Maybe the angels, too, have their problems of belief and doubt. But they, at least, have nothing of the material about them. But we feel that we do have something of the spiritual in us, that we are composite beings. We should know better. The Kabalists do proclaim the human being as far superior to the angels, the latter being stationary, the former progressive, or as it is expressed in Hebrew, the angel is an “*Omed*”, the man is a “*Holek*”. And, because Job speaks of God as the Peace-Maker of His Heavens, it is evident that the angels, too, have their differences.

Nature, as well as religion, is an expressed Will of God. The hidden mysteries of both are concealed from our gross senses. Neither are we permitted to penetrate the purposes of all that we perceive above, beneath, or around us. The greatest of all natural studies is still the make-up as man himself in his physical being.

Now we read in our Torah concerning a red heifer. According to the Mishnah, and the Code of Maimonides, in all there will be ten such sacrifices of red heifers throughout the generations. Nine of them have been already sacrificed, and the tenth one we still anticipate to take place in the Temple which is to be re-built when the Messiah will come.

The paradoxical feature of the red heifer sacrifice is that its ashes purify, whereas the people who carry the ashes, contaminate. Indeed, a strange law surrounded by mystic regulations and symbols, particularly in the limited number of ten such sacrifices and never more. Nevertheless, this is as much a par of the Torah as the commandment “*Thou shalt not kill*”.

To ask the why and the wherefore is equivalent to asking why was *Hor* a grave for Aaron, *Nebah* a grave for Moses, and Sinai a place for Revelation. It was the will of God! And, such, religion must continue to be not as a blind faith, not as an opiate, not based upon Fear, but as a path carved by the Will of God.
