*“When thou goest forth to war against thine enemies”, (Devarim 21:10) - the war herein spoken of is a discretionary war; ‘And the Lord thy God will deliver them unto thy hands ‘ (Ibid) - that is to say, if thou wilt follow all laws concerning this subject as it is written in the Torah (Ibidum 20:1-20) - in the end, the Lord thy God will deliver them into thy hands.”* (Sifre, Devarim 21)

*“Rabbi Hunya opened his discourse, saying: ‘A false balance is an abomination to the Lord, but a perfect weight is His delight’ (Prov. 11-1) if you see a generation which uses false balances, know that the inspector of weights and measures is after them and in the end he will confiscate all of their possessions”* (Pesikta, Devarim 21; Yalkut Shimony Devarim 938 q.v.).

The reward of longevity is promised to those who have one measure and one weight for buying and for selling alike (Devarim 25:13-16), - equal in importance as the commandment of honoring parents, or as the commandment of not to take the mother and its young (Ibid. 22:6-7). Perfect balances are, indeed, important, in fact, too important to be applicable only to merchandise. Weights and measures, in themselves, are the thermometer of the honesty and fairness of the merchant. The prophets, in denouncing the evils of their day, did not elaborate upon the evil of false weights and measures to exclusion of other evils. Ezekiel, in his masterful presentation of the doctrine of Individual responsibility, made no mention of false weights and measures among the other shocking evils of the wicked father, whose sins do not fall upon his righteous son. (Yechezkiel 18 q.v.) Hosea is the only one who mentions this evil of balances of deceit (Hosea 12:8). And yet, the Torah itself makes promise of longevity for honest balances.

*“A man’s yea must be just and a man’s nay must be just”* (Baba Metzia 49a) This maxim is based upon a word-play - Hyne; in Hebrew it means a liquid measure, in Aramaic, yes. Thus *Hyne* must be just within both meanings of the word. Consequently, measure, scale, balance are figures of speech applicable to justice, righteousness and power, as frequently and with as much emphasis as it is applied to merchandise and business. The balance of power, a measure of justice or the scale in the hands of a justice are figures of speech well understood by everybody.

With the general rule that “*the Torah speaks the language of man*”, the admonition and the reward promised is easily understood. When the balance of power among the nations is out of proportion, when the scale of social justice is uneven, when the weight of evil crushes the powerless minorities of the world’s submerged, it is indicative that the proportional measure of civilization is adulterated. Indeed, the main problem lies in the measure of proportion.

In San Francisco, before America entered the war, an enterprising butcher established a Kosher Style sausage factory. The word Kosher was labled in large letters, but the word style appeared in small letters. A visiting Rabbi who had an acquaintance with the butcher’s father-in-law, asked the butcher: “What proportion of Kosher meat do you use in your product?” “Oh,” said he, “an even fifty-fifty.” “Fifty-fifty of what?” asked the Rabbi. “Well”, replied the butcher, “rabbit meat goes well for taste in sausage. It also neutralizes the taste of horse-meat, so I use exactly fifty-fifty - one horse to one rabbit.”

World diplomacy is about as proportionate, when armaments are spoken of in the chambers of peace-conferences. Humanity at large, is as proportionate where the smaller and less advanced races in the arts of war are under consideration around a horse-shoe table in Geneva. The division of wealth is as proportionate, when the hungry masses are being denounced as rabble, communists and loafing agitators. Jewish proverbial richness is as proportionate, when the Anti-Semites ignite the flames of hate, prejudice, and spite. Religion is as proportionate, when the warships, cannons, machine-guns and air-bombs pour fires of destruction and death upon the cities in India, Africa, or China. Judaism is as proportionate when the political religionist runs for office, or when the professional Zionist cries that Zionism is Judaism, or when the hired assimilationist cries that Zionism undermines Judaism. Torah is as proportionate when the ultra-modern pulpiteer reviews a book on the “Downfall of Burlesque” on the Day of Atonement. Charity is as proportionate, when the head of a “racket” distributes Matzoth to the Jewish poor workmen after robbing them of the opportunity to work. Religious freedom in Russia is as proportionate when the taxes on space of houses of worship is being levied, or when religious education is being considered.

The world at large is out of balance. The proportion of things which would balance the scale is so infinitesimal that it is hardly perceptible. Less than 6 per cent of the population on the majority of countries are comfortably situated with fully 94 per cent living a day-to-day life without the least security for themselves and with no prospect at all for their posterity.

The world is out of balance and therefore, out of order, because the nations which have the balance of power wage war, not against their enemies. War is incited; hate is inflamed; prejudice is implanted. Ambition is boundless; lust for power and domination is endless, and love of money is universal. Nazi-ism, Fascism, Communism, Autocracy, dictatorship are the products of war, hate, prejudice, ambition, lust for power and money.

“*When thou goest forth to war against thine enemy.*” Every individual is spoken to. Moreover, the sages declared, this refers to a discretionary war. Thus, every individual may ask himself: Is the German, the Frenchman, the Indian, the Chinaman, the African, the Pole, the Russian or the Briton my enemy? And, if he is, why? The individual will thus be forced to analyze the causes of animosity, if there be any, and by doing so, and finding the causes he may contemplate further and ask his prospective victim or executioner, as the chances of war may bring about. Is it really impossible for both of us to live in peace? The Torah (Devarim 20.) devotes a whole chapter for just this kind of contemplation and reflection. No spell of anger, no wrath, even it be brought about provoking reasons, last long enough without giving the provoked and angry ones a chance to cool off. That was the plan formulated in the Torah. Propagandists, provocators, high tariffs, lack of raw materials, or lack of markets, or lack of territory are the main rings of the iron chain out of which wars are forged in modern times. That again brings back the circle of hate, prejudice, ambition, lust for power and love of money.

Who is thine enemy? Is he without or within?

“*When thou goest forth to war against thine enemy.*” Disease is an enemy. So is poverty, insecurity, idleness, segregation of all resources of a country in the hands of a few, political power in the hands of the misfit, power of production, distribution and transportation in the hands of the unscrupulous, too, are enemies. When each nation will wage war against the enemies from within and establish the balance of justice and righteousness, in its own boundaries, there will be no war between nation and nation, and the sword will not be unsheathed unless it be in the cause of justice.
