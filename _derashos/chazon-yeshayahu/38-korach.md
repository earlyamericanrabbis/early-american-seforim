“*Ye took too much upon you (Bamidbar 16:3) ...said Korah! Behold Moses is the king, Aaron is the high priest, his sons are vice high-priests, heave-offerings belong to the priests, tithes belong to the priests; twenty four different perquisites are enumerated in the Torah of Moses, as the property of the priests. Thereupon, “They assembled themselves together against Moses and against Aaron’.”* (Midrash Tanhuma: Bamidbar 16)

*“Said Rabbi Johanon: Wherefrom in the Torah do we infer that the dead will be quickened? It is from what Holy Writ says: ‘And therefore ye shall give the gift which is set apart unto the Lord, to Aaron the priest’.” (Bamidbar 18:28) Does Aaron live forever that he is given the Gift of the Lord? This is to teach us that he will return to life and Israel will give him the gift which is set apart for the Lord.”* (Sanhedrin 90b)

Human susceptibility to reason after the heart’s inclination, was the opening wedge for the demagogues’ tongue. Now, jealousy is a human tendency which is not always dangerous. The jealousy of scholars increases wisdom, of inventors, comfort of living, of thrift, security, but the jealousy of public office, of power, of territory, of property, has been, and is a source of evil. Ordinarily, the hidden jealousy of the evil kind remains within the bosom of the victim; but the minute some one strikes a note of discord publicly, he is hailed as a leader with the possibilities of creating a movement to upset the established order of things, constituted authority, even civilization itself.

The demagogue is acting a rule which is responsive to his vanity, his egotistical nature, with the same precision and force as the growth of the thorn in the flower-bush. It is his self-expression. His greatest triumph is not in building up himself or his followers, but in destroying the one or the ones, he set out to destroy. No demagogue ever reached the supreme office of a state; he is mainly a tool, a weapon, by some one who appears in the picture when every obstacle was removed. No demagogue ever uses his head. Or appeals to the reason of his followers. His passion being what it is, he ever appeals to the passion of the masses, the mob, the gang, the plotters, the submerged, all of whom are swayed by passions of class-hatred, disappointment, actual or imaginary wrongs.

Korah was not plotting to take away the high-priesthood from Aaron, or the rulership from Moses for himself, for he denounces the very system. He wanted to destroy all loyalty to Moses and Aaron, to do away with the offices they occupied. He was their kinsman, and had an easy time with other princes to get a following. He was against a bad system, he said. The idea of others working for an idle class, of the people giving of their hard-earned substance, to the priests, he said, was not at all in harmony with God’s purposes and charges. Offerings, tithes to priests in the service of Israel, he said, was a pure invention of Moses for the benefit of his relatives. Nepotism, it was, according to Korah; and, he moreover said, to perpetuate the system, Moses proclaimed it as Levitical law.

The Gaon Chain of Walezin, founder of that school, directed some Rabbi of among the student body to visit a few cities and appeal for aid of that famous Yeshivah. The Rabbi returned from his trip very much depressed, relating to the great master that in every instance he was abused by the representative citizens of the community for hiring transportation and also for paying for his own board and lodging out of funds collected for the Yeshivah. “Go back”, said the Gaon to the Rabbi, “and tell the people as follows: Under our sacred Levitical laws, it is permissive to accept a Karban from a Goy but that the Karban must be entirely for God — a burnt-offering. No one may enjoy ought therefrom. But if an Israelite brings a Karban, there are parts of the Karban of which the priest, his wife, and his children partake and enjoy, and there is of course, the parts which are burnt for the sake of God. Now, go back and ask them: Are you Goyim?”

That, in a much greater measure, was the problem of Moses. Korah wanted to know why the priests were given parts of the Karban. He found followers, and a rebellion was organized. Orderly leadership, constituted authority, firm government was not what the demagogue desired. He can never, under law and order, find self-expression. That was the underlying reason for the uprising. Korah conveniently forgot that the entire tribe of Levi did not participate in the land allotment, and they had no property whatever, save what the people gave to them for their sustenance. According to the Midrash, he brought a widow with only one clean animal to her name, and illustrated to his followers how that widow was stripped by the priestly system of the last particle of substance she had. That inflamed the plotters, and they were ready to destroy stem and root of the Tree of Life — of the Torah.

But that which belonged to Aaron under the laws of the Torah, is his forever. Even the share intended for God is Aaron’s. Israel is Israel. “*And thereof ye shall give the gift which is set apart unto the Lord, to Aaron the priest.*” When Israel will return to national life, and the dead within his folds will be quickened, Aaron will receive his full measure. The scholars, in Israel, how abandoned they are now! There are ten Korahs begrudging the Rabbi his meagre salary. “Weddings are his, funerals are his, and many more perquisites he receives — is it not too much that they take upon themselves?” So are the denunciations in many quarters, from countless Korahs. The only institution in modern Jewry which is left unprotected, unsafe, insecure is the Synagogue. No one ever dreams to endow it, to make any bequest for it, or to make its continuance secure. Mush loss secure is the Rabbi, particularly in his old age. And all because of the arguments of Korah and his followers are still ringing in the ears of the thoughtless. Leadership under constituted authority leaves no room for the demagogue, and because of it the destructive elements, under the shadow of oblivion, fear it, detest it, fight it.

The path of the original demagogue, Korah, was not a golden one. The course of nature was changed in order to obliterate it. Will the modern Korahs take heed?
