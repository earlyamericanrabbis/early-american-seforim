WAR AND PEACE

The account given in the Bible of the reunion of Jacob and Esau affords a deeper insight into the personality of the third of the patriarchs than any of the biographical data presented theretofore. That the younger of the two sons of Isaac and Rebecca abhorred bloodshed and would do anything to avoid strife is evident from the fact that he preferred expatriation to coming to blows with his impulsive brother Esau. However it was first when, upon his return from his self-imposed exile, he was compelled to meet the brother, who had once vowed to slay him, that we learn to what lengths he was ready to go for the sake of peace. In order to assuage Esau’s anger and assure himself of the latter’s good-will Jacob not only sent his brother presents. He even humbled himself before him to the point of calling the would-be fratricide “my master” and referring to himself as “thy slave.”[^1] Scripture makes no secret of the fact that these gestures and efforts at appeasement were inspired by fear. “And Jacob was very much afraid and distressed.”[^2] As the rabbinic commentators observe, it was not only about his own fate in the event of a warlike encounter with Esau that the third ancestor of Israel was concerned. He was equally worried about the possibility that, in order to defend himself so as to save his own life, he might take that of his brother. This was the prospect that frightened him, and it was on this account that he left no stone unturned to prevent the outbreak of violence.

Jacob’s behavior in anticipation of his meeting with his brother Esau, which contrasted so sharply with the treatment accorded by his sons Simon and Levi to the Shechemites in revenge for the outrage committed against their sister Dinah, was fully in keeping with the vision of Israel’s prophets and the preachment of its sages. The former, it is known, looked forward to the time when “nation would no longer lift up sword against nation nor practice anymore for war,” but “men would turn their swords into ploughshares and their spears into pruning hooks.”[^3] The latter stated that peace was so desirable that in order to preserve it it was permissible to tell an untruth and to allow the name of God to be erased. Aye even idolatry could be forgiven a people at peace with itself. For peace, which is the note with which the threefold benediction pronounced by Israel’s priests concluded, is the greatest of all blessings, greater than health and wealth and wisdom, since without peace none of these gifts possess any value.[^4] ,

There is no disputing the fact that when Jacob condemned most severely the massacre by his children of the inhabitants of Shechem in retaliation for the rape of Dinah, he acted as the mouthpiece of the Bible, nor that war as such was never glorified by Judaism as an honorable profession. Making a livelihood by killing, “living by the sword,”[^5] might have been an occupation befitting the temperament of an Esau. It could never be in keeping with the character of Jacob. Despite all this Israel’s sages had nought but words of censure for Jacob’s demeanor prior to and during his reunion with Esau. In fact they asserted that, if the descendants of Esau enjoyed sovereignty long before the offspring of Jacob had attained such a status, if Scripture was able to record: “Now these were the kings that ruled in the land of Edom before a king reigned over the children of Israel,”[^6] it was due to the cowardly self-abasement of Israel’s ancestor before that bully and incarnation of evil, Esau.[^7] It would have been enough for Jacob to convince Esau of his good intentions, There was no need for him to humble himself before the unscrupulous rake, who was devoid of all moral principle, by conferring upon him the title of “master” while attaching to himself the label of his flunkey, his slave. By doing what he did Jacob put a premium on vice. He gave prestige to wickedness, which was more regrettable than the worst misfortune that could befall virtue, worse even than the catastrophe of war.

The nature of the problem that confronted Jacob, at the time when he was about to face Esau, is one that is perhaps better appreciated in our age than in any preceding epoch in the annals of the human race. For, if centuries ago already, when it was believed that the state of nature was a state of war, the horrors of war were recognized and the hardest terms of peace were considered preferable to the most righteous war, what shall we say who live in the present era of the atom and hydrogen bombs with their capacity for instant destruction on a scale never conceived of before? It is not at all surprising, therefore, that the mere suggestion of the possibility of the outbreak of a third world war, as a result of the current Middle East crisis, should have driven fear into the hearts of the representatives of the United Nations assembled in New York, that they should be trying desperately to put out the conflagration before it has a chance to spread, that they should be groping frantically for a formula that would put an end to aggression and make it possible for a permanent and lasting peace to be established, so that the temptation to make use of the most devastating instruments of death would be forever removed. The situation is one that calls for cool calculation and caution. No human being with a conscience, no government with any regard for moral law would want to take upon itself the responsibility for having precipitated upon humanity the greatest disaster of all time. That is why there are heads of state who would resort to any expedient, exert every kind of pressure and make any concession necessary to avert war.

Unfortunately not all individuals or nations entertain such scruples. There are, alas, among the rulers and ruling cliques persons and groups that have no inhibitions whatever, that are completely reckless, ready to risk everything in order to feed their insatiable ambitions. These desperados — their names are too well known to have to be listed — who feel they have everything to gain thereby, exploit the fears and worries of the decent part of the world for the purpose of furthering their own advantage. They do it so that they might become stronger and their capacity for enslavement and destruction might become greater than ever before.

There is only one way to deal with them. That way is firmness, courage. No more cringing! No more appeasement! Call their bluff! Hurl their threats into their teeth! The bullies must be taught a lesson or they will continue bullying until it is too late to stop them. It took the descendants of Jacob eight generations to set up an autonomous kingdom. Why? Merely because their ancestor had voluntarily made himself subservient to his hostile brother. The same fate would befall what is left of the free society of the world if its enemies be permitted to get away with their diabolical game. It does not take too much perspicacity to find out who is really desirous of peace and who expects to profit from war. Actions speak louder than words. All the brazen lies of the Khrushchevs and the Bulganins will not wipe out the memory of the bloodbath of Hungary, and every Red volunteer sent to Egypt to aid Nasser will serve only to underscore the real intentions in regard to the Middle East of the Soviet gangsters, that stand already sufficiently revealed by the arsenals of Russian arms accumulated in the Sina: Peninsula that fell into the hands of Israel’s plucky little army.

It is high time that civilized humanity opened its eyes to the truth and took the steps necessary to cope with the situation. “Too long have we been indulgent with Meshekh (Moscow), and coddled the dwellers in the tents of Kedar (the sons of the East). Too long have we put up with them that hate peace. I (have held out the palm of) peace (for nine years, says Israel). But when I make mention of it, they are for war.”[^8]

There will be no peace in the Middle East until the nations of the West will stand together firmly and insist upon it and permit no outsider to interfere. It is the only hope, the sole means of forestalling the otherwise inevitable. Will the government of the United States of America, in whose hands may be the fate of humanity, recognize this fact, or will it throw Israel to the Arab wolves in the mistaken belief that that will keep them satisfied, even as in Munich 20 years ago Czechoslovakia was sacrificed to the ambitions of the Nazi fiends? Only God knows. In the meantime we Jews of America, Israel’s closest relatives, will not let the little nation, the symbol of international justice, of democracy, of human liberty, of civilization and progress in the Near East, down. We shall rally to its assistance in its hour of trial and crisis. If we will do our share, we have reason to feel assured that God, too, will fulfill His promise and that out of the present difficulties will come salvation and a new lease on life for Israel reborn and for all mankind.
