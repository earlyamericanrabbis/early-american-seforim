THE BATTLE ETERNAL

It has long been Jewish custom on the Sabbath immediately preceding the festival of Purim to read in the synagogue as the concluding portion from the Pentateuch the three verses at the end of the section of Ki Teze containing the order for the war against the Amalekites, beginning with the words. “Remember what Amalek did to thee.”[^1] The people for whose annihilation this decree was issued was, according to what information we can gather from the Bible, a clan of Nomads that inhabited the desert south of the Land of Israel.[^2] From the genealogical table of Genesis 36.12 it appears that the Amalekites were descendants of Esau, the none-too-gentle brother of our forefather Jacob, for their ancestor Amalek is mentioned as a grandson of this same Esau. They were the first enemy to be encountered by the Israelites on their way from Egypt[^3] to Canaan, and they continued to be a source of irritation to the tribes of Israel throughout the period of the Judges,[^4] until their extermination. It was supposed to have been carried out by Saul[^5] but was first completed by David[^6] and members of the tribe of Simon.[^7] From then on the Amalekites vanish completely from the arena of history.

Now the vehemence of the sentiment against the Amalekites at the time, when their predatory forays posed a real threat to the lives and property of the settled population of the Land of Israel, is understandable. The only way, as modern Israel has found out to its sorrow, to put an end to the murderous activities of bands of hired assassins, like the Egyptian-organized Fedayeen, is to strike at the root by destroying the bases of operation. What is difficult to explain is why this animosity should have been allowed to linger long after those who aroused it had ceased to exist. And granted that Haman, the villain of the book of Esther, had, as his family name, the Agagite,[^8] indicates, flowing in his veins the blood of Agag, the last king of the Amalekites,[^9] he, too, has long since been dead. Why therefore, go on year after year rehashing what this last representative of the first race of Jew-haters had wanted to do to Israel? Wherefore has it been said that God’s war against Amalek would be fought in every generation?[^10] Furthermore, if the almighty Ruler of the universe had promised that He would Himself blot out the memory of Amalek, why was it necessary for Israel, too, to engage in this task? And finally, once the mission was accomplished and a clean sweep had been made, what need was there for a repeat performance? One is reminded, as one reads the injunction for the wiping out of the Amalekites, of a certain offensive character, whom the leaders of a community wished to punish by ejecting him from the local country club. There was only one difficulty that the proposal presented — the culprit was not a member of the organization from which he was to be excluded. To solve the problem it was suggested that he be taken in and then thrown out. Both Haman and Amalek have long already passed into the limbo of the forgotten past. Why bring them back to life only to return them again to the oblivion they deserve’ Let them rest where they are. Is there any point in waking sleeping dogs? Indeed there isn’t. The only trouble is that the dogs won’t stay asleep. For while Amalek and Haman are dead, their reincarnations are not. In every generation there arise new Amaleks and Hamans to fill the vacancies left by their predecessors and they do their work as effectively as those who came before them.

The reason why this happens is a very simple one. Unscrupulous individuals — and there are such persons in every age — always need a scapegoat upon whom to visit their crimes and hold responsible for their mistakes; and the most convenient victims are the weak and the helpless, minorities like the Jewish people, that are not numerically strong enough to matter politically — that is from the standpoint of votes needed to win an election — nor sufficiently important economically to be worth protecting. Amalek, we are told, attacked the “stragglers, those who were weary and tired and lacking in the strength of their convictions.”[^11] The argument used by Haman in his attempt to exterminate the Jews of Persia was “And it doesn’t pay the king to spare them.”[^12] This same type of reasoning is today in the back of the minds of all those, who are ready, for the sake of Arab oil, to sell Israel down the river. The lives of close to 2,000,000 citizens of the little Jewish state, together with their 5,000,000 coreligionists in America and several millions more in the rest of the world, are expendable. When one asks our non-committal senators whether this is not so, they maintain a discreet silence, but this silence is as revealing as the most vociferous and resounding “yea”.

Antisemitism has invariably been the device whereby conscienceless adventurers had contrived to further their personal ambitions, build up their power and increase their riches. Torquemada’s ruthless prosecution of the secret Jews of Spain by means of the Inquisition, which he had instituted, had the wholehearted support of the most Catholic rulers of that country, Ferdinand and Isabella, and one important reason for their enthusiasm for the cause was the wealth that the forfeited property of the defendants brought into their coffers of state. Nasser in robbing the Jewish citizens of Egypt of all their possessions, under the pretext of their having been collaborators with his country’s enemies, is merely following the example of Hitler, who, at the beginning of his career, bolstered Germany’s deteriorating economy with confiscated Jewish loot.

Will there ever be an end to such tactics? Is there anything Jews can do to make their enemies stop picking on them? There is a solution, but it is one that no person with a modicum of respect would resort to. That is to disappear from the scene, to become completely inconspicuous by committing racial and spiritual suicide. When there will be no more Jews, there will no longer be a Jewish problem. But even this escape from the onus of Jewishness is not always successful. In his need for Jewish wealth, with which to keep his followers contented, Hitler, it will be recalled, had his historians scrutinize the state archives for descendants in the third and fourth generation of Jews who had apostacized on account of the advantages offered them in return for their acceptance of the dominant faith. Whoever was unfortunate enough to have had a Jewish grandfather or great-grandfather, was, for purposes of expropriation in the interests of the Reich, labelled a “Jew”. Also to this very day the offspring of the original Christian population of the island of Majorca, off the coast of Spain in the Mediterranean, refuse to intermarry with a group today professing Catholicism, whose designation Chuetas serves as a reminder that their ancestors more than four centuries back had been Jews.

Hence there is no alternative for those Jews, who wish to survive, not only spiritually but even physically, except to fight Amalek all over again. And though it is true that history, which follows certain divinely established laws, eventually takes its revenge on these enemies of God’s chosen people — for God does His part in blotting out the memory of Amalek — we are cautioned against leaving this function entirely to the Almighty. “Thou shalt blot out the memory of Amalek”[^13] is a command directed to the would-be-victims themselves, to the Jews.

But how is this to be effected by them? Not by looking for an escape, by running away. “He who fights and runs away,” warned Oliver Goldsmith, “may live to fight another day.” The only way to achieve the goal is to join battle, as Joshua did,[^14] to hold our heads high and stand up for our rights in the manner of Mordecai, the hero of Purim,[^15] and to rally our forces and unite for our common defense, as Esther advised the Jews of Persia to do in her time.[^16] To win in this battle we must be strong in faith, for this, as our sages remark,”[^17] was the secret of the success of the soldiers of Israel in their clash with the Amalekites. Last but not least is the support we render to those institutions that build up the resistance of the Jew and render him immune against attack. The shekels, said our sages[^18] driving home this point, that the Israelites had remitted for the maintenance of the service of the Temple of Jerusalem, outweighed the ten thousand talents of silver that Haman had promised king Achasverosh for the right to exterminate Persia’s Jews.[^19]

And let us not shed tears over the hardness of our lot, and think how much better off we might have been had we not been born of Jewish parents. Let us bear in mind the fact that at its best life is a tug of war between the forces of construction and destruction. Darwin referred to it as “a struggle for existence”. Man has to fight for everything worthwhile that he possesses or wants to acquire. Far from being the misfortune, which the Jewish assimilationists of the nineteenth century thought it was, as Heine reports, we, who are loyal to it, regard it as a great boon. We thank God every morning for “not having made us heathens.”[^20] We believe that it “is our bounden duty to praise the Lord of all things for not having rendered us like many other families of the earth.”[^21] Yes, we feel grateful to Him for having shown us particular favor by making us the recipients of His eternal law, and implanting in us thereby the seeds of life everlasting.[^22]
