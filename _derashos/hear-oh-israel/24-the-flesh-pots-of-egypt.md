THE FLESH-POTS OF EGYPT

That the task which had been assigned to Moses the son of Amram of leading the children of Israel through the waterless waste to the Promised Land was no sinecure is easy enough to understand The causes of Moses’ difficulties were entirely natural. They were a part of the process of transition from the condition of servitude to that of freedom. Physically the Israelites had ceased to be slaves the moment they left the soil of their oppressors. Spiritually they continued to be subject to the devastating influences of the life of slavery to which they had been accustomed, and it took years before the after-effects of these influences were completely erased from their souls.

An illustration hereof is what happened in the place in the wilderness called “Graves of Lust.” The manner in which this name arose is explained by the Torah as follows. After the mishap that had occured in Tab’erah (“Conflagration”), in which a portion of the camp of the Israelites had been destroyed by fire, the rabble among the people suddenly conceived an intense hankering for meat; and soon the cry was taken up by the remainder of the desert wanderers[^1] Reminding themselves of the abundance and variety of food that used to be available for them in Egypt, “the fish they ate gratis, the cucumbers, melons, leek, onions and garlic,” they complained bitterly of the diet on which they had to subsist in the desert. They were sick and tired of the Manna, which was their staple of nourishment morning, noon and night. “Oh for a piece of real meat!” they cried with their tongues hanging out.

The reaction of Moses to their petulance and wailing was complete despair. “Where can I get enough meat to fill the mouths of all this multitude consisting of 600,00 foot-soldiers aside from women and children?” he asked God who had appointed him as Israel’s caretaker and guardian.[^2] “The load is too heavy for me. I cannot bear it by myself any longer.” If this is what I must endure because of the office I hold, Thou mayest as well take my life altogether and spare me further heart-ache and anguish.[^3] God thereupon ordered Moses to select seventy elders to share his burdens, so that he would not have to carry them alone As for the meat, such an abundance of quail was swept in from the sea by the wind that they had much more than they needed and the food came literally out from the people’s noses. The result was that many died from overeating and were buried on the spot. This in brief is the account of Scripture.

As it is related the story raises many questions that were already asked by the rabbinic commentators (Sifre). What is puzzling first of all is that there should have been, of all things, an outcry on the part of the Israelites for meat. Is it not stated in the book of Exodus[^4] that, when the children of Israel departed from Egypt, they took along with them whole flocks of “sheep and beef cattle?” And lest anyone imagine that this livestock was consumed in the in the wilderness, the conjecture is negated by the statement in the book of Numbers[^5] that the tribes of Reuben and Gad, in particular, possessed a great deal of this type of movable property, wherefore they were eager to retain as their inheritance the rich pasture-lands on the eastern banks of the Jordan.

Secondly, since when was it considered sinful to entertain a desire and satisfy the longing to eat meat? Does not the Torah say in Deuteronomy:[^6] “When the Lord thy God shall enlarge thy border, as He hath promised thee, and thou shalt say: ‘I will eat flesh’, because thy soul desireth to eat flesh, thou mayest eat flesh, after all the desire of thy soul?”

Thirdly, what possessed the lusters for meat in the wilderness to say that in Egypt they obtained fish to eat for nothing, without pay? If even to secure straw for the bricks they baked for use in building the supply cities for Pharaoh they were compelled to fend for themselves,[^7] is it conceivable that they should have received their food rations gratis?

In the fourth place, the Manna, which was their regular menu in the wilderness had, as the Talmud notes[^8] many different tastes They could savor in it whatever they desired. Why, then, should it have been necessary, since the Manna met their needs so completely, to look for something else? Finally it seems strange that of all persons Moses should, after all the miracles God had wrought for Israel through him, have been the one to express a doubt about His ability to grant His people’s desire in this instance.

Because of these problems that were posed by the account of the incident in the Bible, the masters of the Agada felt that the Israelites probably had no legitimate reason for complaining, and that their dissatisfaction was not due to any shortage of meat or other victuals. There was enough nourishment at their disposal to still the normal hungers of the body and to sustain life. However still being at heart slaves, who in antiquity rarely got too much to eat, they made a fetish of food, as though that were all that man lived for, rather than its being but a means to an end. The result was that they asked for much more than they actually required and overate, with the inevitable consequence, namely death.

The Israelites, who had but a short time before the catastrophe at “the Graves of Lust” risen from the lowest depths of social degradation, were not the only persons to ascribe undue importance to material things like food. Individuals standing on a much higher intellectual level than they not only made the same mistake but committed the even greater error of glorifying it by elevating it into a philosophy of life. It was Karl Marx, the prophet of communism and pretended Messiah of the oppressed masses, who propounded the materialistic interpretation of history. According to him bread was everything. The struggle for the control of the economic resources of the world was the basis of all wars among nations as well as of the uprisings within nations. That is perhaps the reason why the Russians and their satellites are so eager to grab all they can, why they are not satisfied with developing what they have but are forever stirring up trouble so that they can put their hands on their neighbors’ possessions. As Ecclesiastes reminds us,[^9] it is of the nature of the appetite for things material to grow and increase every time it is whetted. He that loves money or power for their own sake never has a satiety thereof. By overexpansion nations as well as individuals create their Frankensteins and then, as it is bound to happen by the logic of necessity, lose control of them. Moses recognized this tendency in the Israelites’? excessive demand for meat. That was why he was skeptical about God’s capacity for meeting this demand. True enough, He who created the world could not lack the means for sustaining it. Has not the psalmist said: “Thou openest Thy hand and satisfiest every living thing with favor?”[^10] But when men lust just for the sake of lust, when their appetite is unlimited, then even God Himself cannot supply their fill.

This is why Judaism has, notwithstanding its recognition of the claims of the flesh, been unalterably opposed to naked materialism and to a purely materialistic interpretation of history. Man needs bread to live, but “he does not live by bread alone.”[^11] This being the case, he must exercise self-control in things material, beware against overstepping the boundary of his needs. The individuals who died at the “Graves of Lust” paid no attention to these warnings. They chafed, so the Midrash observes,[^12] at these restrictions. They wanted to be free of the yoke of the Torah and its commandments as they once were in Egypt. That was their undoing.
