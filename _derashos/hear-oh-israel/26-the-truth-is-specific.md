THE TRUTH IS SPECIFIC

One of the finest sociological studies to be met with anywhere in literature is the description given in the Bible of the rebellion organized against Moses and Aaron by one of their own fellow-tribesmen Korah. Korah, who seems to have been a big-wig in the tribe of Levi, and, according to a tradition already mentioned by Josephus in his Antiquities and corroborated by the Midrash, a man of great wealth, was apparently not satisfied with the relatively insignificant role that was assigned to him and his brother-Levites in the hierarchy of Israel’s clergy. So, gathering around himself other malcontents, including Dathan and Abiram, the sons of Eliab and On the son of Peleth, all of the tribe of Reuben — a total of 250 individuals, not nonentities but distinguished personalities, “princes of the congregation, elect members of the assembly, men of renown,” he and his supporters stood up before Moses and Aaron and accused them of the illegal usurpation of power. “The entire congregation,” they said, “is holy, and the Lord is in their midst. With what right, therefore, did ye take it upon yourselves to lift yourselves up above the congregation of the Lord?”

Moses was completely taken aback by the charge. True enough, his brother Aaron was invested with the dignity of the high-priesthood, while he himself, as commander-in-chief of the hosts of Israel, issued the orders and gave the instructions that regulated the life of the people in the wilderness. But that he, who had never derived the least personal benefit from the position of authority that he occupied, not even to the extent of depriving a single Israelite of his donkey or otherwise infringing upon his rights, that he, who had preferred to be stricken from God’s book rather than allow Israel to be destroyed, though it be in punishment for its sins, that he should be suspected of harboring private ambitions in holding the reins of Israel’s government, that was absolutely fantastic. What made matters worse was that it was so difficult to scotch such a libel. How was he to convince persons who had made up their minds that the entire Torah that he had brought to Israel, all the statutes and ordinances he had presented to them, were pure invention, the creation of his own mind, that is was not so,[^1] but that God, who had commissioned him, was the author of it all? Moses was faced by a situation that could be saved only by the fortuitous elimination of his opponents. But that was an eventuality he could only hope for, not one he was sure of. Fortunately for him and for Israel, the unforeseen and unexpected did come to pass. The earth opened up its mouth and swallowed Korah and his band, and peace reigned once more among the Israelites desert wanderers.

Korah, as he is portrayed in Scripture, represents the typical demagogue, the politician in the sinister meaning of the term, which has been defined as “an individual who stands for what other people fall for.” That is to say he plays on the feelings of the masses in order to gain a following and thus build up his power. Now there is nothing inherently wrong in using the most effective psychological approach to achieve a desired and desirable objective. In fact an understanding of human nature and the employment of methods that take into account the normal reactions of the average person is indispensable for securing sponsors for the worthiest causes, It is well-known that the secret of the success of the late Franklin Delano Roosevelt in initiating the social and economic reforms, that lifted up our nation from the depths of depression, was due in a large measure to his intimate fire-side chats over the radio. By means of these talks to the people he took into his confidence the millions of voters who had four times in succession elected him to the highest office within the gift of the American people. And by winning over public opinion he compelled Congress to pass the legislation necessary to make his program a reality. It must be remembered, however, that President Roosevelt did what he did not for personal gain — which he did not need because he was a man of independent means — but for the common good, whereas politicians of the brand of Korah are interested only in feathering their private nests.

And how do they go about realizing their unholy ambitions? They appeal to the prejudices of the masses, to their lowest rather than their highest instincts. They stir them up by means of grandiose slogans, generalities so broad that they could mean everything and nothing. They obscure the truth by a smoke-screen of words, and, promising the moon, avoid being pinned down to anything specific, lest they be held to account for failing to live up to their commitments.

Thus Korah flattered the Israelites, whose favor he sought, by telling them that they were all saints and that God was in their midst —- which in a broad sense could be considered true. Hadn’t Moses himself stated that by accepting God’s Torah they would become “a kingdom of priests and a holy nation?” Also they had all stood at Sinai and, like Moses, heard the Ten Commandments from the mouth of the Eternal. What Korah had failed to mention — no doubt with deliberate intent — was that in spite of this experience they were not all equally endowed spiritually, and that their presence at the revelation did not make prophets of them, much as Moses himself would have wished it, as he said: “Would that all the Lord’s people were prophets, that the Lord would put His spirit upon them!”[^2] But even if it were conceivable that all Israelites were invested with prophetic power, they could not, if order and discipline was to prevail in their midst, act each one according to his own individual likes. Even in a democracy, where all citizens are supposed to possess equal rights, there can be only one head, to whom is delegated the authority to give the directions that all are obligated to obey.

By demanding that Moses and Aaron resign from their posts on the ground that all Israelites were equally holy and therefore no one had the right to lord it over the other, Korah and his band, who were fully aware of the fact that a people without a leader respected by all was just an unruly mob, were merely angling to place themselves in the positions that would be vacated by the present incumbents. God knows what would have been the fate of the people of Israel if, instead of being governed in its infancy by high-minded selfless idealists of the calibre of Moses and Aaron, they would have fallen into the hands of unscrupulous adventurers and opportunists like Korah and his party!

The moral of the story is to beware of generalities, even the most highsounding and beautiful, for the truth is specific As an illustration one might cite the experience of 2 candidate for a rabbinical position with one of our American congregations. Called upon to deliver a sermon in order to demonstrate to his potential parishioners his qualifications as a preacher, the applicant wondered what theme would be most appropriate. “Shall I speak about Sabbath observance?” he asked the president. “I am afraid that would be a little risky,” replied the lay head of the congregation. “Many of our people are compelled to work on Saturday for their livelihood.” “Do you think Kashrus might be a suitable topic?” “No, that wouldn’t do either. You would be stepping on too many corns.” “How about family purity?” queried the preacher on trial. The president felt uncomfortable. “Rabbi,” he said, “we are living in a modern world. This isn’t Eastern Europe.” “Well, what shall I speak about?” the candidate asked in desperation. “Speak about Judaism,” was the advice he received.

Generalizations, slogans, big words! It was under the pretext of saving the people’s democracy of Hungary from the inroads of the capitalist imperialists of the West that the Russian army with tanks and mortars crushed the uprising of unarmed Hungarian students against the injustices of the Communist regime. “Sanctity of our territorial waters!” say Egypt and Saudi Arabia, but what they really have in mind is the economic strangulation of Israel, which they were unable to defeat by military means. “Rights of belligerency!” shouts Nasser, and thereby circumvents a solemn agreement declaring the Suez Canal to be an international artery of commerce that has to be kept open to all peoples and be closed to none.

No, the truth does not consist of generalities. Slogans, however grandiloquent they be, mean nothing unless their meaning is spelled out in detail, unless they are translated into their practical application in everyday life. It is high time to puncture the fraud of verbiage by insisting on performance. Actions speak louder than words. Let there be a showdown and it will become evident who is sincere, honest, who has been chosen by God as His agent, and who is the mountebank, the impostor, the deceiver, whether it be Moses or Korah.
