THEY MUST NOT DIE AGAIN

It is our custom on this last day of our festival to spend a few moments in the imaginary company of our loved ones, who are unable to celebrate with us anymore in person because the Lord has taken them. In addition to these relatives, who died a natural death when the time allotted to them on earth was up, there are several millions of our coreligionists who, but for the fact that their lives were suddenly snuffed out a decade and a half ago in an orgy of destruction unparalleled in the annals of man, might have been here today to observe the Passover. The recapitulation of even a small portion of the gruesome story of their unspeakable martyrdom would be enough to drive him that relates it to distraction. The massacres of the Crusaders, that are commemorated in the prayer “Father of mercy”, and the torments of the Spanish Inquisition were tame affairs compared to the systematically organized extermination parties arranged for European Jewry by Adolph Hitler during the last three years of world war two. The wild Tatars at their most savage stage were not as brutal as the official representatives of the Kulturvolk in their behavior toward a peaceful, defenseless minority, whose only crime was that they wanted to live and breathe like all of God’s creatures. It would not have been believed, if it were not true, that beings created in the divine image were capable of snatching infants from their mothers’ breasts and cutting them in two, of shooting parents to death in the presence of their children, of raping wives in the sight of their husbands, of disporting themselves by witnessing the misery and degradation of fellow-mortals and even making photographic records of the final agony of their hapless victims. This is the work, not of normal persons, but of madmen, so much so as to make one incline to the view that the German people, or at least the majority of its leaders, must have lost their mental balance for a period of twelve years. What is inexplicable and cannot be condoned, however, is the fact that the socalled sane world should have remained so callous in the face of this spectacle, that it did not stop the madmen run riot before they were able to do most of their damage. And what is even less forgivable is that, after the danse macabre was over, and the forces of destruction were finally subdued, the surviving ringleaders, whose accountability for the atrocities committed was proven beyond the shadow of a doubt, were dealt with so gingerly, that the sentences meted out at the Nuremberg trials against many of the most notorious culprits were so mild. Such clemency is the most dangerous form of injustice, for it encourages criminals in their crimes. Nothing so jeopardizes the peace and security of the world as the appeasement of aggressors. Charity toward incorrigible, impenitent murderers is but an inducement to more murder.

Unfortunately there is nothing that can be done by us about these bygones except to vent our rage. The six million Jewish martyrs, who went to their graves, will not come to life again even if all those responsible for their untimely death were to be brought to the bar of justice and receive the punishment they deserve. Nor can the omission and failure to act of those, who could have prevented all this from happening but refused to assert and exert themselves, be made up for anymore. There is, however, one thing that does lie in our power to do, and that is to save the spiritual heritage left us by our martyred brethren from oblivion; to preserve and protect against the corrosive effects of time their priceless contributions to civilization and culture, to human thought and living; to keep intact the memory of their works and of their excursions into the realm of ideas behind this physical universe, by studying their writings and giving continuity to the institutions they established. This has always been done by us Jews in the past. The once famous Jewish academies of Babylonia, that enjoyed an uninterrupted existence of nearly a thousand years, have long already closed their doors. Their intellectual activity, however, has been forever incorporated in the Babylonian Talmud, that is studied by Jews everywhere. The cities and towns of medieval France, in which Rash: taught and the Tosafists engaged in their legal researches, have not had Jewish congregations since the expulsion of the Jews from them at the end of the fourteenth century. Yet the names of these teachers live and their views have been transmitted in the commentaries and critical notes that are disseminated throughout the world. The only material witnesses extant of the glory of Jewish Spain of the eleventh, twelfth, thirteenth and fourteenth centuries are the Hebrew inscriptions on synagogues, that have since ben transformed into churches, such as the splendid 14th century Moorish edifice still standing in Toledo. But whenever we read the moving Zion odes of Jehudah Halevi or the inspiring verses of Solomon ibn Gabirol, or we look through the well-ordered code of Moses Maimonides or we browse in the Hebrew translation of his stupendous “Guide of the Perplexed”, we know that there was a golden era of Hebrew literature in Spain, that produced all these monuments of the spirit.

This is, then, what must be done by us for the Polish and Lithuanian and Hungarian and German Jewries of our time. The wealth they created must not be permitted to go up in the smoke of apathy and oblivion, to be neglected and forgotten. For if that were to be their fate, the enemy would indeed have been victorious. Hitler would have achieved after his death what he did not see accomplished in his lifetime — namely the annihilation of every trace of existence of those whose memory he had striven to blot out from the face of the earth. This must not be permitted to come to pass, and it devolves upon us, the nearest of kin of the departed, to salvage what can be of their most valuable remains and to maintain their estate. If we fail, we are even guiltier than the murderers and assassins, for while they succeeded only in destroying their bodies, we would, by allowing the forces of decadence to take their natural course, have given the coup de grace to the annihilation of their souls.

It is related that at a certain conference of Jewish writers held in Odessa, which was toward the end of the nineteenth century a center of the Haskalah, the Hebrew enlightenment movement in Russia, one of the speakers delivered a lecture about Moses. Although in his opinion the reputed Jewish lawgiver was a completely mythical figure that never really existed, he conceded that the works attributed to him made so deep an impression on the human mind that it did not matter whether he actually lived or not. After the lecturer had sat down, a tall, stooping, bearded Jew, clad in the costume of the old school, by the name of Mendele Mocher Seforim, the grandfather of Yiddish literature, came up to him and congratulated him. “I don’t understand very much about Bible criticism,” he said very meekly, “and I am no philosopher. Nevertheless I am very grateful to you, because what you told us about Moses this evening cleared up for me a difficulty that had long been bothering me. The Torah informs us that, when Moses our teacher died in complete solitude in the land of Moab, he was laid to rest by God, and that to this day no man has been able to find his grave.[^1] Tonight, thanks to your enlightening remarks, I learned where Moses was buried. It was right over here, in this hall in Odessa.”

Yes, if the chapter written by European Jewry in the annals of our people, in its history and literature, should ever be forgotten, it would not be because of what Hitler and his fiends had done but because of what we, all of whom haul from that center of Jewish life, will have failed to do. It takes only one generation to interrupt the hitherto unbroken chain of Jewish tradition — one generation of complete separation from the sources of Jewish activity and thought — for the labor of thousands of years, for the accumulated wisdom of the ages to be forfeited. It has been seen to happen. There have been grandfathers who were devout, learned Jews, steeped in the glorious teachings of the Jewish past, whose grandchildren were totally ignorant of all this, knowing nothing whatever of that which was the pride of their forbears, having no more connection with what came before them than if they had been the offspring of an alien people, that had grown up in an environment completely foreign to that of their ancestors.

If we are to be worthy of the legacy that is ours, we must at least take time off to explore it, find out what we have and not reject it blithely because we are too indolent to occupy ourselves therewith. The voices of 6,000,000 European Jews are calling unto us from their graves. “We have died once in the flesh. Let us not die a second time in the spirit. Give us a place in your hearts and your minds. Let your lives be our monument. Go on rebuilding the state of Israel, of which our Chalutzim have laid the foundations. Cultivate in the Diaspora as well as in the Jewish homeland the Hebrew language, which we helped to revive. Enlarge your tents of Jewish learning. Expand your schools. Increase the number of your community centers. Enable your synagogues to thrive and flourish. Make up for what the enemy has destroyed by your constructive Jewish accomplishments. Then will our death have been atoned for, for our life will not have been in vain.”

[^1]: Deuteronomy 34.6
