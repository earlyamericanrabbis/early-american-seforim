Friends:

We are assembled here to perform a most solemn duty. We are about to consecrate the ground as a House for the Living-Beth ha-Hayim, or an eternal abode-Beth Olam.

We have no such terminology as a place or house for the dead-Beth ha-Movet.

Our dead are living; in death they find life.

We therefore must provide for them a fit place for their repose-not forever, but until it will please our Living God in heaven to quicken them, and have their souls rejoin them to a better life.

This problem of inhumation of the Jewish dead was ever a serious one for our people. Beginning with Abraham, and ending with our own organization, we know that this problem must be solved before we have the tragedy of death in our midst. We must be prepared in a manner to honor our dead.

Our organization did all that was possible in order to secure this ground. And that is not an easy undertaking. A limited membership had to tax itself to its capacity in order to bring it about.

But we can not bury the dead in an open field. We must make the place presentable, in order to maintain our own self-respect.

Thus it was that the committee and the officers worked ceaselessly, until we are now about to dedicate the Gate, and consecrate the ground in accordance with the traditions of our living religion.

There are a number of honors to be disposed off by auction to the highest bidder. You know well that the proceeds thereof will go into the fund and ease the burden of the officers to meet all obligations. I am expecting a liberal response when the ceremonies will commence.

May this ground be forever a consecrated field without graves. Amen.
