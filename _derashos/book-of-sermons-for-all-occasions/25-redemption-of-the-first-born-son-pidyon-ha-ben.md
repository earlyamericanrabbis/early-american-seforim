The father acknowledged the fact that he desires the son, his wife brought to him as her first-fruit of the happy union between them, more than he desires the five Shekels; he gave the shekels to the descendant of Aaron the priest-Cohen-and took the son and gave it tenderly again into its mother's keeping.

Then follows this feast.

What is it, what does it all mean?

Is there any father who would rather have five Shekels than his son, who already costs him, under the modern system of life and living, almost a fortune?

And why the first-born more than other sons who usually follow the first-born?

\(1\) It means that the child becomes free-born, being chained to no one in his future choice of his life's conduct; it means that the child is valued not on account of what the father could get out of him, at some future time, in things of an intrinsic value.

\(2\) The priest-Cohen-is here present to deny the ancient contention of peoples that the child belongs to the state, that it is born a slave to the society which governs the conditions of life under which he was born. The material value was adjusted; the amount paid; and the only authority to rear and educate the child belongs to the father and mother-not to the state, nor to any other being under the sun.

\(3\) The first-born son for this purpose, must be the first-born of the mother. She brings him here to proclaim to the whole world that under the system of Jewish life every child traces descent through the father; that the practice of tracing descent through the mother was never in vogue in Israel; that Israel's daughters were never nationalized. It is a public act, testifying alike to the freedom of the child, and to the purity of Jewish life.
