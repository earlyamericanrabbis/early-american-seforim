Life has many more sources of happiness than disagreeable duties. A duty should never be looked upon as disagreeable. If it is the duty of one to do something, than it should be a source of pleasure to do it, and do it right. But life has disagreeable duties such as we must do in sadness. Life streams onward, and carries along on its current the many things we lose sight of, and before we know we are left in possession of but part of our former self.

But happy are they whose live's wick does not consume more oil than the required proportion to light up the dark paths for them. In using up the oil of our lamp proportionately we receive a double blessing; we enjoy the light, and we know that our paths will not be darkened of a sudden.

The most extravagant source in using up our life's oil can be traced to unhappiness, misfortune, and quarrels.

We are here assembled to celebrate the Silver-Jubilee of our dear friends. Their lives were blessed with everything which yields happiness; in their middle-age they are as full of life-may God prolong their lives!

-as at the time when they married, twenty-five years ago to-night.

Surrounded by their children, by friends and relatives, they find themselves in a vineyard of happiness. Their life did not consume an undue proportion of the godly ointment. Neither adversity nor prosperity moved them to extremes; in modesty they continued to live on, to carry onward, to bring to a goal everything they had undertaken.

Silver anniversaries are not often in the lives of married couples; they come but once in a lifetime; but they are often to be thought of by all married couples. What is there to life, if not one's posterity, one's children?

And, what has life to offer if the happiness of a married march across the space of time be taken away from it?

I raise my glass and toast the happy couple. May they live to celebrate their golden wedding anniversary as full of vigor of life as they are now!
