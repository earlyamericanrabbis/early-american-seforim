Life is but a passing shadow-the shadow of a bird in his flight-away flies the bird, and there remains neither bird nor shadow. Yet, what great achievement might be accomplished in a lifetime!

The span of life, at its termination, appears to have been but a shadow, a dream; yet, while it did last, lo, how purposeful the shadow, how real the dream can be made to be!

Life's journey might terminate any time: "the soul," as the great sage Hillel said, "is but a guest within the human body."

Shall our endeavors be heavenward, or, shall we quicken our pace upon earth nearer to the grave, to silence, to chaos?

Here lie the remains of a true follower of Judaism, a faithful man who, despite of all struggles, managed to direct his steps heavenward.

As a father he directed all of his efforts to rear and educate his children in a way to make his life's work immortal; they will carry down to posterity that which was dear and near to his soul.

That which you now see, the remains, will terminate in dissolution; dust to dust, earth to earth. His hand will never again be extended in friendship; his lips will never again utter words of comfort to the needy; his eyes will never again look with that kindly gaze which gave self-expression to his dear soul within him; he will no longer speed along on his feet to carry a message of cheer, there where it was mostly needed.

All this is true; and, yet, no man like him ever died, in the sense that our composite being comprehends what the term death means. "The righteous are living even in death."

The soul of man is indestructible. It is not of the earthly elements, and, therefore, can not suffer death in the form of death we behold the earthly remains of this righteous man. The life of the soul does not originate here, on this planet, and no force thereof can do away with it. The soul is a heavenly being; there it originated, and, if it remains pure during its earthly sojourn, its life remains immortal.

This fallen father in Israel kept his soul pure; it ascends heaven in its original state; the good deeds accomplished here follow it up to its heavenly abode. The good deeds of a righteous person are the real friends of his who follow his hearse, stand around his bier as a guard of honor, and give him consciousness in heaven to rejoice there beneath the wings of God's Shekinah.

He did during his lifetime that which will make his posterity here upon earth secure; and establish his soul among the saintly beings in heaven. May Almighty's will be, to grace his soul, and that it be so, Amen.
