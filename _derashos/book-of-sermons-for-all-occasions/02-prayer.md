**II .**

"Raba opened his sermon, saying: What means the Psalmist by saying, ' I love that the Lord should hear'? Says the Assembled congregation of Israel: Lord of the universe, I know that I am beloved of Thee when Thou dost hear my voice and my supplications. The Psalmist, moreover says: 'I was brought low, and He saved me,' by which he means: Though I was brought low by the lack of merit in my neglect to observe the precepts, it is well within His glory to save me". (Tractate Rosh-ha-Shanah, 17.)

We have no other means of expressing the innermost sentiments of our souls than by prayer. When we lay aside all our earthly cares and burdens, when we forget our physical being and natural inclinations, we feel a certain longing for something which we know we must have, and, yet, by obtaining it, it will not satisfy our bodies. Just as if something strange, something unearthly grips our thoughts and leads us to believe that we once enjoyed that something and forgot it, or neglected to retain the taste thereof in our conscience. If we are possessed of a feeling of that kind, it is the full expression of the soul's desire for spiritual food. Maimoni says: "The soul is subject to sickness as well as the body". When the body is hungry, we give it food; when it is weary, we give it rest; when it is sick, we give it medicine; but when we cease feeling any hunger, pain or weariness, our lives are endangered; such disease is a paralysis; it requires special treatment, special attention of some one else besides our own efforts, for then we have no efforts to endeavor to help ourselves.

So it is with the soul. It is inactive for long periods at a time, because we do not feel its demands upon us. We know that the soul requires attention; we know that it is but a visitor within the body; we know that its life is not an earthly gift, and that its career does not depend upon natural accomplishments. Nevertheless, we give the soul but little attention; its own powers we utilize for bodily needs; its genius we harness for material ends and all for the sake of keeping out of our conscience its very purpose of being a heavenly tenant in a dark, lowly mass of clay!

And, yet, "good and evil do not come forth from the mouth of the Most High." Each of us has an inherent free will; each of us may follow any path he chooses. Only we were warned not to follow the path of death, after the path of life was given to us.

Only the prophet who foretells good must show that his prophecy was fulfilled to the letter; the prophet of evil is not required to bring such token. The evil will not come to pass, if repentance chased away the threatening cloud.

The awakening of the soul comes about after a heavenly herald finds attentive ears for a spiritual call. The call comes to a whole people simultan-cously. It comes to all Israel. It is proclaimed by an holy convocation, by a day of rest, and it expresses itself in prayer-the prayer of the soul for spiritual food, for preventive as well as for remedial advice.

All of these we find in the prayers of Rosh-ha-Shanah.

There are three distinct prayers in the Musaph service of Rosh-ha-Shanah; (1) Malchut-Sover eignty; (2) Zichronot-Retrospection; (3) Sho-phrot-Triumph of the Ram's-horn Trumpet.

1\. The reign of God; the victory of the spiritual over the material, the universal acknowledgement of the Unity of God; founded upon verses of the Torah, the Prophets, and the Psalms. Such is the first category of prayers.

2\. The martyrdom of Israel for the sake of his belief in One God, for the sake of saving the Torah from the flame of idolatry, for the sake of preserving the world's soul by remaining true to his traditions, quoting instances from the Torah, the Prophets and the Pslams. Such is the second category of prayers.

3\. The final triumph of the martyred people Israel, the ultimate goal of a hope thousands of years old, the turning of all people toward God and His Laws; emphasized by quoting passages from the Torah, the Prophets, and the Psalmist, and brought home effectively by the sound of the Shophar. Such is the third category of prayers.

The entire being of every member in the house of Israel is re-cast, re-formed, and re-made. Israel's sufferings were not in vain. That is a victory for the soul over the body, for the spiritual over the material. Israel suffered for a great cause, un selfish from beginning to end; that is heavenly, genuine food for the soul. Israel, by his example, will carry along on the wings of triumph all mankind. The ultimate superiority of he soul over the body acknowledged; the materia ends of life placed in a subsidiary position, and the position of the spiritual in life firmly established on the height of man's career upon earth.

Thus, as Raba so beautifully expressed it, we know that we enjoy God's love, when we pray, when God hears our voice of supplication.
