**I.**

"And thou shalt shew thy son in that day, saying: This is done because of that which the Lord did unto me when I came forth out of Egypt." (Ex. 12.8).

Liberty, under organized society, is the foundation of civilization. The economic, educational, moral, sociological, scientific and political development of a State is in proportion to the degree and amount of liberty enjoyed by the smallest minority. After bread, liberty is the first necessity of the human being. The bread of the slave is but bread of affliction; not a source of physical energy, but a prolongation of misery, a stay of execution.

The experience of a nation is mirrored in its history. From the fragmentary chapters of a people's history comes forth a cohesive philosophy which forms an undercurrent of a biological stream flowing throughout the life of that people.

No people can sever its connection with its traditions and, at the same time, lay claim to be what it was destined to be, unless its radical departure is the form of an absorption by another and stronger force than its own, when it ceases to be. But such conduct is usually traced to external, rather than to internal forces. A people must make progress; it can not remain stationery; but neither can it disconnect its spinal joints and remain a living organism.

A people with a rich history should not decay; if it does, it is never the fault of its Maker. The force of evolution in the human race is as powerful as is the force of nature in metamorphizing larva. A people, like an individual, is master of its own destiny; its character is its fate. There is no predestination for nations; they operate under the free-will decree granted to individuals at birth. Nations, like individuals, vary in strength as well as in mentality, but the proportional functioning of the organic total is regulated entirely by the will-power of every individual.

In order to secure the perpetuity of a nation, its course across the ages must be free from destructive elements, such as loss of territory, language, political independence, culture, population or progressive trend toward a higher civilization. Very few nations have achieved the distinction of remaining alive after losing all, or any of these.

Accordingly, there should have been not a trace left of the people of Israel; they suffered the loss of their territory, of their language, of their political independence, and of many more great destructive forces. Nevertheless their existence is a certainty. Almost sixteen millions of them are very much alive in the greatest centers, and in the very midst of the civilized world.

Is not this a miracle? Can any philosopher explain it on a rational, calculative basis?

The hag-Haherut-Feast of Liberty-is a study in the philosophy of Jewish history.

What is most desirable on this day is a thorough understanding of these phenomena by the rising generation.

This was provided for by our Torah.

"And thou shalt shew thy son in that day, saying: This is done because of that which the Lord did unto me when I came forth out of Egypt."

Centuries of suffering, a burning furnace, a forge of affliction were visited upon Israel before they were permitted to partake of the milk-and-honey of liberty under their own government, upon their own soil.
