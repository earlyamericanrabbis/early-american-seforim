**II .**

As a people God graced us with certain tokens. Sabbath is a token of time; Zizit is a token of appearance; Te'philin is a token of the mind and heart; Milah is a token of the body, expressive of life upon earth.

The Midrashists contend that God gave the commandment concerning Milah in order to teach man this lesson: "Nature, as it is, can be improved upon by the hand and knowledge of man; and it is man's duty to remove the crudeness of nature and make it more perfect."

The primary duty of man is to begin the process of improving himself at his very birth. But man is born most helpless among all beings. The one whose mind and body conquers the earth and all that is therein, at birth, gives no sign thereof. He stands in need of aid. Left to himself he would perish for want of protection, nourishment and care. The process of development, too, is much slower in man than in other beings. Childhood is not limited to but one year, or two. At least five years must elapse before the normal child begins to grasp things with a childish fancy. The man child can not reach the top rung of the ladder in one jump.

Left to himself the man child would grow up a savage. He would be in his natural state. The improvement in his make-up, view of life, spiritual perspective, ability to express his natural tendencies in a refined manner-all require a process of time. But analyzing the whole scope of man's growth, both in body and in spirit, we discover the truth of the Midrashic interpretation of Milah.

We begin to improve the man-child at birth, as soon as it is safe for him to undergo the operation. Of course the procedure is connected with our religious conception of God, Man, Nature, and Civilization.

"This precept was received in joy," as the Talmud expresses it, "and is continued to be observed in joy." And it is of right that it be so. We demonstrate the place of man in the make-up of God's intent and purpose in having created this massive universe. No other race conceived it in such true light.
