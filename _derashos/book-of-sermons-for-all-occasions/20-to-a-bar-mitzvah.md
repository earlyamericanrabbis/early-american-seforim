**I.**

My dear Bar Mitzvah Hatan:

You have proclaimed before this assembly, in most sacred, and not in uncertain terms your intentions of being a faithful member in the house of Israel, a true and a devoted friend to your brethern. These are noble sentiments, of which your parents and relatives might be justly proud.

But in this hour of your happiness, I must give you right and true advice, which will also embody caution in your future conduct.

Words-what are they?

They are, if uttered in sincerity, golden threads spun by one's soul, which, when put in the loom of sentences, become the finest material for a spiritual mantle fit even for a prophet such Elijah. But if they are uttered merely as a matter of form, and the soul had nothing at all to do with them, they are coarse yarns spun by the devil, which when put into sentences from a garb to cover every vice which leads man to destruction.

Promises-what are they?

If they come from the soul, they become roots; when fulfilled they become plants bearing the finest fruits which I may name "apples-of-paradise." One may eat them, enjoy them, and still know that the plants will reproduce in kind for all times and never run short.

What you have promised here, the words you employed in making the promise, came from your heart. Your innocence of all the evils of life testifies for you that your heart consents, your soul inspires, and your tongue delivers, that which is most fit for your thoughts.

In doing so, you have carried the mission of a newly initiated member in the House of Israel to its extreme point of sacredness. God rejoices in bestowing His blessings upon such faithful children as you are. May His blessings rest upon your head; may your words be a true prophecy for you and yours, forever, Amen.
