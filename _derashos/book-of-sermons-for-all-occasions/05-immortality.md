"For thus saith the High and Lofty One, That inhabited eternity, whose name is Holy; I dwell in the high and holy place With him also that is of a contrite and humble

spirit, To revive the spirit of the humble, And to revive the heart of the contrite ones."

(Isaiah, 57.15.)

" With him also that is of a contrite and humble spirit," this Verse informs us that he who is humble and practices humility ends up by bringing down the Shekinah of God among man on earth."

(Mehilta; Yalkut Shimony, Prophets, 488.)

The true purpose of the fasting on this day is to bring man down to a level of humility and contrition. A self-satisfied person feels far above his real station in life. Godlessness comes in an era of great prosperity, in a home of great luxury, in a mind filled with material plans for more gold, in a heart swelled with pleasurable indulgence.

Man is a composite being, not because of his physical and material complexities, but because he is more than earthly, because part of his makeup came to him from heaven.

The Midrashic authors declare: When God created the world, He desired to establish for Himself an Abode upon earth as well as in heaven. And, He created Adam. When he transgressed the only commandment which was given to him, God removed His Shekhinah from the earth to the first firmament; when Cain murdered his brother Abel, He removed His Shekhina to the second firmament; when the generation of Enosh began the practice of idolatry, he removed it to the third firmament; when the deluvian generation rose up with all its violence, He removed it to the fourth firmament; when the tower of Bable was built, He removed it to the fifth firmament; when the Sodomites appeared in their garb of sinfulness, He removed it to the sixth firmament; when Am-raphel, king of Shinar and his comrades called forth His anger, He removed it to the seventh firmament, and exclaimed:

Who will rise up for Me against the evil-doers,

Who will stand up for Me against the workers

of iniquity?

Then God set aside all the primitive generations, and established Abraham as the moral force upon earth. Abraham's deeds found favor in His eyes, and He permitted His Shekhinah to descend from the seventh to the sixth firmament; then Isaac arose, and His Shekhina descended from the sixth to the fifth; then Jacob arose, and it descended from the fifth to the fourth; then the tribe of Levi arose, and it descended from the fourth to the third; then Kahot rose up, and it descended from the third to the second; then Amram rose up, and it descended from the second to the first; then came Moses, and he brought down God's Shekhinah from the first firmament to Mount Sinai, even as it is said: "And God descended upon Mount Sinai. " (Midrash Tanhuma, Numbers; Midrash Rabba, Genesis; Midrash Rabba Numbers; Midrash Rabba Song of Songs.)

It is within the power of man to make this earth either a Sinai, an Abode, fit for the She-khina of God, or to turn it into a Godless Eden, a den of fratricides, a deluge of sin, a tower of Bable, a Sodom, an idolatry of Shinar. This earth was governed by the heartless Egyptians, the pagan Greeks, the proud Romans, and the brutal Islam. Everything was done, and everything was not done, in the name of religion; but surely God had no share therein. At times every brother was a Cain, and every other brother an Abel; every king an Amraphel, and every land a Sodom.

That all had to disappear; it thrived upon a deathly micro-organisms, and it dug its own grave.

Not so did the time pass with Israel; his little spot on this sphere was not turned into a cemetery to cover up his misdeeds. He lifted his eyes heavenward in supplication, not in pride; he walked with a bent head, not because of shame, but because of humility; he cleared a path, a narrow bloodstained thread upon the expanse of time, which is indestructible.

On this day we recall in immortal life our departed dead. We have memorial services for them. We fast and abstain from all earthly comforts and pleasures. We mourn the loss of our departed dear and near ones. We do it all to subdue our passions, to give dominion to the soul over the body, to the spiritual over the material in life, to permit our souls to partake of the heavenly feast which is not prepared of ordinary fare to satisfy its cravings and we learn to know that in our humble conduct of this day we rise to a spiritual height which reaches out to Sinai, where God's Shekhinah descended. We do all this to realize that the soul's march in the course of its earthly career is not earthward. We can not call to life the bodies of our near and dear ones; that which moved about through the shadows of this planet has long since terminated in dissolution, back to the earthly elements; but that which caused them to move about, that which gave expression to their thoughts, life to their actions and endurance to their achievements-their souls continue to live. We do not, for we can not, see them; but we feel their presence within us, amidst us, above us. From them we have inherited all that life affords in the realm of immortality. We carry on their work. We march on upon the waves of time; ahead of us we see an identical goal for us, and look down, in a spirit of contrition and humility, upon our posterity.

Our presence here, and the presence of all Israel, in their respective places of worship, throughout the diaspora, is best evidence that life is not limited to its natural phenomena, for, by the laws of nature we should long since have ceased to traverse this earth. But our course in human events can not be interrupted by human hands; its threads can not be severed by human instruments; its light can not be extinguished by either wind, or water, or darkened by either cloud or curtain. Our course is as constant as the course of the sun; it rises from the east, sets in the west, lights up the nether part of the earth, and reappears again in the east to cheer the world anew. For such is our share in immortality.
