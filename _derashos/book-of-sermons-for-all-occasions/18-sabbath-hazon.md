"Therefore saith the Lord, the Lord of hosts, The Mighty One of Israel; Ah, I will ease Me of mine adversaries, And revenge Me of Mine enemies; And I will turn My hand upon thee, And purge away thy dross as with lye, And will take away thine alloy; And I will restore thy judges as at first, And thy counselors as at the beginning; Afterward thou shalt be called The City of right-

eousness, The faithful city." (Isaiah, 1.24-26).

This is Sabbath Hazon-so named because the first word of the portion of prophets we read this morning is-Hazon; meaning Vision. Thus it is the Sabbath of Vision.

This Sabbath falls during the three weeks' mourning we observe annually for the lost glories ofZion.

Zion's glory was not concentrated in the wood and granite of its buildings, in the bronze and marble of its monuments, in the wealth of its citizens, or in the physical power of its army. Zion's glory was the self-expression of Israel as a nation, founded upon laws which were enacted in heaven for men upon earth.

The ancient peoples, both in the days of Nebuchadnezzar and of Titus, did not envy Israel his territory, his wealth, his commerce, his military genius, or his luxuries. He had all these; but the ancient Babylonians and Romans had more. They were not his enemies because his political aggression endangered their independence; they outnumbered him in population; outclassed him in territory and outweighed him in physical strength.

They did not despise him as a disagreeable neighbor. They loved his sons and daughters; they courted his society, and invited him to a game of oneness with them.

But they feared him; they dreaded, lest his being will cause them not to be; lest his civilization will suppress, or destroy theirs.

But the enemies of Israel would never have been able to destroy Zion, if it had remained Zion. Zion is indestructible. When righteousness rules Zion, it is an enemy-proof citadel; no one can scale its walls, or attack successfully its fortifications.

They prepared the ground, and seeded it with the seed of corruption. The judges of Zion became bribe-takers; its citizens, bribe-givers; its counsellors became self-seeking politicians; its scholars, infected with the dross of the age.

The enemies of God did that; they could not reach heaven, so they took the path of least resistance; they spoiled the sacred ground, and made it sterile. Then, Zion fell.

The prophet Isaiah had a Vision of the whole situation; he defied the judges; mocked the counsellors; denounced the citizens; warned the politicians, and lamented the oncoming fate of Zion.

Israel erected no monument to Jeremiah; his lamentation is the self-expression of Israel's afflicted soul; but he was not an Isaiah; he did not express Israel's soul and his future glories in the masterly way Isaiah did.

We call this Sabbath-Sabbath Hazon; a Sabbath of Vision. Verily, saith the Lord:

I will restore thy judges as at first,

And thy counsellors as at the beginning.
