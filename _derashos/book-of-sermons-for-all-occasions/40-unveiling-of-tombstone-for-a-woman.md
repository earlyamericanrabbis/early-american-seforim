You have assembled here to perform the last rites of honoring the memory of your mother.

If your mother now was able to speak, she would say but one word of greeting: "Children!"

If you could but listen, not with mortal ears; if you could but find the voice on the air waves; if you could but understand the language of the soul, that word would convey to you an entirely new meaning.

You, too, would say but one word: " Mother!"

But God formed everything in this world wisely. It is for our own good, and for the good of the departed that we can not hear them, and that we remain separated from them, in God's own way of shaping our lives, and in limiting our faculties.

But you are at the grave of your mother! You are about to unveil this granite monument to her sacred memory. Many will be the times that this dear stone-dear because it tells you that your mother's earthly remains lie beneath it, will be washed by your tears. In joy and in sorrow you will come hither to pour out your hearts. Not that your mother's memory will replace the belief in God. No; but you will come here to pray to God, so that your mother's soul may make a visitation here and listen to your prayers.

Belief and faith bring on comfort even amidst distressed conditions. It was said by the sages of yore, that the soul knoweth all, and understandeth all. Imagine for a moment: Your mother's soul is here; she sees you all; beholds your tears for her sake; noting your devotion to her memory! What bliss she finds in such knowledge! What blessing she bestoweth upon you!

This monument will forever testify to your devotion. Posterity will chronicle all such and similar deeds in golden letters.

This tombstone will mark more than a grave; it will designate a source of hope, a fountain of life, a well of soul-inspiring thought. Here she was a child; then a bride; then a mother; now a living soul in heaven! In this way the chain of the generations is forged; the links are precious; they are of God's own wonder-working forge and anvil. No earthly painter or sculptor could draw or hew on canvas or in bronz the like of this.

And, who is the poet to imagine and write lines of verses in a living tongue to give one a real idea or conception of such phenomenon as we see here so prosaically?

We have within our souls much more than we can give expression to, or comprehend with our gross senses.

That alone is the secret of our being. We dread and love the unknown simultaneously; it fascinates us.

And, for this very reason you unveil a tombstone; you express your feelings in something concrete; you would not be pleased to stand far off and call upon your mother. Here is her grave, and at its head this tombstone. That gives you a physical touch to the spiritual panorama.

And, because it was ordained by Almighty that it be so, I, as a Rabbi in Israel, now, by virtue of this authority vested in me, declare this ground sacred to the memory of (name the woman and her father) and this tombstone, erected by those whom she loved most, dedicated to her undying devotion, by the undying faith of her children.

May Almighty grant you all long life and happiness to the extent that your saintly mother herself would grant you, by praying to God for you, and may the dead be quickened, speedily, and tears be wiped off from the face of the living, forever, Amen.
