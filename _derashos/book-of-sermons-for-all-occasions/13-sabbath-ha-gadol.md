(Malachi, 3.23-24). "Remember ye the law of Moses My servant: This is as the Verse said: 'God understandeth the way thereof. ' Said Rabbi Joshua ben Levi: When Moses descended from Sinai, Satan came before the Holy One, blessed be He, and asked Him: Lord of the universe, Where is the Torah? And He said to him: I gave it to the earth. Then Satan went to the earth and asked: Where is the Torah? And the earth replied: God understandeth the way thereof. Then he went to the ocean, and the ocean answered him, I have it not. Then he went to the deep, and it answered him, It is not in me. Even so it is said: The deep saith, 'It is not in me', and the sea saith, ' I have it not.' Destruction and death said: 'We have heard a rumor thereof with our ears.' Satan then returned to God and said: ' Lord of the universe, I have looked for it through the land, and found it not.' God then said to him: 'It is with the son of Amram.' He then went to Moses, and asked him: 'Where is the Torah which the Holy One, blessed be He, gave to thee?' And Moses said: 'Who am I that the Holy One, blessed be He! would give unto me His Torah? And God said unto Moses: ' Moses, thou art a liar. And Moses said: 'Lord of the universe, such desirable treasure, wherewith Thou dost daily entertain Thyself, how can I claim it for myself?' And God said unto him: ' Because thou didst belittle thyself, the Torah shall be called thine, as it is said, 'Remember ye the law of Moses my servant."

This is the Great Sabbath of the year, the last Sabbath before the Passover holiday is ushered in. It is called the Great Sabbath, according to Midrashic and Rabbinic literature, because of the great miracle which came to pass at the time the tenth plague was brought upon the Egyptians, which happened on the Sabbath before Passover. The miracle was this: The first-born were stricken. They urged the people at large to let Israel go. But the latter said: No, it is to save yourselves that you desire to bring such great loss to Egypt. The argument provoked a civil war, between the Egyptians and the first-born of among them.

Concerning this the Psalmist sang: " He plagued the Egyptians at large by striking down their first-born "-Le'makey Mizraim bi-Behore-hem; so His loving-kindness endureth forever. That was a Great Sabbath, indeed.

Thus in commemeration of the great miracle of the first Great Sabbath we call this day-Sabbath ha-Godol.

But the Torah, too, was given on a Sabbath. And it was on that Sabbath that Satan came to inquire after the welfare of the Torah! A friendlier investigator would have been, I surmise, more to the point. And, Satan, who is ever flying about this earth and traversing it, where was he during the hour of Revelation?

Israel observed the Sabbath before the Torah was given on Sinai. Satan had nothing whatever to do in the camp of Israel on a Sabbath. They were happy to have a day of rest. They just had left their hard labor behind them, and even the tongue of Satan could not make them work. Even the Manna did not fall on Sabbath. "To-day you shall not find it." How many people forget this admonition by thinking that if they add more pipes the vat would deliver more wine.

But Satan was the proper investigator. He witnesses the rise and fall of every man's career upon earth. It is his work to entice, to bring down, and then to gloat over the downfall.

After Moses descended from Sinai, the ex-slaves forgot their erstwhile experiences. The miracles no longer amazed them, no more than any natural phenomenon. Such is the human habit.

Satan came to slander Israel. "Where is the Torah?"

"I gave it to the earth."

The angels can not keep the Torah and its commandments; they have no earthly temptations; they can neither sin, in the sense that man may sin, nor receive reward, in the sense that man may be rewarded.

But Satan came back and reported that he found not a trace of the Torah, save a rumor concerning it, which destruction and death mentioned.

"Go to the son of Amram."

A Rabbi in Israel claims authority direct from Moses. Every Rabbi, therefore, must have a share in the Torah. Satan, of course, omitted all such whom he knew that they were no Rabbis, and he went to the few real scholars among the Rabbis and made inquiry concerning the where-abouts of the Torah.

The true scholar will not claim authority over all Israel, nor allege that the Torah rests within him. Moses answered: "Who am I to lay claim that God gave me the Torah?"

But God said to him: "Moses, thou art a liar!"

"Lord of the universe, I did not deny the existence of the Torah, I merely refused to elevate myself on such height, as to be deserving of such claim," pleaded Moses.

"Then you are the real leader."

Sabbath ha-Godol typifies the leadership of Moses. It was he who understood what Israel's wants were.

Therefore, when the prophet Malachi, wanted to point out the greatest danger in the path of Is rael, he directed the men of his generation toward three cardinal points: (1) The Torah; (2) the spirit of Elijah; (3) linking the generations.

All cultural movements, all plans of a civilized state, all methods of life and living upon earth will crumble under a Torah-less system.

Life and hope will fade to nothingness, without men of spirit, men willing to stand up and defy tyranny, blasphemy, idolatry, and assimilation, the highest example of all these was Elija.

The entire chain of tradition, all interest in a nation's future, all treasures of a nation, accumulated throughout the ages, will become a blank sheet of paper if the father and the son will not understand one another, if their hearts will go apart, if their interests in life will not be united.

\(1\) Remember ye the law of Moses My servant; (2) Behold I will send you Elijah, the prophet; (3) And he shall turn the heart of the father to the children and the heart of the children to the fathers.
