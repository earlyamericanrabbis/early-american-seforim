"And the children struggled together within her. " (Genesis, 25.22).

"Their strife was concerning this world and the world to come. Just then, when this struggle was going on between the children, Samael (Satan) wanted to slay Jacob within his mother's womb; but even then came Michael and opposed it. Moreover, it was then that Michael wanted to destroy Samael by fire. The strife between Michael and Samael continued until the Holy One, blessed be He! appointed a judicial tribunal to judge between them. Then Shem, the son of Noah, told her:

Two nations are in thy womb.

And two peoples shall be separated from thy bowels;

And the one people shall be stronger than the other people;

And the elder shall serve the younger." (Midrash Abkir; Yalkut Shimony, Genesis, 25).

It is related in the Talmudic lore that when Rebekah passed by a synagogue, Jacob troubled her, and when she passed by an idolatrous pillar, Esau troubled her. She thought it was only one child, and that that child had a dual personality, 1 half Hebrew and half pagan. That frightened her. She detested hypocricy. Rather than bring such one in the world it might be best not to suffer him to be born. That prompted her to "inquire of the Lord. " She was satisfied to suffer more pain, the minute she was informed that it was not one child, but twins, a Hebrew and a pagan.

Even till this day that struggle continues, the attraction of the synagogue and the charm of the pagan pillar. When one makes a compromise, and struggles to please both, there is no end to his hypocricy.

We have always had two wings in Jewry, for instance, the Saducees and the Pharasees; but when one wing in Israel claims to represent both, the pain such one produces is really unbearable, and one feels with Rebekaah over much.

It is historically conceivable to find Jewry divided on certain questions of religious practice, such as Reform and Orthodox; but to behold a movement to claim for itself a single creature, a cross between Reform and Orthodoxy, a mixture of synagogue and, lehavdil, a pagan pillar, and claim for it the sanctity of a unit, one is tempted to wish it a still birth, or, rather, a "kissing death" -Mitat Neshikah, be it Conservatism, Modernism, Progressive, or Cultural. A half lie is not the truth; it is often worse than wholly false, because it is misleading, deceiving and destructive.

Samael and Michael, angels both, but of different conceptions as to what is and what is not Godly, holy, or pure.

The creative power is not stronger than the destructive power. There is an ever-present destructive element in whatever the productive element begins to form. Life around us holds out exemplary laws out of which we may learn to know ourselves, our nature, our goal. Every particle in the biological make-up of the universe offers a parallelism to the highest forms of life. It is because of it that scientists are often misled by believing the example to be the very thing itself. The frog is like the human being in structure; but it is not human. It is a parallelism; we derive many anatomacil lessons from its structure, and utilize them for the benefit of mankind. But one who knows the image of a human being merely from a word picture of its anatomical structure, for example, an inhabitant of Mars, could mistake a frog for a human being, if it be a dead frog, or if the inhabitants of Mars do not know that human being have intelligence, speak, and have hair on their head and several other important characteristics which separates the human being from the frog. The same error might fall within the path of a scientist who grapples in the dark concerning matters which he can not fathom. What we consider to-day miraculous might be quite natural tomorrow; all it requires is to remove the gross cover from our eyes. Even natural science has been but touched by the physicist. What wonders nature has in store for the human mind can be but a remote prophecy by the most imaginary mind.

The same is true of life itself, as the human being now lives it. The difference between the viewpoints of ancient Jacob and his twin brother Esau may appear in its true light only thousands of years hence. The existence of time as such, save that it is immortal, does not affect the mysteries of our Torah, or its admonitions, which are, apparently, understood by all as given out in human speech. But who can fathom the exact meaning of some consonants, or identify names, places, words, and ideas mentioned in the Torah, without an unbounded faith in the Masoretic text, as transmitted to us in the original by the succeeding schools since Revelation?

What prompted the sages of Midrash to bring in Samael and Michael in the struggle between Jacob and Esau within their mother's womb? And why could they not have been stopped quarreling without a judicial tribunal set up by Almighty Himself? Particularly why was the decision of that heavenly tribunal withheld from us, save the surmise that no murder was committed either by Samael who wanted to slay Jacob, or by Michael who wanted to burn Samael?

It is an outstanding fact that whenever the sages found the Peshat of a given text satisfactory they did not employ Derash merely for the sake of elaboration, or to sermonize and receive hire-Derosh Vekabel Sekhar. We do not find a single instance of it, even for the sake of increasing learning and adorning it-Yagdil Torah Veyaadir. When some passage was interpreted on the premise of Derash, there was either a philological or other textual reason for it.

The struggle of Jacob and Esau, was staged upon a background which reaches out to unrecorded history. Two currents of civilization are evolved before the student of antiquity; one based upon naturalism, the other upon revelation. Cain and Abel, Enosh and Enoch, Eber and Nimrod, Abraham and Lot, Isaac and Ishmael, are names which define the cross-roads of antiquity upon which the two currents of early civilization found their respective following.

Man's make-up is the climax of creation; its complexities and possibilities will continue to be the most perplexing problem under the sun. We find in man the lowest instincts of beasts of prey and the highest intelligence attributed to spiritual beings.

Rebekah had a physical experience of the problem; she was the sister of Laban and the wife of Isaac. She did not know whose power was predominant within her, when she was conceived with child.

"Two nations are in thy womb,

And two peoples shall be separated from thy

bowels;

And the one people shall be stronger than the

other people;

And the elder shall serve the younger." Rebekah then realized that she would become the mother of two great forces in the future of the world's civilization; she realized that within her were the natural and the metaphysical, the beast and the man.

From their embryonic life and onward the two forces could not follow one and the same course. The soul of man is given to him as soon as life is felt by the mother. According to the Talmudic sages, the unborn child, while its body develops, receives spiritual instruction in all that a composite being might be able to grasp during a life-time. The child forgets all that at birth. The man must work for all he is to obtain in life.

Jacob and Esau struggled "concerning this world and the world-to-come." It was not a struggle over material things. It is within the power of mankind to break down and forget everything of a spiritual character. But the two unborn brothers were incapable to slay one another. Samael, the arch-materialist, Satan who desires only the natural state in the life and career of man, an instinct born in all of us, and an instinct not easy to control but easy to bring it into practical use, wanted to slay Jacob within his mother's womb!

Where the material things are held out as the sole goal of man's life upon earth, Samael is successful in slaying Jacob within his mother's womb. By bringing up a generation without imparting to it the value of the spiritual things in life, Esau's triumph over Jacob, his struggle for only this world, and the complete defeat of the world-to-come, of the future, is in the hand of Samael.

Michael intervenes on behalf of Jacob; he wants to slay Samael by means of fire. And that would not solve the problem of mankind upon earth. The element of fire alone, the spiritual only, has no need for aught that this massive universe can produce, or be interesting to mere spirits. It would be another extreme, and would defeat the very purpose of creation.

A judicial tribunal was established between Samael and Michael. Justice is the only arbiter possible under the conditions which life continues upon earth. The struggle between Jacob and Esau will continue throughout time; but with justice, life upon earth might be made more interesting, more satisfactory, more God-like.
