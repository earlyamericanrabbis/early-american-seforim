"Ye shall dwell in booths seven days."

(Lev. 23.42.)

The sukko is a temporary booth, constructed of any sort of material which grows from the ground, and covered with any kind of roof which permits the stars to pierce it through so that the occupants can see them. It must not be wholly covered; nor must it be left mostly uncovered; the shade must be stronger than the light. "This is done because the children of Israel lived in booths when they went forth out of Egyptian bondage."

The Sukko reveals many inspiring thoughts to the thinking mind. Are we all not living in Sukkot, in temporary booths? Are not our lives like unto the Feast of Sukkot? Are not the palaces standing as monuments throughout the ages as testimony that the succeeding occupants were in reality living in booths?

Has not Israel's entire forward-march across the ages been but a festival of Sukkot? He lives in temporary quarters, in semi-covered Sukkot, where the winds strike first, where the elements find convenience for their fury, where insecurity is a certainty, where life is most uncertain. Yea, in every country where Israel was tolerated, and even increased and expanded to golden proportions, it lasted but a brief span, it was but a mere holiday of Sukkot. But the Sukko-minded people will be rewarded. "And there shall be a Sukko for a shadow;" Rabbi Levi said: "Whosoever observes the precept concerning Sukko in this world, God says this of him: He observed the precept of Sukko, I shall cover him with protection from the heat of the-day-to-come."

The festival of joy; the very day we celebrate as the "season of our joy" (Ze'man Simha-teinu) is surrounded by a token of temporariness, to teach us that our temporal existence is ever unsafe, ever more dark than light, ever exposed to the dangers of the elements.

But this very knowledge is the greatest reason of our joy. Our body, the clay wherein our soul is lodged, too, is but a temporary structure, subject to all the ills and diseases which fill this life. Our soul knows that its stay here is but seven days, or seven decades; it is but a mere visitor in the earthly habitation God provided for it; yet, if we do not abuse our soul, if we tend it with the care of the precepts of God, it spends three score and ten years together with us, becomes part of us, our very selves, and our consciousness never parts from it, nor its consciousness from us even unto the end of time.

We do greet and entertain daily distinguished visitors in our Sukko. They come to greet us, Abraham, David and a whole host of them. We see them not; but we know they are with us; we feel their presence; without them our Sukko would long since have been crumbled and buried us beneath it. They come to shield us from the flames of our enemies, to stand as a guard over us so that the burning anger of any future foes will not consume us.

Wisely and truly did the prophet Zechariah sum up the significance of the " Pavilion of peace," saying: "And it shall come to pass, that every one that is left of all the nations that came against Jerusalem shall go up from year to year to worship the King, the Lord of hosts, and to keep the feast of tabernacles. "
