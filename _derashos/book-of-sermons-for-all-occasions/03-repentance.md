"Return, O Israel, unto the Lord thy God; For thou hast stumbled in thine iniquity... I will be as the dew unto Israel; He shall blossom as the lily, And cast forth his roots as Lebanon. "

(Hosea, 14.2-6)

"Said Israel to the Holy One, blessed be He! 'Lord of the universe, when wilt Thou redeem us?' Said the Holy One, blessed be He: 'When ye shall go down to the nethermost rung of the ladder, even in that moment will I redeem you'... And the Holy One, blessed be He, moreover, said: 'It all depends on you. Even as the lily blossoms forth, while its heart remains upward, so it shall be with you; when ye repent, and your heart shall be upward, directed toward Me, as in the heart of the lily, I shall forthwith redeem you, even as it is said: 'I will be as the dew unto Israel.' When? Even when it will blossom forth as the lily."

(Midrash Shohar Tob; Yalkut Shimony,

Prophets, 533.)

"Jeremiah said: 'Out of the mouth of the Most High proceedeth not the evil and the good.' Mai-moni, thereupon says: "The Creator decrees not that man should be either good or bad. Now, this being so, the consequence thereof is, that the sinner alone brought harm upon himself'... Now do not wonder and ask: 'How is it possible for man to do what his heart desires, and have his entire course of action lodged within himself, seeing that he can not do aught in the world without the permission of his Master and His will, even as the Psalmist says: 'Whatsoever the Lord pleased, that hath He done, in heaven and in earth, in the seas and in all the deeps. Know all: that what man does is in accordance with His will, although our actions are really in our own keeping. Even as is the Creator's will that fire and air should ascend upward, and that water and earth should descend downward, and that the sphere should revolve in a circle, and that the other creatures of the universe should likewise follow their respective natural laws, as it was His will for them to be so, so it was His will that man should have the free choice of conduct in his own hand, and that all of his actions should be lodged within him, and that he should be neither forced nor drawn, but, of his own free will and accord, as God endowed him with, he should exercise in all that is possible for him to do. He is, therefore, judged according to his actions; if he did good, he is rewarded with good; if he did wrong, he is punished."

Malachi said: "This hath been of your own doing; " Isaiah said: "According as they have chosen their own ways;" and Solomon said: "Rejoice, O young man, in thy youth, and let thy heart cheer thee in the days of thy youth, and walk in the ways of thy heart, and in the sight of thine eyes; but know thou, that for all these things God will bring thee into judgment."

If man had no self-will, if Judaism would have followed the doctrine of pre-destination, how could the assertion be made that "God will bring thee into judgment?" Can the forces of nature be held accountable for manifesting themselves as they do, and, oftentimes, bring death and destruction in their wake?

This doctrine proclaims that man is the free agent of his will, within the prescribed limitations, singular to his being, in accordance with the powers God had endowed him at the time of Creation. Man has the possibilities, within his own being, to reach out to heights no other creature can attain. Man's soul is gifted with that heavenly share of intelligence which may comprehend things and objects independent of his nature; he may even chain the forces of his nature and subdue them, and become a being of a much higher intelligence than his own environment, than this sphere, affords. Man may attain prophecy, a degree higher than the uttermost forces of nature, than science in its very extreme, can ever reach. But all of these possibilities in man's soul are direct endowments of God. The exercise thereof, the utilization of these forces in his soul, are man's own; he may become a saint, if he follows the path of good, or a devil, if he follows the path of evil.

Life's threads are made of godly material; but the weaving of the threads into a human garb is in the hands of man; the warp and woof, the shuttlecock and the battle-dore, the spinning jenny and the spinning-jack are man-made. A man may take the finest wool and make rags out of it, or make it useless altogether.

According to the doctrines of Judaism, man's place in nature is to improve, to augment, to make things more beautiful, to remove the coarseness, to refine, to elevate. He is happy in the making of it; makes others happy in setting up a fine example; and reaches a goal of heavenly bliss.

The vineyard of the Lord of hosts must ever be kept free of all ugliness, clear of stones, planted with the choicest seed, a watch-tower in its midst, and a vat hewen out therein. Israel was intended to be the spiritual people of the world; every member of Israel an integral part thereof. His land was to be a holy land; his language, a holy language; his span of life, measured by holy time. It was within his hand to make it so. God gave him the heavenly threads, the silken web; he was to weave for himself that coat of many colors which would proclaim to the world that he is beloved by his Heavenly Father more than the rest of His sons on earth.

We have made a spiritual inventory during Rosh-ha-Shanah; we are now observing the Sabbath of Repentance. What improvements in our own lives, or in the lives of our neighbors, were we able to accomplish since the last Sabbath of Repentance? Have we made the world a more fit place to live in? Have we achieved that which is fit to die for?

We have all material at our disposal; God gave it to us, freely, bountefully. We have our share of the dew of heaven; we have our Torah, our synagogues, our houses of learning; our spiritual guides, our history, our martyrdom to draw upon for inspiration. The work of making ourselves better, of making this earth more beautiful, of clearing away the stubble and refining the kernel, of utilizing our power to set the spiritual wheels in motion and make our spindles produce a true heavenly garb and not a rag, soiled with the filth of gutters that is in our hand. We can blossom as the lily; the dew of heaven is ready for us; all we need is to cultivate our lives, and fill our garden with lilies instead of with thorns and thistles. To attain such goal we must direct our heart upward, heavenward.
