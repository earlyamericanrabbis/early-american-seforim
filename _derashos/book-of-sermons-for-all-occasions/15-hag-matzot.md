**II.**

In the first month, on the fourteenth day of the month at even, ye shall eat unleavened bread." (12.18).

"Said Raba: It is necessary that every one should say: "And us He delivered thencefrom. " (Pesahim, 116b).

Bread of affliction-Lehem Oyni-is the symbol of Passover. Bread of affliction, when partaken of at a time when liberty is at hand, is only a reminder, something like a hunger-banquet in an American hotel, partaken of by millionaires, in order to give atmosphere to the purpose of the banquet, which is to raise funds for the famine-stricken victims of the late world-war. The participants in such banquet can well afford to partake of a hunger-banquet.

The refugees of Egypt ate with relish the hast ily prepared, unleavened bread. They had a pursuing enemy; but they headed toward Canaan.

But to eat bread of affliction because there is no other bread, and no other food either, is the meal of a sufferer.

Bread of affliction, when partaken of for the sake of an ideal, is more than bread. It is the ideal incarnate.

One may enjoy a full measure of liberty being burnt at the stake-the victim offers his all for his ideal; he knows that that will not die.

Martyrdom is a suffering which multiplies myriads of free men. In all history we find no better incentive for the propogation of a cause than martyrdom. A hunger-strike brought about complete liberty for the Irish nation; the martyrs of every revolution were the forerunners of a better civilization.

The bread of affliction which we started to eat during the Exodus never left our table. In the centuries of our national career in Palestine, both during the first and the second Temples, we continued to eat bread of affliction.

In diaspora we have nothing else.

It is a mandatory commandment to eat some bread of affliction the first Seder Night. But we must say:

"And us He delivered thencefrom. "

The holiday would mean much less without saying this.

We shall not continue to eat bread of affliction. We ate it for the first time when liberty was ahead of us; we will eat it once more when an everlasting liberty will be ahead of us there where the sun appears, where stood the cradle of civilization-in the holy of holies of this earth-even in Eretz Israel.
