**I.**

"In the seventh month, on the first day of the month, shall ye have a Sabbath, a memorial of blowing of trumpets, an holy convocation." (Levi. 23. 24.)

"R. Judah bar Nahman, in the name of Reish Lakish, opened his sermon with this text: 'God is gone up amidst shouting; The Lord amidst the sound of the horn': God ascends upon the Seat of Judgment, under the stern rules of the Law, even as it says: 'God is gone up amidst shouting'. But when Israel takes the Shophar, and its blasts go forth, the Holy One, blessed be He!, rises from the Throne of Law, and takes up the Seat of Mercy, even as it says: The Lord amidst the sound of the horn, and immediately the Measure of Law is turned into the Measure of Mercy. And this takes place in the seventh month." (Pe'sikta; Yalkut Shimony; Psalms 645.)

Are the heavens different to-day than they were yesterday? Has there taken place any extraordinary sign, symbol, token, or any change whatever in nature, which, if so, would distinguish this day from any other day in the year? And, What is Time? We count seconds, minutes, hours, days, weeks, months, years, Sabbatical years and Jubilee years. We speak of the times long past as the "olden times ", and of our own lives we say: When we were young, in our younger days. Why? is the world growing younger, and we older?

Time does exist, in the sense that it is infinite. Time does not pass. We do not kill, for we can not, kill time. We do many things in order "to pass the time away", or, to be more colloquially correct, to "kill time".

In reality none of this exists; it is only our fancy which creates such ideas, or notions in our heads, or, rather, in our hearts.

We pass; we are not killing time, if we engage ourselves in folly, but we do kill ourselves. Every day we pass on; we come so much nearer our graves.

On our journey through life mile-posts have been set up for us, as signs, or tokens, to remind us that we have already travelled part of our way. We stop there, at the mile-posts, to rest, to reflect, to render an accounting of the past, and to make plans for the future.

We must know the position of the mile-post, or we miss it, and be left wandering. The first day of Tishri was set apart as an important mile-post in our lives. The calendar, Sabbatical, Jubilee and agricultural year ends with the last day of Ellul, and the New year is ushered in on the First Day of Tishri.

One continuous day (Yoma Arih'ta) of forty-eight hours, was designated as our New Year. Not to keep the time accurate, but ourselves; not to prevent the minutes from encrouching upon the hours in a false calculation, but to prevent ourselves from making a false step; not to recommence our journey before proper and complete preparations are made.

According to the Talmudic authorities, Rosh-ha-Shanah is not appointed in heaven; when the Beth-din beneath the heavan, the earthly authorities declare: This day is Rosh-ha-Shanah; the Heavenly Court is ordered in session. In every man's conscience there exists such court. Books of life and death are produced. We visualize such books in our minds. The weighing of good and evil, not by man's but by God's own standard, takes place. Human conception is so full of errors and grossness that at the very opening of the Heavenly court the evil seems to outweigh the good manifold. The dread which fills every one's heart is for the decision, which must come, measure for measure, on the scale of justice. God's Throne is then erected on a Dias of Law.

But the ram's-horn trumpet changes the introspection to retrospection. The blasts penetrate the mind and conscience of every individual. What does it all mean? Why a trumpet from an animal? Why from a ram? Why number the sounds? Why change the notes, which are no notes?

At night, the stillness of nature carries the voice of a crying babe much further than the notes of the best musical instrument in the day-time. During all dark ages, beginning with the age of Abraham, when all mankind was grappling in the dark, the carriers of the Light of God were the ever-ready scapegoat. Israel was the world's scapegoat throughout the ages, and is so now. It is still dark. The light of the Torah pierced through but little of the folded darkness of the nations. Prejudice and bigotry still find ears and sanction. And God seats upon His mighty Throne of justice.

Then the ram's-horn, the emblem of the everlasting scapegoat, is taken up, and its unmusical notes begin piercing the heavens; the spiritual waves carry the blasts to the farthest ends of the universe. Every heart becomes heavy. Lo, what a true, what a loyal people Israel has been, and is!

The Holy One, blessed be He! can not continue to judge each member individually, and have evil turn the balance of justice in condemnation for wrongs done. Instead, He rises from the Throne of Law, and takes up the Seat of Mercy. The sacrifices made in the past, the steadfastness of the present, demand justice tempered with mercy for the future..
