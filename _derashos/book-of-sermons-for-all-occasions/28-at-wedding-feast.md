**II.**

Mr. Toastmaster, Bride and Groom, Ladies and

Gentlemen:

We are partaking of some of the joys of the people mostly concerned in this affair. We are indulging quite freely in a manner to show that we expected it. Each one of us brought along a genuine and sincere appetite, and we are now proudly manifesting that our profoundest expectations are being realized.

But in partaking of a feast of the Bride and Groom, we were admonished by the sages, we must be careful to add as much joy to the occasion as we can find at our command. Of course, the occasion entirely belongs to the bride and groom; we are here as spectators, to make an audience, to applaud, to show appreciation, and to add color to the festive occasion for the happiness and enter tainment of the newly-married couple. That is just the reason why we call a wedding-feast -Se'udat Mitzvah.

Once upon a time we had a very bad alien princes, Izebel, wife of Ahab. As queen she courted the false prophets who permitted, or rather encouraged her, to her heart's content, to worship idolatry, and to do away with the true prophets. She died an un-natural death; dogs devoured her; but her hands and feet remained untouched. The dogs spared them. And, our Midrashists tell us the reason why. Whenever she saw a wedding procession passing by her palace, she came out on the balcony and clapped her hands and tapped with her feet to the music.

But we are here near the bride and groom; enjoying ourselves because of their happiness, sending them off on their journey of life in a happy mood, as an omen that happiness be theirs throughout the journey. That is a real Mitzvah.

I know that the groom will find in his life's companion all and more than he anticipates now. And, she is deserving to have this upstanding young man from our midst as her life's guide and protector. They both are a pride to their people.

In saying Mazol Tov to them, I merely voice the sentiments I long ago cherished in my heart to be able to say it at their wedding. May they find the full measure of joy and happiness they both so richly deserve.
